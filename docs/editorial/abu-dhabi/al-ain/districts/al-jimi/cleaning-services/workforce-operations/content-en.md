# Workforce and operational staffing in Al Jimi

Preparatory guidance for company and establishment services, not confirmation of staff, provider availability or an appointment. Verify the supplying company's credentials and applicable labour and site permissions before engagement.

## 1. Cleaning staff supply

**When to request it:** A site needs recurring cleaners rather than a one-off deep-clean team.

**Photos, quantities and specifications:** Provide floor areas, washroom counts, supply-store photos and occupied hours; state whether carts, consumables and floor machines are supplied on site.

**Scope and exclusions:** Define zones, daily checklists and absence cover; high-level facade cleaning and pest treatment are separate scopes.

**Quote comparison and agreement:** Compare productive hours, supervision, consumables, handover and attendance evidence; assign responsibility for keys, stores and incident reports.

**Operating precautions:** Agree rest breaks, drinking-water access and chemical storage. Require training against mixing cleaning chemicals and on safe handling of sharps or suspicious waste. Working at height or in confined spaces must not be assigned to ordinary cleaners without separate competence and controls.

**Acceptance checks:** Match attendance to washroom, touchpoint and floor checks; log missed tasks before signing off a shift.

**Quotation inputs:** Specify cleaners per shift, working days and holidays, absence cover, and who supplies consumables and PPE. Ask for supervision and materials to be itemised separately from staffing.

## 2. Loading staff supply

**When to request it:** Moving heavy furniture or cartons requires planning around weights, stairs and carrying distances.

**Photos, quantities and specifications:** Send an item list, largest dimensions and estimated weights, plus corridor, lift and truck-bay photos.

**Scope and exclusions:** Specify loading, unloading, safe handling and protection; trucks, hoists and packing are not automatically included.

**Quote comparison and agreement:** Compare crew size, experience, dollies, overtime and damage responsibility; agree movement routes and stop-work conditions.

**Handling precautions:** Crew size must not be based on weight alone: assess grip points, load stability, lift capacity, stair routes and the need for dollies or qualified lifting equipment. Stop manual handling if no safe route or method is available rather than asking workers to carry an unsuitable load.

**Acceptance checks:** Count items and inspect edges, floors and corners after handling; record damage immediately.

**Quotation inputs:** State item counts, heaviest weights, longest carrying distances, floors and loading windows. Request crew and waiting-hour rates, with packing or hoists separately priced where needed.

## 3. Maintenance technician supply

**When to request it:** Repeated electrical, HVAC or plumbing faults call for a trade-qualified technician, not a generic worker for every system.

**Photos, quantities and specifications:** Provide equipment lists, fault codes, external panel or unit photos and maintenance history without opening hazardous equipment.

**Scope and exclusions:** Define permitted inspection and maintenance tasks and isolation authority; parts, permits and major repairs are separate unless stated.

**Quote comparison and agreement:** Compare trade credentials, test instruments, callout response and records; require approval for additional repairs.

**Acceptance checks:** Request a fault report, safe test readings, completed work and next actions, with agreed operational checks.

**Quotation inputs:** Name the required trade, system counts and locations, coverage hours and recurring faults. Ask for callout labour, parts, materials and after-hours response to be priced separately.

## 4. Site supervisor supply

**When to request it:** Multiple site crews make quality, safety coordination and area handover a distinct role.

**Photos, quantities and specifications:** State crew numbers, site layout, working hours, inspection points, reporting format and supervisor authority.

**Scope and exclusions:** The role can coordinate schedules, checks and records; it is not automatically a licensed engineer or certifying consultant.

**Quote comparison and agreement:** Compare project experience, coverage, escalation and reporting frequency; document sign-off and stop-work authority.

**Acceptance checks:** Review snag logs, corrective actions, meeting attendance and area handover status.

**Quotation inputs:** Supply the project programme, crew count, inspection checkpoints and reporting requirements. Specify continuous presence or periodic visits and request clear supervisor authority and overtime rates.

## 5. Residential compound operations

**When to request it:** Residential compounds need coordinated shared-area services and resident requests without intruding on private units.

**Photos, quantities and specifications:** Provide building and unit counts, common facilities, service layout, ticket history and quiet hours.

**Scope and exclusions:** Define ticket intake, common-area cleaning, maintenance coordination and escalation; in-unit work and specialist security require separate terms.

**Quote comparison and agreement:** Compare helpdesk hours, ticket closure evidence, data privacy and emergency coverage; name subcontractor accountability.

**Acceptance checks:** Sample closed tickets, common areas, maintenance logs and overdue explanations before accepting reports.

**Quotation inputs:** Give unit, building and shared-facility counts, helpdesk hours and required service measures. Ask for shared-area operations, in-unit maintenance and emergencies to be priced distinctly.

## 6. Commercial facility operations

**When to request it:** Retail and commercial facilities need service windows that respect visitors and deliveries.

**Photos, quantities and specifications:** Provide circulation plans, entrances, peak hours, tenant counts, shared equipment and permit requirements.

**Scope and exclusions:** Define common-area cleaning, ticket handling and contractor scheduling; tenant fit-outs or system replacements are not automatic.

**Quote comparison and agreement:** Compare off-peak scheduling, barriers, tenant notices and leak response; agree incident reporting.

**Acceptance checks:** Check safe access routes, entrance condition, fault reports and closed work orders.

**Quotation inputs:** Identify common areas, tenant numbers, peak periods, permit needs and overnight service windows. Ask for shift cover, tenant coordination and incident response as separate cost items.

## 7. Project support services

**When to request it:** Projects may need logistics, inventory and housekeeping support without assigning unqualified technical work.

**Photos, quantities and specifications:** Send storage layouts, material lists, delivery volumes, gate photos, delivery windows and lifting hazards.

**Scope and exclusions:** Define stocktaking, sorting, internal distribution and non-hazardous housekeeping; crane operation, electrical and structural work are not implicit.

**Quote comparison and agreement:** Compare stock accountability, loss reporting, PPE, shifts and damage notifications; fix each shift's scope.

**Acceptance checks:** Reconcile receipt and issue sheets to stock, check clear walkways and close exception logs.

**Quotation inputs:** Share delivery volumes, storage areas, distribution points and project duration. Request separate inventory, handling and shift costs, excluding equipment operation requiring specialist competence or permits from general support labour; include it only under a separate scope with suitably qualified personnel and verified licences and permits.
