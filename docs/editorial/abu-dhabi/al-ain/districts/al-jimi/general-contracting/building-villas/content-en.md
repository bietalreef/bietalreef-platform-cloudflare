# Building and villa construction in Al Jimi — practical guide for company enquiries

This is planning information for owners and project teams, not a statement that a contractor is currently listed or available. Structural decisions, permits and safety-critical checks require the relevant qualified professionals and applicable approvals.

## 1. Villa construction

This service covers the overall villa construction contract and its design, structural, finishing and handover responsibilities; the separate new-villa service focuses on early layout, privacy and shading choices. A family planning a villa should settle bedroom count, majlis access, parking and privacy before asking builders to price a design.

**What to document and measure:** Provide plot boundaries, approved architectural and structural drawings where available, floor areas, soil report and site-access photographs. Mark road-to-plot level differences, excavation close to neighbours, and mixer and concrete-pump access. Ask for separate allowances for foundations, shell, waterproofing, finishes and utility connections.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare concrete and steel specifications, nominated finishes, provisional sums and whether design coordination, permits, boundary walls and landscaping are excluded. Identify who supplies materials, coordinates design and permits, supervises the structure and finishes, and pays for testing and utility connections. Record sample approvals and reinforcement inspection hold points before casting.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Before certifying a stage payment, reconcile executed quantities with signed measurement records; do not accept concealed work without its earlier inspection record. Ask the appointed qualified consultant to review soil and foundation records, reinforcement hold-point inspections before casting, concrete test reports and waterproofing tests before backfilling or concealment. At handover compare column grids, openings and levels with approved drawings, document defects and rectification deadlines, and identify who pays for retesting. This checklist does not replace professional sign-off or authority inspections.

**Quote request:** Attach the room schedule, floor areas, available approved plans and site-access photographs. Request separate foundation, structural and finishing quantities, plus an explicit included/excluded schedule for boundary walls, external paving, landscaping, electricity, water and drainage connections and their fees. Identify who arranges each connection and at what stage; do not assume they are included in a floor-area rate.

## 2. Residential building construction

For a multi-unit residential building, the number of apartments is only part of the scope: stairs, lifts, parking, shared meters and service shafts affect cost and coordination.

**What to document and measure:** Supply unit schedules, floor plans, lift requirements, access constraints and drawings of common areas. Identify shared pump and electrical rooms, service risers and heavy-equipment access. Clarify fire-safety design and approval responsibilities with qualified professionals.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare shell-and-core versus fully finished unit pricing, common-area finishes, mechanical and electrical systems, testing, commissioning and authority-related exclusions. Assign responsibility for coordinating lift, ventilation and fire-safety tests, and for resolving conflicts between apartment services and shared risers before ceilings and walls are closed.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Keep separate defect and completion registers for each apartment and for common areas, identifying corrective owners; completion of one unit does not establish building-wide readiness for occupation. Before closing service shafts and suspended ceilings, compare riser routes, access hatches and maintainable equipment clearances with coordinated drawings and inspection records. At handover, check corridor lighting, shared ventilation, pumps, isolation valves and control-panel access; obtain the required lift and life-safety test records from qualified parties and relevant authorities. Log defects by common area and require documented retesting rather than treating a visual walk-through as statutory approval.

**Quote request:** Attach the apartment count and area schedule, parking, stair and lift drawings and shared plant-room plans. Request separate prices for apartments, common-area finishes, lifts, shared mechanical/electrical and life-safety systems, and commissioning. Identify apartment fit-out boundaries, permit and utility-connection responsibilities and fees, written exclusions, and staged handover dates.

## 3. Commercial building construction

A retail or office building must fit its intended occupancy, deliveries, tenant access and future fit-out rather than merely resemble a reference façade.

**What to document and measure:** Provide the proposed use and floor-by-floor tenancy area schedule, clear heights, loading-bay and delivery-door dimensions, floor loading requirements, façade drawings and allocated electricity, water, drainage and ventilation interfaces for each unit. Photograph adjoining roads, delivery access and boundaries; distinguish design assumptions from confirmed tenant requirements.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Distinguish base-building work from tenant fit-out, signage, specialist ventilation and utility upgrades. Record the intended use of each commercial floor, responsibility for fire-safety and exit approvals, the testing of tenant service connection points and the agreed handover stages. Require a written schedule of finishes and interfaces.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Record each tenancy's service handover interface separately, including the agreed supply capacity; do not assume the base-building provision suits any later tenant use without qualified review. Measure clear shop heights, delivery-door widths, service corridors and façade openings against approved drawings. Confirm that each tenancy has its agreed electrical, water, drainage and ventilation connection points, with test records and applicable fire-stopping at penetrations. Record whether handover covers the base building only; tenant fit-out and any separate approvals must not be silently treated as complete.

**Quote request:** Attach intended floor uses, tenancy areas, clear heights, loading and entrance layouts, and service plans. Request separate shell-and-core and tenant-fit-out pricing, including signage and specialist ventilation exclusions. Identify who provides and tests each tenant connection point, who secures the applicable safety approvals, and which future works and fees are excluded.

## 4. General building works

Masonry work can look straightforward but misaligned openings or poorly coordinated services cause expensive rework after plastering.

**What to document and measure:** Supply a wall layout with lengths, heights, block types and thicknesses, distinguishing internal from external walls and any wall needing special ties or insulation details. Photograph door and window openings, adjoining columns and beams, material access, storage and scaffold locations. Lintel, tying and local reinforcement details must be checked against approved drawings by the responsible specialist rather than inferred from photographs.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Ask bidders to apply the same opening-deduction rule and state whether wall area is measured on one face or by another agreed bill-of-quantities convention. Separate block supply from laying, mortar, lintels, ties, local reinforcement, scaffolding, delivery and debris removal. Agree written approval before changes to wall height or opening position, and allocate responsibility for clashes with electrical and plumbing routes rather than leaving chasing and making-good as an undefined allowance.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Before plastering, measure wall locations and thicknesses, clear door and window openings and lintel levels against approved drawings. Check plumb, course alignment and ties to structural elements; photograph cracks or gaps at block-to-column junctions. Have the responsible specialist confirm electrical and plumbing chase positions before cutting. Record nonconforming work, who will remedy it and the reinspection date. Visual checks do not constitute structural approval.

**Quote request:** Send approved wall layouts, the opening schedule and access photographs. Ask bidders to return a comparable quantity sheet showing gross and net wall areas, the opening-deduction convention, the programme and the block-delivery sequence. Require explicit inclusions or exclusions for block-to-frame junction treatment, permitted service chasing and making good, and waste removal. Changes to openings or service chases need the responsible technical approval first; quantity differences should be documented as written variations before work proceeds.

## 5. Concrete works

Concrete pricing depends on the actual elements, specified mix, access for delivery and the planned pour sequence.

**What to document and measure:** Provide structural drawings, element volumes, reinforcement schedules, formwork details, access photos and the consultant's concrete specification.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Price concrete supply, reinforcement fixing, formwork, pumping, sampling and laboratory testing, curing, night pours and rejected loads separately. Record responsibility for delays, mix rejection and pre-pour reinforcement approval.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Obtain pre-pour formwork, reinforcement, concrete-cover and service-embed inspection records. Match delivery tickets to the approved mix and sampling/test logs; record pour sequence, construction joints, curing and formwork striking under the responsible engineer's instructions. Refer visible honeycombing or cracking for qualified assessment before concealment or repair; appearance alone does not establish concrete strength.

**Quote request:** Attach a pour schedule showing each element, its volume, the approved concrete grade, permitted construction-joint locations and pump access. Ask bidders to separate concrete supply, reinforcement, formwork, pumping, laboratory sampling and testing, and curing. Identify who bears delivery delays, rejected loads and retesting costs. Require pre-pour inspection records, a curing plan and a qualified engineer's approval of any honeycombing repair method; this guidance is not engineering approval.

## 6. Structural frame construction

**When this service is needed and what it covers:** Structural frame construction involves foundations, columns, beams and slabs built to approved drawings, with coordinated formwork, temporary supports and reserved service openings. Structural design, drawing revisions, blockwork and finishes are not automatically included; the contract must define these boundaries.

**Evidence, dimensions and specifications:** Supply the soil report, revision-controlled structural drawings, grid and floor levels, reinforcement schedules and reserved HVAC, plumbing and electrical penetrations. Photograph access routes, reinforcement storage and crane or pump positions; identify adjoining buildings and site movement restrictions.

**Compare quotations and agree responsibilities:** Request separate measured quantities for concrete, reinforcement, formwork, scaffolding, surveying, pumping and tests, including the basis of measurement and temporary works. Identify who approves shop drawings and changes, resolves service-opening conflicts, supplies cranes and access equipment, and authorizes the pour and formwork striking sequence under the responsible engineer.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Before each pour, have the consultant document surveyed grids and levels, bar sizes and spacing, cover, reserved penetrations and temporary supports. Retain floor-specific sample and test results, curing records and approved defect-remediation reports. Do not remove props or conceal work before required hold-point checks and technical approvals; this editorial checklist does not replace professional sign-off.

**Quote request:** Send the approved frame drawings, element-by-floor schedule, pour volumes and site-access constraints. Request a staged offer separating supply, installation, formwork, propping, surveying and testing, with pre-pour hold points, service-opening responsibilities, written exclusions and variations, and floor-by-floor handover evidence.

## 7. Turnkey project delivery

A turnkey promise is only meaningful when the client and contractor agree exactly what is ready for use at handover.

**What to document and measure:** Prepare a room-by-room finish schedule with measured areas and sample references. Specify kitchen appliances, air conditioning, lighting, sanitary fittings, external works, connection fees and any owner-supplied equipment; record model numbers and commissioning requirements where known.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Agree binding material grades, substitution approvals, sample sign-off dates and payment milestones. Itemise kitchens, appliances, AC, lighting and sanitary fittings separately from loose furniture; identify owner-supplied items and contractor installation duties. State whether permits, connection charges, meter activation, boundary walls, external paving and pools are included, and allocate commissioning, final cleaning, warranty files and as-built documentation before handover.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Reconcile the approved finish schedule room by room; witness operation of the contracted AC, lighting, pumps and appliances with the responsible specialists. Record model numbers, warranties, commissioning results and outstanding snags, with an agreed remedy before final sign-off.

**Quote request:** Send a room-by-room schedule of measured areas, finishes and approved samples, specifying brands, models or measurable performance grades for equipment and fittings. Require separate prices for supply, installation, commissioning, testing, handover documents and warranties, with explicit responsibility for fitting owner-supplied items, utility connection and meter activation charges, and external works. Link final payment to applicable documented electrical, water, AC and waterproofing tests, as-built drawings, operating manuals, warranties and a dated defects list identifying repair responsibility and reinspection dates. “Ready to occupy” alone is not an acceptance specification.

## 8. New villa construction

This service helps an owner turn the needs of a new household into a measurable construction brief before appointing a contractor. Unlike the broader “Villa construction” service, which concentrates on the overall delivery contract, the early decisions here concern separate guest and family circulation, parking, service access, window privacy and shading. A possible future extension is not permission to build extra foundations or alter structural openings; its design and approvals must be assessed separately.

**What to document and measure:** Gather the available plot documents, survey, approved setback information and soil report if available. Prepare a room schedule with approximate areas, door and window orientation, vehicle access widths and proposed utility connection points. Photograph the plot from all four sides, including road levels and visible access constraints. Ask the responsible professional to confirm levels and applicable requirements rather than treating photographs as a survey. Specify intended façade, glazing and insulation performance rather than selecting products solely by appearance.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Require each bidder to state whether design coordination, permissions, structure, finishes, testing and handover are included or priced as separate stages. Compare insulation, glazing and shading against approved performance specifications. Allocate responsibility for unforeseen ground conditions, substitutions and sample-approval delays. Price future-extension provisions, boundary walls and gates, interlock paving, landscaping, pools and utility connection fees separately from the core villa; a rate per square metre alone is not a comparable quotation. Link payments to documented stage inspections and agree how variations are priced before work starts.

**Inspection and acceptance:** With the consultant, check building grids, finished levels, room dimensions and door/window openings against approved plans. Collect foundation, frame and concealed waterproofing inspection records before closure. Test drainage in the relevant areas and operate the lighting, cooling and sanitary installations included in the contract. Obtain test certificates where applicable, warranties, as-built information and a dated defect list with reinspection responsibilities. A visual walkthrough or this guide is not structural certification or occupancy approval.

**Quote request:** Send the plot survey, available plans, room schedule and site-access photographs. Request a stage-by-stage bill of quantities separating design and approvals, structural work, finishes, testing, connections and external works. Where insulation or façade specifications differ, ask for clearly scoped alternatives rather than comparing a single headline “new villa” price. ## 9. Residential annex construction

**When this service is needed and what it covers:** An annex can add bedrooms, living space or separate utility rooms within a property. Habitable rooms, storage and kitchens have different ventilation, drainage, loading and insulation requirements. State the intended use and floor area, and whether the contract includes finishes, cooling, access paths and external works. Do not assume the annex may be attached to the existing villa structure, or built at all, without appropriate assessment and required approvals.

**Photographs, dimensions and specifications:** Supply wide views of the proposed site from several angles and close-ups of meters, drainage chambers, access paths and any visible settlement or cracking. Provide a sketch with plot dimensions, setbacks, proposed footprint, ground levels, room heights, doors, windows and equipment access. Include available existing drawings, expected occupancy, and requirements for thermal and acoustic insulation, floor finishes, sanitary fixtures and cooling. Buried utility routes must be investigated before excavation.

**Comparing bids and agreeing terms:** Ask bidders to price surveys, engineering design, permits and fees, excavation, foundations, frame, waterproofing, finishes, electrical, water, drainage and cooling connections, and reinstatement of disturbed paving or planting separately. Compare an independent structure with any engineered and approved connection to the existing villa, not just the total price. Allocate responsibility for soil testing where needed, protection of the occupied house and utilities, temporary supports, waste removal, programme, concealed conditions and written variations. Excavation and utility tie-ins must await applicable requirements and approvals.

**Inspection and handover:** Obtain inspection records for foundations, reinforcement, waterproofing and concealed work before closure, together with the agreed water, drainage, electrical and cooling test results. Check surface falls and rainwater drainage at the junction, reinstated access routes, and dated defect closure and reinspection. Visible finishes alone do not demonstrate that a structural connection is sound.

**Requesting a quotation:** Send the site sketch, dimensions, photographs, proposed use and available plans, and request a survey visit before a final price. Ask for an itemised bill of quantities, explicit exclusions, responsibility for design and approvals, inspection hold points and handover records. A quotation request can be submitted through Biet Al Reef even where no company is currently listed for the specialty; company availability, price and timing are not guaranteed.

## 10. Outdoor majlis construction

**When and why:** A detached majlis should accommodate the intended guest numbers while keeping guest access separate from the family's private circulation. Decide whether the contract covers only the building or also the approach, parking, pantry, fixed seating and sanitary facilities. Do not assume an addition is permitted before the relevant drawings and requirements are checked.

**Evidence, measurements and specifications:** Supply a dimensioned seating and entrance plan showing door swings, washbasins, washrooms, pantry and external AC-unit locations. Photograph ground levels, guest access and existing service routes; measure distances to electrical, water and drainage connections and mark proposed wall or roof penetrations. State preferred flooring, cladding, glazing and thermal/acoustic performance, while leaving AC capacity to a qualified assessment.

**Compare bids and agree the contract:** Require separate lines for foundations and structure, insulation, façade, lighting, AC, plumbing, serving facilities, fixed joinery and external paths. Check whether excavation, paving reinstatement, equipment supply and connections back to the villa are included. Allocate responsibility for plans and applicable approvals, sample sign-off, outdoor AC placement, condensate drainage and written variation approvals.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Walk the guest and serving routes and check privacy; operate lighting and cooling, test washroom and pantry drains for leakage and odours, and verify external paving falls away from entrances. Inspect roof-to-glazing joints and concealed-service test records before closure; record finish defects and reinspection dates.

**Quote request:** Send the seating plan, guest capacity, ground-level and service-route photographs, and proposed utility connections. Ask for a measured schedule separating construction and finishes from utility tie-ins, pantry, furniture, paths and parking, with exclusions, permit responsibilities, system tests and handover records.

## 11. Villa extensions

Adding a room or floor to an existing villa requires checking the old structure and services before promising a construction method.

**What to document and measure:** Provide as-built plans if available, photographs of columns and roof junctions, the intended extra area and an assessment by a qualified structural engineer.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Separate specialist structural assessment, design, applicable approvals, temporary supports, engineer-approved strengthening, new foundations or framing, waterproof junctions, diverted utilities and reinstatement. Name the party responsible for drawing submissions and approvals before removal or strengthening, and agree concealed-connection inspection hold points. Do not promise a fixed structural connection before assessment.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Obtain inspection records for engineered connections and temporary supports under the approved design; inspect movement and waterproof joints between old and new construction and test diverted services. Visual inspection alone cannot establish structural adequacy.

**Quote request:** Supply a sketch showing the added floor area and where it joins the villa, photos of roof, wall and floor junctions, utility and meter locations, and any existing drawings. Commission a separate engineering assessment before seeking a fixed construction price. Then ask for measured items for temporary propping, approved foundations or connections, junction waterproofing, diverted electrical, water and drainage services, and finish reinstatement. Allocate responsibility for concealed-condition discoveries, design changes, permits and testing in writing; a structural connection price cannot be treated as final before assessment.

## 12. Residential building alterations

Changing a flat layout may involve hidden pipes, electrical routes or structural elements even when the visible work seems minor.

**What to document and measure:** Mark walls to remove or build on before-and-after plans; photograph ceilings, risers and any suspected load-bearing members.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Separate investigation of load-bearing walls and hidden services, safe utility isolation, permitted demolition, debris routes, new partitions, fire-stopping and reinstatement. Identify who arranges engineering review, drawings and applicable authority approvals before structural or escape-route changes, how occupied units are protected, and how restored utilities and fire-stopping will be tested.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Check new partitions and openings against approved alterations, test isolated and restored utilities, verify required acoustic separation and fire-stopping at penetrations, and confirm shared corridors are clean and accessible.

**Quote request:** Attach numbered existing and proposed wall and opening plans, photos of distribution boards, pipes, ceilings and shared corridors, and permissible working hours if occupied. Ask for separate items covering investigation of load-bearing elements and hidden services, safe isolation, authorised demolition, debris removal, partitions and finishes, and retesting of electrical, plumbing and required fire-stopping. Record who obtains approvals, protects residents and lifts, repairs damage outside the work zone, and prices defects discovered only after opening up.

## 13. Villa restoration

This service treats villa restoration as a coordinated project across structure, waterproofing and finishes; the separate restoration-and-renovation specialty may be more appropriate for an individual defect or element without assuming a whole-villa project. Restoration should address the cause of cracking or dampness, not merely cover its appearance with new paint.

**What to document and measure:** Keep a dated log of crack progression and damp patches, with wide photographs locating each defect in the villa and close-ups beside a ruler. Note whether changes follow rainfall or water use, previous repair locations and finishes that may conceal damage; attach leak-investigation or structural reports if available.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare the diagnostic method, correction of the underlying cause, compatible repair materials, drying time and repair warranty, not the appearance of the new finish. State how concealed damage will be opened, assessed, priced and approved before variations, including reinstatement of finishes. If cracks show displacement, progressive widening, settlement or visible distortion, suspend work in the affected area and seek a qualified structural engineer's assessment and site-safety direction; do not authorize structural removal or strengthening on a decorating quotation. Have the responsible professional check whether drawings, permits or authority approvals are required for structural alterations, and assign responsibility for obtaining them and recording inspection before concealment.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Compare dated crack and dampness photographs before and after repair; obtain diagnosis, evidence that the cause was addressed and pre-concealment inspection records. Agree any specialist-recommended monitoring period; repainting alone is not proof of structural safety.

**Quote request:** Send dated crack or dampness photographs and request a diagnostic stage before repair pricing, with concealed-defect contingencies and monitoring responsibilities.

## 14. Residential building renovation

Renovating an occupied building requires careful sequencing so residents retain safe access and essential services.

**What to document and measure:** Prepare a schedule of affected unit numbers, rooms and corridors, whether each unit is occupied and permitted working hours. Photograph defects, electrical isolation panels, water shut-off valves and lift entrances; mark waste-transfer routes and material storage locations without obstructing emergency exits.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare unit-by-unit sequencing, resident notification lead times, dust-barrier specifications, lift protection, daily waste removal and cleaning responsibilities. Agree shutdown and restart testing windows with building management, keep emergency exits unobstructed, and require a separate written record and approval of costs before proceeding with newly exposed hidden defects. Specify the complaint contact and a separate handover record for each unit.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Hand over each occupied unit separately with electrical, water and AC tests and a closed snag register. Confirm restoration of shared services, removal of waste and barriers, and safe access to emergency exits and lifts.

**Quote request:** Request a phased, occupied-building programme covering resident notices, safe access, service outages, dust barriers, waste routes and per-unit handover.
