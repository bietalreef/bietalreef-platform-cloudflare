# Building and villa construction in Al Jimi — practical guide for company enquiries

This is planning information for owners and project teams, not a statement that a contractor is currently listed or available. Structural decisions, permits and safety-critical checks require the relevant qualified professionals and applicable approvals.

## 1. Villa construction

A family planning a villa should settle bedroom count, majlis access, parking and privacy before asking builders to price a design.

**What to document and measure:** Provide plot boundaries, approved architectural and structural drawings where available, floor areas, soil report and site-access photographs. Ask for separate allowances for foundations, shell, waterproofing, finishes and utility connections.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare concrete and steel specifications, nominated finishes, provisional sums and whether design coordination, permits, boundary walls and landscaping are excluded. Record approval responsibilities and inspection stages.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Before closing foundations or walls, request consultant inspections; at handover check entrance levels, room dimensions, water ingress and a documented defects list.

**Quote request:** Request a villa-specific bill of quantities, including soil-dependent foundations, utilities, majlis and parking interfaces, and a schedule of consultant inspections.

## 2. Residential building construction

For a multi-unit residential building, the number of apartments is only part of the scope: stairs, lifts, parking, shared meters and service shafts affect cost and coordination.

**What to document and measure:** Supply unit schedules, floor plans, lift requirements, access constraints and drawings of common areas. Clarify fire-safety design and approval responsibilities with qualified professionals.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare shell-and-core versus fully finished unit pricing, common-area finishes, mechanical and electrical systems, testing, commissioning and authority-related exclusions.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Inspect communal plant and service shafts, lift and safety-system commissioning records, apartment interfaces and the defects register for shared spaces.

**Quote request:** Ask bidders to price apartment units separately from lifts, stairwells, parking, common MEP systems and authority testing, identifying tenant-fit-out exclusions.

## 3. Commercial building construction

A retail or office building must fit its intended occupancy, deliveries, tenant access and future fit-out rather than merely resemble a reference façade.

**What to document and measure:** Include intended use, clear heights, loading access, floor loading requirements and any tenant service allocation; photograph adjoining roads and boundaries.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Distinguish base-building work from tenant fit-out, signage, specialist ventilation and utility upgrades. Require a written schedule of finishes, interfaces and approvals.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Verify agreed clearances, delivery routes, exit arrangements and shell-and-core connection points against the approved drawings and inspection records.

**Quote request:** Provide intended tenant uses and request separate shell-and-core, loading-bay, fire-system interface and future fit-out allowances.

## 4. General building works

Masonry work can look straightforward but misaligned openings or poorly coordinated services cause expensive rework after plastering.

**What to document and measure:** Mark block types, wall lengths and heights, door/window opening dimensions, lintels, wall ties and service penetrations on drawings; photograph the workface.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare whether scaffolding, mortar, lintels, waste removal and making good around services are included. Specify how measured quantities and variations will be certified.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Check wall plumb, level, joint consistency and opening sizes before plaster conceals the work.

**Quote request:** Request measured wall quantities with deductions for openings, stated block specifications, scaffolding, lintels, service chases and waste handling.

## 5. Concrete works

Concrete pricing depends on the actual elements, specified mix, access for delivery and the planned pour sequence.

**What to document and measure:** Provide structural drawings, element volumes, reinforcement schedules, formwork details, access photos and the consultant's concrete specification.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Ask whether pumping, formwork, reinforcement fixing, testing, curing, night pours and rejected loads are included. Agree pour notices and inspection hold points.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Review reinforcement sign-off before casting, delivery tickets, sample-test results, curing records and any honeycombing remediation approved by the engineer.

**Quote request:** Request a pour-by-pour breakdown of concrete grade, reinforcement, formwork, pumping, laboratory testing, curing and contingency for rejected loads.

## 6. Structural frame construction

The frame ties foundations, columns, beams and slabs together; errors at an early stage can affect every subsequent trade.

**What to document and measure:** Provide structural drawings and revisions, soil information, setting-out references and the proposed floor-by-floor sequence.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare responsibility for temporary supports, shuttering, reinforcement, concrete supply, survey checks and consultant inspections. Identify who pays for redesigns or delayed approvals.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Verify surveyed levels and positions, pre-pour reinforcement records, test results and documented acceptance of each structural stage.

**Quote request:** Ask for a staged structural-frame offer identifying survey setting-out, temporary works, reinforcement checks, pour approvals and responsibility for design revisions.

## 7. Turnkey project delivery

A turnkey promise is only meaningful when the client and contractor agree exactly what is ready for use at handover.

**What to document and measure:** Prepare a room-by-room finish schedule and specify kitchen appliances, air conditioning, lighting, sanitary fittings, external works and connection fees.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare named material ranges, substitution approval, warranties, commissioning, cleaning and final documentation. Identify owner-supplied items and any permit or utility exclusions.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Walk through every room, operate installed systems, reconcile the snag list and collect manuals, test records and warranties before final settlement.

**Quote request:** Attach a room-by-room completion schedule and ask bidders to itemise appliances, AC commissioning, utility activation, external works, warranties and handover documents.

## 8. New villa construction

A new villa allows early coordination of sunlight, privacy, circulation and future family requirements before structural decisions are fixed.

**What to document and measure:** Send the accommodation brief, site survey, preferred room relationships, parking needs and available approved plans; note possible future extensions.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Separate design-development allowances from fixed construction quantities and clarify whether boundary works, external paving, pools and connections are included.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Measure key rooms and openings against approved plans; inspect levels, drainage falls and finishes through agreed milestones.

**Quote request:** Ask for alternatives covering insulation and shading, future extension provisions, privacy, outdoor drainage and owner-supplied finishes.

## 9. Residential annex construction

A bedroom annex, pantry or store each creates different plumbing, ventilation and electrical demands alongside an occupied home.

**What to document and measure:** Show the proposed footprint, distance to the main house, existing utilities, boundary constraints and photographs of the proposed connection points.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare standalone foundations versus any engineered connection, waterproofing, drainage tie-ins, air conditioning and reinstatement of affected paving. Confirm approvals before work.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Inspect joints, water run-off, utility testing and safe separation or integration with the existing building.

**Quote request:** Send annex footprint and service tie-in photos; request separate investigation, permits, foundations, drainage connections and reinstatement allowances.

## 10. Outdoor majlis construction

A detached majlis needs comfortable guest circulation, private access, cooling and suitable washroom arrangements.

**What to document and measure:** Record guest capacity, entry route, seating layout, floor levels, outdoor exposure, AC needs and proposed sanitary connections.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare shading and insulation, doors and glazing, drainage, kitchen or pantry provision, external paving and whether furniture is supplied.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Check guest access, door operation, air-conditioning performance, drainage falls and water-tightness of exposed joints.

**Quote request:** Specify guest capacity and majlis access; request separate prices for cooling, acoustic comfort, washrooms, external paving and fixed versus loose furniture.

## 11. Villa extensions

Adding a room or floor to an existing villa requires checking the old structure and services before promising a construction method.

**What to document and measure:** Provide as-built plans if available, photographs of columns and roof junctions, the intended extra area and an assessment by a qualified structural engineer.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Ask for a separate price for investigation, temporary protection, strengthening if approved, new works and reinstatement. Never treat structural capacity as assumed.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Inspect the engineered connections, differential-movement details, waterproof junctions and reinstated services with the supervising consultant.

**Quote request:** Request a structural assessment before pricing a fixed extension; separate investigation, approved strengthening, temporary support, new construction and restoration.

## 12. Residential building alterations

Changing a flat layout may involve hidden pipes, electrical routes or structural elements even when the visible work seems minor.

**What to document and measure:** Mark walls to remove or build on before-and-after plans; photograph ceilings, risers and any suspected load-bearing members.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare investigation, isolation of utilities, controlled demolition, debris removal, new partitions and making good. Require engineered approval where structural work is involved.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Confirm approved opening dimensions, safe reinstatement of utilities, repaired fire or acoustic separations and clean common areas.

**Quote request:** Attach marked existing and proposed plans; ask for separate rates for investigation, utility isolation, authorised demolition, new partitions and fire-stopping repairs.

## 13. Villa restoration

Restoration should address the cause of cracking or dampness, not merely cover its appearance with new paint.

**What to document and measure:** Photograph defects with dates and scale references, note previous repairs and leaks, and seek a specialist assessment for suspected structural distress.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare diagnostic investigations, treatment method, compatible repair materials, affected finishes and monitoring requirements. State what concealed damage is excluded pending opening up.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Check evidence that the source was treated, inspect repairs before concealment and document any agreed monitoring period.

**Quote request:** Send dated crack or dampness photographs and request a diagnostic stage before repair pricing, with concealed-defect contingencies and monitoring responsibilities.

## 14. Residential building renovation

Renovating an occupied building requires careful sequencing so residents retain safe access and essential services.

**What to document and measure:** List affected apartments and shared areas, access hours, service shutdowns, lift protection and waste routes; provide photos and room schedules.

**Compare quotations and agree boundaries:** Compare temporary barriers, dust control, resident notices, disposal, phased handovers and the treatment of unforeseen hidden defects.

**Inspection and acceptance:** Inspect concealed work before closing walls, verify service restoration, clear shared routes and record defects separately for each completed area.

**Quote request:** Request a phased, occupied-building programme covering resident notices, safe access, service outages, dust barriers, waste routes and per-unit handover.
