# Landscaping and garden maintenance in Al Jimi — company and establishment guide

Use this guide to prepare site-specific enquiries and compare work scopes, not as evidence of an available supplier. **This category is currently non-selectable**; these notes are preparatory and do not activate it. Confirm site conditions, permits and safety requirements with qualified parties before work.

## 1. Home garden design

**Service-specific preparation note:** Before choosing seating layouts, separate everyday use from maintenance-equipment routes. Specify whether the deliverable is a concept or buildable pricing drawings; irrigation design is not included unless expressly listed.

**When and what to commission:** Define whether seating, play or planting takes priority; map circulation and sun exposure.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Photograph doors, facades and shade; provide plot dimensions, service points and threshold levels.

**Pre-work decision:** Before approving the design, check that seating and play areas leave doors and maintenance routes clear. A visual concept is not a construction drawing without verified site levels.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare layout alternatives, planting and irrigation drawings; installation and supply are excluded unless contracted.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Agree the drawing scale, approved survey version, number of options and revision rounds, and who signs off tree and path locations before construction drawings.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Share a dimensioned plan showing entrances, sun exposure and intended uses. Ask for two layout options, the number of revision rounds and deliverable formats; identify whether irrigation and lighting design are included.

**Acceptance inspection:** Check usable path widths, unobstructed doors and runoff away from the building. Match the final layout against measured site dimensions, check maintenance-gate swing and irrigation and power access, and record revisions before design approval.

**Additional acceptance and contract decision:** For design handover, request a schedule linking each area to its intended use, sun exposure and irrigation source. Flag potential clashes with existing services for site verification rather than assuming the ground is clear.

## 2. Villa landscaping

**Service-specific preparation note:** For an established garden, distinguish trees to retain from plants that may be moved and document exposed roots and old irrigation. Price protection of retained planting separately from replacement supply.

**When and what to commission:** Inventory existing trees, beds and seating to retain; agree privacy and access needs.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Supply planted-area measurements, machinery access and utility locations with multiple site photographs.

**Pre-work decision:** Locate buried services before excavation and isolate retained trees and seating from machinery routes; do not remove existing planting without an agreed record.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Price grading, soil, plants, irrigation and lighting separately; assign responsibility for protecting existing finishes.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Record protection boundaries for retained plants, machinery access dates and responsibility for repairing damaged paving, supported by a pre-work inventory.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Provide a photo inventory of trees and seating to retain and the areas to change. Request itemised protection, soil, planting, irrigation, lighting and waste-removal prices, plus plant establishment follow-up.

**Acceptance inspection:** Inspect levels, irrigation coverage and maintenance access around seating and trees. Walk the completed layout with the installation drawing to confirm valves and access covers remain reachable; obtain an as-planted list with locations and initial care dates.

**Additional acceptance and contract decision:** Before plant procurement, record a retain, relocate or remove decision for each existing feature and assign relocation risk. Obtain protection photographs before machinery enters and condition photographs after completion.

## 3. Outdoor space design

**Service-specific preparation note:** At each path-to-villa entrance, record threshold and nearby drain levels and request a paving build-up section. A 3D perspective cannot establish safe drainage falls.

**When and what to commission:** Plan pedestrian, vehicle and seating routes before selecting paving or features.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide entrance plans, path widths, ground falls, drains and service locations.

**Pre-work decision:** Check rainwater and wash-water paths at thresholds and entrances before approving gradients; attractive paving is not adequate if it drains towards the building.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare level plans, facade interfaces and paving specifications; permits and structural works are separate unless stated.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Assign responsibility for the level survey, drainage and utility clashes, and confirm whether buildable junction details are included in the design deliverables.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Supply entrance and drain levels and pedestrian and vehicle routes. Request a grading plan, threshold junction details and design alternatives; price design separately from paving construction and permits.

**Acceptance inspection:** Check accessibility and ensure runoff does not reach door thresholds or walls. Check threshold levels at every paving transition, turning space at entrances and any slope directing runoff towards foundations before signing off the drawings.

**Additional acceptance and contract decision:** Before approving circulation routes, request a typical threshold section showing paving build-up, fall and drain location. Perspective renderings alone must not set construction levels.

## 4. Green area installation

**Service-specific preparation note:** If irrigation leaves standing water, request a field drainage assessment before pricing green cover. Separate soil improvement from plant supply and establishment follow-up.

**When and what to commission:** Assess drainage and water supply before choosing a planted ground cover.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Measure square metres, available soil depth, levels and irrigation points; photograph low spots.

**Pre-work decision:** Assign responsibility for checking soil permeability and excess-water discharge before ordering ground cover; extra watering will not cure poor drainage.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare soil preparation, planting layer, plant source and establishment care; identify drainage and waste exclusions.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Specify settled growing-medium depth and source, settlement remediation, and establishment period; price irrigation installation separately from planting.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Provide the lawn area, available soil depth and ponding locations. Ask for drainage assessment, soil preparation, ground-cover specification, irrigation and establishment care, with measurable acceptance criteria for gaps.

**Acceptance inspection:** Test irrigation, ponding and initial coverage; agree a follow-up period. Check planted-soil depth at several points, record settlement or erosion after an irrigation trial, and agree a second inspection once the cover has established.

**Additional acceptance and contract decision:** For newly planted green areas, tie acceptance to soil-preparation records, an irrigation trial and observations of ponding. Distinguish the plant-establishment period from any subsequent maintenance contract.

## 5. Tree and shrub planting

**Service-specific preparation note:** Request a record for each tree covering species, approximate size, root-ball condition and planting position. Substitute species require written approval before delivery.

**When and what to commission:** Site trees for mature size, not just nursery size, clear of utilities and boundaries.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** List species, quantities, container sizes, planting positions and clearances to structures and pipes.

**Pre-work decision:** Assess future root clearance from foundations and pipes and mature canopy spread; do not position a tree solely by its nursery size.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare nursery stock, pit preparation, soil, staking, watering and replacement terms; utility relocation is separate.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Approve the species and delivered size schedule, substitution approval process, stress-monitoring period and replacement conditions.

**Service-specific quotation request:** List tree species, quantities, container sizes and clearances from walls and utilities. Request separate prices for pits, growing medium, stakes and initial watering, with conditions for replacing unhealthy stock.

**Acceptance inspection:** Inspect planting depth, stability, loose ties and foliage condition. Reconcile plant tags, quantities and container sizes with the delivery schedule; check exposed root flares and watering basins that do not discharge against walls.

**Additional acceptance and contract decision:** For trees and shrubs, agree container size, root condition and stem condition checks at delivery, with a written substitute approval process if the specified species is unavailable; a trade name alone is insufficient.

## 6. Natural turf installation

**Service-specific preparation note:** For natural turf, document water source, sunlight and the agreed time before use. Obtain initial irrigation and mowing instructions and a measurable standard for repairing bare patches.

**When and what to commission:** Confirm sunlight, irrigation and surface preparation before turf delivery to avoid drying.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Measure area, water access, watering schedule, soil and shaded zones; photograph levels.

**Pre-work decision:** Test irrigation coverage and soil levels before laying turf rolls; agree who corrects settlement or dry patches.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Specify turf variety and source, grading, establishment period and initial watering responsibility; long-term care is separate.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** State who accepts the prepared base before turf arrives, who provides water during rooting, and how dry patches or settlement will be corrected.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Send turf area, shaded zones, soil type and irrigation points. Request the turf cultivar, base preparation, delivery sequence and rooting-period watering responsibility, plus terms for replacing failed sections.

**Acceptance inspection:** Check close turf joints, initial rooting, even watering and absence of flooding. Check that turf-roll edges meet without overlapping; request a record of the first mow and sprinkler uniformity after rooting begins.

**Additional acceptance and contract decision:** For natural turf, specify checks for sod-to-soil contact, seam levelling and initial watering. Record yellowing patches at initial handover and set a follow-up inspection rather than claiming immediate successful rooting.

## 7. Artificial turf installation

**Service-specific preparation note:** For children's play areas, request an artificial-turf sample and surface, infill and edge-fixing specifications. Discuss sun-exposed surface heat and drainage without assuming a universal safe temperature.

**When and what to commission:** Choose pile height and density for use; a draining stable base matters more than laying rolls over loose soil.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Photograph the existing base; measure lengths, edges and drains and note sun exposure.

**Pre-work decision:** Review likely sun-exposed surface heat, drainage and product evidence, particularly in children's play areas; artificial turf should not be assumed to provide a cool surface.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare roll specifications, sub-base, seam adhesive, edge fixing and any infill; approve a physical sample.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Approve a physical roll sample, pile direction, edge-fixing method and sub-base build-up; define correction of wrinkles and poor drainage after installation.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Provide dimensions, drain and edge locations and base photographs. Ask for samples specifying pile height, density, sub-base, seams and infill, with drainage preparation priced separately from rolls.

**Acceptance inspection:** Inspect pile direction, seams, secured edges and water drainage. Test drainage at seams and low spots and inspect for exposed fasteners or adhesive at edges. For children's play or seating areas exposed to sun, request product information on surface heat and suitability for the intended use, and assess surface feel and temperature under representative use conditions before handover; a green appearance alone does not establish thermal comfort or safety.

**Additional acceptance and contract decision:** For artificial turf, request a product sample, drainage-permeability data, infill specification and warranty exclusions. Separate a sun-exposure surface-temperature check from any blanket claim that the surface is safe at all times.

## 8. Plant and tree installation

**Service-specific preparation note:** Price planting-bed preparation separately from seedlings and require a planting map with species, quantities and spacing. Relocations caused by buried-service conflicts need documented approval.

**When and what to commission:** Distinguish decorative beds from permanent planting; match species to light, watering and bed size.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide plant schedule, counts, bed dimensions, pipe positions and any damaged root photographs.

**Pre-work decision:** Match species and supplied sizes to sun exposure, salinity and irrigation availability, and approve any substitution before delivery.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare plant sizes, soil mix, mulch, supports and replacement terms; irrigation alterations are not automatic.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Fix plant quantities, container sizes, growing medium and positions on a plan; assign responsibility for irrigation-point changes if needed.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Supply species and quantity schedules, planter dimensions and existing soil photographs. Separate plants, soil mix, mulch, irrigation and staking, and identify who replaces unsuitable or damaged plants.

**Acceptance inspection:** Inspect intact root balls, spacing and correct planting depth at the crown. Match planting locations to the plan and bed dimensions; log damaged plants and schedule a separate review rather than automatically accepting replacement. For plant and tree installation, obtain an as-planted location plan showing expected mature clearances from walls and services. Separate relocation and replanting charges from supply of new stock.

## 9. Tree pruning

**Service-specific preparation note:** For pruning near cables or buildings, require an access and falling-material risk assessment and an exclusion-zone plan. Severe cutting is not a substitute for species-appropriate pruning.

**When and what to commission:** Identify dead, crossing or obstructing branches; avoid indiscriminate topping or work near power lines without specialists.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Photograph the full canopy and target limbs; estimate height, lift access and debris collection space.

**Pre-work decision:** Assess branch condition, nearby wiring and buildings, and the potential fall zone before pruning; hazardous work near power lines requires coordination with the competent utility.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare pruning plan, access equipment, pedestrian protection and green-waste removal; live-line work needs specialist coordination.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** List the trees, permitted working heights, exclusion zones and branch-removal routes, and identify who authorises hazardous limb treatment.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Photograph the canopy and target branches, noting tree height and nearby power lines. Request a pruning method, access equipment, exclusion zone and green-waste haulage; separate work needing utility coordination.

**Acceptance inspection:** Check clean cuts, no hanging limbs and no damage to fences or nearby planting. Inspect pruning cuts relative to branch collars, ensure cuttings are cleared from drains and check nearby cables and fences for visible damage before demobilisation. Before pruning tall trees, request an access and exclusion-zone plan and clarify who assesses proximity to power lines. Inspect cut locations and tree condition; do not assume removing every damaged limb is appropriate.

## 10. Lawn maintenance

**Service-specific preparation note:** For lawn maintenance, compare dry patches against sprinkler coverage before increasing watering or fertiliser. The quotation should specify visit frequency and a return check for damaged areas.

**When and what to commission:** Map yellowing, compaction and bare patches before setting mowing and feeding frequency.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide lawn area, photographs of weak zones, sprinkler type and any watering logs.

**Pre-work decision:** Distinguish drought caused by irrigation faults from disease or compacted soil before fertilising or replacing lawn areas.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare visit frequency, cutting height, clipping disposal and sprinkler checks; reseeding and pest treatment need explicit scope.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Specify mowing, irrigation and aeration frequencies, weed-control limits and how turf condition is documented before and after each visit.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Provide lawn area, irrigation logs and photographs of weak patches. Request visit-based prices specifying cutting height, frequency, clipping disposal and sprinkler checks; price turf replacement separately.

**Acceptance inspection:** Review visit records, cut consistency and photographed progress of weak patches. Compare photographs of weak patches between visits, inspect blocked sprinklers and scalped turf, and list remedial work needing separate approval. Where weak patches recur, request comparative soil-moisture readings in healthy and affected areas and check sprinkler overlap. Discolouration alone does not justify pesticide use or heavier feeding. Record whether sprinkler repairs are included or require separate authorisation.

## 11. Plant feeding and treatment

**Service-specific preparation note:** Before treating yellowing plants, assess irrigation, soil and pests as possible causes. Record the proposed product, application responsibility and follow-up rather than purchasing an unspecified cure.

**When and what to commission:** Do not assume yellow leaves require fertiliser; check watering, drainage, salinity and possible pests first.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Photograph both leaf surfaces and soil; list species, affected counts and irrigation condition.

**Pre-work decision:** Diagnose symptoms and check irrigation and soil before treatment; do not specify a chemical or dosage without identifying the issue and following product instructions.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare diagnosis, any required tests, product, dosage and follow-up; recovery of severely damaged plants cannot be assumed.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Require diagnosis before treatment selection; record materials and application rates according to authorised labels and instructions, with a reinspection date.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Send symptom photos, plant species, watering records and any test results. Require a written diagnosis before product and dose selection, a priced follow-up visit and realistic improvement criteria.

**Acceptance inspection:** Request application records, safe watering instructions and later symptom review rather than instant colour change. Receive the product name, quantity, application location and date; verify plant-specific instructions and the review date before authorising repeat treatment. Price diagnosis separately from material purchase; identify who retains product packaging and application records and how beds near water sources will be protected. Where disease or infestation is suspected, do not promise recovery before follow-up evidence.

## 12. Garden pest control

**Service-specific preparation note:** When insects appear, photograph the affected plant and location and note proximity to children, animals and water. Chemical treatment requires suitably qualified operators and clear re-entry instructions.

**When and what to commission:** Identify the pest and affected area before choosing treatment; avoid unnecessary blanket spraying.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide close-up pest images, affected plant counts, water sources and presence of children or pets.

**Pre-work decision:** Record the pest, its location and proximity to children, pets and water sources; a qualified provider should determine controls, exclusion measures and re-entry conditions.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare integrated pest management, authorised product, re-entry precautions and follow-up visits; indoor pest control is separate.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Identify the pest, affected area and authorised applicator; agree protection for residents, pets and planting, plus a treatment record.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Provide pest photographs, affected plant counts and details of children, pets and water features. Request pest identification, an integrated treatment plan, authorised product, re-entry precautions and follow-up cost.

**Acceptance inspection:** Check treatment records, re-entry instructions and a scheduled reassessment. Check treatment locations against the pest map, receive child and pet precautions and re-entry times, and schedule a follow-up to assess remaining activity. Before chemical treatment, request evidence that the product is suitable for the intended application and identify the responsible authorised applicator. Plan to prevent drift into edible planting, pools or neighbouring plots. Re-entry conditions must follow product instructions and applicable authority requirements, not a blanket time claim.

## 13. Garden cleaning

**Service-specific preparation note:** For garden clearance, separate leaves and green waste from glass, sharps and chemical residues. Specify disposal destination and whether drains and planting beds are included.

**When and what to commission:** Separate leaf and branch collection from bulky waste or pond cleaning to avoid scope disputes.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Photograph waste volumes, paths, drains and furniture or planting requiring protection.

**Pre-work decision:** Agree green-waste removal routes and separation of sharps and unknown substances; suspected hazardous material must not be mixed with routine garden clippings.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare collection, sorting, loading, lawful disposal and transport; pressure washing is not necessarily included.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Separate leaf collection from pruning and bulky waste hauling; agree the collection point and evidence of lawful removal.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Photograph paths, beds and waste piles and describe vehicle access. Price leaf collection separately from bulky branch haulage and pressure washing, and require lawful disposal and plant protection.

**Acceptance inspection:** Check clear paths and drains and no cuttings left in beds or damage to plants. Inspect beneath seats, along planting-bed edges and at drain grates; ensure no sharp debris or bags remain against plants or block drainage. Agree before-and-after photographs of collection points and drains, the waste types to be removed and responsibility for hauling charges. Segregate unknown containers or potentially hazardous material for qualified handling rather than including them in routine green-waste removal.

## 14. Garden maintenance contracts

**Service-specific preparation note:** For maintenance contracts, attach a baseline inventory of trees, lawns and irrigation, and define visit intervals, fault response and exclusions. Compare monthly evidence reports, not visit counts alone.

**When and what to commission:** Create a schedule separating mowing, irrigation, pruning, feeding and monitoring with urgent-call procedures.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide a garden plan, tree counts, lawn areas, irrigation assets and baseline photographs.

**Pre-work decision:** Before contracting, inventory plants, pumps and valves with their condition and identify existing faults excluded from routine visits so they cannot be confused with new maintenance charges.

**Quotation comparison, contract and exclusions:** Compare visit frequency, labour hours, consumables, spare parts and replacements; define response and missed-visit remedies.

**Detailed agreement checkpoints:** Set visit frequency, seasonal tasks, fault-response boundaries, consumables and replacement exclusions, and the report template for each visit.

**Service-specific quotation request:** Provide a garden plan, tree inventory, planted areas and irrigation assets. Request a seasonal visit schedule covering mowing, pruning, feeding, inspections and reporting, with consumable prices and missed-visit remedies.

**Acceptance inspection:** Match visit logs to schedules, before/after images, plant health notes and outstanding repairs. Reconcile visit logs and extra charges with the contract, and check that deferred faults, plant replacements and corrective-action dates are documented. For a garden maintenance contract, request a traceable seasonal schedule and visit records, with separate approval for replacements or pump repairs. Close each visit against photographs and reviewable observations.
