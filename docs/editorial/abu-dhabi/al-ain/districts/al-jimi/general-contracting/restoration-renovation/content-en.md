# Restoration and renovation in Al Jimi — company services

This is guidance for preparing real project enquiries, not confirmation of a listed contractor, price or appointment. Structural assessment and strengthening require competent professional review and applicable approvals.

## 1. Older building restoration

**Need and scope:** Survey moisture, floor settlement and wall condition before deciding which existing fabric should be preserved. Define retained fabric, temporary support and controlled removal; exclude structural changes without assessment and approval.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide dated defect photographs, crack dimensions, original finish details and a structural assessment where necessary. Record original mortar and cladding types, affected areas and protection of retained details.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare root-cause treatment against cosmetic work, including temporary support and protection of sound existing elements. Agree inspection hold points after opening up and before closing concealed or historic fabric.

**Handover checks:** Inspect repaired areas and moisture test evidence before concealed finishes are closed. Receive concealed-work photographs, moisture results and a retained-element defect register.

**Request a quotation:** Attach dated moisture and crack photographs, a plan marking fabric to retain and any available structural report. Request separate prices for investigation, limited opening-up, temporary support and repairs, with exclusions and a written variation procedure for concealed damage.

## 2. Villa renovation

**Need and scope:** Identify rooms to change and floors or cabinetry worth retaining rather than stripping the whole villa. Phase work room by room, identify occupied areas and exclude unlisted service relocations or furniture works.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Supply a room schedule, kitchen and bathroom photographs and marked service relocation points. Provide room dimensions, chosen floor and paint finishes, cabinetry details and temporary storage needs.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare demolition, protection, supply and installation separately; approve finish samples and concealed-defect variations. Set room-by-room milestones, approved samples, furniture protection and a process for hidden defects.

**Handover checks:** Test doors, drainage and electrics, and check junctions between retained and replacement finishes. Test doors, drainage and switches, and compare each room with approved samples and the work schedule.

**Request a quotation:** Send a room schedule showing finishes to remove or retain, with photographs and whether the villa remains occupied. Request room-by-room pricing for strip-out, protection, materials and installation, plus utility shutdown windows, sample approvals and change control.

## 3. Apartment renovation

**Need and scope:** An apartment renovation in an occupied block needs agreed working hours and lift protection. Specify lift access, noise windows, waste routes and permissions; exclude changes to common property without consent.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Document room sizes, kitchen and bathroom defects, access windows and building management rules. Attach apartment dimensions, door and lift clearances and protection specifications for shared flooring.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare corridor protection, debris transport, water shutdowns and noisy-work arrangements. Agree permitted work hours, lift protection, debris handling and liability for shared-area damage.

**Handover checks:** Check utilities and finishes, then inspect shared corridors and lifts for damage. Test fixtures and inspect lifts and corridors with building management before close-out.

**Request a quotation:** Attach the apartment plan, doorway and lift clearances, before-work corridor photographs and building management rules. Ask for shared-area protection and waste removal to be priced separately from finishes, with noise windows and responsibility for reinstating common property.

## 4. Building rehabilitation

**Need and scope:** A change of use may require reassessment of ventilation, electrical capacity and exits. Define the intended use and life-safety gaps; exclude occupancy before required approvals.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide the proposed occupancy, existing drawings and condition reports for structure and services. Document available switchboard and cooling capacity, intended occupancy and existing exit drawings.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Separate life-safety and essential-system upgrades from decorative work, and allocate design and approval duties. Assign responsibility for testing reports, permits and delivery of safety and operating documentation.

**Handover checks:** Review commissioning records and exit arrangements before the new use begins. Check commissioning evidence for ventilation and power, evacuation provisions and applicable approvals.

**Request a quotation:** Provide the intended new use, as-built plans and available building-services condition reports. Ask for separate pricing of studies and approvals, life-safety upgrades, commissioning and finishes, naming the party responsible for each test and handover record.

## 5. Structural crack remediation

**Need and scope:** Do not fill a crack cosmetically before assessing whether it reflects structural movement or surface shrinkage. Commission engineering diagnosis and movement monitoring separately; do not assume injection or strengthening is appropriate.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide dated photographs with a width scale, its position on a plan and any observed progression. Measure crack width, length and direction, with dated monitoring points before specifying repair materials.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare investigation, monitoring and engineer-designed strengthening or injection where appropriate. Tie injection or strengthening to the engineer's report, monitoring evidence and written acceptance criteria.

**Handover checks:** Review the diagnostic report, repair records and monitoring plan before repainting. Receive the engineer's findings, final monitoring readings and any follow-up monitoring plan.

**Request a quotation:** Provide dated crack photographs with a scale, plan locations and any movement history. Commission engineering diagnosis as a separate stage before pricing injection or strengthening; require monitoring responsibility, written repair acceptance criteria and follow-up provisions.

## 6. Façade refurbishment

**Need and scope:** Investigate loose cladding, peeling coatings and rainwater entry before choosing replacement finishes. Map removal and repair zones and safe access; exclude unassessed structural alterations.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide full-elevation photos, close-ups of joints and edges, access heights and existing material types. Measure elevation areas, joint locations, cladding types and required access-platform heights.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare access equipment, safe removal, substrate repair, waterproofing and the new fixing system. Agree access certification, cladding fixings and the method for checking water ingress.

**Handover checks:** Check cladding security, movement joints and drainage, and inspect the site after access equipment removal. Check cladding fixings, movement joints, rainwater drainage and the site after access removal.

**Request a quotation:** Send full-elevation photographs, close-ups of loose cladding and joints, and access heights. Request separate prices for access and protection, removal, root-cause repair and replacement finishes, specifying fixings and water-ingress checks.

## 7. Preparing a building for reuse

**Need and scope:** A vacant building may still be unfit for occupation because of failed cooling, electrical faults or leaks. List essential readiness tests and repair limits; exclude redesign of systems unless separately commissioned.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide an equipment and defect register, meter photos and any available maintenance history. List equipment identifiers, operational condition, valve and panel locations and available maintenance records.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Separate operational repairs from appearance upgrades, including testing, cleaning and key handover. Agree a test checklist for each system, restart responsibilities, warranties and variation boundaries.

**Handover checks:** Witness essential-system operation and receive closed defect records, keys and agreed documents. Witness essential systems operating and collect keys, closed defects and equipment documentation.
