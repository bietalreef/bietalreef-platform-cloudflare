# Restoration and renovation in Al Jimi — company services

This is guidance for preparing real project enquiries, not confirmation of a listed contractor, price or appointment. Structural assessment and strengthening require competent professional review and applicable approvals.

## 1. Older building restoration

**Need and scope:** Survey moisture, floor settlement and wall condition before deciding which existing fabric should be preserved. Define retained fabric, temporary support and controlled removal; exclude structural changes without assessment and approval.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Photograph both faces of a damp wall where accessible, showing the stain's position relative to floor and ceiling; date the images and include a scale beside cracks. Record plaster thickness, verified original mortar and cladding types, affected dimensions and details to retain. Ask the competent specialist whether material sampling or moisture tests are needed before selecting a repair; do not assume modern cement mortar is compatible with every older wall.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare root-cause treatment against cosmetic work, including temporary support and protection of sound existing elements. Agree inspection hold points after opening up and before closing concealed or historic fabric. Require a separate compatibility trial for repair materials against existing fabric, with moisture investigation priced apart from surface finishes.

**Handover checks:** Inspect repaired areas and moisture test evidence before concealed finishes are closed. Mark repaired zones on the building plan, inspect concealed-layer photographs before close-up, and record any deferred defects or areas requiring further observation rather than covering them.

**Contract-specific check:** Before opening up old fabric, photograph and tag elements to retain; allocate the decision process and cost of emergency propping if hidden weakness appears, and pause affected work for specialist review.

**Pre-work decision:** Ask whether salts, rising damp or leaking services caused the damage. Stripping plaster or repainting is not a permanent remedy until the water source is identified. If opening-up exposes weak structural fabric, secure the area and obtain competent assessment before further removal.

**Request a quotation:** Attach dated moisture and crack photographs, a plan marking fabric to retain and any available structural report. Request separate prices for investigation, limited opening-up, temporary support and repairs, with exclusions and a written variation procedure for concealed damage.

**Older-building decision check:** Before stripping historic layers, record roof, floor and load-bearing conditions and unknown service routes; require staged opening and specialist-designed support where needed. Separate correction of the damage source from cosmetic repair, and approve newly exposed work through a written variation.

## 2. Villa renovation

**Need and scope:** Identify rooms to change and floors or cabinetry worth retaining rather than stripping the whole villa. Phase work room by room, identify occupied areas and exclude unlisted service relocations or furniture works.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Supply a room schedule, kitchen and bathroom photographs and marked service relocation points. Provide room dimensions, chosen floor and paint finishes, cabinetry details and temporary storage needs.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare demolition, protection, supply and installation separately; approve finish samples and concealed-defect variations. Set room-by-room milestones, approved samples, furniture protection and a process for hidden defects. Price bathroom waterproofing and its agreed flood test separately from tile replacement; identify who reinstalls sanitary fixtures.

**Handover checks:** Inspect each room against its approved layout and finish schedule: operate cabinet doors and drawers, inspect tile levels and joints, and have competent trades test lighting, sockets and drainage. For bathrooms, obtain the agreed waterproofing test record and concealed-pipe photographs before covering up. Record defects with owners and rectification dates, then re-inspect before closing the room's handover sheet.

**Contract-specific check:** Attach an occupied-room schedule; assign furniture moving, protection and staged cleaning, and require written approval of tile or cabinetry substitutions before ordering.

**Pre-work decision:** For every bathroom, establish whether only tiles are removed or whether waterproofing and pipework are replaced, and who signs off waterproofing tests before concealment. For occupied rooms, define dust isolation and the point at which each room is safe to use.

**Request a quotation:** Send a room schedule showing finishes to remove or retain, with photographs and whether the villa remains occupied. Request room-by-room pricing for strip-out, protection, materials and installation, plus utility shutdown windows, sample approvals and change control.

**Villa-renovation decision check:** Require a room-by-room schedule of works, approved finishes, electrical and water isolation points and waste routes. Do not treat layout changes or removal of load-bearing walls as routine decoration; obtain engineering assessment, design and applicable approvals first.

**Additional occupied-villa protection check:** Before stripping a kitchen or bathroom in an occupied villa, mark the affected water isolation valves and electrical circuits on the room plan, together with debris routes and temporary storage. Agree dust barriers, floor and furniture protection, safe access and advance notice of shutdowns. On reinstatement, obtain documented leak checks and competent testing of affected electrical circuits before those services return to use.

## 3. Apartment renovation

**Need and scope:** An apartment renovation in an occupied block needs agreed working hours and lift protection. Specify lift access, noise windows, waste routes and permissions; exclude changes to common property without consent.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Photograph the apartment, approach corridor and lift before work. Supply room plans, floor areas, door openings and the lift's internal height, depth and management-stated load limit. Attach permitted hours, noise rules, lift booking and debris procedures; identify authorized water and electrical isolation points without assuming permission to alter shared systems.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare corridor protection, debris transport, water shutdowns and noisy-work arrangements. Agree permitted work hours, lift protection, debris handling and liability for shared-area damage. Specify the material-delivery route, lift booking, any management charges and advance notice for shared-water shutdowns.

**Handover checks:** Check utilities and finishes, then inspect shared corridors and lifts for damage. Compare the lift and shared-corridor condition against pre-work photographs with the building representative, recording scratches, leftover debris and agreed reinstatement actions.

**Contract-specific check:** Agree lift bookings and material routes with building management, photograph common corridors and lift condition before and after, and assign responsibility for damage to shared property.

**Pre-work decision:** Do not remove partitions or reroute services using a marketing floor plan alone; obtain reliable drawings or competent inspection and building-management or statutory approvals where applicable. Agree controls against dust and water migration to neighbouring units.

**Request a quotation:** Attach the apartment plan, doorway and lift clearances, before-work corridor photographs and building management rules. Ask for shared-area protection and waste removal to be priced separately from finishes, with noise windows and responsibility for reinstating common property.

**Apartment-renovation decision check:** Record corridor, lift and neighbouring-unit conditions before work, agreed noisy-work hours and liability for leaks to the flat below. For renewed bathroom or kitchen floors, request waterproofing test records before tiling, not just finished photographs.

## 4. Building rehabilitation

**Need and scope:** For rehabilitation of an existing building, whether to restore its performance or support a different use, first establish the intended activity, structural and building-services condition and maintenance history before selecting finishes. Separate the existing-condition assessment and design of modifications from construction. Neither a change of use nor increased occupancy is automatically permitted; competent professionals and relevant authorities must review the proposal. Findings may require a revised scope or suspension of affected works pending safety assessment.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Supply existing-condition drawings, the proposed activity, expected occupant numbers and operating hours, with photographs of switchboards, cooling plant, entrances, stairs and exits. Ask qualified designers to document anticipated electrical and structural loads, ventilation, escape routes and accessibility, and identify gaps against the intended use; photographs alone do not establish compliance.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare proposals in two stages: an existing-condition assessment and rehabilitation brief, followed by modifications specified by competent designers and relevant authorities. Require separate allowances for escape routes, ventilation, electrical capacity, accessibility and fire-safety systems versus cosmetic finishes. Do not compare a lump sum with a bid that omits testing, drawings or authority fees. Identify who appoints the consultant, submits applications, pays for retesting after failure and approves unforeseen remedial work in writing.

**Handover checks:** Obtain as-built records, applicable load and ventilation test reports, and inspection records for escape routes and accessibility against the proposed use. Keep a snag register with an owner, deadline and retest evidence for each item; request operator training and maintenance records where relevant. A successful trial run or closed technical snag list is not a substitute for applicable authority approvals or occupancy permission.

**Contract-specific check:** State who supplies original drawings and records, who verifies structural and services suitability, and who pays application fees, tests and failed-test repeats. Make opening-up investigations and assessment a priced stage with defined deliverables; do not authorize substantial alterations before the required design approvals. Agree written change-order rules for concealed defects, and distinguish contractor completion records from statutory approvals.

**Pre-work decision:** Do not compare construction-only bids before the safety assessment and proposed-use requirements are known. If exit capacity or system capacity cannot be substantiated, commission an independent assessment rather than assuming compliance.

**Request a quotation:** Send the Al Jimi site location, present and proposed uses, floor areas, anticipated occupant numbers and operating hours, existing-condition plans and any inspection reports. Request staged prices for measured survey, investigation, engineering assessment, approval drawings and fees, construction, commissioning tests and finishes. Require stated assumptions, exclusions and a schedule of handover evidence. Biet Al Reef customer service may help prepare the enquiry but cannot guarantee a company, price or date.

**Building-rehabilitation decision check:** Maintain separate defect and test registers for structure, envelope, electrical, plumbing, cooling and life-safety systems, with a named acceptance authority for each. Link payments to closed defects and witnessed tests; a repainted façade does not establish systems readiness.

## 5. Structural crack remediation

**Need and scope:** Do not fill a crack cosmetically before assessing whether it reflects structural movement or surface shrinkage. Where cracking is accompanied by visible displacement, progressive settlement or distorted doors and walls, stop work that could aggravate the hazard and seek urgent assessment by a qualified structural engineer; restrict access to affected areas as they advise. Commission engineering diagnosis and movement monitoring separately; do not assume injection or strengthening is appropriate.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Photograph each crack square-on under consistent lighting with a ruler and date; mark its position, direction, approximate width and length on the floor plan. Record when it appeared, any changes after rain or adjacent works, and related settlement or sticking doors. A qualified structural engineer must specify monitoring instruments, reading intervals and interpretation. Do not expose or inject a load-bearing element on photographs alone.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare investigation, monitoring and engineer-designed strengthening or injection where appropriate. Tie injection or strengthening to the engineer's report, monitoring evidence and written acceptance criteria. Distinguish monitoring an active crack from repairing a diagnosed stable one; visible crack length alone cannot price injection.

**Handover checks:** Review the diagnostic report, repair records and monitoring plan before repainting. Compare dated crack-width readings before and after repair, and obtain the engineer's recommended monitoring period and triggers for reassessment if movement resumes.

**Contract-specific check:** Agree crack gauge positions, reading dates and who interprets results; keep injection or strengthening decisions separate from repainting and record diagnosis before concealment.

**Pre-work decision:** If a crack widens or fresh settlement or distortion appears during monitoring, suspend cosmetic repair and obtain renewed engineering assessment before approving injection or strengthening. Record who remains responsible for post-repair monitoring.

**Request a quotation:** Provide dated crack photographs with a scale, plan locations and any movement history. Commission engineering diagnosis as a separate stage before pricing injection or strengthening; require monitoring responsibility, written repair acceptance criteria and follow-up provisions.

**Structural-crack decision check:** Photograph each crack with a scale, date, position and direction; distinguish surface defects from potential ongoing movement or settlement without diagnosing from photographs alone. A qualified structural engineer should decide whether monitoring, support or area evacuation is needed before injection or concealment.

## 6. Façade refurbishment

**Need and scope:** Where panels are loose or water marks appear around windows, first identify falling-material hazards and protect people below before inspection. Separate investigation of substrate, movement joints and water paths from stripping and replacement; repainting alone will not resolve concealed water ingress. Changes to heavy cladding fixings or supporting elements need competent design review and a safe work-at-height access plan.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Provide full-elevation photos, close-ups of joints and edges, access heights and existing material types. Measure elevation areas, joint locations, cladding types and required access-platform heights.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Compare access equipment, safe removal, substrate repair, waterproofing and the new fixing system. Agree access certification, cladding fixings and the method for checking water ingress. Where the cladding system requires it, price substrate checks or sample fixing trials and treatment of joints and window junctions.

**Handover checks:** Check cladding security, movement joints and drainage, and inspect the site after access equipment removal. Inspect sample cladding anchors, joint terminations at openings and drainage edges, documenting loose panels or staining while safe access remains in place.

**Contract-specific check:** Require an approved finish and fixing sample, a pedestrian-protection plan for work at height, and photographs of repaired water-entry paths behind cladding before closure. Assign competent review of substrate suitability and cladding loads, approval of scaffold or access-platform arrangements and applicable permits, and a written process for newly discovered loose fabric so no elevation or public route is left unsafe.

**Pre-work decision:** Before installing new cladding, obtain approval of edge, opening and joint fixing details against the actual substrate; an appearance sample alone does not establish system safety. Keep pedestrian routes protected until inspections are complete.

**Request a quotation:** Send full-elevation photographs, close-ups of loose cladding and joints, and access heights. Request separate prices for access and protection, removal, root-cause repair and replacement finishes, specifying fixings and water-ingress checks.

**Façade-refurbishment decision check:** Survey cladding anchors, window perimeters, movement joints, water-entry points and safe access, assigning responsibility for protecting pedestrians and vehicles. Inspect fixing samples, joints, weatherproofing and pre-scaffold-removal records, not colour consistency alone.

## 7. Preparing a building for reuse

**Need and scope:** A vacant building may still be unfit for occupation because of failed cooling, electrical faults or leaks. List essential readiness tests and repair limits; exclude redesign of systems unless separately commissioned.

**Photos, dimensions and specifications:** Prepare an asset register with each equipment ID, location and last known operating date, supported by switchboard, meter, valve, leak and fault-display photographs. Record how long the building has been vacant and the condition of ventilation, water, drainage and life-safety systems; attach available services drawings and maintenance reports. Have qualified trades assess safe re-energisation and restart before testing rather than assuming previously operating plant is safe.

**Quotation comparison and agreement:** Separate operational repairs from appearance upgrades, including testing, cleaning and key handover. Agree a test checklist for each system, restart responsibilities, warranties and variation boundaries. Classify defects as barriers to safe restart or deferrable items, and name who supplies parts and approves safety tests.

**Handover checks:** Witness essential-system operation and receive closed defect records, keys and agreed documents. Witness and record a separate functional test for each essential system, including its reading or result and sign-off owner; reconcile keys and equipment identifiers with the handover register.

**Contract-specific check:** Stage recommissioning with recorded electrical, water, cooling and life-safety tests, a residual-defects register and named repair decisions; partial test passes are not occupancy approvals.

**Pre-work decision:** Identify which systems require testing before reoccupation, who is authorised to energise and restart them, and which defects prohibit use. A passed test on one item of equipment is not proof that the whole building is ready.

**Request a quotation:** Attach an equipment and defect register, meter readings, panel and valve locations and any maintenance records. Request separate prices for readiness tests, critical repairs, cleaning and witnessed restart, with clear repair exclusions and delivery of keys and records.

**Reuse-readiness decision check:** Record former and proposed occupancy, electrical capacity, ventilation, drainage and escape routes; price suitability assessment separately from repairs and recommissioning. Restarting equipment or handing over keys is not permission for a change of use or an occupancy certificate.
