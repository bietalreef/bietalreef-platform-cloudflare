# Air conditioning and refrigeration in Al Jimi — company quotation guide

This guide covers company and establishment services, not individual tradesperson listings. Qualified technicians and permissions may be required depending on the work. Informational listings do not establish that a provider is published or available; Biet Al Reef customer support can help prepare a quotation request without guaranteeing a company, price or appointment.

## 1. Split AC installation

**When to request it:** A new room or undersized unit needs a load assessment using floor dimensions, ceiling height, glazing exposure, insulation and occupancy.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Photograph both proposed unit locations, measure the copper route, identify the drain and electrical panel, and show safe outdoor access.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Compare the exact unit model, brackets, pipe insulation, drainage or pump, penetrations and making good; exclude civil works and supply upgrades unless priced. Agree the split unit model and capacity, copper run, condensate drainage, wall penetrations, commissioning tests and finish repairs.

**Acceptance check:** Request manufacturer-compliant leak and evacuation checks, then verify cooling, condensate flow, vibration and noise.

**Request a quotation:** Attach room dimensions, both unit locations and the refrigerant and drain routes; request separate prices for equipment, brackets, piping, power alterations and wall repairs.

**Safety and scope clarification:** A qualified AC technician should verify room load, power supply and condensate drainage before drilling. Unit installation does not itself authorise façade alterations or a new electrical circuit. Assign responsibility for penetrations, insulation and reinstatement, and obtain route photographs and a drainage test.

## 2. Central AC installation

**When to request it:** For multiple rooms, unit price alone is insufficient without room-by-room cooling loads, plant access and air distribution design.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Provide floor plans, ceiling levels, supply/return routes, plant room details, power capacity and maintenance access.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Compare design duty, duct and insulation quantities, diffusers, controls and balancing; allocate ceiling reinstatement and any necessary approvals. Record design loads, duct and diffuser layout, controls, balancing, commissioning records and suspended-ceiling responsibilities.

**Acceptance check:** Require commissioning records, airflow and temperature readings, condensate checks and accessible filters.

**Request a quotation:** Supply plans, area schedule and ceiling heights; request itemised load calculations, ductwork, insulation, controls, air balancing and commissioning.

**Safety and scope clarification:** Check room-by-room loads, return-air paths and filter service access before approving equipment and ductwork. Ceiling changes, fire-system alterations and electrical upgrades are excluded unless expressly priced. Request coordinated drawings, airflow balancing results and a named party to resolve service clashes.

## 3. Concealed AC installation

**When to request it:** A concealed unit needs adequate ceiling void and service access without clashing with lighting or sprinkler services.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Show ceiling clearances, access hatch, return grille, diffusers and drain route.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Specify unit dimensions, access panels, plenum insulation and acoustic criteria; separate gypsum and painting costs. Specify ceiling clearance, service hatch, return-air and drain routes, and whether gypsum and painting are included.

**Acceptance check:** Check that filters and drain pan can be serviced without demolition; test airflow distribution and drainage.

**Request a quotation:** Show the ceiling void, service hatch, supply and return positions; ask for separate costs for the concealed unit, drainage, gypsum and access panels.

**Safety and scope clarification:** Confirm ceiling-void clearance and that filters and the condensate tray can actually be serviced through an accessible hatch. Do not accept a unit sealed behind fixed gypsum. Price access panels and ceiling reinstatement separately, then demonstrate service access and drainage at handover.

## 4. AC refrigerant piping

**When to request it:** Relocating a unit or roughing-in before finishes depends on correct copper sizing, length and protection.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Record unit model, each run length, elevation difference and penetration locations; photograph before concealment.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Compare copper specification, insulation, joints and UV protection; electrical work and condensate piping are separate unless included. Fix copper sizes and route lengths, insulation, joint limits and tightness tests before concealing the installation.

**Acceptance check:** Use manufacturer-appropriate tightness testing and retain route photographs and test records before closing surfaces.

**Request a quotation:** State the unit model, copper sizes, run length and height difference; request per-metre piping, insulation, fittings, tightness testing and concealed-work photographs.

**Safety and scope clarification:** Set copper diameters, run lengths and elevation differences to the equipment manufacturer's requirements and protect insulation from UV and abrasion. Photograph joints and obtain a qualified technician's appropriate tightness-test record before concealment; power and condensate lines are separate unless expressly included.

## 5. Air duct installation

**When to request it:** Poor distribution or a revised layout calls for duct sizing and planned supply and return locations.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Share reflected ceiling plan, corridor dimensions, obstructions, diffusers and access points.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Compare sheet gauge, joints, supports, insulation, dampers and cleanup; assign fire-stopping and ceiling making good. Specify duct cross-sections, insulation, supports, access panels and inspection requirements before ceiling closure.

**Acceptance check:** Request joint tightness checks to the project specification and measured airflow at agreed outlets against the approved design; inspect supports, vibration and whistling rather than accepting a simple power-on demonstration.

**Request a quotation:** Send the ceiling plan, outlets and obstacles; request duct quantities, metal specification, insulation, supports, inspection access and penetration sealing.

**Pre-closure decision:** Coordinate duct routes against sprinklers, cables and service hatches on the ceiling plan. Assign fire-barrier penetration sealing and reject access points concealed behind fixed gypsum. The quote must state whether air balancing and duct leakage tests are included or separately charged.

## 6. AC unit replacement

**When to request it:** A failed or inefficient unit should not be replaced without checking capacity and compatibility with existing services.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Photograph old nameplate, outdoor mount, refrigerant lines, drainage and power supply.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Clarify removal, compliant refrigerant recovery, disposal, line or bracket modifications; do not assume old piping can be reused. Identify old and replacement units, removal and disposal, mounting compatibility, connections and refrigerant handling.

**Acceptance check:** Confirm new model and warranty documentation, operating checks and free condensate drainage.

**Request a quotation:** Provide old-unit nameplate and route photographs; ask for removal, refrigerant recovery, mounting and connection changes, disposal and warranty as distinct items.

**Safety and scope clarification:** Check new-unit capacity against existing mounts, electrical supply, drains and pipework before assuming reuse. Itemise removal, compliant refrigerant recovery and lawful disposal of the old unit. Record the replacement model, commissioning and warranty; a new unit alone may not correct poor air distribution.

## 7. System testing and commissioning

**When to request it:** After installation or major repair, commissioning must document performance rather than just power-on.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Provide system drawings, equipment IDs, installation records, prior faults and available test points.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Agree a checklist for electrical safety, cooling, drainage, controls and air balance; identify who rectifies defects. Agree commissioning readings, test duration, setting adjustments and the fault report to be handed over.

**Acceptance check:** Obtain a dated report linking each reading to equipment ID, measurement point, operating conditions and agreed acceptance limit, with pass/rectify status and documented retesting after corrections.

**Request a quotation:** Provide equipment IDs, drawings and previous faults; request a measurement schedule, test duration, signed results and any retest charge.

**Commissioning acceptance decision:** Passing a single-unit test is not proof of room-by-room air distribution. Record ambient conditions, filter condition and fan settings during measurements. Do not accept unexplained readings without test locations, or unresolved defects without an assigned owner and retest date.

## 8. AC unit cleaning

**When to request it:** Weak airflow, odours or dust build-up may call for cleaning before assuming low refrigerant.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Photograph filters, coil, drain tray and leak points; list unit count and access height.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Compare filter removal, coil and tray cleaning, drain flushing and furniture protection; refrigerant leak repair is separate. Define coil, filter and drain-pan cleaning and surface protection; repairs and replacement parts require separate approval.

**Acceptance check:** Check visible cleanliness, drainage and restored airflow without cleaning chemical residue in occupied rooms.

**Request a quotation:** List unit counts, access heights and filter and drain photographs; request exact cleaning areas, chemicals, furniture protection and wastewater handling.

**Safety and scope clarification:** Isolate electrical power and protect furnishings before coil and condensate-tray washing. Agree wastewater capture, cleaning products and surfaces where chemicals are unsuitable. Cleaning excludes refrigerant leak repair and replacement components; verify free drainage after restart.

## 9. Refrigerant charging

**When to request it:** Poor cooling does not by itself prove low refrigerant; diagnose and investigate leaks first.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Record refrigerant type from nameplate, symptoms, measured conditions and previous charging history.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Separate diagnosis, leak repair, tightness testing, evacuation and weighed charge; no refrigerant mixing or deliberate venting. Require diagnosis of refrigerant loss, correct refrigerant type, charge quantity and leak testing before recharge.

**Acceptance check:** Obtain the qualified technician's leak-location and repair record, tightness and evacuation results, refrigerant type and weighed charge against the unit nameplate, plus post-repair operating readings; topping up alone does not establish a successful repair.

**Request a quotation:** Share the nameplate refrigerant type, charging history and symptoms; ask for separate diagnosis, leak repair, tightness test, evacuation and weighed recharge.

**Diagnosis before charging:** Establish whether refrigerant was lost through a leak or previous servicing and how the underlying cause will be verified as resolved. If the leak location is inaccessible, record that limitation and do not describe a temporary top-up as a permanent repair. Document compliant recovery and handling of refrigerant.

## 10. AC fault repair

**When to request it:** No-start, icing and water leakage have different causes; diagnose before replacing parts.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Provide fault video, error code, model, timing and whether the circuit breaker trips.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Ask for diagnostic fees followed by a repair quote naming cause, parts, labour and warranty; require evidence before compressor replacement. Define diagnostic steps, prior approval for replacement parts, callout fees and post-repair performance tests.

**Acceptance check:** Reproduce the original fault conditions, verify no leakage or repeat breaker trip, and retain a short service report.

**Request a quotation:** Send fault codes, symptom video and model details; request diagnostic fees, written cause, each part cost, labour and repair warranty.

**Safety and scope clarification:** Do not authorise compressor or control-board replacement from symptoms alone. Obtain a qualified technician's documented fault diagnosis, error codes and operating measurements. Quote components, labour and warranty separately, and retest the original failure condition after repair.

## 11. Air duct cleaning

**When to request it:** Dust inside ductwork, especially after renovations, requires defining affected runs rather than merely wiping grilles.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Photograph supply/return grilles, access points and visible deposits; identify zones and system type.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Compare access method, containment, suitable filtered extraction and plenum/grille cleaning; chemical spraying needs justification and consent. Specify duct sections covered, dust capture, protection of occupied rooms and before-and-after outlet evidence.

**Acceptance check:** Require internal before-and-after photographs at agreed access points, a list of inaccessible duct runs, and confirmation that access openings are resealed and no debris remains in ducts or outlets.

**Request a quotation:** Specify duct zones, access points and deposit photographs; ask how dust is contained, extracted, documented and whether any chemical treatment is proposed.

**Cleaning scope decision:** State whether coils, plenums and extraction fans are included or whether the work covers duct runs only. Agree repeatable internal photo locations for before-and-after comparison. Clean grilles alone do not prove that internal duct runs were cleaned; list excluded sections at handover.

## 12. Central AC maintenance

**When to request it:** Recurring rooftop or plant-room faults need a structured review of controls, fans and heat exchangers.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Gather equipment schedule, run hours, fault history, roof access and filter/belt condition.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Compare motor, vibration, drain, sensor and cleaning checks; identify consumables and crane access excluded from base price. List central units, permitted downtime, filters, operating measurements and safe access responsibilities.

**Acceptance check:** Receive unit-by-unit readings, photos, critical defects and an approved remedial plan before extra parts are changed.

**Request a quotation:** Supply unit inventory, run hours, fault history and roof access; request unit-by-unit checks with consumables, parts and lifting separately priced.

**Safety and scope clarification:** Rooftop and plant-room maintenance requires safe-access arrangements and electrical isolation, with shutdowns and lifting equipment clearly identified. Log each unit's ID, readings, filters, belts and drains; require written approval before additional parts are replaced.

**Additional operational review:** Before central-unit servicing, request fault history, previous operating readings and an agreed shutdown window compatible with building occupancy. The quote should identify safe energy isolation, checks of filters, belts, fans, heat exchangers and condensate drainage as relevant to the equipment, and excluded replacement parts. At handover, compare recorded before-and-after readings under stated operating conditions and list faults requiring separate repairs; routine servicing does not guarantee every fault is resolved.

## 13. AC spare-part replacement

**When to request it:** A fan, sensor or capacitor fault does not justify replacing an entire board without diagnosis and compatibility checks.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Photograph model label, failed part number, connectors and visible damage; retain diagnostic result.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Specify genuine or approved equivalent part, part number, warranty, installation cost and return of old component where feasible. Record exact part number, model compatibility, part warranty, installation charge and return of the old part.

**Acceptance check:** Test the affected operating mode and retain part identifier and warranty invoice.

**Request a quotation:** Provide unit and component numbers with diagnostic findings; ask whether the part is genuine or approved equivalent, its warranty, labour and old-part return.

**Safety and scope clarification:** Match the replacement part to the exact equipment model, electrical ratings and manufacturer requirements; visual similarity is not proof of compatibility. Specify origin, warranty and return of the old component where practical, then document a functional test of the original fault.

**Replacement compatibility review:** Provide the unit nameplate, old part number and diagnosed reason for replacement. Compare the proposed part number, ratings and documented compatibility with the equipment model before accepting a quote. Separate the part, diagnosis, labour and any refrigerant work; agree component and installation warranties and disposal or return of the removed part. At handover, record the installed part number and test the function associated with the original fault rather than accepting an unsupported claim of an 'equivalent' part.

## 14. Scheduled AC maintenance contracts

**When to request it:** Multiple units or locations benefit from planned visits with defined response terms rather than ad-hoc calls.

**Site evidence and measurements:** Prepare asset register, locations, operating hours, fault history and site access windows.

**Scope and quote comparison:** Compare visit frequency, checklist, filter service, call-out response, emergency hours, excluded parts/refrigerant and variation approvals. Agree annual visit frequency, fault response terms, inspection checklist, consumables and excluded spare parts.

**Acceptance check:** Review visit reports, fault log and open actions; a maintenance contract is not a guarantee against breakdowns.

**Request a quotation:** Send equipment inventory, locations and operating hours; request visit frequency, response terms, emergency coverage, exclusions and parts approval procedure.

**Safety and scope clarification:** List covered assets, visit frequency, tasks, response windows and fault-reporting procedure. Spare parts, refrigerant and lifting are excluded unless expressly included and priced. Review visit reports and unresolved actions; response time does not guarantee repair completion.
