# Integrated finishing in Al Jimi

A preparation guide for company quotations, not confirmation of a listed contractor, price or appointment. Regulated work requires appropriately qualified parties and applicable approvals.

## 1. Villa finishing

**Specifications, materials and equipment:** Specify slip resistance for entrance and wet-area flooring, tile adhesive compatibility with the substrate and movement-joint locations; substitutions to approved samples require written acceptance.

**When needed:** Majlis, bedrooms and wet rooms need different specifications; begin with a floor-by-floor plan showing completed structure and services.

**Photos and measurements:** Provide room photos, floor areas, ceiling heights, outlet locations and proposed flooring and paint samples.

**Scope and exclusions:** Separate flooring, gypsum, paint, doors, electrical and plumbing; assign material supply and testing, and exclude furniture or appliances unless stated.

**Comparing quotes and contract:** Compare measured quantities, waste allowances, product grades, work sequence and waterproofing cure periods; link payments to inspected milestones.

**Acceptance check:** Check floor levels, joints and wet-area falls, operate lights and water fixtures, and log defects room by room.

**Agreement and acceptance control:** Require an approved room-by-room sample schedule linking tile and paint references to actual locations. Treat wet-area waterproofing and fall tests as hold points before tiling; installation photographs do not replace a test record.

**Variation and approval boundary:** New openings, wall relocation or changes to structural loads are not ordinary finishing items; obtain separate assessment and approvals before pricing them. Record changes to tile type, area or service points in a variation showing cost and programme effects before work starts.

**Quotation request details:** Attach floor-by-floor areas and the condition of electrical and plumbing rough-ins. State who supplies materials and request itemised trade prices and inspection hold points before concealed work is closed.

## 2. Apartment finishing

**Specifications, materials and equipment:** Measure lift dimensions and the delivery route for long panels before ordering; define dust containment and protection of fire detectors under building-management procedures, never by unqualified isolation of life-safety systems.

**When needed:** Occupied apartments and shared buildings require lift booking, noise control and common-area protection.

**Photos and measurements:** Photograph entrances and retained furniture; record room dimensions, existing tiles and service condition, plus building rules.

**Scope and exclusions:** Specify stripping, debris removal, lift protection, work windows and water shutdowns; structural changes and main-service alterations need separate assessment.

**Comparing quotes and contract:** Compare protection and disposal costs, material specifications, duration and kitchen or bathroom downtime rather than lump sums.

**Acceptance check:** Inspect common-area damage, floor edges, sealant, sanitary operation and final cleaning.

**Agreement and acceptance control:** Photograph lift and corridor condition before and after the works and identify who authorises water shutdowns. Record damage to common property separately rather than treating apartment finish warranties as a substitute.

**Variation and approval boundary:** Distinguish replacing finishes within the apartment from penetrating shared walls or rerouting drainage or HVAC. Obtain building-management and competent-authority approvals where required and document reinstatement of common areas.

**Quotation request details:** Attach building management rules, permitted work hours and occupancy status. Request separate allowances for lift and corridor protection, strip-out and new finishes.

## 3. Retail shop finishing

**Specifications, materials and equipment:** Check floor suitability for stock trolleys, display-surface abrasion and cleaning resistance, and any required fire-performance documentation before procurement; 'commercial grade' alone is not a specification.

**When needed:** A retail unit needs coordinated frontage, customer circulation, display fixtures and lighting before surface finishes.

**Photos and measurements:** Provide a layout, storefront dimensions, power and data points, ceiling and floor photographs and landlord requirements.

**Scope and exclusions:** Separate partitions, flooring, counter, lighting and signage; permits, life-safety systems and external signage are not automatically included.

**Comparing quotes and contract:** Compare display loads, floor wear ratings, fixture specifications, sample approval stages and frontage lead times.

**Acceptance check:** Walk the customer route, test door clearance and power points, assess merchandise lighting and display stability.

**Agreement and acceptance control:** Sign off dated counter and display layouts before electrical rough-in, and have the competent party check exit-door operation and escape routes. Working display lighting does not establish life-safety compliance.

**Variation and approval boundary:** Do not combine retail finishes with alarm, sprinkler or sign approvals by assumption. Require a responsibility matrix naming the designer, contractor and approving party for each system before works could conceal it.

**Quotation request details:** Send customer circulation drawings, storefront dimensions and display loads. Request separate prices for counters, cladding, lighting and signage, identifying who handles approvals.

## 4. Office finishing

**Specifications, materials and equipment:** Set the required acoustic performance of partitions, including perimeter seals and service penetrations, plus chair-traffic floor finishes and data outlet locations; board thickness alone does not establish acoustic performance.

**When needed:** Office layouts depend on workstations, meeting rooms, acoustic privacy and network routes.

**Photos and measurements:** Provide furniture layouts, headcount, data outlet locations, ceiling heights and HVAC or sprinkler constraints.

**Scope and exclusions:** Separate glazed and gypsum partitions, flooring, service penetrations and data cabling; active networking and fire-system changes are not assumed.

**Comparing quotes and contract:** Compare partition acoustic performance, floor durability, service access and downtime; agree a coordinated trade drawing.

**Acceptance check:** Check door swings, task lighting, data outlets, access panels and finishes around services.

**Agreement and acceptance control:** Obtain a coordinated above-ceiling drawing showing HVAC, sprinklers, data routes and access panels before closing gypsum ceilings. Test network connectivity separately from visible finishing quality.

**Variation and approval boundary:** Changes to office layouts affecting exits, sprinkler coverage or ventilation need competent review. Each variation should identify who revises coordinated drawings and tests affected systems after partitions move.

**Quotation request details:** Provide workstation counts, network layouts and meeting-room needs. Request acoustic partition specifications, service-routing responsibilities and the planned office downtime.

## 5. Interior refurbishment

**Specifications, materials and equipment:** Measure substrate moisture and soundness before new cladding and agree a compatible adhesion test patch; do not conceal friable or damp surfaces beneath adhesive panels.

**When needed:** Before replacing damaged finishes, establish whether moisture, substrate movement or ordinary wear caused failure.

**Photos and measurements:** Photograph damage and its history, measure affected areas, inspect substrate condition and list items to retain.

**Scope and exclusions:** Specify investigation, removal, treatment, reinstatement and waste disposal; concealed leaks or structural faults require separate scope.

**Comparing quotes and contract:** Compare targeted repair against full refurbishment, disturbance to adjacent work and matching of old and new materials.

**Acceptance check:** Inspect the dry repaired substrate, colour, levels and edges; document the cause before concealing it.

**Agreement and acceptance control:** Photograph exposed layers after strip-out and approve the root-cause treatment before reinstatement. Do not proceed with concealed-defect extras without a written scope, quantities and agreed price or pricing mechanism.

**Variation and approval boundary:** If strip-out reveals structural damage, extensive mould or an active leak, hold reinstatement in the affected area until the cause and remedial scope are assessed. Document separately contracted remedial work before concealment.

**Quotation request details:** Identify the affected area, any known cause and elements to retain. Ask for priced local-repair and full-refurbishment options and a process for unforeseen concealed defects.

## 6. Decorative fit-out works

**Specifications, materials and equipment:** Match suspended cladding and cabinet loads to the wall construction and anchoring system; specify luminaire ratings, operating temperature and ventilation clearances, with engineered reinforcement where gypsum cannot carry the load.

**When needed:** Decorative work may combine wall cladding, light coves and fixed joinery; clarify boundaries between finisher, carpenter and electrician.

**Photos and measurements:** Supply wall photographs, elevations, socket and AC locations, finish samples and detailed drawings.

**Scope and exclusions:** Specify fixings, panel edges, joints, lighting and painting; distinguish movable furniture and appliances from fixed work.

**Comparing quotes and contract:** Compare corner mock-ups, joint treatment, moisture suitability and access to lighting drivers.

**Acceptance check:** Inspect line alignment, colour consistency, secure fixing and maintenance access without breaking the feature.

**Agreement and acceptance control:** Approve a physical corner mock-up combining joinery, cladding and lighting, with finish references and service openings. Do not conceal a lighting driver behind a panel that cannot be safely removed.

**Variation and approval boundary:** Changes to suspended loads or wiring routes after mock-up approval are not merely cosmetic. Require revised fixing details, wall-capacity checks and circuit protection review before additions are installed.

**Quotation request details:** Provide wall elevations, joint details, finish samples and lighting-driver positions. Ask for a corner mock-up and separate joinery, cladding and electrical allowances.

## 7. Integrated finishing handover

**Specifications, materials and equipment:** Collect product and colour references, delivery batches, maintenance manuals and concealed-work test records; agree suitable instruments and owner attendance for level and drainage-fall checks.

**When needed:** Handover is more than cleaning: each item must be evidenced and outstanding defects closed before acceptance.

**Photos and measurements:** Collect as-built drawings, agreed quantities, concealed-work photos, product invoices, warranties and a snag list.

**Scope and exclusions:** Name the party responsible for electrical, water and waterproofing tests, keys and manuals; statutory certificates are not assumed.

**Comparing quotes and contract:** Compare document schedules, defect rectification periods and final payment retention; list exclusions expressly.

**Acceptance check:** Walk each room, operate lights, switches, drains and doors, check falls, and sign a dated outstanding-items record.

**Agreement and acceptance control:** Track every snag by number, location, photograph, owner and reinspection date; document keys and manuals in a separate handover record. A deferred defect is not automatically accepted because the walk-through was signed.

**Variation and approval boundary:** Separate technical acceptance of each system from visual acceptance of finishes, and record warranty terms and commencement for each trade. Receipt of keys does not certify uninspected defects as closed.

**Quotation request details:** Attach the snag list, contracts, available as-built drawings and warranties. Request a test and document handover schedule and named responsibility for closing each defect.
