# Painting services in Al Jimi — company quotation guide

A useful quotation specifies substrate condition, coating system, quantities and protection before cost. No company, price or completion date is guaranteed. Moisture and structural damage require separate diagnosis.

## 1. Villa painting

**Pre-approval check:** Trial the colour on a representative wall under daylight and artificial lighting; record the approved product, colour code and sheen on the sample rather than relying on a phone photo alone.

**When needed:** refreshing several rooms or preparing a villa for occupancy at an Al Jimi property.

**Photos, measurements and specifications:** Record wall/ceiling areas, stairwell heights and room counts, add an overall view of each room and close-ups of cracks or holes; assign the proposed colour to each room.

**Scope, materials and equipment:** Ask for room-by-room colour schedule, protection, filler, primer and specified coats, specifying primer compatibility with existing substrates, topcoat count, floor and furniture covers, and safe ladders or platforms for stairwells. **Exclusions:** leak repairs or gypsum replacement unless priced.

**Compare quotations and agree terms:** compare paint grade, washability, coats and preparation. Agree a room-by-room colour sample, whether ceilings and doors are included in measured quantities, and a phased room handover so the entire villa need not be closed.

**Acceptance inspection:** inspect daylight colour, corners and behind doors. Sign off rooms individually; check that masking has been removed and no paint marks remain on floors or furniture.

**Additional quality gate:** Distinguish active damp patches from superficial defects in the room handover sheet; do not paint over an unresolved leak, and approve colour and sheen only after the sample has dried.

**Request a quotation:** Provide a room-by-room schedule of approximate wall and ceiling areas, stairwell heights and occupancy. Request separate allowances for furniture protection, defect preparation, coats and colour changes, with an on-site check of high-access areas before quantities are finalised.

## 2. Apartment painting

**Pre-approval check:** Test adhesion in a discreet area behind furniture, agree ventilation arrangements, and base room reoccupation on actual drying and the product instructions, especially in occupied apartments.

**When needed:** repainting an occupied or rental apartment at an Al Jimi property.

**Photos, measurements and specifications:** Record room sizes, wall condition, furniture positions and access hours, include photos of furniture, outlets and ceiling junctions, and identify the intended colour for each room.

**Scope, materials and equipment:** Ask for surface protection, fixing anchor holes and agreed repainting, specifying anchor-hole filler, spot primer and finish coats, appliance covers and dust barriers suitable for an occupied apartment. **Exclusions:** moving heavy furniture or wallpaper stripping without agreement.

**Compare quotations and agree terms:** compare drying time, odour, protection and cleanup. Set permitted access hours, who clears rooms and moves light furniture, and when protective coverings can be removed and the apartment safely reoccupied.

**Acceptance inspection:** check ceiling edges, skirting and clean outlets/floors. Check outlets, skirting and areas behind furniture for splashes, and note any room still needing ventilation or remedial work.

**Additional quality gate:** Agree protection for vents, air-conditioning units and switches, phased room access and furniture return; check ventilation and residual odour before the space is occupied again.

**Request a quotation:** Share a floor plan or room count, approximate wall areas, hole photographs, furniture layout and permitted working hours. Ask for separate costs for light-item moving and protection, patching, painting and cleanup, plus a realistic room re-entry period after curing.

## 3. Exterior building painting

**Pre-approval check:** Distinguish poor coating adhesion from active moisture; investigate the source of dampness before painting, and record repair quantities and safe access without presenting paint as a leak remedy.

**When needed:** faded or peeling facades exposed to sun and moisture at an Al Jimi property.

**Photos, measurements and specifications:** Record facade area, heights, crack photos and exposure, include full-elevation views and close-ups of peeling around openings and movement joints.

**Scope, materials and equipment:** Ask for washing, sound-surface preparation, primer and suitable exterior coating, specifying facade washing, checks on existing coating adhesion, primer and finish systems, scaffolds or access platforms and pedestrian protection. **Exclusions:** structural crack repair or roof leak remediation.

**Compare quotations and agree terms:** compare coating system, UV resistance, access equipment and permits. Require a safe access plan for each elevation, protection of vehicles, pedestrians and glazing, and clear responsibility for permits and scaffold-related damage.

**Acceptance inspection:** Check colour uniformity, edges and clean glazing from safe access points. Appearance alone does not prove adhesion; request a suitable documented adhesion test where the coating specification requires one. Inspect window and movement-joint edges from safe viewing points, and verify scaffold removal leaves no damage or debris.

**Additional quality gate:** Specify façade access and protection for passers-by and windows, and assess coating adhesion and surface salts; do not coat over unresolved flaking or dampness.

**Request a quotation:** Send views of each elevation where possible, heights, existing coating type and locations of peeling or cracks. Separate substrate preparation, exterior coating system, access equipment, public/window protection and any required permits; do not include structural crack remediation without assessment.

## 4. Door and timber painting

**Pre-approval check:** Test the proposed finish on an inconspicuous part of the door to assess veneer absorption and compatibility with existing coatings; protect hinges and locks from spray.

**When needed:** scratched doors, faded timber or refinishing at an Al Jimi property.

**Photos, measurements and specifications:** Record leaf count, substrate/veneer type, hinges and previous finish, photograph both faces, edges, frame and existing finish; specify the required colour and sheen.

**Scope, materials and equipment:** Ask for sanding, spot repairs, compatible primer and paint or varnish, specifying sanding appropriate to the veneer, filler, primer and paint or varnish, hardware masking and a ventilated curing location. **Exclusions:** warped door repair or veneer replacement.

**Compare quotations and agree terms:** compare application method, odour, sheen and colour sample. Specify whether leaves are removed or finished in place, how hinges and locks are protected, and approval of a sheen sample before all doors are coated.

**Acceptance inspection:** check free opening, even sheen and unpainted hardware. Operate each door after curing and inspect bottom edges, hinges and lock surrounds for tackiness or unwanted coating.

**Additional quality gate:** Record timber species and existing coating before selecting sanding and primer; protect hinges, locks and seals and test door closure after the coating cures.

**Request a quotation:** State the number and dimensions of door leaves and whether both faces, edges and frames are included. Provide photos of veneer and old finish. Ask for an approved colour/sheen sample and separate preparation, primer and topcoat or varnish costs, including work location and no-use period.

## 5. Decorative paint finishes

**Pre-approval check:** Approve a fully dried texture sample on the actual substrate, noting lighting direction and how corners and returns are measured; a small swatch can look different across a full wall.

**When needed:** creating textured, metallic or concrete-effect feature walls at an Al Jimi property.

**Photos, measurements and specifications:** Record feature-wall area, lighting, reference image and flatness, photograph the wall under daylight and artificial lighting and provide a clear texture and colour reference.

**Scope, materials and equipment:** Ask for approved sample, finish layers, application tools and agreed sealer, specifying texture application tools, layer sequence, protective sealer and trial-panel timing, with lighting and adjoining edges masked. **Exclusions:** major wall levelling or new lighting design.

**Compare quotations and agree terms:** compare sample size, method, cleanability and upkeep. Approve a test panel under the actual lighting, acceptable texture variation, and treatment of corners, ceiling junctions and transitions.

**Acceptance inspection:** compare finished texture to the approved sample under site lighting. Compare the finished feature wall with the approved sample under both daylight and artificial light, recording texture or sheen differences at junctions.

**Additional quality gate:** Approve a sufficiently large texture sample under representative lighting, showing application direction and joint boundaries; agree how future patch repairs will be judged.

**Request a quotation:** Provide a reference image, feature-wall dimensions, lighting conditions and photos showing substrate flatness. Request a sample panel or on-site trial, define the coated area, sealer and cleaning method, and clarify whether sample costs are credited to the final work.

## 6. Stripping and preparing old paint

**Pre-approval check:** Assess unknown old coatings before stripping and require a safe dust and waste containment method; do not assume dry sanding or heating is suitable before evaluating hazards and the substrate.

**When needed:** recurring flaking or blistering before repainting at an Al Jimi property.

**Photos, measurements and specifications:** Record defect photos, any moisture readings and substrate type, include a wide image of the affected area and close-ups of loose coating edges; avoid disturbing unidentified coatings before assessment.

**Scope, materials and equipment:** Ask for removal of loose coatings, dust containment, substrate repair and priming, specifying safe scraping, dust extraction and waste containment, plus primer after substrate assessment; no dry sanding of unidentified coatings. **Exclusions:** fixing moisture sources or hazardous materials without specialist assessment.

**Compare quotations and agree terms:** compare stripping limits, dust containment and disposal. Make acceptance of a dry, sound substrate a separate hold point before repainting; assign responsibility for moisture diagnosis and stopping work if suspect hazardous coatings are found.

**Acceptance inspection:** Document removal of unsound coating and assess substrate dryness against the product's applicable limits and the agreed measurement method before priming; visual dryness alone is insufficient. Photograph the dry, sound substrate before priming and record moisture readings if taken; do not accept a new coat concealing unresolved blistering.

**Additional quality gate:** Assess coating age, composition and possible hazardous constituents before scraping or sanding; do not dry-strip or disperse dust of unknown composition without competent assessment and containment.

**Request a quotation:** Photograph peeling boundaries, blistering and any suspected water source; include moisture readings if available. Request a cause assessment and itemised removal, dust containment, disposal and substrate preparation, keeping leak repairs and suspected hazardous material assessment separate.

## 7. Paint touch-up and recoating

**Pre-approval check:** Compare the dried repair in daylight and evening lighting; where the colour difference remains visible, document the full-wall repaint option instead of accepting a mismatched patch.

**When needed:** local scratches or colour mismatch after service work at an Al Jimi property.

**Photos, measurements and specifications:** Record defect photos, colour code, affected area and previous paint age, include close-up and normal-viewing-distance photographs of each patch to assess colour visibility.

**Scope, materials and equipment:** Ask for spot preparation, feathered edges and agreed repainting extent, specifying patch filler, feathering method, spot primer and finish coating, with protection for adjacent finishes. **Exclusions:** guaranteed invisible patch against aged paint.

**Compare quotations and agree terms:** compare patch versus whole-wall repainting for consistency. Mark repair boundaries on photos and agree whether a slight colour difference is acceptable or a full wall must be recoated, including how scope increases are priced.

**Acceptance inspection:** check colour from several angles and smoothness. View the patch from normal occupancy distance under two lighting conditions and photograph whether full-wall repainting is required.

**Additional quality gate:** Distinguish ageing-related colour difference from coating adhesion failure; inspect the patch boundary in daylight and reject touch-ups that conceal ongoing dampness or cracking.

**Request a quotation:** Share close and wide photos of each patch, the colour code if known, approximate age of the coating and the full wall area. Ask for two prices—local touch-up and full-wall repainting—and agree in advance how visible colour differences will be assessed.
