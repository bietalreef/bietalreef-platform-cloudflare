# Waterproofing and thermal insulation in Al Jimi

Preparatory guidance for company and establishment quotations, not a claim of a listed provider, price or appointment. Hazardous specialist work requires qualified contractors and appropriate safety controls.

## 1. Roof waterproofing

**When needed:** A leak or an aged membrane can justify work, but ponding around outlets may point to defective falls that a new membrane alone will not correct.

**Evidence and measurements:** Photograph the whole roof, drains, parapets, upstands and old layers; measure area and joint lengths and locate equipment.

**Scope and exclusions:** Specify removal of failed layers, fall corrections, substrate preparation, membrane type and thickness, drain detailing and protection from traffic and sunlight. Structural concrete repairs and moving AC units are separate unless priced.

**Quotes, agreement and acceptance:** Compare system warranty, measurement rules, corner details and waste removal. Agree a safe, suitable water test after checking loading and drainage, before covering the system; inspect the test record and the ceiling below.

**Additional pre-cover check:** Record drain outlet levels and membrane terminations at equipment plinths; do not add ponding-test loads without a competent assessment of roof capacity.

**Inspection limits and responsibility:** Cracks in the slab or settlement around outlets require separate structural assessment before waterproofing is approved. Passing one leak test does not cover future drainage faults.

**Quotation brief for this service:** Ask for a breakdown of measured roof area, upstand and joint lengths, drain counts, removal of old membrane, fall correction and protective finish, with a defined leak-test procedure.

**Service-specific agreement:** Name who protects rooftop equipment and outlets during work, who reinstates tiles or protective layers, and when concealed membrane-detail photos must be handed over.

## 2. Bathroom waterproofing

**When needed:** A bathroom renovation or dampness below tiles may call for waterproofing, but first rule out supply-pipe or drainage leaks.

**Evidence and measurements:** Share dimensions and photos showing the shower, toilet, drain, door threshold and wet wall zones.

**Scope and exclusions:** Specify tile removal where necessary, drying, corner and pipe-penetration detailing, wall upstand heights and a membrane compatible with tile adhesive. Plumbing replacement, tiles and sanitaryware should be separately identified.

**Quotes, agreement and acceptance:** Compare curing time, protection from punctures and reinstatement cost. Require a safe ponding test before tiling and obtain concealed-work photographs, test results and confirmation of falls to the drain.

**Additional pre-tiling check:** Inspect membrane continuity around the shower base, floor drain and threshold; a ponding result does not rule out separate supply or waste-pipe leaks.

**Inspection limits and responsibility:** Do not cover a membrane before its specified curing period. A water-retention test is not a substitute for checking drain falls or the integrity of supply and waste pipes.

**Quotation brief for this service:** Provide floor area, shower perimeter, required wall upstand height and penetration counts. Price demolition and tile reinstatement separately from the membrane, safe water test and concealed-work records.

**Service-specific agreement:** Identify who removes and reinstalls sanitary fixtures, how the doorway threshold will be protected, and who pays for tiles damaged during investigation.

## 3. Water tank waterproofing

**When needed:** Water loss or a damaged lining can warrant intervention, but check fittings and float valves before blaming the tank shell.

**Evidence and measurements:** Provide tank material, dimensions, approximate capacity, location, crack and fitting photos, and whether the water is potable.

**Scope and exclusions:** Require documented suitability of the lining for the water use and substrate, plus safe emptying, cleaning, drying and ventilation. Confined-space entry needs qualified personnel and safety controls; pumps and supply pipes are not automatically included.

**Quotes, agreement and acceptance:** Compare material suitability evidence, curing and hygiene arrangements, and return-to-service steps. Agree leak testing and cleaning before use, and obtain records without assuming an official certificate.

**Additional return-to-service check:** Do not return a potable-water tank to use until lining cure, cleaning and applicable water-quality checks are completed; a watertightness test alone does not establish potability.

**Inspection limits and responsibility:** Keep lining work separate from verification of drinking-water quality. No worker should enter an enclosed tank without a risk assessment, appropriate permit and rescue arrangements.

**Quotation brief for this service:** State tank capacity, water use, shell material, access opening and entry conditions. Request lining suitability documentation, a confined-space safety plan if relevant, and separate emptying, cleaning, testing and recommissioning costs.

**Service-specific agreement:** Record the expected tank outage and interim water supply, plus responsibility for entry safety and water-quality checks on recommissioning; do not promise drinking-water suitability in advance.

## 4. Building thermal insulation

**When needed:** High cooling demand or renovation may justify insulation, though thermal bridges and air leakage can also be responsible.

**Evidence and measurements:** Supply roof and wall photographs, layer details if known, surface areas, openings, AC penetrations and sun exposure.

**Scope and exclusions:** Ask for declared thermal resistance, thickness, relevant density, continuity at edges and bridges, and fire and moisture detailing. AC repairs or window replacement are separate work.

**Quotes, agreement and acceptance:** Compare documented performance rather than thickness alone and compatibility with the roof assembly. Inspect fastening and gaps before closing and retain installation photos and product data.

**Additional pre-closure check:** Photograph insulation continuity around openings and thermal bridges; verify required ventilation paths remain clear and approved fire details are not compromised.

**Inspection limits and responsibility:** Do not accept a promised percentage reduction in energy bills without a separate assessment. Check condensation risks, fire performance and compatibility with the existing assembly.

**Quotation brief for this service:** Supply roof and wall areas, known assembly layers and AC penetrations. Require declared thermal resistance, installed thickness and details for thermal bridges, fire safety and condensation control, rather than a board-only price.

**Service-specific agreement:** Tie material approval to product data, fixing details and applicable fire requirements; assign responsibility for addressing condensation risks at junctions before closing finishes.

## 5. External wall waterproofing

**When needed:** Damp around facade cracks, joints or windows may originate outside, but concealed internal pipe leaks must also be considered.

**Evidence and measurements:** Photograph the facade overall and close-up; record orientation, height, crack and joint lengths, window edges and interior damp locations.

**Scope and exclusions:** Specify crack treatment appropriate to its cause, cleaning, primer, coating or membrane system and window perimeter details. Access scaffolds, structural crack repair and replacement cladding require explicit pricing.

**Quotes, agreement and acceptance:** Compare joint movement tolerance, UV exposure and safe access cost. Agree a sample area and checks of adhesion, coverage and junctions; inspect for voids and untreated openings.

**Additional acceptance check:** Inspect window-frame junctions, movement joints and cladding terminations; flag structural cracks for separate assessment rather than concealing them under waterproofing.

**Inspection limits and responsibility:** Surface coatings must not conceal moving structural cracks or unsafe cladding fixings. Require cause assessment and a safe-access plan for elevated work.

**Quotation brief for this service:** Share facade length and height, joint and window photographs, and internal damp marks. Separate diagnosis, crack preparation, coating system, access scaffolds and sample testing, excluding unassessed structural repairs.

**Service-specific agreement:** State the facade access method, who arranges permits where required, and who protects windows and adjacent property and reinstates finishes after joint repairs.

## 6. Rainwater ingress repair

**When needed:** Moisture appearing only during rain may enter at window seals, blocked outlets or roof-wall junctions. Recoating an entire roof before tracing the path may waste money.

**Evidence and measurements:** Record when staining occurs, photograph during and after rain, and mark water paths, outlets, penetrations and the affected area.

**Scope and exclusions:** Price investigation of outlets and joints first, with controlled local spray testing only where safe, then the identified repair and specified reinstatement. Whole-facade and interior repairs are not assumed.

**Quotes, agreement and acceptance:** Compare how each contractor proposes to demonstrate the source, not just material brands. Agree responsibility for opening and reinstating finishes; record an appropriate retest, noting that final confirmation may await rainfall.

**Additional post-repair check:** Target any controlled spray test at the diagnosed ingress path, protect electrical fittings and finishes, and record areas whose performance can only be confirmed during later rainfall.

**Inspection limits and responsibility:** Any spray test needs an agreed location, duration and site permission. Do not direct test water near exposed wiring or unprotected electrical equipment.

**Quotation brief for this service:** Record dates and locations of rain-linked dampness and possible window, outlet or roof paths. Ask for two stages: documented source tracing, then defined repair, reinstatement and appropriate retesting—not blanket recoating without evidence.

**Service-specific agreement:** Price rain-ingress diagnosis separately from repair, and require client approval and before/after photos before opening any additional finishes, with documented test limits.

## 7. Waterproofing maintenance

**When needed:** Detached edges, punctures or failed protective layers may need maintenance; a patch is appropriate only if the remaining substrate and system are sound.

**Evidence and measurements:** Share the installation date if known, old membrane type, defect photos, damaged area and adjacent areas exposed to traffic.

**Scope and exclusions:** Require compatibility checks, defined opening and cleaning limits, new laps and protection. A local patch does not automatically renew the warranty for the whole roof or include interior damage repairs.

**Quotes, agreement and acceptance:** Compare targeted repair with wider replacement and their risks and limited warranties. Agree a local test before covering and obtain photos of laps and a record of untreated areas.

**Additional maintenance check:** Compare before-and-after photos at drains, joints and blisters; record patch locations and compatibility with the existing membrane rather than relying on surface appearance.

**Inspection limits and responsibility:** A brittle membrane of unknown composition may not accept a new patch. Request an adhesion test or system manufacturer's guidance before choosing local repair.

**Quotation brief for this service:** Provide damaged membrane area, lap-edge photographs and system age if known. Compare a compatible local patch with sectional replacement, including warranty limits and seam testing.

**Service-specific agreement:** Define warranty limits for the new patch without extending them to the old membrane; agree who inspects after the next rainfall or suitable water exposure and remedies a failed joint.
