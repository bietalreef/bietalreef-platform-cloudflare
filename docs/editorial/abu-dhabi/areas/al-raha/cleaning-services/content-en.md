# Cleaning, Disinfection and Pest Control in Al Raha

## Scope of this guide

This sector covers property, upholstery, façade, site, tank and pool cleaning; pest control and disinfection; and building operations and staffing support. A useful request starts with area, surface type, site condition and occupancy, then states access arrangements, working windows, water and power sources and equipment storage. In Al Raha, coordinate lifts, service areas, material entry and work windows with property management where required; access rules and hours should not be assumed to be identical across buildings.

Company and establishment listings remain separate from individual tradespeople. If no suitable cleaning, pest-control or operations company is displayed, Bait Al Reef customer service can receive a quotation request stating property type, service, area, photos, timing, frequency, sensitive surfaces and access restrictions. A request does not establish that a provider is available or confirm a price or date.

## Companies and establishments

### Home and building cleaning

#### 1. Villa cleaning
Scope a villa clean by rooms, bathrooms, floors, windows and any outdoor areas included. Provide a layout or photos, state whether routine or deep cleaning is required, and identify delicate surfaces and furniture needing protection. Request a room-by-room task list, products, crew and duration; inspect corners, bathrooms, glass and all agreed areas against a checklist.

#### 2. Apartment cleaning
State apartment area, bedroom and bathroom count, balcony and windows, and whether it is occupied or vacant. Provide kitchen, bathroom and floor photos, and note lift or service-entry arrangements if building management requires coordination. Compare whether cabinet interiors, appliances and glass are included; inspect rooms, bathrooms and balcony before closing the job.

#### 3. Office cleaning
Inventory floor area, workstations, meeting rooms, facilities and washrooms, and specify hours that avoid disrupting staff. Provide a plan showing devices, records and shared surfaces, and request product details and procedures for screens, equipment and waste. Compare visit frequency, staffing and daily tasks; review the completion log with the site contact after each visit.

#### 4. Building cleaning
For a multi-storey building, define common areas, entrances, stairs, lifts, corridors and service rooms, together with cleaning frequency. Provide floor plans, surface photos and water and storage points, and state who supplies consumables and handles urgent requests. Compare coverage hours, supervision and inspection logs; check each area against its own schedule rather than relying on a general impression.

#### 5. Post-fit-out cleaning
This differs from routine cleaning because it may involve cutting and gypsum dust, installation residue and paint or grout marks. Send photos of floors, glass, kitchens and bathrooms and state whether contractor debris has already been removed. Request a small-area cleaner test and protection method for stone and metal; inspect corners, joints, labels and residue without scratching finishes.

#### 6. Pre-move-in cleaning
State the property handover date and condition and identify cabinets, appliances or curtains that will remain. Provide a room list and photos of soiled areas. Explicitly include cabinet, drawer, wardrobe, appliance and window interiors rather than assuming they are covered. Compare completion time and any re-visit terms; record the property's condition after cleaning and before furniture arrives.

#### 7. Recurring cleaning contracts
Define property type, area, zones, visit frequency, working hours and expected service level. Attach daily, weekly and monthly task schedules and assign responsibility for products, equipment, staff replacement and issue logging. Compare hours, visits, tasks, consumables and supervision; review performance records and each cycle's observations before renewal.

### Upholstery and furnishing cleaning

#### 8. Steam sofa cleaning
Provide the fabric-care label and full sofa photos, describe stains and their age, and identify delicate dyes or embroidery. The method should be tested on a hidden area before wider treatment, with moisture level and drying time explained. Compare spot and full cleaning and whether loose cushions are included; check colour change, odour and residual dampness after drying.

#### 9. Rug cleaning
Identify rug fibre, dimensions, pile height, backing and any stains or odours. Provide front and reverse photos and the care label if available. Request a fibre-appropriate method, colourfastness check, transport and drying plan. Compare on-site treatment with off-site collection and clarify stain removal; inspect edges, colour and texture once dry.

#### 10. Carpet-tile and fitted-carpet cleaning
Measure the carpeted area and state whether the carpet is fixed or removable, its traffic level and stained zones. Provide a layout and corridor or office photos. Request the soil-extraction and moisture method and any ventilation or temporary closure needs. Compare equipment, drying time and reopening arrangements; check edges, residual moisture and odour.

#### 11. Arabic majlis cleaning
List majlis seating areas, cushions, attached curtains, fabric and filling types, and any wood or metal decoration. Send close-up photos of stains, stitching and care labels. Separate textile cleaning from wood or hardware treatment. Compare spot and full cleaning and drying time; check colourfastness, odour, embroidery and cushion condition after reinstatement.

#### 12. Mattress cleaning
State mattress size and type, sleeping sides, stains or odours, and provide a care-label photo without exposing personal information. Ask whether the method is compatible with its layers and manufacturer instructions and how moisture and drying are controlled. Do not assume cleaning will remove every stain or its cause; accept only when dry and free of unwanted residue or odour.

#### 13. Curtain cleaning
Identify fabric and lining, curtain length, fixing method and whether removal and rehanging are required. Provide care-label, track and window photos. Confirm whether washing, dry cleaning or on-site treatment is suitable for the material. Compare removal, transport, reinstallation and pressing costs; check shrinkage, colourfastness, rings and curtain movement after rehanging.

#### 14. Upholstery disinfection
Treat this as distinct from soil removal. State furnishing type, material, treatment area and reason, and provide care-label photos. Request the product identity, application method, compatible surfaces, contact time, ventilation and return-to-use interval in line with product instructions. Avoid claims of complete sterilisation without evidence; check for colour change or residue and retain product details.

### Façade and site cleaning

#### 15. Glass-façade cleaning
Record glass area, height, available access, obstacles and pedestrian areas below the façade. Provide internal and external photos and elevation drawings. Request the access method, equipment, work-zone isolation and protection for frames and joints. Coordinate entry and working windows with property management; inspect for water marks, scratches and edge damage without assuming an access method is suitable before site assessment.

#### 16. Building-façade cleaning
Identify façade material—such as stone, panels or coating—the type and location of soiling, height and joint condition. Provide close and wide photos. Request a limited trial to confirm cleaner and pressure compatibility before full treatment. Compare access, water collection and protection of windows and entrances; check colour consistency and ensure the finish has not eroded or leaked inward.

#### 17. Parking-area cleaning
State the number of levels, area, floor type, oil-stain locations, drains and vehicle entrances. Provide a parking plan and photos of affected areas. Request a work-zone sequence, water and odour controls and a safe route for users. Compare equipment, detergents, drying time and traffic management; check drains and ensure no slippery film or debris remains.

#### 18. Industrial-site cleaning
Describe site activity, work zones, known contaminants, equipment that must remain untouched and whether an operational shutdown is needed. Provide zone plans, photos and available safety information. Request the work method, products, collection and waste route, and isolation responsibility. Do not treat unidentified substances as ordinary waste; follow the site's and competent authority's requirements before starting, then inspect against a handover checklist.

### Water-tank and swimming-pool cleaning

#### 19. Water-tank cleaning
State tank type, material, capacity, location and access method, and whether it serves one or multiple units. Provide photos of the cover, interior, access points and supply lines. Request the draining, cleaning, rinsing and refilling sequence and how water is protected from contamination during work. Compare inspection records and any owner-requested sampling; receive a service log and tank condition record before return to use.

#### 20. Water-tank disinfection
Disinfection follows assessment and cleaning. State tank capacity, material, water source and reason for treatment, and provide cleaning records or inspection photos if available. Request product identity, concentration, application, contact time, rinsing and refilling method in accordance with product instructions, and whether water testing is required. Do not assume the tank is ready for use until the specified steps and owner or specialist checks are complete.

#### 21. Swimming-pool cleaning
Provide pool dimensions, approximate depth, finish type, filter and pump condition and usage level, with photos of the water, edges and plant room. Separate basin cleaning from circulation-system maintenance and request water readings and a record of added products. Compare visit frequency, tasks and materials; the pool should not be used again until the responsible specialist confirms water and operating conditions are suitable.

### Pest and rodent control

#### 22. Cockroach control
Record sighting locations and frequency, damp areas and food sources, and whether activity is confined to one unit or several. Provide photos and a zone plan without disturbing the infestation. Request an inspection to identify the pest, entry source, treatment method and precautions for children and pets. Compare follow-up and written service terms; review activity at the agreed follow-up rather than assuming one visit guarantees elimination.

#### 23. Ant control
Photograph trails and sighting points and note proximity to food, wall gaps or planted areas and how often activity occurs. Request identification of the pest and entry source before choosing bait or local treatment, together with food, surface and child-area precautions. Compare source treatment with surface spraying alone; obtain follow-up instructions and occupant preparation steps.

#### 24. Rodent control
Map signs of rodents, droppings, gnawing and possible openings, as well as food-storage areas, without handling evidence directly. Provide photos and a layout. Request an entry-point assessment, safe trapping or control plan, proofing and follow-up. Compare visit count and trap and waste handling; check that access points are addressed and retain observations after each visit.

#### 25. Bed-bug control
Inspection should cover mattress seams, bed frames, cracks, nearby furniture and the units affected. Provide clear photos and note any recent furniture or linen movement. Request room-preparation, laundry, treatment and follow-up instructions, including re-entry guidance. Compare the number of visits and whether adjacent units are assessed; do not discard furniture before specialist assessment and an agreed handling plan.

#### 26. Periodic pest-control contracts
Define property type and area, sensitive zones, previously observed pests and required visit frequency. Request an initial survey, treatment map, product details, visit records, urgent-call process and follow-up, with exclusions stated. Compare coverage, reporting, retreatment and cancellation terms; review activity records and observations rather than judging the contract only by visit count.

### Disinfection and sanitisation

#### 27. Home spraying and disinfection
Specify home area, rooms, high-touch surfaces and the presence of children, pets or occupants needing special precautions. Provide surface photos and identify sensitive materials. Request the product label, application method, contact time, ventilation and return-to-use interval. Distinguish cleaning and soil removal from disinfection; obtain written instructions and avoid unsupported claims of complete sterilisation.

#### 28. Office and facility disinfection
State facility activity, area, occupancy hours, devices, records and surfaces that cannot tolerate direct spraying. Provide a zone plan. Request methods for devices, ventilation, evacuation areas and product contact time according to its instructions. Compare timing, records and products; do not reopen the area until the stated interval has elapsed and the site contact confirms readiness.

### Building operations and maintenance

#### 29. Maintenance management
Start with an asset register, recurring faults, locations and priorities, and identify who approves repairs and supervises contractors. Provide building plans, system lists and existing contracts if available. Request the process for logging, assigning and closing work orders and monthly reporting. Separate management fees from repairs and parts; review sample reports and agreed response times before contracting.

#### 30. Facility operations
Define included facilities, operating hours, common areas, service points and coordination responsibilities with property management. Provide zone plans, system lists and peak periods. Request team structure, operating logs, issue escalation and service-interruption procedures. Compare coverage hours, tasks, exclusions and performance measures; receive a written operating plan and reviewable periodic records.

#### 31. Preventive maintenance
Prepare an asset list with models, operating hours, fault history and manufacturer instructions, then define inspection, cleaning, lubrication and replacement tasks for each asset. Provide the register and data-plate photos. Request a calendar of visits, materials, spare parts and reports. Compare included work with items needing separate approval; review completed checklists, readings and observations after each visit.

#### 32. Electrical-system monitoring
Identify panels, circuits, affected zones, symptoms or alarms and fault history without opening panels or touching components unless qualified. Provide distribution drawings, external panel photos and equipment details. Request inspection reports, readings and safe-isolation methods. Separate monitoring from repairs and parts; receive readings, observations and recommended corrective action from a qualified technician.

#### 33. HVAC-system monitoring
Record air-conditioning units, locations, models, operating periods and complaints such as weak cooling, condensation or noise. Provide data-plate photos and unit and filter locations. Request monitoring of operating readings, cleaning, drainage and alarms in line with manufacturer instructions. Compare visit frequency, spare parts and reporting; review each unit's log rather than accepting a general statement that the system works.

#### 34. Plumbing and drainage monitoring
Identify tanks, pumps, meters and areas with recurring odours, blockages or leaks, including fault dates. Provide network drawings and access-point photos. Request supply and drainage inspections, repair testing and records of replaced parts. Compare emergency and routine visits and clarify any wall or floor opening; receive the test record, fault location and work completed.

#### 35. Building-condition reporting
Define whether the report is for operational monitoring, defect inventory or maintenance planning, and list the areas and assets to inspect. Provide plans, prior records and fault photos. Request numbered observations linked to location, image, priority and recommendation. Compare report detail and inspection limits; a visual report is not a structural-safety certificate. Receive a dated copy identifying items requiring specialist assessment.

### Staffing and operational support

#### 36. Cleaning-staff provision
Specify site area, surfaces, tasks, shifts, working hours and which party supplies equipment and materials. Provide task schedules, storage areas, washrooms and waste points. Request headcount, supervision and absence-cover arrangements. Verify the provider and workers' eligibility and site requirements with the competent authorities before engagement; review attendance and task logs and do not assume staff availability before confirmation.

#### 37. Loading-worker provision
Define load type, weights, unit counts, floors and loading and unloading times, and identify trolleys, lifting equipment or narrow routes. Provide site photos and movement paths. Request crew size, supervisor, handling tools and property-protection responsibility. Compare working and standby hours and damage terms; check quantities and the site after completion. Do not automatically treat labour supply as a transport service.

#### 38. Maintenance-technician provision
Specify trade, system, fault, equipment model and work location, and whether the task is diagnosis, repair or routine monitoring. Provide data-plate photos, error codes and prior repair history. Request technician qualifications, scope, tools, parts and test method. Verify qualifications and any task-specific authorisations before assignment; receive a report of cause, work, parts and testing.

#### 39. Site-supervisor provision
State the number of work teams, project type, shifts, areas of responsibility and required reporting level. Provide the organisation chart, schedule and inspection forms. Request the supervisor's relevant experience, authority and limits for approving changes. Compare coverage, attendance, reporting and escalation; receive daily records of observations, delays and safety without treating the supervisor as a substitute for the consultant or authorised approver.

#### 40. Residential-complex operations
Define building and unit counts, common areas, service hours, help-desk points and assets included in operations. Provide the complex plan, facility register and existing contracts. Request a plan for cleaning, maintenance, issue handling and coordination with property management and suppliers. Compare team scope, response measures, reports and exclusions; review work orders, inspections and resident feedback through a clear process.

#### 41. Commercial-facility operations
State business type and opening hours, public areas, stores, back-of-house zones and systems requiring monitoring. Provide space plans, equipment register and peak schedule. Request clear allocation across operations, cleaning, maintenance, waste and issue handling. Compare after-hours coverage, reporting and fault response; receive operating, observation, incident and action logs.

#### 42. Project-support services
Describe the project phase, support tasks, required roles or headcount, assignment period and muster point. Provide the schedule, work list, material routes and access windows. Clarify whether support includes coordination, handling or field monitoring only. Compare supervision, attendance, deliverables and tool responsibility; receive a task-linked completion record without assuming headcount or availability in advance.

## Requesting a quotation when no company is listed

Send Bait Al Reef customer service the property type and location, requested service, area and room or floor count, photos of surfaces and affected zones, occupancy, timing and frequency, water and power sources, lift and access restrictions, and any products or equipment the customer expects to provide. For pest control or disinfection, add the observed issue and required precautions; for building operations, attach an asset and task list. Ask the provider to separate labour, products, visits, transport, waste handling and exclusions. This is a way to submit a requirement, not confirmation of a supplier, price or appointment.

## Questions that help define the scope

- Is the request for soil removal or disinfection using a specified product and procedure?
- Are any surfaces or fabrics sensitive and in need of a hidden-area test?
- Does the quotation include products, equipment, labour, transport, drying and a return visit?
- Does the work require property-management coordination or temporary area closure?
- How will completed tasks, observations and products used be recorded?

## Technical and regulatory limits

This draft does not claim that a particular provider is licensed or that a product is approved. Before contracting for pest control, disinfection, waste handling or system operations, request product details, application instructions, operator qualifications and any requirements applicable to the site, and verify them with the competent authority and property management. Electrical and mechanical work and unidentified substances require assessment by a qualified specialist. Do not use claims such as “complete sterilisation”, “permanent elimination” or guaranteed pest-free conditions without verifiable written evidence.
