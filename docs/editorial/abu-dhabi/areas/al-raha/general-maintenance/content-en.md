# General Maintenance & Finishing in Al Raha

## Scope of this guide

This sector covers maintenance and finishing services that may be requested for residential or commercial property in Al Raha. Before arranging a visit, describe the property type, affected area and whether it is occupied, and attach clear photographs of the issue and its location. If the work requires contractor access, material deliveries, service-lift use or temporary interruption of a facility, coordinate with the property manager or responsible site party before confirming a date; building procedures should not be assumed to be identical.

Company and establishment services are kept separate from the individual tradespeople list. If no suitable company is displayed, a request can still be sent to Bait Al Reef customer service. Include the service, property location, condition, available photos or drawings, dimensions, existing materials or equipment, preferred timing and any access or work restrictions. A submitted request is not confirmation of a price, appointment or provider availability.

## Companies and establishments

### General maintenance

#### 1. Periodic villa maintenance
This service sets a recurring inspection plan for villa components instead of waiting for failures. Define the scope during a site walk covering doors, windows, visible finishes and any equipment the owner wants checked. Send a defect list and location photos, and ask the quotation to state included tasks, exclusions, visit frequency and spare-parts treatment. At handover, check each item against the inspection list and record anything deferred.

#### 2. Periodic building maintenance
Multi-unit or mixed-use properties need a shared maintenance plan that separates common areas from private units and clarifies reporting and access responsibilities. The site manager should provide a defect log, floor plans and permitted work windows. Compare proposals by visit frequency, response hours, reporting and contract inclusions. Accept each visit against a dated service record showing the inspection result.

#### 3. Repair of internal damage
Use this service for a defined defect in a wall, ceiling, finish or interior component after its cause and extent have been considered. Send close-up and room-context photos, explain when it appeared and whether it was repaired before; cosmetic patching will not resolve a continuing source. The quote should identify removal, substrate preparation, replacement materials, furniture protection and cleanup. Check colour, texture and repair stability at handover.

#### 4. Door and window repairs
Request this work when opening, closing, hinges, locks, seals or frame alignment are faulty. Record the door or window type, material and opening dimensions, and photograph the hinges, lock and point of friction. Do not replace a component until compatibility with the existing system is checked. Compare adjustment, repair and replacement options, then test movement, closure, locking and unintended gaps.

#### 5. Moisture treatment
Treatment should begin by identifying the moisture source, not simply painting over a stain. Possible causes include leakage, condensation, poor ventilation or water entry from an adjacent element. Send dated photos, locate the mark relative to bathrooms, kitchens or the façade, and say whether it spreads or returns after drying. The quote should explain diagnosis, repair, drying time and reinstatement, while identifying what cannot be confirmed before inspection. Do not accept repainting until the source has been addressed and the surface is suitably dry.

#### 6. Crack treatment
A hairline crack in paint is different from a crack that may indicate movement or a defect in the building element. Attach a photograph with a scale reference and wider views, and report whether the crack is changing or appears elsewhere. The proposal should define the repair limits, materials and finish reinstatement. Any concerning structural indication should be referred for specialist assessment before it is concealed. Check continuity of the repair and finish quality at handover, and record any required monitoring.

#### 7. Building preparation before handover
This service consolidates outstanding items before a property is handed over or returned. It is not a formal approval or a guarantee that another party will accept the property. Provide the snag list, available drawings and a photo for each item, and classify each as repair, cleaning, adjustment or incomplete installation. Request itemised pricing, material responsibility and retesting requirements. Conduct a joint walk-through and close each item or record what remains open.

#### 8. Minor household maintenance
This covers small tasks that may not justify separate projects, but combining them in one request helps estimate time and tools. List each task separately with its location and a photo, and identify the surface, component and any site instructions. Compare quotations by task count, minimum visit charge and included materials. Test every completed item before the technician leaves and note anything requiring a return visit.

#### 9. Curtain installation
Installation requires the opening width and height, the position of any curtain pocket or track, wall type and access conditions to be checked. Send full-window photos and details of the curtain and existing track, and state whether supply is included or installation only is needed. The quote should specify brackets, fixing method and final height. At handover, test smooth movement, alignment, fixing stability and clearance from the window opening.

#### 10. Shelf installation
The fixing method depends on expected load, shelf length and material, and the wall construction. Send a wall photo, shelf dimensions and intended contents, and identify known electrical or plumbing routes before drilling. Ask the quote to state bracket and anchor types and whether the shelf is client-supplied. Check level, fixing stability and the surrounding finish for drilling damage.

#### 11. TV mounting
Safe mounting depends on screen size and weight, bracket type, wall material and cable routes. Provide the TV model or dimensions and weight if available, a photo of the proposed location and the power/data outlets, and say whether cables should be concealed. Compare proposals by bracket, fixing and cable-routing scope. Test screen stability, viewing angle and connections before acceptance.

#### 12. Accessory installation
This may include mirrors, towel rails, hooks and bathroom or kitchen accessories. Surface type and item design determine the appropriate fixing method. Attach an item list, photos of the locations and dimensions, and state whether the client supplies the items. The quote should specify fixing points, anchors, drilling and any sealing. Check alignment, stability and the surrounding tile or finish.

#### 13. Furniture assembly
Use this service for flat-pack furniture or pieces that need reassembly after relocation. Time depends on the number of items and their complexity. Send package photos, an item list and manufacturer instructions if available, along with the room and delivery route. Clarify whether packaging removal and wall anchoring are included. Check all components, joints, doors and drawers, and confirm that no parts or fasteners remain unused without explanation.

#### 14. Minor fault repairs
This service is for limited faults that may be diagnosed and repaired during a short visit. Describe the symptom rather than writing only “general maintenance”: identify a noise, leak, failed component or intermittent issue, when it occurs and any useful photo or short video. Ask for diagnosis labour to be separated from possible parts. Do not approve an unexplained replacement. At handover, test the repair with the customer and record any replaced components.

### Integrated finishing

#### 15. Villa finishing
Villa finishing coordinates related items such as surfaces, floors, paint, doors, lighting and fixtures; it is more than a single isolated trade. Define the areas, building condition and completed work, and attach drawings, a finish schedule and site photos. Compare quotations against a bill of quantities, specifications, samples, payment stages and tests. Accept each stage before concealed work is covered.

#### 16. Apartment finishing
Start with an inventory of rooms, bathrooms, kitchen and corridors, and clarify whether the apartment is new or occupied during renovation. Provide a plan, dimensions and current-condition photos. Identify work that needs building-management coordination, such as deliveries or noisy work windows. The quote should separate materials, labour, protection, cleaning and testing. Accept each item individually and record exclusions.

#### 17. Shop fit-out
A shop fit-out depends on the business activity, customer circulation, service areas and storage. Fix the space plan before pricing the façade, lighting and floors. Attach the layout, dimensions, visual identity and equipment requirements, and distinguish permanent work from removable fixtures. Compare proposals by item scope and suitability for the intended operation. Inspect workmanship, connection safety and maintainability before the shop is equipped for use.

#### 18. Office fit-out
An office fit-out should count workstations, meeting rooms, reception and support areas while coordinating circulation, power, data and lighting. Send the furniture plan, expected user count and site photos, and request separate pricing for partitions, ceilings, flooring and services. Where relevant, compare acoustic treatment, finish quality and test procedures. Accept each room after checking sockets, lighting and doors.

#### 19. Interior-space renovation
Renovation changes the layout or appearance of an existing space while retaining elements that remain serviceable. Identify what will be removed and reused, and provide photos, dimensions and a plan showing existing furniture and services. Ask the contractor to separate strip-out, disposal, repair, supply and installation costs. If the property remains occupied, specify protection for active areas. Accept against the agreed layout, materials and scope.

#### 20. Decorative works
Decorative work turns a concept into buildable drawings, materials and details, such as wall cladding, display units or feature elements. Send reference images, wall dimensions, heights and the positions of electrical and AC services. Request a physical sample or material board before fabrication. Compare joint details, finish, edges and maintenance requirements, then inspect alignment, fixing stability and clean joints.

#### 21. Complete finishing handover
Use this service to close a finishing scope delivered by several trades or subcontractors. Prepare a room-by-room checklist covering surfaces, doors, visible electrical and plumbing work, and available material or warranty documents. Appearance alone is not enough: test functions, record defects with photos, assign responsibility and set a reinspection date. The handover record should distinguish accepted items from open items and must not be presented as government approval.

### Gypsum and decor

#### 22. Gypsum ceiling installation
Ceiling design should follow measured room dimensions, finished heights, AC outlets, lighting positions and maintenance access. Provide a reflected ceiling plan and photos of the existing slab. Ask for board, framing, joint and access-panel specifications; accept after checking level, suspension stability and clean joints.
#### 23. Gypsum partition walls
Gypsum partitions divide space or form wall linings. Specify height, thickness, door openings and any loads to be supported. Provide dimensioned plans and service locations, then compare framing, board layers, insulation and infill. Check alignment, anchoring and joint treatment before painting.
#### 24. Cove lighting
A cove detail must coordinate the recess shape, light source, wiring route and future access. Provide a section drawing showing depth, width and lighting type, and request a lighting test before closure. Accept for even illumination, accessible maintenance and concealed wiring without visible hotspots.
#### 25. Decorative gypsum work
Decorative profiles need shop drawings showing dimensions, repetition and how corners meet doors and ceilings. Attach a reference image and wall or ceiling measurements, and request a sample or profile mock-up before production. Check pattern consistency, straight edges, secure fixing and smooth finishing.
#### 26. Wood-look panel installation
Panel selection depends on location, moisture exposure, cleaning and fixing method; a similar image colour is not enough. Provide wall area, obstructions and socket positions. Ask for panel type, thickness, surface layer and edge treatment, then inspect joint alignment, grain continuity and fixing stability.
#### 27. Marble-look panel installation
Review panel size, thickness, surface, moisture suitability and joint method, especially around sinks and corners. Send area dimensions and opening photos, and specify vein direction and joint count. Compare adhesive, edge and sealing details; inspect flatness, pattern matching and clean joints.
#### 28. Gypsum repair and maintenance
First establish whether damage came from impact, moisture, framing movement or failed fixing. Send close and wide photos and report any leak or vibration. The quote should separate removal, support reinforcement and reinstatement. Accept after checking stability, texture match and paint finish.
### Flooring and cladding

#### 29. Ceramic tile installation
Measure the area and inspect the substrate, door levels and drain positions before pricing, including cutting waste. Provide tile size and type with floor photos. Ask for adhesive, joint width and laying pattern; accept after checking level, falls, alignment and hollow-sounding tiles.
#### 30. Porcelain tile installation
Porcelain work requires the tile size and thickness, substrate flatness, cutting method and joint specification, particularly for large formats. Attach a layout plan and batch number if the tiles are already purchased. Compare installation tools, setting materials and joints; inspect lippage, cut edges and consistent joint widths.
#### 31. Parquet installation
Check substrate moisture and flatness and plan board direction and door transitions before installation. Provide parquet type, thickness and floor photos. Ask for underlay or moisture barrier details and perimeter expansion gaps; accept after checking joints, edges and any unusual movement or noise.
#### 32. Vinyl flooring installation
The finish depends on substrate condition, vinyl type and whether it is glued or click-fit; surface irregularities must be addressed first. Provide room area, product specification and base photos. Compare wear layer, thickness and edge details; inspect adhesion, seams and any bubbles.
#### 33. Interlock paving
Interlock requires defined area, intended use, vehicle loading, drainage and preparation of the base layers. Attach site dimensions, level photos and drainage points. Ask for paver thickness, base build-up and edge restraints; accept after checking level, pattern, edge stability and absence of standing water.
#### 34. Wall cladding installation
Select cladding by weight, wall construction, moisture exposure, fixing system and corner details. Send wall height and width and photos of switches and openings. Request a layout, sample and edge details; inspect straight lines, secure panels, pattern consistency and access to concealed services.
#### 35. Floor repairs
Identify whether the issue is a broken tile, debonding, settlement or failure in the bedding layer; replacing the surface alone may not solve it. Send photos, affected area and level difference, and say whether matching spare tiles are available. Compare local repair with wider replacement, then test stability, falls and colour match.
### Electrical work and lighting

#### 36. Building electrical first fix
First-fix work must follow drawings, load calculations, room use and equipment schedules; point counts should not be guessed from floor area alone. Provide plans, appliance list and panel locations. Ask for circuit, cable, protection and inspection details. Inspect concealed work before walls are closed and retain test records.
#### 37. Wire and cable routing
Cable work requires the route, length, conductor size, load, protection method and surrounding conditions to be defined. Attach route drawings, distances and load information. Specify cable type, conduit or tray and connection points. Check identification, insulation, continuity and secure support against the project specification before energising.
#### 38. Distribution board installation
Review the board against circuit count, loads, available space, protection and labelling needs. Send a photo of the existing board and any load schedule, and clarify whether this is replacement or an addition. Compare board capacity, components and circuit arrangement. Acceptance should include labelling, enclosure checks and testing by a qualified technician.
#### 39. Socket and switch installation
Socket and switch positions should reflect furniture, use and appliances, while remaining compatible with existing wiring. Provide a plan or photos with heights and furniture positions, and state faceplate type and finish. Check box security, faceplate alignment, operation of each point and safe connections.
#### 40. Electrical supply to extensions
An extension requires a schedule of new loads, feeder route, lighting and socket points, and the connection to the existing system. Attach the extension plan, appliance positions and distance to the panel. Ask for circuit capacity, protection and route details. Test and label circuits before closing containment.
#### 41. Electrical network rehabilitation
This is appropriate for ageing wiring, repeated faults or added loads. Begin with a survey of existing circuits rather than indiscriminate replacement. Provide available drawings, fault history and panel photos. Request a condition report and phased scope; compare replacement strategy, testing and isolation arrangements. Accept updated drawings and test results.
#### 42. Electrical load inspection
Inspection is useful when breakers trip, connections become hot or new high-demand appliances are added. Describe symptoms, equipment and when they occur, and provide panel data if available. Request documented measurements showing circuits, readings and recommendations. Do not replace a breaker or cable based on guesswork; obtain a report explaining findings and proposed action.
#### 43. Indoor lighting installation
Define fitting type, ceiling height, supply points and control method. Provide a lighting layout, ceiling photos and fixture models. Ask about cut-outs, mounting and drivers. Test every fitting and switch, colour consistency and secure mounting before accepting the room.
#### 44. Outdoor lighting installation
Select fittings for their location, water and dust exposure, fixing method and cable route. Attach façade or pathway photos, dimensions and supply locations. Ask for the fitting's protection specification and sealing method. Check mounting, sealed entries and night-time performance without unintended glare.
#### 45. Chandelier installation
Confirm chandelier weight, support capacity, hanging height and clearance below it. Provide the model, weight, dimensions and a photo of the ceiling support, and identify any assembly or lifting requirement. Accept after checking the suspension base, electrical connection, balance and operation of all lamps.
#### 46. Concealed lighting
Concealed lighting needs coordination of the recess, LED position, power supply, ventilation and future component replacement. Provide a ceiling section, route lengths and colour and power specification. Request a light sample before closure. Check continuous output, service access and absence of harsh dots or shadows.
#### 47. Garden lighting
Garden lighting should consider pedestrian routes, planting, power supply and protection of cables from future gardening work. Provide a garden plan, path photos and tree locations. Request a fitting layout and protection details. Test after dark and check stable bases and unobstructed movement.
#### 48. Motion sensor installation
Sensor performance depends on detection range, mounting height, coverage angle and the load being switched. Provide a plan and photos of circulation areas and lighting positions. Ask for sensitivity and timer settings. Test movement from several directions and check that irrelevant movement does not trigger repeated switching.
#### 49. Lighting unit maintenance
Diagnosis should distinguish a failed lamp, driver, connection or control component, with supply isolated before inspection. Provide fitting type, label photo, symptoms and number of affected units. Compare repair with compatible replacement. After restoration, check secure mounting, operation and absence of flicker.
### Plumbing and leak detection

#### 50. Water-supply pipework first fix
Begin with a supply-point plan, pipe sizes, valve positions and fixture schedule, and review routes before walls are closed. Provide bathroom and kitchen plans and the supply source. Specify pipe and fitting materials and pressure testing; record test results and valve locations before concealment.
#### 51. Drainage pipework first fix
Drainage requires fixture locations, pipe diameters, falls, cleanouts, venting and connection routes to be defined. Attach plans, finished floor levels and drain positions. Ask for materials and test methods. Check flow, absence of backflow and access to maintenance points before closing the routes.
#### 52. Sanitary fixture installation
Toilets, basins, mixers and showers differ by model and by supply and drainage positions. Provide product references, bathroom dimensions and outlet photos, and state whether removal and supply are included. Accept after checking fixing, sealing, drainage, leak-free operation and mixer controls.
#### 53. Water tank installation
Define tank capacity, type, location, base loading, delivery route, connections and ventilation. Provide site dimensions, water source and installation-point photos. Specify accessories, float valve and isolation valves. Check base stability, connections, filling, discharge and absence of leakage.
#### 54. Water pump installation
Select a pump by flow, pressure, head and actual use rather than nominal power alone. Provide network data, number of outlets and a photo of the pump location. Request the performance curve, accessories and protection. Test pressure, flow, vibration, automatic operation and shut-off.
#### 55. Pipework rehabilitation
Start by identifying the ageing or damaged section, pipe routes and existing material. Provide available drawings, leak photos and repair history. Request a phased replacement plan that limits finish removal. Compare materials, joints and testing, then accept an updated route drawing and network test results.
#### 56. Drainage network maintenance
Define the work by the blockage location, slow drainage or odour, distinguishing a local obstruction from a main-line issue. List affected fixtures, timing and access points. Ask for the clearing method and whether camera inspection is needed. Test drainage after work and confirm covers are restored and the area cleaned.
#### 57. Water leak detection
Detection aims to narrow the likely source before extensive demolition. Provide meter readings if available, stain photos, plumbing plans and when the symptom appears. Ask which detection method is proposed, its limitations and whether opening-up is required. Accept a report identifying the suspected location and next test without claiming certainty beyond the evidence.
#### 58. Pipe leak repair
Repair depends on pipe material, location, operating pressure and access. Provide site photos and any earlier detection report, and say whether the pipe is exposed or concealed in a wall or floor. Compare joint repair with section replacement and require a post-repair test before reinstatement. Check dryness, pressure stability and no recurrence.
#### 59. Tank leak detection
Identify tank type, material, water level and damp location, while checking connected fittings and pipes as possible sources. Send tank, base and connection photos and any record of level loss. Request a test suitable for the material. The report should distinguish the tank body from its accessories and state inspection limits.
#### 60. Bathroom waterproofing
Define floor area, wall upstands, drains and penetrations before tiling. Provide bathroom drawings and substrate photos. Specify the system, preparation, curing period and water-test method. Do not cover the membrane until the test is documented and any failed point repaired.
#### 61. Roof waterproofing
Choose the system after checking falls, drains, joints, penetrations and the existing layer. Provide roof area, detail photos and standing-water locations. Ask for layer build-up, protection and edge termination. Inspect drains and junctions and test the membrane before reinstating protection layers.
#### 62. Water-tank waterproofing
Confirm tank material and use, the condition of internal or external surfaces and compatibility of the proposed product. Provide internal and external photos and tank dimensions. Request cleaning, preparation, application and return-to-service steps. Accept after checking coverage, details and leakage testing appropriate to the specified system.
#### 63. Moisture remediation after leakage
This follows repair of the water source and focuses on drying affected layers, removing damaged material and reinstating finishes. Provide before-and-after photos and any leak-test result. Ask for a clear dryness criterion before closure. Check that staining does not return and the substrate is sound; active moisture must not be hidden with paint.
### Air conditioning and refrigeration

#### 64. Split AC installation
Select the unit against room area, heat load, indoor and outdoor locations, condensate route and electrical supply. Provide room dimensions and height with wall and outdoor-unit photos. Specify capacity, pipe length, insulation and mounting base. Test cooling, condensate drainage and vibration after commissioning.
#### 65. Central AC installation
A central system requires review of loads, zones, equipment locations, ductwork, controls and drainage. Attach plans, area schedules and existing-system details if available. Compare capacity, efficiency and supply, installation and testing scope. Accept with a commissioning report and airflow and temperature readings for each zone.
#### 66. Concealed AC installation
Coordinate the concealed unit with ceiling height, access panels, air routes, drainage and power. Provide ceiling drawings, available void dimensions and route lengths. Specify supply and return locations and acoustic treatment. Check maintenance access, air balance and condensate leakage.
#### 67. AC refrigerant pipe installation
Define pipe diameters, route length, elevation differences, insulation type and joint locations. Attach the proposed route and photos of penetrations. Request leak testing and evacuation before charging. Inspect supports, continuous insulation and route protection before concealment.
#### 68. Air-duct installation
Ductwork needs a plan showing sections, lengths, bends, supply and return outlets and ceiling clearances. Provide drawings, dimensions and equipment data. Specify material gauge, insulation, suspension and joint sealing. Inspect duct cleanliness, support stability, air leakage and distribution during operation.
#### 69. AC unit replacement
Before replacement, compare the old unit's condition and failure cause with system capacity and available connections; the old capacity should not automatically be assumed correct. Send the data plate and location photos. Clarify removal, disposal, connection changes and warranty. Accept after operation, cooling and drainage tests.
#### 70. System commissioning and inspection
Request commissioning after installation, shutdown or a performance complaint. Prepare system drawings, fault history and control-panel photos. Ask for measurements such as temperatures, pressures, airflow and alarms as relevant to the system. Accept a report showing readings, adjustments and defects requiring separate repair.
#### 71. AC unit cleaning
Cleaning scope depends on unit type, filter and coil condition and condensate drainage; one chemical method is not suitable for every component. Send unit photos, quantities and access details. Ask which parts will be removed and how the room is protected. Check clean and dry components and clear drainage before restart.
#### 72. Refrigerant charging
Do not request charging for weak cooling before checking why the charge is low and whether a leak exists. Provide unit type, refrigerant data, available readings and last service date. Request leak detection and repair, then charging by the manufacturer's specified quantity. Record refrigerant type, amount and operating readings.
#### 73. AC fault repair
Start by identifying the symptom: no operation, poor cooling, noise, water leakage or repeated tripping. Provide model, error code and a short noise recording if useful. Compare diagnostic labour, parts and warranty, and ask why replacement is proposed. Test the unit under operating conditions and record the repair.
#### 74. Air-duct cleaning
Define the duct sections, units, outlets and any internal lining or filters. Provide AC drawings, outlet count and grille photos. Ask how access is created, dust is contained and the area protected. Request before-and-after images and a record of cleaned sections, then check that all openings are resealed.
#### 75. Central AC unit maintenance
Maintenance covers equipment, electrical and mechanical components, drainage and filters as appropriate to the system. Provide model, service history and symptoms. Request a checklist of inspections, consumables and recommendations. Compare included and separately charged work, then accept a condition report and operating readings.
#### 76. Spare-parts replacement
Confirm part number, equipment model, failure cause and electrical or mechanical compatibility before purchase. Provide the data plate and a photo of the old part if possible. State whether the replacement is original or equivalent and its warranty. Check part number and function, test the unit and agree whether the removed part is returned.
#### 77. Periodic maintenance contracts
The contract should define visit frequency, covered equipment, cleaning and inspection tasks, emergency response and excluded parts. Provide an asset list with models, locations and fault history. Compare response times, reports, parts allowances and extra-visit charges. Receive a maintenance calendar and a record for every visit.
### Smart systems and surveillance

#### 78. Smart lighting control
Start with a schedule of circuits, switches, lamp types and desired control method, and confirm compatibility with existing wiring. Provide lighting plans and panel and switch photos. Specify controllers, connectivity and network dependency. Test every circuit and manual control when connectivity is unavailable.
#### 79. Smart AC control
Identify AC models, communication interfaces, operating zones and required sensors. Provide equipment models and a zone plan. Clarify which functions are genuinely supported and whether extra interfaces are needed. Accept after testing each zone, temperature readings and local control continuity.
#### 80. Smart lock installation
Check the lock against door type, thickness, handing, cut-out and power source. Send photos of both sides of the door and the proposed lock model. Confirm compatibility and backup-key arrangements. Test the agreed access methods, alerts and manual door operation where required.
#### 81. Smart curtain installation
Measure track width, curtain weight, motor location, power and maintenance clearance. Provide window, track and curtain photos and dimensions. Specify load limit and manual override. Test full travel, end stops and control pairing without rubbing or obstruction.
#### 82. Connecting devices to a phone app
Inventory devices, protocols, network and accounts, and define who will administer the system. Provide model list and controller photos; do not send passwords in a general request. Compare integration scope, support and updates. Test every device and assign clear account permissions.
#### 83. Smart-scene programming
Scenes should be based on clear triggers and conditions, such as time, sensor state or user departure. Describe the desired action and what must not happen, and list affected devices. Request documented logic and exceptions, then test normal operation, network failure and manual cancellation.
#### 84. Smart-home system maintenance
Begin by checking controllers, connectivity, updates and devices that have lost pairing. Provide system diagrams, error messages and affected-device list, withholding credentials. Request a backup or settings record before changes. Accept after function tests, restored access and a change log.
#### 85. CCTV camera installation
Define the purpose, coverage zones, blind spots, power, network route and recording location. Provide site plans and entrance and corridor photos. Specify viewing angles, lens, storage and protection. Test day and night images, recording and retrieval, and verify applicable privacy and site requirements with the responsible authority.
#### 86. Recording-device installation
Choose the recorder based on camera count, resolution, recording rate and required retention period. Provide camera specifications, network plan and equipment location. Request storage, port and backup calculations. Test simultaneous recording, search, export and recovery after power interruption.
#### 87. Alarm-system installation
Design begins with the risks to be detected, sensor types and zones, notification paths and backup power. Provide site plans and required system type. Specify coverage limits, testing and maintenance. Test every sensor and alert path and retain fault logs; do not claim formal approval without documentary evidence.
#### 88. Access-control system installation
Define doors, readers, user permissions, exit and emergency arrangements. Provide door plans, user count and card or credential type. Specify lock integration and power. Test authorised access, denied access, event logs and safe release arrangements.
#### 89. Intercom installation
Select the system by unit count, wiring type, external panel location and audio or video requirements. Provide entrance layout, screen count and supply-point photos. Confirm device compatibility. Test calls to every unit, audio and video clarity and door release if included.
#### 90. CCTV maintenance
Maintenance checks lenses, mounts, power, network, recording and time settings, not only the outer glass. Provide a camera list, identifiers, locations and fault details. Request a report identifying failed cameras and causes. Test live view, recording and playback after service.
#### 91. Connecting surveillance to a phone
This requires recorder model, app, network and account and notification settings. Provide the device model, phone type and error message without sharing passwords. Ask for secure access and user-management instructions. Test live view, alerts and playback outside the local network as supported by the system.
### Painting

#### 92. Villa painting
Measure rooms, ceilings and internal façade areas, and assess filler and existing coats; separate repairs from surfaces ready for paint. Provide room areas and daylight photos. Specify paint type, coat count, preparation and floor protection. Check colour consistency, coverage and edges after drying.
#### 93. Apartment painting
Define included rooms and whether the apartment is furnished or vacant, and inspect wall condition after any repairs. Send photos of each room and specify colour and sheen. State crack and stain treatment and coat count. Compare area, preparation, materials and cleanup, then accept each room after drying.
#### 94. External building painting
Inspect façade condition, existing coating, cracks, moisture sources and safe access. Provide photographs from several angles, building heights and approximate areas. Specify coating system, surface preparation, access and protection. Check coverage, colour consistency and details around openings after curing.
#### 95. Door and wood painting
The method depends on wood type, existing finish, indoor or outdoor exposure and the condition of hinges and edges. Send close-up photos and wood details if known, and say whether door removal is included. Compare sanding, filler, primer and finish coats; inspect smoothness, edges and door movement.
#### 96. Decorative paint finishes
Define the technique, texture, sample and lighting that will reveal the effect. Send a reference image, wall area and light direction. Request a sample on a comparable surface before full application. Compare coat count, application method and local repairability, then inspect pattern consistency and edge transitions.
#### 97. Removal and treatment of old paint
Clarify whether only peeling areas need removal or whether accumulated coating and damaged substrate must be treated, and identify why adhesion failed. Send photos and moisture or repair history. Specify containment, waste collection and substrate preparation. New paint should not begin until the surface is sound and dry.
#### 98. Paint maintenance and repainting
Use this for local repairs or repainting after staining, damage or colour mismatch. Define the area and the boundary between old and new paint, and provide the colour code if available. Compare spot repair with repainting the full wall. Check colour under normal lighting and clean edges.
### Waterproofing and thermal insulation

#### 99. Roof waterproofing
Select the system after inspecting roof area, falls, outlets, joints, penetrations and existing layers. Provide plans and photos of ponding and drain details. Specify layer build-up, protection and system warranty. Accept after an appropriate test and documentation of edges and drains before covering.
#### 100. Bathroom waterproofing
Define floor area, wall upstands, pipe penetrations, drains and thresholds. Provide bathroom plans and substrate photos. Specify preparation, thickness, curing time and ponding-test method. Do not install tiles until the test result is recorded and any leakage is corrected.
#### 101. Tank waterproofing
Identify tank material, intended use, surface to be treated and compatibility of the waterproofing product. Provide capacity, dimensions and internal and external photos. Request cleaning, preparation, application and return-to-service steps. Check continuous coverage, joints and leakage-test result before filling.
#### 102. Building thermal insulation
The solution depends on the element, location, thickness, thermal bridges and possible moisture. Provide wall or roof sections, detail photos and available construction data. Specify material, performance values and gap prevention. Inspect continuity and junction treatment without blocking required ventilation openings.
#### 103. External wall waterproofing
Review façade condition, cracks, joints and window junctions before selecting a system. Provide close and wide façade photos and opening details. Specify compatibility with existing finishes, fixing and protection. Check joint treatment, system continuity and that drains or openings remain unobstructed.
#### 104. Rainwater ingress treatment
First trace the water path; entry may occur at the roof, joints, window perimeters or service penetrations. Provide photos during and after rain if possible, and locate the internal stain relative to the exterior. Request source inspection before sealing the visible symptom. Accept after testing the repair and monitoring for recurrence.
#### 105. Waterproofing maintenance
Identify the waterproofing type, age, damaged location and whether a protection layer covers it. Provide photos, drawings or previous installation records, and mark peeling, separation or ponding. Compare local repair with system replacement. Inspect adhesion, edges, drains and test results before closing the work.
## Requesting a quotation when no company is listed

Bait Al Reef customer service can receive a service request even when no suitable establishment appears in the directory. Provide a name and contact method, property type and location, service name, problem or desired outcome, photos and dimensions, existing materials or equipment, expected scope, access restrictions and working hours, and whether the request is for inspection, supply, execution or a combination. This information is not a binding quotation; it helps clarify the request and determine the next step without inventing a company, price or appointment.

## Questions that improve a service request

- Is the defect visible, or does it require investigation inside a wall or ceiling?
- Is the property occupied, and are there access windows or building procedures?
- Are materials and equipment already available, or does the request include supply?
- What practical test will be performed before the work is considered complete?
- What is excluded from the price, and how will additional work be priced?
