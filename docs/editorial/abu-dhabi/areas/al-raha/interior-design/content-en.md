# Engineering Consultancy, Design and Décor in Al Raha

## A practical guide to design and consultancy services

Designing residential spaces in Al Raha requires verified site measurements and coordination of furniture, lighting, sockets and services before selections are fixed. Commercial projects or alterations affecting structure or use need a distinct brief, clear responsibility boundaries and confirmation of competent-authority requirements. This guide explains the companies and establishments services in the source taxonomy; it does not establish that an office exists, is approved, or has secured an approval, price or appointment.

## Company and establishment services by specialty

### Architectural design and drawings

#### 1. Villa design

Start with the household brief, expected occupants, circulation and privacy needs, then verify plot dimensions, setbacks and available levels. Request layout options before fixing the façade, with net areas and service locations shown. Compare options for buildability and operating cost, and receive reviewable drawings, a decision log and material information.

#### 2. Residential building design

Define unit count and types, common areas, parking and operational and maintenance needs. The design should explain vertical and horizontal circulation and the relationship between units, services, lighting and ventilation. Request area schedules and floor plans, and review repeatable details and common-area quality before approving the package.

#### 3. Commercial building design

Provide the intended business use, expected operating hours, user numbers and storage and service areas. The design should coordinate customer, staff and delivery circulation with service access, while system capacities remain subject to engineering review. Request a plan distinguishing public and back-of-house spaces, and assess flexibility before fixing the layout.

#### 4. Architectural drawing preparation

Provide the project brief, site survey and previous drawings if available. Define the required package—plans, elevations, sections, schedules and details—with clear revision numbering. Compare proposals by detail level, review rounds and editable source files, then receive a coordinated set with consistent dimensions and levels.

#### 5. Façade design development

Identify the existing façade and intended changes, with straight-on photographs, opening dimensions, heights and current materials. Request alternative concepts showing shading, joints, lighting and material interfaces, not only a decorative rendering. Review cleaning, maintenance and fixing details, and receive a material schedule and colour samples before approval.

#### 6. 3D modelling

Specify whether the model is an early massing study, detailed interior design or a material and lighting presentation. Supply drawings, dimensions, heights and reference images, and agree viewing angles and revision rounds. Do not treat a model as a substitute for construction drawings; receive it with the material and dimension schedule it represents.

#### 7. Revision of existing drawings

Provide the latest approved drawing set and identify the requested change, its reason and elements that must remain unaffected. Request a register of revised, superseded and dependent drawings, including service and detail impacts. Compare fees by sheet count and revision scope, and receive a dated, clearly versioned digital set.

#### 8. Permit drawing preparation

This service prepares drawings and technical information for submission; it does not guarantee approval. State the project and proposed change and provide available documents. Request a deliverables list and identify who will review deficiencies and responses. Confirm the competent authority’s requirements before contracting, and retain submission receipts, review comments and final revisions.

#### 9. Building application submission

Identify the application type, owner or authorised representative, available documents and current stage. The quotation should say whether it includes file preparation, checking, submission and follow-up on comments, or only part of that process. Do not share personal credentials without a documented need, and do not treat submission as approval. Receive the reference number, submission proof and correspondence log.

#### 10. Amendment of approved drawings

Attach the approved drawings and mark the proposed change, its location and reason. Request a clear comparison between the approved and proposed conditions, identifying affected disciplines and whether resubmission may be required. Do not build from an unapproved revision; receive the revision register and evidence of the file’s status with the relevant authority.

#### 11. Obtaining maintenance permits

Describe the maintenance work and its limits, including whether it affects structural elements, façades or shared services. Ask the office to identify the documents it will prepare and the authority with which it will verify the applicable procedure; do not assume every repair requires the same permit. Completion should be evidenced by the submitted application and official response, not a verbal promise.

#### 12. Obtaining extension permits

Specify the extension area, location and connection to the existing building, and provide drawings, survey and photographs. Request an initial review of planning, structural and service requirements, identifying responsibility for drawings, submission and comment follow-up. Do not start extension work until required approvals have been confirmed and documented.

#### 13. As-built condition drawings

These drawings are needed when older plans no longer reflect the actual property. Agree survey boundaries, detail level and measurement of openings, levels and visible services, identifying what cannot be measured without opening-up. Receive editable files with the survey date and measurement notes, clearly distinguishing observed, assumed and unavailable information.

#### 14. Coordination of government approvals

A project may involve reviews by more than one authority depending on the property and work. Request a matrix listing the authority, document, responsible party and preceding or subsequent approval, with application references and comments. Do not assume the consultant can issue an approval; its role is to prepare and follow the file under a clear authorisation. Handover should include official evidence for each application.

### Structural design

#### 15. Concrete structural design

Design requires load and use information, spans, materials, soil data and architectural constraints. Ask the responsible engineer to identify the applicable design basis after confirming its relevance, together with calculation and drawing deliverables. Compare team capability and independent checking scope; never derive member sizes or reinforcement details from a visual concept.

#### 16. Foundation design

Provide the geotechnical report, site survey, load information and levels. The designer should state assumptions about soil, groundwater, settlement and the proposed foundation type, and identify missing data. Request calculations and detailed drawings coordinated with underground services, then review them with the responsible engineer before construction.

#### 17. Structural drawing preparation

State the project stage and elements requiring drawings, and provide architectural plans, soil information and available loads. Define whether the package includes foundation, column, beam and slab plans, details and schedules, with coordinated grids and levels. Drawings must agree with calculations and avoid conflicts with service openings or architectural design.

#### 18. Structural load review

State why the review is needed—such as new equipment, a change of use or altered partitions—and provide member details and existing and proposed loads. Request a report identifying load sources, assumptions, inspection limits and findings for each element. Do not add loads or change use based on verbal estimates; obtain a documented decision from a qualified engineer.

#### 19. Building safety assessment

Provide available building age and maintenance records, defect photographs and previous alterations. Agree inspection scope and whether non-destructive tests or sampling are required and who performs them. The report should distinguish observations, confirmed findings and recommendations, state limitations and identify urgent items requiring direct assessment.

#### 20. Structural strengthening design

Identify the affected element, reason for strengthening and constraints on vacating the site or removing finishes. The designer needs condition and load information and the construction sequence; a site inspection may be necessary. Request material and connection details, temporary support, installation stages and inspection points. Strengthening should not start before the design and method are approved by the responsible professional.

#### 21. Structural report preparation

Define the report purpose, intended recipient and decision it must support. Provide drawings, photographs, test results and previous records, and request a statement of inspection, tests, limitations and assumptions. Findings should be linked to evidence and recommendations prioritised, rather than relying on generic statements about building safety.

### Supervision and engineering consultancy

#### 22. Construction supervision

Define disciplines, stages, expected visits and the supervisor’s authority to issue observations or stop work. Request an inspection plan with hold points before concealed work is closed and a daily or weekly reporting format. Supervision should remain distinct from the contractor’s execution responsibility, with observations and close-out documented.

#### 23. Project management

Project management needs a defined scope, baseline budget, programme and responsibility matrix for owner, designer, contractor and suppliers. Request procedures for changes, risks, payments, procurement and meetings, with verifiable progress measures. Compare proposals by delegated authority, reporting and allocated time; receive decision, risk and change logs at close-out.

#### 24. Work quality review

Identify the work elements to be reviewed and provide project specifications, approved drawings and material samples. Request activity-specific checklists and a method for recording defects and reinspection after correction. Reports should do more than show general photographs: each observation should identify its location, requirement and close-out status.

#### 25. Project stage handover

Identify the stage to be handed over, its boundaries and evidence of completion. Request a checklist covering drawings, tests, materials, open defects and work that will become concealed after the stage. The handover record should list reservations, the party responsible for closing them and the agreed date; stage acceptance is not acceptance of the entire project.

#### 26. Progress reporting

Agree reporting frequency, baseline programme and measurable site progress. The report should distinguish completed work from delivered materials and delayed activities, and state variance causes, corrective action and ownership. Request dated photographs, programme comparisons and a decision log for matters affecting time or cost.

#### 27. Quantity take-off

Identify drawing revisions, measurement rules and how waste and allowances are treated. Request a schedule linking each quantity to drawing reference, location and unit, distinguishing measured from provisional items. Check a sample independently and receive an editable file with assumptions and exclusions.

#### 28. Contractor quotation review

Give the reviewer a common scope, contractor bids, bills of quantities and specifications. Request a comparison matrix exposing differences in materials, quantities, exclusions, programme, warranty and payment terms—not a price-only ranking. Identify unresolved queries and alternatives needing approval, then receive a recommendation with its limitations stated.

### Interior design and materials

#### 29. Majlis design

Define seating capacity, hosting style, entry routes, doors and windows, and electrical and lighting points. Provide verified measurements and photographs, and request seating layouts, circulation clearances, seat heights and material options. Compare comfort, cleanability and furniture fit, then receive a dimensioned plan and clear item schedule.

#### 30. Living room design

Start with seating, television, windows, light sources and circulation routes. Request a furniture plan showing viewing distances, clearances and item dimensions, with alternatives for lighting and socket placement. 3D visuals should match verified site dimensions and include a list of items requiring supply or installation.

#### 31. Bedroom design

State the occupants, storage needs and positions of the bed, wardrobes, doors and electrical points. Provide room dimensions, ceiling height and AC locations, and request a layout that allows drawers and doors to open without conflict. Compare materials for durability, cleaning and ventilation, and receive furniture and wardrobe dimensions before fabrication.

#### 32. Kitchen design

Survey walls, openings and water, drainage, gas or electrical and extractor points. Request a layout showing work zones, counter and cabinet heights and appliance positions, with service drawings before fabrication. Compare door, worktop and hinge materials and maintenance needs, and receive shop drawings checked against final site measurements.

#### 33. Bathroom design

Provide bathroom dimensions, drainage and supply points, ventilation, door positions and floor levels. Request fixture layouts, door swings, shower zones, drainage falls and waterproofing details. Tile selection should be checked against room dimensions and cuts, and coordinated service drawings should be issued before construction.

#### 34. Furniture layout

Provide a dimensioned room plan showing openings, columns and sockets, together with a list of existing or proposed furniture. Request alternative layouts showing circulation, use clearances and storage. Compare movement, space efficiency and flexibility, and receive a plan with each item’s dimensions and surrounding clearances.

#### 35. 3D visualisation

Specify rooms, views and the required realism of materials and lighting, and provide approved plans and material samples or references. Agree the number of views, revision rounds and whether source files are included. Use the visualisation to review proportion and colour, then match final selections to a material schedule and construction drawings before procurement or fabrication.

#### 36. Office design

Define staff numbers, work patterns, meeting rooms, reception, support spaces and visitor routes. Provide site dimensions and power, data and AC locations, and request a layout balancing privacy, circulation and maintenance. Compare storage, comfort and reconfiguration options, and receive furniture plans, service points and a finishes schedule.

#### 37. Retail shop design

Start with the storefront, customer entry route, point of sale, storage and delivery access. Define product types, display method and operating and security needs, and request a layout showing shelving, counters, lighting and signage. Compare display flexibility and replenishment access, and receive dimensioned display-unit and construction drawings before fabrication.

#### 38. Restaurant and café design

Define service style, seating count, kitchen, preparation, washing and storage areas, and staff and guest routes. Request coordination of furniture, lighting, ventilation and service points, while confirming activity-specific requirements with competent authorities before fixing the plan. Compare operational efficiency and cleanability, and receive equipment and finishes schedules.

#### 39. Salon design

Define workstations, services offered, waiting, washing, storage and privacy zones. Provide site dimensions and water, power and ventilation locations, and request a layout that supports staff movement and surface cleaning. Review finishes against products used in the business, and receive workstation plans, service points and furniture schedules.

#### 40. Clinic design

Define the medical specialty, patient reception and routes, waiting, examination and storage areas. Ask the designer to identify matters requiring coordination with competent authorities for licensing and medical fit-out; do not assume the design is automatically approved. Compare privacy, cleanability and circulation, and receive coordinated material, equipment and drawing schedules.

#### 41. Exhibition design

Define exhibit types and sizes, visitor sequence, display, storage and lighting needs. Provide the space plan and site photographs, and request a layout considering sightlines, circulation, installation and maintenance zones. Compare flexibility for changing exhibits and fixing safety, and receive display-unit, lighting and material drawings before fabrication.

#### 42. Reception area design

Define expected visitor numbers and the reception, waiting, enquiry and entry functions. Provide dimensions, doors, sockets and data points, and request a layout balancing clear access, privacy and staff routes. Compare counter and waiting-seat materials for durability and cleaning, and receive fabrication drawings and service locations.

#### 43. Flooring selection

Define use, foot traffic, moisture exposure, acceptable slip characteristics and the existing floor condition. Request representative samples and data for thickness, wear, installation and cleaning, including skirtings and transitions. Compare total cost including preparation and waste, and receive a schedule identifying product, colour and quantity for each area.

#### 44. Paint colour selection

Provide room photographs under different lighting and identify window orientation, artificial lighting and fixed furniture. Request physical wall samples rather than relying on a screen, and specify sheen and cleanability. Compare colours in actual conditions and receive colour codes, paint system and required surface preparation.

#### 45. Marble and ceramic selection

Define application, dimensions, thickness, finish and wet or heat-exposed areas, then request samples from the same batch where possible. Compare shade variation, absorption, slip characteristics and care requirements, and request a layout showing cuts and skirtings. Record batch numbers and an agreed spare quantity for future replacement.

#### 46. Furniture selection

Begin with room dimensions, delivery routes, doors and sockets, then define use, occupants and durability needs. Request dimensions and information on frame, padding, fabric, cleaning and warranty, with a colour or material sample. Compare comfort, expected service life and maintenance, not photographs alone.

#### 47. Lighting selection

Define each space’s function, required lighting level, ceiling height and switching or control points. Request power, colour temperature, beam angle, dimming and electrical compatibility data, with a layout showing fixture positions. Compare energy use, maintenance and glare, and receive a luminaire schedule before purchase.

#### 48. Material board preparation

Define the rooms and elements covered and the level of material approval required. Request physical samples for flooring, paint, timber, metals and fabrics, with product, supplier, colour and finish references. The board should show how materials work together under expected lighting and record acceptable alternatives and items needing renewed approval.

#### 49. Finishes schedule preparation

Assign each room or area a clear finish code for floors, walls, ceilings, doors and accessories. Specify product, colour, size, installation method, quantity and approval source, distinguishing provisional from final selections. Check the schedule against drawings and specifications, and receive a version-controlled file that contractors and suppliers can price without guesswork.

## Craftspeople and individual trades — separate from companies and establishments

- **Architectural drafting and modelling:** drawing preparation; drawing revisions; 3D modelling; building information modelling.
- **Interior design:** majlis design; space planning; material and colour selection; 3D visualisation.
- **Quantity surveying and measurement:** site measurement; bill-of-quantities preparation; quantity review; pricing data preparation.

This list must not be merged automatically with the company directory. Preparing a drawing or application does not mean it has been approved; the consultant and client must verify the applicable requirements and authority for each project.

## Quotation request brief

Provide property type and location, requested service, verified measurements or survey, photographs and previous drawings, design objective, user numbers, reference budget if desired, required level of detail and expected deliverables (drawings, model, schedules or submission follow-up). Ask how many options and revision rounds are included, whether source files and usage rights are supplied, and whether site survey, submission and follow-up are part of the fee.

When comparing proposals, separate architectural, structural and interior design, supervision and project management. Identify the professional responsible for each discipline, signing and review boundaries, delivery schedule and exclusions. If no office is published, Bait Al Reef Customer Service can register the same request through the intake form without inventing an office, fee or approval.

## Handover and revision control

Receive drawings with clear revision numbers and dates. Check consistency between plans, elevations, sections and schedules, including dimensions, levels and service locations. Record changes, comments and accepted alternatives, and distinguish concept drawings from those approved for construction or submission. Independent technical review is required before approval or building.
