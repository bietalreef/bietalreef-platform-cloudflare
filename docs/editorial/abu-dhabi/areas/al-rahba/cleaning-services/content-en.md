## Home and Building Cleaning

### 1. Villa cleaning

For villa cleaning in Al Rahba, first distinguish routine housekeeping from deep cleaning or cleaning after maintenance, since each calls for different methods. Provide the number of floors, rooms and bathrooms, outdoor area and photographs of the kitchen, glazing and difficult-to-reach areas. Ask bidders to price oven interiors, cupboards and exterior windows separately from the basic work and to state crew size, duration and who supplies chemicals. At handover, inspect corners, floor edges and grease deposits, and check that finishes are undamaged and surfaces dry.

### 2. Apartment cleaning

Explain whether the apartment is furnished or empty and whether the visit is for move-in preparation or regular upkeep. Include room and bathroom counts, photographs of stains or kitchen grease, and any known building access, lift or parking restrictions. Compare quotations that expressly include or exclude windows, appliances, cupboard interiors and curtains rather than assuming they are covered. At handover, inspect shelves, accessible areas behind appliances and bathroom edges, and ensure no cleaning residues remain on food-contact surfaces.

### 3. Office cleaning

An office cleaning plan must avoid disrupting staff and protect documents and equipment. List workstations, meeting rooms, kitchenettes and washrooms, permitted access hours and areas where contents must not be touched or moved. Request a schedule for waste collection and high-touch surface cleaning, consumable supply responsibilities and damage reporting. Routine cleaning should not be represented as specialized disinfection; the latter requires suitable products and correct application. Check the work log and inspection results for washrooms and shared spaces.

### 4. Building cleaning

For a building, identify entrances, corridors, staircases, lifts, floors and accessible service rooms, along with floor areas and finishes. Separate elevated glazing or façade work from common-area cleaning because access equipment and safety procedures differ. Request a location-by-location schedule covering frequency, consumables and waste handling; compare actual visits rather than headcount alone. Handover should include checks of corners, lift doors and dry floor conditions, with inaccessible areas documented.

### 5. Post-construction cleaning

Construction and painting may leave fine dust, adhesive and grout residues on easily scratched finishes. Send recent room photographs, identify tile, glass and cabinet materials, and confirm whether contractors have finished. Quotations should distinguish bulk debris removal, suitable filtered vacuuming, detailed wiping and carefully tested residue treatment. Avoid indiscriminate scraping of glazing or stone. Once surfaces dry, inspect window edges, ventilation openings and floors; record pre-existing construction defects separately from cleaning damage.

### 6. Pre-move cleaning

If furniture delivery is imminent, specify the property handover date and when any remaining maintenance will finish. Clean spaces that furniture will later obstruct first, including empty cupboards, kitchen corners, bathrooms and door edges. Share site photographs and indicate fixed appliances, unusual stains or odours. Compare drying arrangements and the earliest safe time for moving furniture in, not just the crew's departure time. At handover, verify interior surfaces are clean and dry and that strong chemical odours have dissipated.

### 7. Recurring cleaning contracts

A recurring contract needs more than a promise of comprehensive cleaning. Prepare a task schedule for each area, separating daily, weekly and separately authorized extra work. Supply site area, visiting hours and high-use zones; ask who supervises, covers staff absence and provides materials and tools. Compare complaint handling, visit records and measurable quality checks, such as task completion and washroom condition. Review logs regularly and document any change in scope when the premises' use changes.
