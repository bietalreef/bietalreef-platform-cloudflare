## Home and Building Cleaning

### 1. Villa cleaning

For villa cleaning in Al Rahba, first distinguish routine housekeeping from deep cleaning or cleaning after maintenance, since each calls for different methods. Provide the number of floors, rooms and bathrooms, outdoor area and photographs of the kitchen, glazing and difficult-to-reach areas. Ask bidders to price oven interiors, cupboards and exterior windows separately from the basic work and to state crew size, duration and who supplies chemicals. At handover, inspect corners, floor edges and grease deposits, and check that finishes are undamaged and surfaces dry.

### 2. Apartment cleaning

Explain whether the apartment is furnished or empty and whether the visit is for move-in preparation or regular upkeep. Include room and bathroom counts, photographs of stains or kitchen grease, and any known building access, lift or parking restrictions. Compare quotations that expressly include or exclude windows, appliances, cupboard interiors and curtains rather than assuming they are covered. At handover, inspect shelves, accessible areas behind appliances and bathroom edges, and ensure no cleaning residues remain on food-contact surfaces.

### 3. Office cleaning

An office cleaning plan must avoid disrupting staff and protect documents and equipment. List workstations, meeting rooms, kitchenettes and washrooms, permitted access hours and areas where contents must not be touched or moved. Request a schedule for waste collection and high-touch surface cleaning, consumable supply responsibilities and damage reporting. Routine cleaning should not be represented as specialized disinfection; the latter requires suitable products and correct application. Check the work log and inspection results for washrooms and shared spaces.

### 4. Building cleaning

For a building, identify entrances, corridors, staircases, lifts, floors and accessible service rooms, along with floor areas and finishes. Separate elevated glazing or façade work from common-area cleaning because access equipment and safety procedures differ. Request a location-by-location schedule covering frequency, consumables and waste handling; compare actual visits rather than headcount alone. Handover should include checks of corners, lift doors and dry floor conditions, with inaccessible areas documented.

### 5. Post-construction cleaning

Construction and painting may leave fine dust, adhesive and grout residues on easily scratched finishes. Send recent room photographs, identify tile, glass and cabinet materials, and confirm whether contractors have finished. Quotations should distinguish bulk debris removal, suitable filtered vacuuming, detailed wiping and carefully tested residue treatment. Avoid indiscriminate scraping of glazing or stone. Once surfaces dry, inspect window edges, ventilation openings and floors; record pre-existing construction defects separately from cleaning damage.

### 6. Pre-move cleaning

If furniture delivery is imminent, specify the property handover date and when any remaining maintenance will finish. Clean spaces that furniture will later obstruct first, including empty cupboards, kitchen corners, bathrooms and door edges. Share site photographs and indicate fixed appliances, unusual stains or odours. Compare drying arrangements and the earliest safe time for moving furniture in, not just the crew's departure time. At handover, verify interior surfaces are clean and dry and that strong chemical odours have dissipated.

### 7. Recurring cleaning contracts

A recurring contract needs more than a promise of comprehensive cleaning. Prepare a task schedule for each area, separating daily, weekly and separately authorized extra work. Supply site area, visiting hours and high-use zones; ask who supervises, covers staff absence and provides materials and tools. Compare complaint handling, visit records and measurable quality checks, such as task completion and washroom condition. Review logs regularly and document any change in scope when the premises' use changes.

## Upholstery cleaning

### 8. Steam sofa cleaning

Record the upholstery fabric, care label, seat count and photographs of stains or fading. Steam is unsuitable for some fabrics: request a patch test, extraction method and drying estimate, then inspect for residual dampness or rings once dry.

### 9. Rug cleaning

Provide each rug's dimensions, fibre type and whether it is handmade or dye-sensitive, with edge and stain photos. Compare on-site treatment with off-site washing, document its condition before collection and check colour stability and edges on return.

### 10. Carpet cleaning

Measure the carpeted area in square metres and identify fibre, fixed furniture and traffic lanes. Ask about pre-vacuuming, spot treatment, extraction and residual moisture, and agree a reopening time; inspect seams and stains after drying.

### 11. Arabic majlis cleaning

Count seating sections, backrests and loose cushions and identify upholstery materials, with close-up photographs. Price frames, cushions and curtains separately, test dye stability and check even appearance and dry fillings at handover.

### 12. Mattress cleaning

State mattress size, cover material, stain age and any odour or prior dampness. Avoid soaking the core; compare vacuuming, targeted treatment and ventilation time, and confirm full dryness before replacing bedding.

### 13. Curtain cleaning

Provide curtain dimensions, fabric, lining and fixing method, and whether removal and refitting are required. Check shrinkage and colourfastness risks, record pieces and fittings before collection, then inspect length, pleats and hardware after refitting.

### 14. Upholstery disinfection

Distinguish soil removal from disinfection and explain the purpose, fabric type and chemical compatibility. Request the product label, contact time and ventilation instructions; reject unsupported sterilization guarantees and inspect fabric condition afterwards.


## Façade and site cleaning

### 15. Glass façade cleaning

Share façade height, area, glazing type and ground or roof access. The quotation should separate access equipment and pedestrian protection from washing, with non-abrasive stain treatment; inspect reflections and edges once dry.

### 16. Building façade cleaning

Identify cladding such as stone, paint or aluminium and photograph dirt and existing cracks. Require a trial patch and suitable water pressure and chemicals, with repairs priced separately; check for water ingress or discolouration.

### 17. Car park cleaning

Specify parking area, floor finish, oil staining, drains and lane closure windows. Compare machine scrubbing, degreasing and site-approved wastewater handling, with work-zone segregation; inspect slip hazards and oil residue before reopening.

### 18. Industrial site cleaning

Describe the facility, soil sources, restricted zones and potentially hazardous materials, using photographs cleared by the site manager. Industrial waste is not household rubbish: require segregation, PPE and waste classification plans, then inspect the cleaning log and exclusions.


## Tank and pool cleaning

### 19. Water tank cleaning

Provide tank capacity, material, location, isolation and drainage arrangements, with safe photographs of access and sediment. Request a sequence for emptying, sediment removal, rinsing and lining inspection, plus refill contamination controls; obtain before-and-after records.

### 20. Water tank disinfection

Disinfection is separate from sediment removal. State whether the tank supplies drinking water and request documented chemical, concentration, contact time and rinsing in accordance with product instructions and applicable requirements. Odour alone is not proof of safety; obtain treatment and any required testing records.

### 21. Swimming pool cleaning

Provide pool dimensions, depth, finish, filter and pump condition, and algae or scale photographs. Separate wall and floor cleaning from filtration maintenance and water chemistry adjustment; agree safe reopening checks for clarity, test readings and edge condition.


## Pest and rodent control

### 22. Cockroach control

Photograph sightings and note times, kitchens and moisture sources without disturbing bait stations. Ask for crack and drain inspection, staged treatment, child and pet precautions and follow-up timing; assess sightings alongside food and entry-point controls.

### 23. Ant control

Mark ant trails, entry points and proximity to food or plants, with photographs for identification. Spraying visible trails may miss the colony; request suitable bait or targeted treatment, hygiene advice and follow-up, then check activity over the agreed interval.

### 24. Rodent control

Document droppings, gnaw marks and entry routes without directly handling contamination. Request inspection, secure numbered traps, checking frequency and responsibility for proofing openings; review trap logs, safe removal and residual activity.

### 25. Bed bug control

Identify affected rooms and photograph insects or traces; bites alone cannot confirm infestation. Request mattress seam and furniture inspection, preparation instructions, treatment and repeat-visit plan; assess physical evidence after treatment.

### 26. Recurring pest-control contracts

Define premises, monitoring points, sighting history and food or child-sensitive areas. The contract should specify visits, incident response, trap mapping, product records and prevention—not spraying alone; review trends in monitoring reports.


## Disinfection

### 27. Home spraying and disinfection

Identify surfaces and the purpose of treatment, noting children, pets and sensitivities. Ask about prior cleaning, product compatibility, labelled contact time and ventilation; fragrance spraying is not disinfection and no blanket pathogen-elimination guarantee should be assumed.

### 28. Office and facility disinfection

Provide high-touch area maps, vacancy windows and sensitive equipment and finishes. Require separate cleaning and disinfection tasks, product details and re-entry timing, with electronics protected; obtain a treatment log without unsupported health claims.


## Building operations and maintenance

### 29. Maintenance work management

Supply an asset register, recurring faults, priorities and warranty information. Request a ticket workflow for approvals, parts, photographs and closure; compare response and escalation arrangements, then audit completed work orders.

### 30. Facility operations

Define operating hours, critical systems and owner-versus-operator responsibilities. Request shift plans, inspections, performance measures and handover logs, including out-of-hours faults; verify records and service continuity.

### 31. Preventive maintenance

Collect equipment manuals, installation dates, service history and manufacturer intervals, then prioritize by risk. Compare inspection, testing, lubrication, replacement and exclusion schedules; receive readings, findings and corrective-action reports.

### 32. Electrical systems monitoring

Identify distribution boards, critical loads and nuisance-trip history; live panels must not be opened by unqualified staff. Request safe visual checks and measurements with planned shutdowns where needed; review test results and recommendations.

### 33. HVAC systems monitoring

List units, types, temperature and noise complaints, and energy records if available. Require temperature, filter, drainage, coil and control fault logs, with repairs and parts priced separately; compare readings before and after corrective work.

### 34. Plumbing and drainage monitoring

Identify pumps, tanks, inspection chambers and prior leaks, with drawings where available. Request pressure, leakage, odour and drain checks with defect locations documented; distinguish monitoring from repairs and receive a prioritized action list.

### 35. Building condition reporting

Agree the survey scope: visible structure, finishes, services and common areas, with limits of non-invasive inspection stated. Require dated photographs, risk prioritization and preliminary actions—not unsupported structural certification; verify each finding maps to a location.
