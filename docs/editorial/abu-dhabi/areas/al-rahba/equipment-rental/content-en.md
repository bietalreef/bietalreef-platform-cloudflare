# Al Rahba — Factories and Equipment Hire

Equipment selection starts with the site and work requirements rather than the machine name alone. These business-service descriptions do not establish that a provider is available in Al Rahba.

## Construction and Lifting Equipment Hire

### 1. Scaffolding hire

State working height, facade length, ground conditions and intended loads, with photos of access and obstructions. Ask for separate pricing of design, erection, inspection, dismantling, hire and transport, including who certifies safe use. At handover have a competent person check bases, guardrails and inspection records.

### 2. Crane hire

Provide load weight and dimensions, pick-up and set-down points, travel path, reach and ground photographs. Clarify crane type, operator, rigging, lift plan, fuel and any site-specific approvals without assuming they apply universally. Compare booking and standby charges, and verify inspection records before operation.

### 3. Excavation equipment hire

Attach excavation limits, depth, width, known soil conditions and turning space, and flag underground services for verification. Compare excavator size, attachments, operator, transport, fuel and spoil disposal as separate items. Inspect equipment and confirm service locations before breaking ground.

### 4. Compaction equipment hire

Specify area, fill layer thickness, material and proximity to edges or sensitive services. Request suitable equipment for the working space and clarify operator, fuel, transport and any project-required compaction tests. Check consistent layers; machine passes alone do not prove adequate compaction.

### 5. Concrete mixer hire

State required batch volume, power or fuel availability and material access. Compare drum capacity, output, cleaning, transport and operator responsibility; do not confuse mixer hire with concrete supply or mix design. Check drum rotation and guards before accepting equipment.

### 6. Concrete cutting equipment hire

Identify member type, thickness, cut position, opening dimensions and potential reinforcement or concealed services. Clarify blade selection, dust or water control, waste removal and responsibility for structural assessment. Separate equipment hire from operator cost and inspect safety arrangements before use.

### 7. General project equipment hire

Prepare an equipment schedule with individual hire periods, locations and delivery/return dates. Request condition reports and clear breakdown, replacement, late-return, insurance and fuel terms; compare repeated transport costs. Record serial numbers and operating hours before return.


## Light Tools and Equipment Hire

### 8. Generator rental

Size the generator from running kW, motor starting loads, phase count and voltage rather than a vague site estimate. Send an equipment list, expected operating hours, access photographs and a proposed ventilated location. Ask about fuel, noise, distribution protection, earthing and connection by a competent electrician. Compare transport, hire, operator, fuel and breakdown response separately. Check output voltage and frequency under a suitable load and log fuel and running hours; never operate a generator indoors.

### 9. Cleaning equipment rental

Identify whether the job needs a floor scrubber, wet-and-dry vacuum, pressure washer or upholstery extractor. Supply surface photographs, floor area, access widths and available power, water and drainage. Specify brushes, filters, cleaning solutions and any restrictions on delicate finishes. Compare hire charges, consumables, delivery and return-cleaning fees. Check cables, hoses and leakage and test on a small inconspicuous area before acceptance.

### 10. Air compressor rental

State each pneumatic tool's pressure and airflow demand, duty cycle, hose length and number of simultaneous users. Tank size alone does not establish suitable capacity. Clarify power or fuel, noise and air drying or filtration requirements, particularly for spray finishes. Compare compressor hire, hoses, regulators, fuel and delivery. Inspect the pressure gauge, relief valve, connections and hoses, then confirm automatic cut-out at the intended pressure.

### 11. Power tool rental

List the materials, hole diameters, drilling depths and cutting thicknesses before selecting drills, grinders or saws. Confirm mains supply or batteries and chargers, as well as included bits, blades and discs. Compare hire period, consumable replacement and damage terms. Inspect guards, switches and cables, and conduct a safe no-load function test before accepting the tools.

### 12. Welding equipment rental

Describe metal type, thickness, joint access and weld length to select arc, MIG or TIG equipment and suitable accessories. Confirm power, ventilation, combustible materials, shielding gas, electrodes or wire, protective equipment and who operates the set. Compare machine rental, consumables, delivery and inspection. Have a competent person check cables, return clamps, hoses and gas connections before use.

### 13. Water pump rental

Provide water source, estimated volume, lift height, discharge distance and whether the liquid contains grit or debris. Flow rate and total head matter more than hose diameter alone. Photograph intake and discharge points and identify an acceptable drainage destination, power supply and operating duration. Compare pump, hoses, strainer, fuel and monitoring costs. Verify flow, leakage and dry-run protection, ensuring discharge will not damage adjacent property.

### 14. Garden equipment rental

Specify mowing, hedge trimming, branch pruning or debris removal, together with working area, vegetation height, branch diameter and access width. Request suitable cutting attachments, protective equipment, noise information and fuel or batteries. Price waste collection and disposal separately. Inspect blades, guards and stop controls, and agree cleaning and fuel condition on return. Keep bystanders and exposed cables clear of cutting operations.

## Aluminium and Glass Factories

### 15. Aluminium profile manufacturing

A request for extruded profiles needs more than a description such as “heavy-duty section”. Provide a section drawing or specified system reference, cut lengths, quantities, finish and colour, and the intended use in doors, windows or façades. Ask the manufacturer to identify wall thickness, applicable tolerances and alloy grade where the project specification calls for them, together with surface treatment and scratch-resistant packaging. Compare net lengths, waste allowance, dies, finishing and transport separately rather than accepting an unexplained weight-based total. On delivery, check profile shapes against the approved sample, measure lengths and straightness, and record coating damage before accepting quantities.

### 16. Door and window manufacturing

Fabrication should follow verified opening measurements, not only early architectural dimensions. Specify opening direction, frame system, thresholds, window drainage, glazing, handles, locks and any insect screens. Share opening photographs and finished-floor levels; request shop drawings showing fixings, perimeter gaps and sealing interfaces. Compare fabrication, glass, fitting, sealants, surrounding making-good and warranty as separate items. After installation, test alignment, opening and closing, drainage and seals, and list scratches or unfinished joints in the handover record.

### 17. Toughened glass manufacturing

Toughened glass must be cut and drilled to final dimensions before tempering. Supply a drawing with width, height, thickness, polished edges, holes and hardware positions, and explain whether the panel is for a door, partition or façade. Request the glass specification, edge treatment, supporting documentation and a safe upright transport and storage method. Compare cutting, drilling, tempering, transport, lifting and fitting separately. Check panel dimensions, edges, markings and visual finish against the agreed specification before installation. Structural suitability must be assessed through the project design, not guessed from thickness alone.

### 18. Glass façade preparation

A façade package needs drawings showing panel divisions, support system, movement allowances, design loads and junctions with framing, waterproofing and drainage. Provide elevations, access photographs and clear responsibility for design, approvals, lifting and installation. Ask for glazing schedules, framing sections, accessories and finish samples. Distinguish a glass-only supply quotation from a complete façade package. Compare testing, documentation and damaged-panel replacement arrangements. At handover, review panel identification, alignment, joints, seals and contract-required test records with the responsible specialist.

### 19. Partition manufacturing

Clarify whether the partition is intended for visual separation, privacy, acoustic performance or a particular fire-resistance requirement specified by the designer. Send a room plan, ceiling heights, door locations and interfaces with air-conditioning, sprinklers and services. Request details of glazing, frames, supports, joints and seals, and identify excluded electrical or builder's work. Compare actual partition area, door count, hardware and programme. Check stability, joint alignment, door operation and exposed edges at handover; never assume an acoustic or fire rating without suitable test evidence.

### 20. Glass supply

Specify whether each panel is clear, tinted, insulated, laminated or toughened, together with thickness, dimensions, quantities, edge finish and any documented performance requirement. Provide a consolidated panel schedule, unloading arrangements and a protected storage location. Compare product specification, processing, lead time, delivery and treatment of defective panels; glass supply does not automatically include fitting. Inspect labels, measurements, edges and visible damage on delivery, and record shortages before signing the delivery note.

### 21. Project order fulfilment

A project order may combine profiles, doors, glazing and partitions across multiple schedules. Submit one coordinated list linking every item to its drawing reference, location, quantity, specification and required delivery date; flag measurements still awaiting site verification. Ask for phased fabrication and delivery, sample and shop-drawing approvals, and clear coordination between supplier and installer. Compare itemised inclusions, waste, storage, lifting and variation terms rather than headline totals. Reconcile deliveries with approved drawings and close out defects and replacements at each milestone.

## Marble and stone factories

### 22. Marble and stone cutting

Prepare a cutting schedule identifying stone type, slab thickness, finished dimensions, quantities, grain direction, mitres, openings and edge finish. Distinguish confirmed workshop dimensions from measurements awaiting a site survey; cutting before checking wall alignment can cause expensive rework. Compare straight and curved cuts, holes, polishing, packing and transport as separate items. At receipt, check dimensions, grain alignment, edges and cracks, and record rejected pieces before installation.

### 23. Kitchen worktop fabrication

A kitchen worktop requires a template showing each run, depth, sink and hob cut-outs, tap holes, exposed edges and slab joints. Measure after cabinets are fixed to verify levels and appliance clearances. Specify the surface material, thickness, support and joint treatment; fabrication does not necessarily include the sink, plumbing or appliance fitting. Compare cutting, edging, cut-outs, lifting and installation separately. At handover, inspect level, support, cut-out edges and sealed joints around wet areas.

### 24. Stone stair fabrication

Provide a stair drawing with measured risers, treads, flight width, landings, nosings and the specified stone and slip-resistance requirements from the project design. Confirm finished floor levels before cutting, especially at the first and last steps. Compare treads, risers, skirtings, nosing treatment, cutting and fitting, and identify who levels the substrate. Inspect dimensional consistency, secure bedding, edge alignment and landing transitions with the responsible technical supervisor.

### 25. Façade stone preparation

Façade stone preparation needs a numbered panel layout, not merely a square-metre total. Record each panel's size, thickness, fixing holes, vein direction and corner details. The project designer must define the support system, loads, joints and movement allowances before fabrication; panel weight alone is not a fixing design. Compare cutting, drilling, finishing, labelling, packing and delivery separately from lifting and installation. Inspect panels against approved drawings, including edges, holes and damage, before façade installation.

### 26. Decorative stone carving

Submit a dimensioned drawing or digital pattern showing carving depth, repeat spacing, edge clearances, stone type and required finish. Approve a physical sample before full production because texture, tooling and polishing affect the result. Compare programming, sampling, manual or machine carving, finishing and packing, and agree how breakage or colour variation will be handled. Inspect definition, pattern consistency, fragile edges and conformity with the approved sample.

### 27. Marble polishing and treatment

Clarify whether the issue is dullness, scratches, staining or uneven tile levels; these call for different treatments. Provide well-lit photos, area measurements and the stone type if known, and request a small discreet test patch. Agree grinding and polishing stages, protective treatments and realistic limits for stains that have penetrated the stone. Compare furniture protection, clean-up and slurry disposal as well as polishing. Inspect uniformity after drying and check for machine marks or new staining.

### 28. Contractor stone order preparation

For a contractor's stone order, consolidate approved shop drawings and quantities into one schedule linking each piece to a drawing number, installation location, stone type, thickness, size, finish and required date. Flag dimensions awaiting final survey, delivery priorities, packing, labelling and unloading arrangements. Compare waste allowances, samples, cutting, openings, transport, permitted substitutions and replacement terms rather than total price alone. Reconcile labels, batches and quantities with the schedule and document damage or shortages before signing delivery records.
