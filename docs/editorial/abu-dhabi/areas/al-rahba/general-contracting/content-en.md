# General contracting and construction in Al Rahba

This editorial guide covers company and establishment services in Al Rahba; it is not a list of available contractors or an indication of regulatory approval. Project drawings, measurements, scope and site conditions determine what can be priced reliably. Individual tradespeople are listed separately from contracting companies.

## Building and villa construction

### 1. Villa construction

A villa enquiry should identify plot area, rooms, floors and parking requirements. Attach the plot plan and available drawings, and specify whether the price includes structure, finishes and external works. Compare contractors against the same bill of quantities, concrete and waterproofing specifications, MEP scope and measurable payment milestones. Handover should include relevant tests, concealed-work records and a documented snag list.

### 2. Residential building construction

For a residential building, state the unit count, floors, shared areas, lifts and building services. Supply approved drawings where available, a finish schedule and system specifications. Separate structural, façade and MEP pricing, and clarify coordination responsibilities. Acceptance must cover commissioning, plant spaces, circulation and common areas as well as individual units.

### 3. Commercial building construction

Define the intended commercial use, lettable areas, floor heights, floor loads and loading access before seeking prices. Distinguish shell-and-core delivery from tenant fit-out. Compare offers on façade, central services, operational interfaces and the documented life-safety design scope. Handover checks should cover system testing, measured spaces, service access and operating documentation.

### 4. General building works

This broad category needs a defined scope: blockwork, plastering, concrete, site works or a combination. Provide drawings, dimensions and quantities, and request separate allowances for materials, labour, transport and waste removal. A square-metre rate is meaningless without wall thicknesses, openings and preparation requirements. Check measured quantities, alignment, finish and drawing compliance at acceptance.

### 5. Concrete works

Identify the concrete elements—footings, columns, beams or slabs—and provide structural drawings, reinforcement schedules and the designer's specified concrete grades. Quotations should state formwork, steel, placement, curing, tests and pump responsibility. Loads must not be assumed verbally. Review pour records, test results and the supervising engineer's observations before acceptance or formwork removal.

### 6. Structural frame construction

Structural-frame scope covers the designed sequence of foundations, columns, beams and slabs; finishes are not automatically included. Define excavation, backfill, waterproofing and blockwork boundaries. Ask for a programme tied to pours and inspections, and coordinate service penetrations before casting. Acceptance should verify levels, dimensions, material test results and stage approvals.

### 7. Turnkey project delivery

Turnkey does not define scope by itself. Attach a responsibility schedule covering design, approvals, structure, finishes, air conditioning, kitchens, landscaping and furniture, with explicit exclusions. Compare named or performance-based specifications, alternatives and variation procedures. Handover should include commissioned services, site cleaning, warranties, as-built information and defect tracking.

### 8. New villa building

For a new villa, begin with the plot information, family accommodation brief and room layout before requesting an overall estimate. Separate design fees, construction and optional fit-out. Clarify groundworks, external utility connections, windows and insulation in the contractor's scope. Approve material samples and finish mock-ups, then compare the delivered work against them.

### 9. Residential annex construction

An annex brief should state its purpose, footprint, position relative to the existing building and access routes. Supply site photos, levels and existing service locations; assess connections to the original structure before construction. Quotes should define foundations, waterproofing, plumbing, electrical works and finishes. Inspect junctions, drainage and leak protection at handover.

### 10. Outdoor majlis construction

Specify seating capacity, use, entrances, toilets and any pantry for an outdoor majlis. Site photographs and measurements help coordinate cooling, drainage, shading and door swings. Compare thermal and acoustic insulation, ventilation, finishes and hospitality facilities. Test cooling, drainage, lighting and doors before acceptance.

### 11. Villa extensions

For a villa extension, identify added rooms, affected walls and occupied-area access during construction. A qualified specialist should review existing drawings, foundations and structural condition before proposing new openings or loads. Separate connection, strengthening, making-good and service diversions in the quote. Inspect cracks, waterproof junctions and new connections at handover.

### 12. Residential building alterations

Describe whether the alteration changes room partitions, door openings, kitchens or bathrooms, with existing and proposed layouts. A wall's appearance does not establish that it is non-load-bearing; verify before removal. Compare protection, demolition, service relocation and reinstatement allowances. Check doors, levels and functioning services after the change.

### 13. Villa restoration

Document damage with photographs and its history: cracks, dampness or failed finishes. Identify causes before specifying paint or filler to avoid recurrence. Request an inspection that distinguishes local repairs from major refurbishment and identifies moisture or safety checks. Compare repaired locations with baseline photos and review performance after drying.

### 14. Residential building refurbishment

State which units are included and whether residents remain in place; record the condition of entrances, lifts and corridors. Split the quote into electrical, plumbing, cooling, finishes and shared works, with resident protection and waste plans. Agree finish samples and a floor-by-floor programme. Handover requires service testing and closed snag lists for units and common areas.

## Restoration and refurbishment

### 15. Restoration of older buildings

Document damaged concrete, plaster and services using photographs and available drawings. Distinguish worn finishes from structural deterioration; obtain a technical diagnosis before selecting repairs or strengthening. Compare investigation, protection, removal and testing scopes. At handover review inspection records and photographs of repaired locations.

### 16. Villa refurbishment

Define whether villa refurbishment covers kitchens, bathrooms, cooling, floors and façades, and whether occupants remain. Supply dimensions, existing service photographs and a materials schedule; request separate demolition, reinstatement and furniture protection allowances. Compare samples, programme and exclusions. Test waterproofing, drainage and electrical work before acceptance.

### 17. Apartment refurbishment

State apartment size, floor, delivery access and protection for shared lifts and corridors. Photograph rooms and identify items to retain. Separate removal, plumbing, electrical, painting and waste charges. At handover test doors, sockets and water points and record damage to common areas.

### 18. Building rehabilitation

Rehabilitation is more than cosmetic improvement and may address systems affecting safe use. Provide condition reports, drawings and available test results, identifying urgent defects. Compare staged work, supervision, testing and recommissioning. Do not accept until critical defects are closed and tests documented.

### 19. Structural crack remediation

Photograph each crack with location, direction, width and history, noting changes or accompanying leaks. Photographs alone cannot establish whether filler, injection or structural repair is appropriate; obtain competent diagnosis. Request inspection, monitoring, repair and verification methods. Acceptance relies on measurements and test records, not appearance alone.

### 20. Façade refurbishment

Identify façade height, area, cladding and locations of leakage or peeling. Require safe access and pedestrian protection, and correction of the underlying cause before recoating. Compare materials, joints, scaffolding and warranty scope. Inspect adhesion, uniformity and drainage around windows.

### 21. Preparing a building for reuse

Describe the intended new use and provide existing plans, power, cooling, drainage and access information. Structural, safety or permitting reviews may be needed; a service listing is not permission. Ask for a gap assessment and separate design, alteration and commissioning scopes. Verify systems and agreed design at handover.

## Demolition and concrete works

### 22. Demolition works

Before pricing demolition, mark what must be removed, the plot boundaries and what must remain, with photographs and plans showing buried services and adjoining walls. Internal strip-out differs from full structural demolition in temporary support, isolation, dust and vibration controls. Request a method statement for disconnecting utilities, protecting neighbours, segregating waste and obtaining any applicable approvals from the competent authority. Compare methods, safety responsibilities and the required final site condition; inspect for remaining debris and unintended damage before acceptance.

### 23. Removal of buildings and annexes

Removing a building or annex requires its footprint, height, structural type and proximity to retained buildings or boundary walls. Supply photographs of elevations, roof and access, and specify whether foundations, slabs and services are included. A survey should establish the dismantling sequence, equipment access, vehicle routes and protection for retained structures. Quotes must separate loading, transport and disposal rather than hide the removal boundary in a lump sum. Record the initial condition and check levels and neighbouring structures after clearance.

### 24. Concrete cutting and core drilling

Concrete cutting may create a doorway or service trench; core drilling may accommodate a pipe. State opening dimensions, element thickness and location, and supply structural drawings and photographs of both faces where possible. A competent specialist must establish reinforcement, embedded cables and structural implications before cutting. Compare the cutting technique, water and dust containment, temporary support and removal of cores. Measure the completed opening and check edges and any new cracking at handover.

### 25. Casting footings and foundations

Foundation quotations require a ground investigation, structural drawings, excavation dimensions and founding levels—not merely the building floor area. Identify responsibility for any dewatering, backfill, compaction, waterproofing, blinding concrete, reinforcement and formwork. Require hold-point inspections and records for compaction, steel and concrete before concealment. Confirm starter-bar locations and penetrations early. Compare measured quantities, specified materials, test results and the supervising engineer's inspection records before accepting the stage.

### 26. Casting concrete slabs

For a concrete roof or suspended slab, provide slab drawings, spans, thicknesses, levels and planned openings or embedded services. Quotes should identify formwork, propping, reinforcement, concrete supply, pumping, curing and weather precautions. Openings and prop removal must follow the designer's requirements, not an on-site guess. Inspect reinforcement and cover before pouring; review sampling and curing records afterwards. Check levels, edges and surface defects at acceptance.

### 27. Construction of concrete walls

Define whether the concrete wall is retaining, shear-resisting, a barrier or another engineered element, with its length, height, thickness and approved design. The scope should identify construction and movement joints, service penetrations, waterproofing and drainage where soil is retained. Compare reinforcement, formwork, concrete, curing and stripping protection; do not treat a load-bearing wall as an ordinary partition. Check alignment, levels, honeycombing, joints and material inspection reports at handover.

### 28. Demolition waste removal

Before arranging demolition-waste removal, estimate the waste types and volumes, collection location and truck access; provide photographs identifying heavy, sharp or potentially special-handling material. Separate sorting, loading, containers, haulage and lawful disposal in the quotation, and allocate responsibility for protecting pavements, entrances and roads. Different materials may require different disposal routes, which should be confirmed with the relevant operator and authority. Obtain transfer or receipt evidence where applicable, and inspect the cleared site for buried remnants.

## Garden design and maintenance

### 29. Home garden design

Begin with a measured site plan showing doors, windows, sunlight and shade. Identify seating, circulation and play areas before choosing plants. Request dimensioned layouts, drainage levels, irrigation and lighting points. Compare executable drawings and planting schedules rather than visual renders alone; check dimensions, access and maintenance clearances at handover.

### 30. Villa landscaping

Villa landscaping may cover soil preparation, planting, paths, bed edging and lighting. Photograph existing surfaces, slopes and water points and identify what must be retained. Price grading, soil, plants, irrigation and hardscape separately, including initial care. Inspect path levels, edging stability and plant condition at handover.

### 31. Outdoor space design

Describe whether the space is for guests, dining or utility use, with occupancy and access points. Measure boundaries, thresholds and stormwater routes. Request furniture layouts, outdoor-rated finishes and junction details where new work meets the building. Compare shade, privacy, cleaning and maintenance before approving the design.

### 32. Establishing green areas

State the lawn or planted-area dimensions and intended use. Assess soil, drainage and irrigation supply before installation. Quotes should describe soil preparation, grading, plant or turf specification, establishment and initial watering. A square-metre price is not comparable without soil depth and plant quality. Inspect coverage and ponding after irrigation.

### 33. Planting trees and shrubs

Provide proposed tree counts and locations, bed dimensions and nearby walls or buried services. Select species for mature size, root behaviour and water demand, not appearance alone. Quotes should state nursery stock size, planting pits, soil amendments, staking and wind protection. Check alignment, stability, irrigation and agreed establishment care.

### 34. Natural turf installation

Measure the area and assess shade, foot traffic and drainage. Confirm turf species, delivery freshness, soil preparation, grading and watering method. Separate weed removal, imported soil and turf rolls or plugs in the quotation. Inspect tight joints and even levels, then allow roots to establish before heavy use.

### 35. Artificial turf installation

State whether the artificial turf serves landscaping, play or another exposed surface, and measure obstructions. Compare samples for pile height, density, backing and suitability, but also specify sub-base and drainage. Quotes should describe seams, perimeter anchoring and any infill. Inspect joint security, levels and drainage, and obtain cleaning guidance.

### 36. Plant and tree installation

For mixed planting, supply a schedule of species, quantities, sizes and locations, plus site photos and water availability. Distinguish bed plants from trees needing greater spacing and pit dimensions. Compare nursery quality, soil, delivery, installation and establishment care. Verify each plant against the schedule and ensure root collars are not buried.

### 37. Tree pruning and shaping

Photograph each tree in full and note height, nearby cables, walls and paths. Explain whether pruning is for deadwood, clearance or form. Avoid excessive cutting without assessing tree health. Quotes should identify access equipment, protection and green-waste removal. Inspect cut quality, bark damage and site cleanliness.

### 38. Lawn maintenance

Record lawn area, turf type, irrigation condition and desired visit frequency. Request a seasonal programme for mowing, aeration, weed control and sprinkler checks, stating consumables and exclusions. Compare visit counts, dry-patch response and defect reporting. Review service logs, mowing consistency and edging damage.

### 39. Plant fertilisation and treatment

Describe yellowing or poor growth with photos of foliage, soil and irrigation; drainage, salinity or pests can mimic nutrient deficiency. Require diagnosis before selecting products, dose and application. Compare assessment, follow-up and safety instructions rather than product volume alone. Record treatments and monitor response.

### 40. Garden pest management

Identify affected plants and photograph insects or damage, noting children and pets. Request pest identification and a management plan using suitable least-disruptive measures first, with product details and any re-entry precautions. Verify operator and product eligibility with competent authorities. Review treatment records and follow-up inspection timing.

### 41. Garden clearance and cleaning

Describe the garden area and waste types—leaves, branches, soil or construction debris—and what must be protected. Photograph paths, beds and removal access. Compare sweeping, collection, light trimming and haulage as separate items; distinguish special waste from green waste. Check drains and ensure plants and irrigation remain undamaged.

### 42. Garden maintenance contracts

Inventory planted areas, trees, irrigation, pumps and lighting, and set access hours. Contracts should specify visit frequency, seasonal tasks, fault response, replacement plants and exclusions for major repairs. Compare reporting, before-and-after photographs and performance measures—not monthly fees alone. Review completed tasks and defects each cycle.


## Irrigation networks

### 43. Irrigation network design

Begin with a scaled garden plan showing beds, plant types and the water connection. The designer should zone plants by water demand and available flow and pressure, then map pipe routes, valves and controls before excavation. Request pipe sizes, irrigation zones, estimated demand and drainage details; compare maintainability rather than sprinkler count alone. At handover reconcile the drawing with installed routes and test pressure, coverage and leaks.

### 44. Drip irrigation installation

Drip irrigation targets root zones in planting beds and shrub rows. Provide row lengths, plant quantities, soil conditions and supply location; specify filtration, pressure regulation, emitter flow and flushing points. The quotation should cover securing and protecting tubing and separating zones with different demands. Commission every line, checking emitter consistency, end-of-line flushing and access for filter cleaning.

### 45. Sprinkler installation

Measure the lawn and identify walls and walkways that should not be sprayed. Head spacing must reflect actual pressure, throw and overlapping coverage; specify sprinkler models, valves and burial depth. Compare proposals on pressure testing, arc adjustment and ground reinstatement after trenching. Run each zone at handover and check for dry patches and overspray onto paths.

### 46. Irrigation tanks and pumps

Establish irrigated area, estimated demand, refill source and tank location before sizing equipment. Review tank capacity, support base, maintenance access, pump curve, required pressure and dry-run protection. Request a connection schematic for suction, discharge, level controls, valves and electrical supply, including appropriate backflow protection. Commission filling, automatic cut-off, pressure, leakage and vibration.

### 47. Irrigation network maintenance

Symptoms may include dry patches, pooling or unexplained consumption. Identify affected zones and photograph heads, valves and controller; request systematic checks of pressure, flow, filters and joints before replacing components. Separate diagnostic charges from parts, excavation and repair. After service, run every zone and document replaced items and controller settings.

### 48. Automatic irrigation controller programming

Programming should reflect plant types, seasonal needs, soil and each zone's output rather than identical runtimes. Supply zone information and garden-use times; confirm any rain or moisture sensors and remote connectivity. Ask for a documented schedule, manual override and fault alerts, verifying app compatibility by controller model. Test each programme and demonstrate changes to the operator.

### 49. Irrigation leak detection

When pressure drops or the meter moves with irrigation supposedly off, record timing, affected zones and wet spots. Diagnosis should isolate zones, check meter and valves and, where appropriate, pressure-test or trace buried lines before targeted excavation. Request findings identifying the likely location, confidence and whether repair is included. Retest under operating pressure and reinstate disturbed ground.

## Shade structures and external works

### 50. Car parking shade installation

Measure bays, vehicle dimensions, door-opening clearance and entry levels, and photograph the slab and utilities. Request a design showing columns, foundations, clear height, membrane or panels, drainage and engineer-assessed wind resistance. Compare corrosion protection, finishes, tensioning, fixings and warranty scope. Check clearance, anchorage, drainage fall and connections at handover.

### 51. Pergola construction

Clarify whether the pergola provides shade, supports climbing plants or covers seating, with dimensions and fixing locations. Timber, aluminium and steel have different treatment and maintenance needs. Request foundation and building-connection details, shading, drainage and lighting interfaces; do not add loads without design review. Inspect alignment, stability, edge finishes and protection at acceptance.

### 52. Outdoor seating area construction

Start with occupancy, furniture dimensions, circulation and privacy, supported by site photographs and sun exposure. Define whether the scope includes paving, built-in benches, shade, electrical or water services. Compare weather-suitable materials, cleanability, levels and rainwater drainage rather than renders alone. Test lighting and drainage and inspect edges and clearances on completion.

### 53. External fence installation

A fence may serve privacy, boundary definition or security; specify its purpose, length, height and gate locations. Verify plot boundaries, buried services and levels before footing excavation. Request post, infill, corrosion protection, fixing and appropriate movement-joint details, pricing gates separately. Check line, post stability and smooth gate operation at handover.

### 54. Garden pathway construction

Mark the walking route, width, turns and any trolley access, then survey falls and ponding points. Stone, pavers and gravel need different bases, edge restraints and drainage. Request slip-appropriate surface samples, layer build-up and finished levels at door thresholds. Acceptance should check evenness, restrained edges and drainage away from buildings.

### 55. Interlocking paver installation

Provide area dimensions and distinguish pedestrian use from vehicle parking because loads affect base construction and paver thickness. Specify excavation, subbase compaction, falls, edge restraints and joint sand, with an approved colour and laying pattern. Compare layer depths, compaction checks and existing utility treatment rather than unit price alone. Inspect settlement, edge stability and water runoff.

### 56. Waterfall and fountain construction

Define the feature's dimensions, basin position and desired flow height, with electrical, water and maintenance access. Design should address basin volume, pump and filtration capacity, waterproofing and splash control near walls or paths. Request service schematics, safe electrical provisions and a water-cleaning plan. Run a full operating cycle at handover, checking leaks, noise, drainage and filter access.
## Preparing a quotation request

Biet Alreef customer service can receive an enquiry even when no company listing is published. Include your name, contact number, area (Al Rahba), service, property type, dimensions or available drawings, site photos, preferred survey date and required materials or works. Send these details through the approved customer-service channel without assuming a price or contractor availability. Confirm project-specific permits and licensing with the competent authorities before work; a service listing is not an approval.
