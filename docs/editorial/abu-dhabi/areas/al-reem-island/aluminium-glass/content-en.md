# Metal, aluminium, glazing and joinery workshops — Al Reem Island

This company-service editorial draft does not confirm a provider or appointment availability. Survey actual dimensions and check building-specific access and working arrangements before fabrication or installation. Individual tradespeople are listed separately and no provider approval is implied.

## Wooden kitchens, wardrobes and doors

### 1. Wooden kitchen design

Begin with a layout showing the sink, hob, refrigerator and doors. Survey finished wall dimensions and the plumbing, extraction and electrical points. Request plans and elevations showing cabinet storage, appliance openings and circulation clearances; specify board grades, edge sealing and moisture protection. Compare design fees, revisions and whether fabrication drawings are included. Approve only after checking clashes with services.

### 2. Kitchen fabrication

Fabrication should follow approved shop drawings and actual appliance dimensions, not a reference photo alone. Specify board thickness, surface finish, hinges, runners and sealed edges near the sink. Compare quotations by cabinet and drawer counts and hardware specifications; inspect a finish sample and check dimensions and squareness before dispatch.

### 3. Kitchen installation

Before installation, confirm finished floors and walls are dry and plumbing and electrical points are ready. Record cabinet levels, wall construction and suitable fixings for overhead units; protect existing finishes. Clarify whether trimming, sealing and appliance connections are included. At handover, check door alignment, drawer movement, anchorage and perimeter gaps.

### 4. Wardrobe design

Measure the wall, ceiling height, skirtings, switches and AC outlets; decide between hinged and sliding doors. Arrange hanging space, drawers and shelves around actual storage needs, allowing sufficient depth and door clearance. Compare drawing deliverables, revisions, lighting and ventilation details. Approve only when doors operate without obstructing services.

### 5. Wardrobe fabrication

Provide approved dimensions for every module, the finish, board type and locations of drawers and hinges. Specify back-panel and shelf thicknesses, support for long spans and edge treatment. Compare sliding-door hardware, accessories and lighting as separate items. Check finish samples, joint quality and component dimensions against the cutting schedule.

### 6. Walk-in dressing room fit-out

Plan circulation so drawers open and shelves remain accessible without blocking the passage. Survey room dimensions, lighting, ventilation and mirror locations, and specify open and closed storage and hanging heights. Compare whether flooring, mirrors, doors and lighting are included. Check illumination, cabinet stability, drawer travel and exposed sharp edges at handover.

### 7. Kitchen and wardrobe repair or refurbishment

Identify the fault before specifying replacement: swollen boards, loose hinges, failed runners or worn fronts. Photograph affected areas, measure components and identify existing materials. Compare targeted repairs, unit replacement and refacing, including limits of colour matching. Test repaired doors and drawers and inspect the finished edges.

### 8. Wooden door fabrication

Measure the finished opening, swing direction and whether the door is internal or external. Specify the door core, veneer or paint, edge construction, hinge and lock preparation, accounting for exposure and use. Compare prices with and without frames and hardware. Inspect leaf flatness, corners and finish before delivery.

### 9. Door installation

Check opening dimensions, frame plumb and final floor level before hanging the leaf, particularly after flooring changes. Specify fixings, clearances, latch position and doorstop. Clarify removal of old doors and making-good of plaster or paint. Test swing, latching and floor clearance at handover.

### 10. Fire-rated door fabrication

This is a life-safety product: a wooden door cannot be described as fire-rated merely by its material. Establish the required rating from the project design and competent authority, and request evidence for the complete tested doorset, including leaf, frame, seals, hinges and self-closing hardware. Do not approve unverified changes to dimensions or components; require competent installation and documentation before acceptance.

### 11. Door frame fabrication and fitting

Measure the finished wall thickness, opening width and height before making the frame. Select frame material, finish and fixing method to suit the door weight, including hinge and threshold positions. Compare trim, packing and sealing allowances. Check plumb, secure fixing and even gaps around the installed leaf.

### 12. Wooden partition installation

Define whether the partition provides visual privacy, space division or shelving, since structural support differs. Supply length, height, floor and ceiling interfaces and concealed service locations; specify slat spacing and finish. Compare framing, finishing and optional lighting separately. Inspect rigidity, alignment and safe finished edges.

### 13. Door maintenance

Diagnose binding or poor latching before replacing parts: hinge sag, leaf warp, moisture movement or frame displacement. Send photographs and a short video of the door in motion, with edge-clearance measurements. Price adjustments, hardware and replacement components separately. Test hinges and locks afterward; fire doors require preservation of their certified configuration.

### 14. Wood painting and refinishing

Inspect the existing coating and whether the substrate is solid timber or veneer before sanding or stripping. Quantify surfaces and doors, specify colour and sheen, and protect hardware and flooring. Compare preparation, primer, coat count and curing time; approve a sample. Inspect consistency, runs and sticking surfaces after reassembly.


## Wooden furniture and interior joinery

### 15. Custom furniture fabrication

Define the item's use and location before choosing its appearance: a dining table and a storage bench have different load and cleaning requirements. Provide site dimensions, openings and delivery routes. Request drawings showing thicknesses, structure, fixings and finish. Compare material, hardware, warranty, delivery and fitting, then check stability, surface level and dimensions at handover.

### 16. TV unit fabrication

Measure screen width, viewing height, sockets and router position; decide whether the unit is floor-standing or wall-hung. Drawings should show device ventilation, cable routes and fixings suited to the actual wall. Compare drawers, hinges and finishes. At handover, check load support, door operation and access to connections.

### 17. Storage unit fabrication

List stored items and their weights; photograph the wall, skirtings and switches and measure the space. Request internal compartments, suitably supported shelves and secure anchorage. Tall units need an appropriate anti-tip solution. Compare board thickness, runner capacity and maintainability, then inspect alignment, doors and anchorage.

### 18. Timber wall cladding

Check wall moisture, flatness and concealed electrical or AC services before covering it. Measure the net area including openings, and specify backing, movement joints and removable access where necessary. Compare veneer or panel type, edge protection and moisture suitability. Inspect consistent joints, colour and freedom from bulging.

### 19. CNC decorative woodwork

Provide approved vector artwork or a dimensioned pattern showing board thickness and narrow cut-outs. Clarify whether the panel is purely decorative, a partition or load-bearing. Approve a cut sample to inspect edge quality and detail before batch fabrication. Compare material, finishing, fixing and waste allowances; reject sharp or fractured edges.

### 20. Counter fabrication

Define whether the counter is for reception, service or retail, then measure circulation, standing space, devices and utility points. Request elevations showing working height, storage, corner protection and floor junctions. Compare framework, wear-resistant tops, electrical provisions and installation. Check stability, cleanability and staff access to equipment.

### 21. Retail and office furniture fabrication

Start with a layout showing staff and customer circulation, desks, shelves, power and network outlets. Define display or storage loads, scratch resistance and whether units may be reconfigured. Compare repeated-unit and bespoke pricing, lead time and out-of-hours installation. Check shelving stability, clearances and cable management.

### 22. Interior space design

Survey room dimensions, columns, doors, windows and AC outlets before approving a layout. Request measurable furniture, lighting, material and construction drawings, separating design fees from fabrication and installation. Compare alternative concepts, revision allowances and final deliverables. Check circulation, maintenance access and site compatibility.

### 23. Woodwork fabrication and installation

Compile a component schedule with approved shop drawings and dimensions, distinguishing workshop fabrication from site adjustments. Review anchorage, concealed services and protection of existing floors. Compare fabrication, finishing, delivery, fitting and alterations as separate quotation items. Inspect dimensions, joints, gaps and finish for each installed component.

### 24. Majlis fit-out

Determine seating capacity, furniture layout and entry routes before selecting timber panels and finishes. Photograph walls and ceilings and locate lighting, AC and sockets. Price fixed joinery separately from upholstery and loose furnishings. Compare materials, cleanability and repair access, then inspect circulation, anchorage and edge finishing.

### 25. Bedroom fit-out

Measure bed position, wardrobe opening clearance, drawer travel, sockets and bedside lighting. Request detailed drawings for the bed, headboard, side units and wardrobes, including anchorage of tall components. Compare board, coating, hardware and optional lighting. Test bed rigidity, noise and drawer movement at handover.

### 26. Office and shop fit-out

List fixed counters, partitions and shelving separately from electrical, data and signage works. Record site dimensions, permitted work hours and delivery access, then request a phased installation plan to limit disruption. Compare protection, final cleaning and making-good. Inspect doors, drawers, fixtures and a recorded defects list.

### 27. Decorative timber installations

Choose details for the installation's function and location, not from a photograph alone: ceiling slats and a TV feature wall have different fixing and access needs. Specify dimensions, grain direction, colour and lighting interfaces; approve material and joint samples. Compare concealed supports, edge treatment and maintenance access, then inspect line alignment and finish.

### 28. Interior finishing and handover

Prepare a room-by-room snag list covering joinery, coatings, edges, doors and affected services. Request photographs of concealed work, updated drawings, care instructions and agreed warranties. Clarify final cleaning, remedial work and contractual exclusions. Do not sign off before correcting scratches, irregular joints and faulty moving parts.


## Aluminium doors and windows

### 29. Aluminium window fabrication

Measure finished openings, opening direction, sill height and exposure to wind and rain. Request profiles, glazing thickness, drainage, gaskets and locking details; compare documented performance rather than colour alone. Check frame dimensions and corner joints before dispatch.

### 30. Aluminium window installation

Check opening alignment and wall substrate before choosing anchors and sealants, and plan safe facade access. Compare removal, perimeter making-good and weather sealing. Test opening, locking and drainage, and inspect the perimeter for leakage using a suitable method.

### 31. Aluminium door fabrication

Define internal or external use, traffic and clear passage width, then record dimensions and handing. Request profile, hinge, lock, threshold and glazing details. Compare durability, finish and warranty, and inspect leaf alignment and joints.

### 32. Aluminium door installation

Confirm finished floor level, swing clearance and suitable wall anchorage while protecting adjacent finishes. Clarify whether frame, hardware, sealing and making-good are included. Check balance, repeated closing, handle security and floor clearance.

### 33. Mesh screen installation

Clarify whether the screen is for insects, security or ventilation because materials and fixings differ. Measure each opening and plan removable cleaning access without obstructing window operation. Compare frame, mesh and latch, then check tension, edges and gaps.

### 34. Door and window maintenance

Photograph faults and identify water ingress, difficult operation, failed gaskets or sagging leaves. Diagnose frames, hinges and drainage before replacing the entire assembly. Compare parts, labour and adjustment, and retest operation and sealing after repair.

### 35. Hardware and glass replacement

Provide photographs and dimensions of existing components and system references where available. Specify glass type and breakage risk; do not substitute non-equivalent safety glazing without technical review. Compare supply, fitting and safe disposal, then inspect fit, edges and operation.

### 36. Aluminium kitchen fabrication

Survey appliances, sink, plumbing and electrical positions before approving cabinet layout and working heights. Specify profiles, panels, edges, hinges and shelf load capacity. Compare drawer counts, worktops and finishes; inspect cuts and joints before delivery.

### 37. Aluminium kitchen installation

Check finished floors, walls, service positions and overhead cabinet anchorage suitability. Clarify cut-outs, adjustments, sink sealing and appliance connections in the quote. Inspect level worktops, secure cabinets, drawer travel and appliance clearances.

### 38. Aluminium cabinet fabrication

Define location, moisture exposure and shelf loads; provide dimensions and surrounding openings. Request drawings for doors, dividers, profiles, hardware and finish. Compare component thickness, reinforcement and locking; inspect shelf stability and door alignment.

### 39. Bathroom cabinet fabrication

Measure the basin, pipework and door clearance, and establish direct water exposure. Specify edges, countertop, service cut-outs and access to isolation valves. Compare moisture-suitable materials and anchorage, then inspect edges and areas where water could collect.

### 40. Outdoor storage unit fabrication

Define stored items and exposure to sun, rain and wind, and measure door swing clearance. Request drainage, ventilation, anchorage and outdoor finish details. Compare maintenance, warranty and material suitability; inspect stability, closure and water shedding.

### 41. Aluminium kitchen refurbishment

Identify whether the problem lies in fronts, hinges, worktops or damaged profiles and record existing system dimensions. Compare refacing and hardware replacement against full reconstruction, including colour-matching limitations. Check fixing and operation after refurbishment.

### 42. Kitchen hinge and hardware maintenance

Photograph faulty hinges and runners and record dimensions and any part references. Request door adjustment before deciding which components need replacement, checking replacement compatibility with existing holes. Compare labour, parts and warranty, and cycle each door and drawer repeatedly.


## Glazing and glass facades

### 43. Glass facade installation

Facade work requires engineering for wind loads, building movement, drainage and structural support, not area measurements alone. Request system drawings, profiles, glazing, fixings and movement joints reviewed by qualified specialists. Compare performance testing, maintenance and warranties; verify anchors, seals and project documentation at handover.

### 44. Tempered glass installation

Establish panel location, purpose and design thickness, then survey openings and fixing points before fabrication: tempered glass cannot be cut after toughening. Compare product documentation, edges, holes and hardware. Check scratches, metal contact and safe handling during installation.

### 45. Shopfront fabrication and installation

Record entrance width, customer flow, signage, doors and any escape requirements in the approved shop design. Specify glass, frames, thresholds, locks and rainwater management. Compare supply, fitting and protection during works; test doors and inspect alignment and display visibility.

### 46. Glass door installation

Measure opening, height, clearance and swing, and establish leaf weight, hinges or pivots and closing mechanism. Specify safety glazing appropriate to the system and visibility markings where needed. Compare hardware and maintenance provisions; test safe closing and collision clearance.

### 47. Glass partition installation

Define privacy, acoustic performance, circulation and concealed floor or ceiling services. Request drawings showing profiles, doors, glazing and anchorage. Compare performance, finish and removability; check partition alignment, stability and joint sealing.

### 48. Glass balustrade installation

This is a fall-protection element requiring an engineered design for the relevant loads and anchorage to a suitable slab or structure. Record height, dimensions, glass specification, edges and hardware; do not accept an unengineered decorative solution. Inspect design records, anchors and stability before handover.

### 49. Glass facade maintenance

Photograph leaks, breakage or failed gaskets from a safe position, identifying access height and pedestrian protection needs. Diagnose support and drainage systems before replacing panels. Compare sealants, compatible components and access plans; verify weather sealing after repair and record inspections.

## Steel and metal fabrication

### 50. Steel door fabrication

Measure the opening, handing and use, and establish leaf weight, frame, hinge and lock positions. Request sections, weld details, corrosion protection and finish. Compare fitting, hardware and delivery; inspect alignment, operation and weld quality before final coating.

### 51. Metal balustrade fabrication

Define the guard's location, safety function, height and anchorage to stairs or slab. Request drawings for design loads, sections, openings and finish, with engineering review as required. Compare anchors and corrosion protection; inspect stability, edges and fixings.

### 52. Canopy fabrication

Record dimensions, vehicle and pedestrian paths, drainage direction and fixing locations. Require structural consideration of wind, loads, framing and covering rather than selecting by dimensions alone. Compare foundations, coatings, drainage and warranty; inspect slope, anchors and fabric or panel tension.

### 53. Steel structure installation

Use approved structural drawings showing loads, members, connections, anchors and erection sequence. Identify lifting access and exclusion zones before pricing. Compare fabrication, inspection, corrosion protection and erection; verify drawings, welding records and bolted connections.

### 54. Stainless-steel fabrication

Select steel grade and finish for intended use, moisture exposure and cleaning agents. Provide dimensions for rails, counters or cladding, including edges and joints. Compare metal thickness, welding and polishing; inspect stains, scratches and joint consistency.

### 55. Welding services

Identify metal, thickness, joint purpose and whether the connection is structural; a light repair differs from structural welding. Request suitable procedures, qualifications where needed and spark and fire controls. Compare preparation, inspection and recoating; check visible defects and required test records.

### 56. Metalwork maintenance

Diagnose corrosion, cracked welds, loose anchors or binding gates before prescribing repairs. Photograph and measure damaged parts, and specify root-cause treatment and corrosion protection. Compare repair against replacement and warranty limits; inspect joint integrity and operation after work.
