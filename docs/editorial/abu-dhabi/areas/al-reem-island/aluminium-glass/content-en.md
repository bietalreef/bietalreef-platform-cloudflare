## Wooden kitchens, wardrobes and doors

### 1. Wooden kitchen design

Begin with a layout showing the sink, hob, refrigerator and doors. Survey finished wall dimensions and the plumbing, extraction and electrical points. Request plans and elevations showing cabinet storage, appliance openings and circulation clearances; specify board grades, edge sealing and moisture protection. Compare design fees, revisions and whether fabrication drawings are included. Approve only after checking clashes with services.

### 2. Kitchen fabrication

Fabrication should follow approved shop drawings and actual appliance dimensions, not a reference photo alone. Specify board thickness, surface finish, hinges, runners and sealed edges near the sink. Compare quotations by cabinet and drawer counts and hardware specifications; inspect a finish sample and check dimensions and squareness before dispatch.

### 3. Kitchen installation

Before installation, confirm finished floors and walls are dry and plumbing and electrical points are ready. Record cabinet levels, wall construction and suitable fixings for overhead units; protect existing finishes. Clarify whether trimming, sealing and appliance connections are included. At handover, check door alignment, drawer movement, anchorage and perimeter gaps.

### 4. Wardrobe design

Measure the wall, ceiling height, skirtings, switches and AC outlets; decide between hinged and sliding doors. Arrange hanging space, drawers and shelves around actual storage needs, allowing sufficient depth and door clearance. Compare drawing deliverables, revisions, lighting and ventilation details. Approve only when doors operate without obstructing services.

### 5. Wardrobe fabrication

Provide approved dimensions for every module, the finish, board type and locations of drawers and hinges. Specify back-panel and shelf thicknesses, support for long spans and edge treatment. Compare sliding-door hardware, accessories and lighting as separate items. Check finish samples, joint quality and component dimensions against the cutting schedule.

### 6. Walk-in dressing room fit-out

Plan circulation so drawers open and shelves remain accessible without blocking the passage. Survey room dimensions, lighting, ventilation and mirror locations, and specify open and closed storage and hanging heights. Compare whether flooring, mirrors, doors and lighting are included. Check illumination, cabinet stability, drawer travel and exposed sharp edges at handover.

### 7. Kitchen and wardrobe repair or refurbishment

Identify the fault before specifying replacement: swollen boards, loose hinges, failed runners or worn fronts. Photograph affected areas, measure components and identify existing materials. Compare targeted repairs, unit replacement and refacing, including limits of colour matching. Test repaired doors and drawers and inspect the finished edges.

### 8. Wooden door fabrication

Measure the finished opening, swing direction and whether the door is internal or external. Specify the door core, veneer or paint, edge construction, hinge and lock preparation, accounting for exposure and use. Compare prices with and without frames and hardware. Inspect leaf flatness, corners and finish before delivery.

### 9. Door installation

Check opening dimensions, frame plumb and final floor level before hanging the leaf, particularly after flooring changes. Specify fixings, clearances, latch position and doorstop. Clarify removal of old doors and making-good of plaster or paint. Test swing, latching and floor clearance at handover.

### 10. Fire-rated door fabrication

This is a life-safety product: a wooden door cannot be described as fire-rated merely by its material. Establish the required rating from the project design and competent authority, and request evidence for the complete tested doorset, including leaf, frame, seals, hinges and self-closing hardware. Do not approve unverified changes to dimensions or components; require competent installation and documentation before acceptance.

### 11. Door frame fabrication and fitting

Measure the finished wall thickness, opening width and height before making the frame. Select frame material, finish and fixing method to suit the door weight, including hinge and threshold positions. Compare trim, packing and sealing allowances. Check plumb, secure fixing and even gaps around the installed leaf.

### 12. Wooden partition installation

Define whether the partition provides visual privacy, space division or shelving, since structural support differs. Supply length, height, floor and ceiling interfaces and concealed service locations; specify slat spacing and finish. Compare framing, finishing and optional lighting separately. Inspect rigidity, alignment and safe finished edges.

### 13. Door maintenance

Diagnose binding or poor latching before replacing parts: hinge sag, leaf warp, moisture movement or frame displacement. Send photographs and a short video of the door in motion, with edge-clearance measurements. Price adjustments, hardware and replacement components separately. Test hinges and locks afterward; fire doors require preservation of their certified configuration.

### 14. Wood painting and refinishing

Inspect the existing coating and whether the substrate is solid timber or veneer before sanding or stripping. Quantify surfaces and doors, specify colour and sheen, and protect hardware and flooring. Compare preparation, primer, coat count and curing time; approve a sample. Inspect consistency, runs and sticking surfaces after reassembly.
