# Building materials and supply on Al Reem Island

This editorial guide helps customers prepare material enquiries on Al Reem Island; it does not confirm inventory or endorse a supplier. Provide a quantity schedule and project specifications, and confirm actual building access and unloading arrangements with site management rather than assuming one rule applies everywhere. Supply does not include installation unless explicitly agreed.

## Building and finishing materials

> The source taxonomy marks this specialty as currently not selectable. Editorial information does not change that status or certify a provider.

### 1. Cement supply

Identify whether the cement is intended for structural concrete, mortar or plaster and provide the project specification rather than ordering a generic grade. Quantify bags or tonnes against the work schedule, arrange dry storage, compare unit price and transport, and inspect manufacturing dates, packaging and delivered counts.

### 2. Sand supply

Plastering sand, fill sand and mixing sand have different grading and cleanliness requirements. State the use and cubic metres, photograph the unloading point and clarify how the load is measured and sourced. Inspect for visible contamination and reconcile the delivery ticket with the agreed volume.

### 3. Aggregate supply

Match aggregate size and grading to the concrete mix design or sub-base requirement; do not substitute sizes without technical approval. Provide quantities and truck access, request a sample when useful, compare grading, cleanliness and freight, and check the delivered load against the order.

### 4. Block and masonry unit supply

Provide wall thicknesses, opening dimensions and the specified masonry unit, including insulation or strength requirements where relevant. Calculate units with breakage allowance and clarify pallet and delivery charges. Inspect dimensions, corners and cracks before distributing stock around the site.

### 5. Reinforcing steel supply

Use the structural drawings and bar-bending schedule to define diameters, lengths, grades and weights. Clarify cutting, bending and unloading charges, review material certificates and bundle tags, and do not accept a grade substitution merely because it is in stock.

### 6. Ready-mix concrete supply

Order against the engineer's specified concrete grade, workability, pumping method, pour volume and schedule. Confirm formwork, labour and access before dispatch. Check batch tickets, arrival times, quantities and the agreed sampling and testing procedure at handover.

### 7. Project-wide building materials supply

Build a single schedule listing item, code, specification, unit, quantity and required date, highlighting special storage needs. Compare suppliers against the identical schedule, including substitutions, transport and returns. Record shortages and damage on each staged delivery.

### 8. Gypsum materials supply

Distinguish gypsum boards, plaster, framing channels, screws and jointing compounds. Specify board dimensions, thickness and suitability for the room or project requirement, allowing for cuts around openings. Inspect flatness, edges and dry storage on receipt.

### 9. Paint materials supply

A colour name alone is insufficient: specify substrate, primer, filler, topcoat system, sheen and area. Compare coverage rates, product compatibility, colour codes and batch numbers, pricing the complete coating system. Inspect sealed containers and dates upon delivery.

### 10. Waterproofing and insulation materials supply

Identify whether the material is for a roof, bathroom, tank or wall, and whether the objective is waterproofing or thermal performance. Request the full system data, including primers, joints and protection, and quantify area plus waste. Compare application-dependent warranties and inspect packaging and storage.

### 11. Flooring materials supply

Provide room plans, surface areas, intended use and moisture exposure, then specify format, colour and slip resistance where relevant. Separate cutting allowance, skirting, adhesive and underlay in the quantities. Check batch consistency and damage before installation.

### 12. Sanitary fixture supply

Attach the bathroom plan, water and waste connection points and clearances around the basin, WC and shower. Request model numbers, connection dimensions, finishes and accessories. Compare warranties and spares, and inspect ceramics, glazing and mixers before acceptance.

### 13. Electrical installation supplies

Derive cable, breaker, switch and conduit quantities from approved electrical drawings and load schedules. Specify sizes, ratings, counts and accessory compatibility; do not substitute protective devices or cables without qualified review. Match labels, documentation and quantities at delivery.

### 14. Plumbing installation supplies

Compile pipes, fittings, valves and insulation from water and drainage drawings, specifying diameters, operating pressure and joint type. Compare whole-system compatibility and fitting costs rather than pipe price alone. Check sizes, threads and seals before installation.

### 15. Retail building materials

For smaller purchases, identify the task, item, quantity and an image of the required pack or dimensions, especially when matching previous work. Clarify returns for unopened surplus and delivery charges. Verify model and specification against the receipt before collection.

### 16. Retail sanitary ware

Measure the installation opening, connection spacing and waste arrangement before choosing a basin, mixer or WC. Do not rely on a product photograph alone; request dimensional drawings and included components, inspect finish and damage, and retain the model number for warranty support.

### 17. Retail electrical accessories

For a replacement switch, socket or luminaire, photograph the old item and rating plate and identify the mounting system, voltage and rating. Confirm compatibility with the installer rather than relying on appearance. Inspect markings and packaging before use.

### 18. Retail plumbing parts

When replacing a valve, fitting or trap, bring a photograph and measured diameter and thread or connection type. Check material suitability for the intended water system, include required washers and seals, and verify part matching before starting the repair.

### 19. Retail paint

For touch-ups or repainting, retain the colour code, product line and sheen and describe the surface area and existing coating. Ageing and batch variation can affect matching; test a sample before purchasing the full quantity and check storage and shelf life.

### 20. Retail insulation products

For a localised repair, photograph the leak area and identify the substrate and existing waterproofing system if known. Seek compatible materials plus preparation and protection accessories. A single coating is not a reliable remedy for an undiagnosed leak.

### 21. Delivery of materials to site

Give the supplier the site address, access point, vehicle constraints, unloading method, receiving contact and suitable delivery window. Separate transport, lifting and waiting charges from material costs. Photograph damage and record quantities and arrival time before signing receipt.

## Timber and boards

### 22. MDF board supply

Specify board thickness, density and finish, including whether the installation is in a dry interior or exposed to moisture. Send a cutting schedule and lift or access dimensions; compare board, cutting and edge-finishing charges, and inspect swelling and flatness.

### 23. Solid timber supply

Identify timber species, grade, sections, lengths and whether it is for doors, furniture or cladding. Ask about seasoning and suitable moisture content and the intended finish; inspect warping, splits and visible knots, and compare realistic cutting waste.

### 24. Plywood supply

Specify plywood grade, thickness, bonding suitability and exposure conditions; not every sheet is suitable outdoors. Provide part dimensions and a cutting list. Inspect veneer layering, edge voids, bowing and transport damage.

### 25. Melamine-faced board supply

Select the surface colour, texture, batch, board thickness and core for the cabinetry. Request a physical sample because screen colours vary; specify grain direction and matching edge-band lengths. Check batch consistency and scratches before cutting.

### 26. HPL sheet supply

Specify laminate thickness, surface texture, wear requirements and whether the quote covers sheets alone or the backing substrate. Check colour samples, pattern direction and adhesive compatibility; inspect corner chips and product codes on receipt.

### 27. Joinery hardware supply

Provide cabinet drawings, door and drawer counts and approximate weights to select hinges, runners, handles and mechanisms. Compare load ratings, warranties and replacement parts, and test a fitted sample before ordering the full quantity.

### 28. Board cutting and preparation

Supply a cutting schedule with final dimensions, quantities, grain direction, part labels and visible edges. Agree saw tolerances, drilling, edge-banding, packaging and delivery. Measure sample pieces from each size and inspect labels and edge quality.

## Marble and granite

### 29. Natural marble supply

Specify stone variety, origin, colour range, slab thickness and finish; natural veining differs between slabs. Send area drawings and openings, and establish whether slab selection, transport and cutting are included. Inspect actual slabs and record visible cracks or resin repairs before acceptance.

### 30. Granite supply

State whether granite is for flooring, stairs or counters, together with the finish, slip considerations, thickness and dimensions. Request photographs of the actual slabs rather than catalogue samples alone. Compare cutting, freight and waste costs, then inspect colour, edge chips and piece references.

### 31. Custom marble cutting

Provide a numbered cutting schedule showing length, width, thickness, vein direction, edge profiles and fixing holes. Agree tolerances and joint locations before fabrication; site measurements may differ from drawings. Check finished pieces against a template or accurate dimensions before dispatch.

### 32. Marble grinding and polishing

Identify stone type, scratches, staining and lippage, with photographs under side lighting. Ask for grinding stages, abrasive grades and protection for skirting and furniture, and request a small trial. Inspect consistent sheen and absence of waves or chemical damage.

### 33. Marble floor installation

Supply the tile layout, joints, finished levels and vein direction, and describe the substrate. Compare quotations including surface preparation, bedding materials, cuts and post-installation protection rather than labour per square metre alone. Check flatness, adhesion and joint filling.

### 34. Marble wall and facade cladding

Define cladding height, slab sizes, substrate and proposed fixing method. Anchors and adhesives need a suitable design for loads and exposure. Request corner, joint and opening details and an access plan; inspect fixing security, alignment and finished edges.

### 35. Marble repair and treatment

Identify the cause of stains, cracks or dullness before choosing a treatment; acidic products can damage calcite-based stone. Document defects and affected areas, and clarify the limits of repair and possible colour differences. Approve a test patch and obtain aftercare instructions.

## Kitchen and vanity worktops

### 36. Kitchen worktop fabrication

Measure cabinets after installation and mark sink, hob, joints and overhangs. Specify material, thickness and support at exposed edges, and approve shop drawings before cutting. Compare edge profiling, cut-outs and transport separately, then verify dimensions before fitting.

### 37. Kitchen worktop installation

Check cabinet levelling and fixing before lifting heavy slabs. Agree joint positions, compatible sealant, supports and movement clearance around appliances. Inspect stability, flush joints and sealing around the sink; avoid loading joints before curing.

### 38. Marble vanity fabrication

Specify integrated or countertop basin arrangement, tap and waste positions, and drainage falls. Supply a site template and images of the wall and base, and request corner reinforcement and edge details. Check cut quality and hole positions against the selected fixtures.

### 39. Sink and appliance cut-outs

Use manufacturer templates for sinks and hobs, not rough external measurements, including fixing and ventilation clearances. Agree corner radii, edge distances and reinforcement of weakened sections. Test-fit before permanently fixing appliances or bonding the worktop.

### 40. Quartz worktop installation

Identify the engineered quartz product, thickness and its manufacturer-stated heat and sunlight limitations, particularly near hobs and windows. Compare seam count, support, adhesives and edge detailing. Check pattern alignment, flush joints and sink perimeter sealing.

### 41. Porcelain slab worktop installation

Large-format porcelain needs suitable lifting and cutting equipment to avoid chipped corners. Provide surface dimensions and openings, slab thickness and support details. Agree edge and joint treatments and inspect hairline fractures and cut accuracy before acceptance.

### 42. Worktop repair and repolishing

First establish whether the surface is natural marble, engineered quartz or porcelain: repair methods differ significantly. Photograph chips, stains and heat marks, and test treatments discreetly. Do not mechanically polish products whose manufacturers prohibit it; assess results under working light.

## Ceramic and porcelain tiles

### 43. Ceramic tile supply

Specify wall or floor use, size, finish and suitable slip performance. Quantify area plus cutting waste and identify grout colour and skirting if needed. Request batch and grade references, and inspect matching cartons and corner damage on receipt.

### 44. Porcelain tile supply

Define tile thickness, size, water absorption and appropriate finish, accounting for the handling weight of large formats. Compare supply price with transport, cutting and wastage. Check batch and shade codes, sample flatness and edge condition.

### 45. Outdoor paving tile supply

Describe use, drainage falls and pedestrian or vehicle loading, then select a finish and strength suitable for the design. Compare wet samples where practical, and define thickness, laying system and quantities. Inspect dimensional consistency, breakage and colour against the approved sample.

### 46. Tile cutting and preparation

Provide a layout marking cuts around drains, columns and corners, with final site dimensions. Specify tile type, thickness and cutting equipment, distinguishing exposed edges from concealed cuts. Inspect edge straightness and fine cracking around openings.

### 47. Ceramic tile installation

Check substrate flatness, moisture and waterproofing where needed before tiling. Define layout direction, joint width and bathroom floor falls to drains. Compare quotes including preparation, adhesive and grout; inspect alignment, hollow-sounding tiles and drainage.

### 48. Porcelain tile installation

Large tiles require careful substrate levelling, adhesive coverage, handling and joints; labour price alone is not a sufficient comparison. Request a setting-out plan showing start lines, cuts and room transitions. Inspect edge lippage and secure bedding before handover.

### 49. Skirting tile supply and installation

Specify skirting height, material, colour and actual wall lengths, including corners and door terminations. Agree whether pieces are cut from floor tiles or purpose-made, and how joints and wall interfaces are finished. Inspect consistent height, fixing and clean edges.
