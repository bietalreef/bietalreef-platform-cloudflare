# Cleaning, disinfection and pest control on Al Reem Island

This is an editorial guide to company and establishment services; it does not confirm a published provider, appointment or price. Methods depend on property type, surfaces, site condition and actual access arrangements. Where a building requires advance coordination, confirm lift bookings, working windows and equipment routes with the relevant management. Individual tradespeople are listed separately from company services.

## Homes and buildings cleaning

### 1. Villa cleaning

State the number of floors, rooms and bathrooms, and whether outdoor areas are included. Explain whether the villa is occupied or vacant and whether the request is routine or deep cleaning. Send photographs of kitchens, bathrooms, glazing, floors and any stains or previous-work residue; identify delicate surfaces such as natural stone or timber. Request a quotation showing crew size, hours, products, equipment and whether windows, cabinet interiors or gardens are included. Compare exclusions and extra-work charges, then inspect rooms, surfaces and storage areas before accepting the job.

### 2. Apartment cleaning

An apartment enquiry needs more than total floor area. State bedrooms, bathrooms and balconies, furniture condition, floor level, lift access and where equipment can be loaded. Photograph the kitchen, bathrooms, glazing and floors, and clarify whether the service is routine, deep cleaning or pre-occupancy preparation. Compare cabinet and appliance interiors, windows, balconies and stain treatment, including products suitable for each surface. If building access requires coordination, confirm the procedure and time in advance. At completion, inspect corners, openings, balcony drains and any cleaning-product residue.

### 3. Office cleaning

Before pricing an office, count rooms, workstations, washrooms, pantry and reception areas, and identify occupied hours and suitable times for moving furniture. Provide a simple floor plan and photographs of surfaces, equipment and flooring. Distinguish routine cleaning from high-touch-point wiping, glass cleaning and carpet care. The quotation should state visit frequency, staff, products, consumables responsibility and waste handling while protecting documents and electronic equipment. Compare each visit's scope and checklist, then inspect shared areas, washrooms and the spaces around workstations.

### 4. Building cleaning

A multi-storey building needs a clear area schedule covering entrances, corridors, stairs, lifts, washrooms, parking and shared spaces. State whether façades are included or require a separate service. Provide floor count, approximate areas, floor types, peak periods, water and power points and waste collection locations. Request a staff and equipment plan, visit timetable and method for recording defects, with clear limits for lift interiors and high glazing. Compare coverage hours, materials, extra visits and complaint handling. Handover should include a walk-through of the listed areas and a record of outstanding items.

### 5. Post-construction cleaning

After construction or renovation, a generic cleaning request is not enough. Identify remaining residue such as gypsum dust, mortar, paint or protective film, and list newly installed surfaces. Photograph floors, glass, aluminium, cabinets and sanitary fittings, and state whether bulky construction waste has already been removed. Ask how each residue will be treated and which products are compatible with the finishes; require a small test area on delicate surfaces before wider use. Compare stages, crew, equipment and lift and corridor protection. At handover, inspect corners, joints, glazing and cabinets without allowing tools that could scratch new finishes.

### 6. Pre-move cleaning

This service prepares a property before furniture arrives or after it has been vacated. State the key handover date and the condition of kitchens, bathrooms, cabinets and windows, including adhesive residue or internal dust. Photograph each room and identify cabinet interiors, appliances and balconies that need attention. Compare inside and outside cleaning, feasible odour or stain treatment, crew size, duration and products. If key handover includes an inspection, leave time to review the work and revisit agreed items. Check drawers, cupboards, bathroom drains and glazing before belongings are moved in.

### 7. Periodic cleaning contracts

For a recurring contract, specify visits per week or month, the areas included in each visit and which tasks are daily, weekly or scheduled deep cleans. Attach a room and common-area schedule, access windows and clarify whether products, consumables and machines are supplied by the provider or client. The quotation should state staff numbers, coverage hours, supervisor, attendance records, extra-visit procedure and complaint resolution. Compare actual service levels, visit scope and variation or termination terms rather than total contract value alone. Review visit reports and checklists regularly and record work that was not completed.


## Upholstery and furnishings cleaning

### 8. Steam sofa cleaning

State the number and dimensions of sofa pieces and the fabric type if known. Photograph stains, worn areas and removable cushions. Ask whether steam is suitable for the fabric, filling and backing, and request a test on an inconspicuous area before treating the whole piece because some textiles, timber or adhesives may react to heat and moisture. The quotation should explain extraction, drying time, any period when the sofa must remain unused and stain-treatment limits. Compare method and protection for floors and wooden parts. Inspect colour, odour and residual moisture after drying rather than judging the surface while it is still wet.

### 9. Carpet cleaning

Identify carpet type, dimensions, fibre, pile and backing, and note stains, odours or loose edges. Photograph both face and underside and provide the care label if available. Clarify whether the carpet will be collected for off-site cleaning or treated in the property. Ask how dust is removed, which washing and drying method is used and how colour is protected, including whether a colourfastness test is available. Compare area-based pricing, fibre treatment and transport. Once dry, inspect edges, pile, colour and odour before returning the carpet to use.

### 10. Fitted carpet cleaning

Fitted carpet differs from a removable rug. State room and corridor areas, installation method and whether underlay is present, with photographs of stains and traffic lanes. Identify vacant periods, furniture movement and ventilation because residual moisture can delay reoccupation. Ask for the cleaning method, water or foam use, extraction equipment and drying time, and clarify that edges will not be lifted unless agreed. Compare area, machine type and process stages. At handover check that edges remain secure and colour is consistent, and confirm that walking routes are dry before furniture is replaced.

### 11. Arabic majlis cleaning

An Arabic majlis may contain seating, cushions, fabrics and decorative details with different care needs. Photograph the full seating area, upholstery, cushions and nearby curtains, and identify fixed and removable pieces. Describe stains and their likely source where known. Request a fabric-appropriate test before steam, washing or spot treatment is selected. Compare the number of seats and cushions, treatment of seams and decoration, drying time and furniture rearrangement. At handover inspect colour, stitching and moisture inside cushions, and confirm that each piece has been returned to its correct position.

### 12. Mattress cleaning

State mattress sizes and quantities, cover type and any stains, odours or moisture marks. Photograph the surface and identification label if available. Ask which cleaning method is suitable for the filling and internal layers and whether both sides and edges are included or only the visible surface. The quotation should explain extraction, drying and the waiting period before reuse, as well as limits on old stains. Compare mattress count, sizes, collection and drying arrangements. After full drying, check for trapped dampness, cleaning residue or visible changes to the cover.

### 13. Curtain cleaning

Count curtain panels and record their dimensions, fabric and lining. Clarify whether they must be removed from the track and reinstalled or can be cleaned in place. Photograph care labels, hooks and operating mechanisms, and identify blackout, layered or decorative fabrics. Ask how shrinkage, colourfastness and accumulated dust will be handled, and who is responsible for removal, labelling, transport, pressing and rehanging. Compare pricing per panel or metre while accounting for height and lining. After installation, check finished length, track movement, panel alignment and hook condition.

### 14. Upholstery disinfection

Identify the upholstery type, area, material and use, and clarify whether the request is cleaning followed by an approved surface product or a limited surface treatment only. Provide photographs and mention relevant sensitivities to odours or products where this affects method selection. Ask for the product name, label instructions, compatible surfaces, contact time, ventilation and safe return-to-use interval. Avoid accepting claims of “complete disinfection” without a defined product, method and limitations. Compare treatment coverage, drying and documentation, then check that no unsuitable residue or moisture remains.


## Façade and site cleaning

### 15. Glass façade cleaning

State façade height, area, divisions and glass type or coating if known. Provide ground-level and internal photographs showing staining, access points and obstructions. The quotation should explain the access method, protective equipment, water or cleaning product and protection for frames, sealants, entrances and pedestrians. Where building coordination is required, confirm access and work arrangements with management before setting a date. Compare crew size, stages, drying and water-mark removal rather than price per square metre alone. Inspect a sample area after cleaning to check for scratches, streaks or cleaning residue.

### 16. Building façade cleaning

Stone, aluminium, painted and composite façades need different methods. Identify the façade material, finish age, soil type and likely source, and provide both close-up and wide photographs. Request a small test patch to assess the effect of pressure, brushing or detergent on colour, joints and surface coating. The quotation should explain access equipment, wash-water collection and protection for windows, openings and signs. Compare cleaning method, coverage, frequency and excluded restoration work. At handover, check colour consistency, joint and paint condition and confirm that water has not entered the building.

### 17. Parking-area cleaning

Specify the number of levels, parking area and floor material, and identify oil stains, tyre dust, litter or standing water. Provide a plan or photographs of aisles, ramps and drains, and state when parking bays can be cleared or temporarily closed. Ask which cleaning method, scrubbing and extraction equipment will be used and how stains and wash water will be managed without directing it into unsuitable areas. Compare crew, equipment, visits, drying time and warning signage. After cleaning, inspect corners, drains and ramps, and confirm that routes are dry and floor markings remain clear.

### 18. Industrial-site cleaning

Before requesting industrial cleaning, describe the activity, areas involved and the type of dust, residue or oil, and state whether machinery or production lines will remain operating. Provide area plans and photographs of visible hazards, power and water points and circulation routes. Identify materials or surfaces that cannot tolerate water or strong chemicals. Request a work plan covering isolation, protective equipment, products, waste collection and disposal, duration and coordination with the site representative. Compare the team's ability to work around equipment, protect assets and document completion. Unknown contaminants require specialist assessment before work begins; handover should follow an inspection of the agreed areas and waste removal.


## Water tanks and swimming pools cleaning

### 19. Water tank cleaning

State the number, location, approximate capacity and material of each tank, and whether it is underground or elevated. Photograph access openings and the surrounding area and explain how safe access is arranged. Confirm whether the supply can be isolated and the tank drained during the work, and who will refill it afterwards. Ask how sediment is removed, internal walls and base are washed and rinsed, which tools and products are used and how openings are protected from contamination during the job. Compare tank count, capacity, isolation, drying and recommissioning. At handover, inspect internal surfaces, lid condition and sealed openings and request a record of the work performed.

### 20. Water tank disinfection

Tank disinfection usually follows cleaning but should be scoped as a separate task. Identify tank material, type and capacity and request the product name and manufacturer instructions for concentration, contact time, rinsing, ventilation and refilling. Do not rely on a generic dosage without reference to the product label and a competent assessment of the tank. The quotation should explain who isolates the tank and supply, and how use is prevented during treatment, plus whether an inspection or service record is included. Compare product compatibility and documentation. At handover, confirm that treatment and rinsing have followed the instructions, the lid is restored and users are told when the tank can return to service.

### 21. Swimming pool cleaning

For a pool-cleaning enquiry, provide length, width, approximate depth and water volume, lining or tile type, and the condition of the filter and pump. Photograph the water, walls, floor and equipment room, noting algae, scale or a long period without operation. Ask whether the service includes floor and wall brushing, waterline, steps and skimmers, inspection of circulation and water readings before and after treatment. Compare a single visit with recurring service and identify chemical, filter and spare-part costs; cleaning alone should not be presented as a pump repair. At handover, review water clarity, safe equipment operation, test readings and instructions for returning the pool to use.


## Pest and rodent control

### 22. Cockroach control

Describe where cockroaches are seen, how often and at what times, and whether activity is noticed in kitchens, bathrooms, service rooms or near drains. Photograph affected areas and mention children, pets or food storage nearby. Ask the provider to explain inspection, identification of food and water sources and entry points, and the difference between baits, targeted treatment and spraying where appropriate. The quotation should state site preparation, any room-clearance period, ventilation and follow-up timing according to product instructions. Compare the treatment plan, report and return visit, then monitor activity points and record recurrence rather than relying on an immediate guarantee.

### 23. Ant control

Map ant trails, entry points and nearby food or moisture sources. Explain whether activity is limited to the kitchen or extends to balconies, planters or corridors. Photograph trails and gathering points; avoid disturbing them before inspection if that would make the source harder to identify. Request a plan for tracing the route, treating entry points and removing attractants, with clear information on the product, application locations and precautions. Compare indoor and outdoor coverage, follow-up and recurrence handling. At handover, obtain storage and cleaning advice and instructions for monitoring trails, and request reassessment if activity continues.

### 24. Rodent control

Record evidence such as droppings, gnaw marks or sounds, where it occurs and when, and photograph possible openings, storage areas and waste points. Do not handle droppings or unidentified bait, and keep children and pets away from affected areas until assessed. Request an inspection identifying entry routes, food and water sources and hiding places, and explaining traps, exclusion work and any specialist treatment. The quotation should identify trap locations, inspection method, removal responsibility and sealing work. Compare prevention and follow-up rather than trap count alone. At handover, review the location report and corrective actions, confirm the next visit and address attractants where practical.

### 25. Bed bug control

If bed bugs are suspected, identify affected rooms, beds and furniture and when signs appeared. Photograph seams, edges and furniture joints without moving items between rooms. Mention adjoining units or recently introduced second-hand furniture because inspection may need to extend beyond the mattress. Ask for a plan covering inspection, treatment method, preparation of bedding and clothing, room-clearance and re-entry instructions, and whether a follow-up visit is needed. Compare heat or chemical methods according to site suitability and product instructions; do not assume one visit will always be sufficient. At handover, obtain monitoring instructions and avoid bringing unchecked items back into treated rooms.

### 26. Periodic pest-control contracts

Prepare a schedule of buildings, rooms and outdoor areas, known pest activity and required visit frequency. Provide a simple plan showing sensitive locations such as kitchens, waste rooms and stores, and mention children, pets or activities that need special coordination. The quotation should define each visit's scope, products and application methods, reporting, re-treatment where activity persists and contract exclusions. Compare visit frequency, reports, response to complaints and variation terms. Review recurring findings and monitoring records regularly, and request a preventive plan addressing entry points, food and moisture sources rather than relying only on scheduled spraying.


## Disinfection services

### 27. Home disinfection spraying

Identify the rooms and surfaces to be treated and the reason for the request. State whether the property is occupied and whether children, pets, food or sensitive equipment are present. Provide photographs and describe flooring and furniture materials. Ask for the product name, application method, compatible surfaces, contact time, ventilation and return-to-use interval according to the label. The quotation should clarify whether prior cleaning is included or whether spraying is applied only to prepared surfaces, and what must be covered or removed. Compare treatment coverage, products and records; do not accept an undefined claim of “complete disinfection”. At handover, review safety instructions and remove any residue as directed.

### 28. Office and facility disinfection

An office or facility request should identify area, occupancy hours, frequently touched points, meeting rooms, reception and washrooms, along with equipment or materials that may be affected by spray. Provide a floor plan and photographs, and select a time that allows rooms to be vacated and ventilated before staff or visitors return. Request a plan stating product, application method, concentration, contact time, ventilation and excluded surfaces in line with the product label and competent advice. Compare crew size, stages, treatment records and repeat-visit charges. At handover, reconcile treated areas against the plan, confirm that equipment and furniture are restored and tell the site representative when the premises may safely be reoccupied.


## Building operations and maintenance

### 29. Maintenance-work management

Maintenance management starts with an asset and work-request register, not simply receiving fault calls. Identify property types, common areas and systems in scope, and provide equipment lists, fault history and existing contracts where available. Ask how requests are logged and prioritised, how a contractor is assigned and how approval is obtained before additional work, with reports showing cost, timing and closure. Compare follow-up coverage, response hours, supplier coordination and warranty administration rather than visit count alone. At handover, review open and closed requests, deferred work and the person responsible for each follow-up.

### 30. Facilities operation

Facilities operation covers day-to-day oversight of areas and systems that support building use, such as entrances, corridors, shared amenities and operational services. State operating hours, occupancy, number of areas, service points and management contacts, and provide a site plan. Request a shift and inspection-round plan, recording of readings and faults, coordination of cleaning and waste, and a route for escalating issues that require specialist technicians. Compare team size, coverage hours, reports and each shift's duties. At handover, confirm daily logs, responsibility matrix and shift-to-shift handover method; do not assume that operation includes technical work not agreed in the scope.

### 31. Preventive maintenance

Prepare an asset register showing equipment type, model, location, operating hours and fault history. Define preventive tasks by manufacturer guidance and actual asset condition. Provide equipment schedules and possible shutdown windows, and request a plan covering routine inspections, consumables, anticipated replacement parts and record keeping. Compare visit frequency, included checks, parts costs, system downtime and whether emergency repairs are outside the contract. Handover should include dated checklists, readings before and after service and recommendations for assets needing follow-up, with a named person responsible for closing each finding.

### 32. Electrical-system monitoring

Identify the panels and areas in scope. Provide a single-line diagram if available, panel and breaker references and records of trips or outages. Ask the provider to define inspection scope, readings to be collected and escalation for heat, odour or unusual loads, and to state who is authorised to carry out repairs. Panels must not be opened or live work undertaken without competent personnel and suitable safety controls. Compare inspection rounds, records, measurement equipment and fault reporting rather than panel count alone. At handover, review readings, findings, corrective actions and any work requiring a separate repair order.

### 33. Air-conditioning system monitoring

Prepare a list of units with locations, models and operating hours, and record complaints about weak cooling, temperature variation, noise or condensate leakage. Provide nameplate, air outlet, filter and plant-area photographs and identify room access windows. Request a plan covering operating and temperature readings, filters, drainage, controls and air distribution, while separating cleaning from fault repair or parts replacement. Compare inspection frequency, reports, spare parts and response to complaints. At handover, review unit readings and deferred findings and confirm that covers are closed and thermostats and work areas have been restored.

### 34. Plumbing and drainage monitoring

Identify tanks, pumps, isolation points, meters and drains, and record leaks, odours, slow drainage, pressure changes and when they occur. Provide photographs of affected areas and plumbing drawings where available. Clarify whether common areas, individual units or both are included. Request inspection rounds, pressure readings, pump, valve and drain checks and repair records, including how services will be isolated if needed. Compare the number of points and visits, materials and responsibility for reinstating finishes after repairs. At handover, test treated points, review leak history and replaced parts and list areas requiring further monitoring.

### 35. Building-condition reports

State the report purpose—routine monitoring, handover or pre-maintenance assessment—then identify the building and areas to be inspected and whether units are occupied. Ask the quotation to define inspection scope, systems reviewed, inspection depth and access limitations. The report should include dated photographs, location of each finding, description, priority and recommended next action. Compare a simple defect list with a report that links each observation to its location, impact and supporting evidence. It should identify inaccessible areas and matters requiring specialist testing or a separate engineering opinion. At handover, check area coverage, photographs, finding references and follow-up responsibility.


## Workforce and facilities operations services

### 36. Cleaning staff provision

Specify property type, area, floor and room count, zones requiring coverage, working hours, shifts and supervision level. Attach a task schedule for daily and periodic duties such as floor care, washrooms, entrances and waste collection, and state who supplies machines and consumables. Request a quotation showing staff numbers and hours, supervisor, training for delicate surfaces, attendance records and absence cover. Compare task scope, consumable costs, extra visits and complaint handling. At mobilisation, review zone allocation, checklists and attendance; headcount alone does not prove that the agreed work has been completed.

### 37. Loading labour provision

Loading labour should be sized from the load, not guessed from property area. Describe materials or furniture, item dimensions and approximate weights, quantities, floors and internal carrying distance. Photograph entrances, corridors and the vehicle standing point, and state whether lifting equipment or trolleys are available. Ask for crew and supervisor numbers, duration, tools, protective equipment and responsibility for heavy or fragile items. Compare team size, working hours, waiting, loading and unloading, separating any equipment charges. At completion, reconcile moved quantities, inspect routes and items and record damage or shortages before accepting the service.

### 38. Maintenance technicians provision

Identify the trade precisely—electrical, plumbing, air-conditioning, finishing or another discipline—then describe the fault or task, number of units, location and access hours. Provide equipment nameplates, defect photographs, available drawings and previous repair history. Clarify whether one visit or continuing coverage is needed. Ask about the technician's relevant experience, duties, tools and materials, approval of additional work, required tests and any workmanship warranty. Compare trade competence, hours, materials responsibility and supervision. At handover, obtain a work report listing tasks completed, parts replaced and test results.

### 39. Site supervisors provision

Before requesting a site supervisor, define project type and size, current stage, number of teams and shifts and the authority the supervisor needs for coordination and follow-up. Provide the programme, site plan and required reporting format, and identify who approves variations and work orders. The quotation should state relevant experience, attendance coverage and responsibility for monitoring quality, safety coordination, deliveries, work interfaces and reporting findings, without assuming the supervisor replaces the consultant or authorised decision-maker. Compare coverage hours, report frequency, escalation and absence cover. At mobilisation, approve a responsibility matrix, daily report template and decision log.

### 40. Residential compound operation

Specify the number of buildings and units, shared areas and amenities in scope, operating hours, expected service requests and how residents submit observations. Provide a compound plan and list building systems and current contractors. Clarify whether cleaning, maintenance, landscaping, waste and facilities follow-up are all included or only selected tasks. Request shift coverage, inspection rounds, a service-request register, follow-up dates and performance reports, with escalation for matters requiring management approval or specialist technicians. Compare team size, service boundaries, response arrangements, reporting and exclusions. At handover, review open requests, escalation procedures and the condition of shared areas.

### 41. Commercial-facility operation

Begin by defining facility use, opening hours, tenant count, shared areas and visitor activity, then allocate cleaning, maintenance and daily coordination responsibilities. Provide a site plan, systems list, delivery windows and work periods that avoid disrupting tenants. Ask for supervisor and staff coverage, service-request handling and monitoring of air-conditioning, electrical and plumbing systems, waste and common areas, including coordination limits with property management and tenants. Compare shifts, coverage hours, performance measures, reports and out-of-scope costs. At handover, review the operating plan, fault and request register, maintenance dates and the person responsible for each action.

### 42. Project support services

Describe the project stage and day-to-day support required, such as delivery coordination, material handling, cleaning, technicians or supervisors. State site, duration, shifts and team numbers. Attach the programme and work-zone plan and identify activities that must not conflict. Clarify who supplies equipment, materials and protective items. Request a quotation specifying personnel and trades, coverage hours, daily reports, attendance, received materials and coordination with the main contractor. Compare capacity for peak periods, absence cover, waiting-time charges and extra-work terms. At close-out, reconcile labour, tasks, equipment and material records against the agreed support scope and document any outstanding follow-up.

## Requesting a quotation when no company is published

A request can be submitted to Biet Al Reef customer service even when no company is displayed for the service. Prepare the following information to help review it:

- Customer name, contact number and preferred contact method.
- Area: Al Reem Island, with building or site name and available access instructions.
- Sector, specialty and exact service name used in this guide.
- Property type, area and relevant room, zone or equipment counts.
- Description of the need, current condition and expected outcome.
- Site, surface or equipment photographs, dimensions, drawings or maintenance records where available.
- Required materials or equipment and any access or working-hour restrictions.
- Preferred timing or urgency, understood as a request rather than a confirmed booking.

Customer service receives the enquiry, reviews the information and coordinates appropriate follow-up. Submission does not confirm a company, price, appointment or performance.
