# Construction and lifting equipment hire on Al Reem Island — Equipment rental sector

This is editorial guidance for company services, not a claim that equipment or a published supplier is available. Selection depends on the task, site access and safe operation. Biet Al Reef customer service accepts quotation enquiries even where no company is listed.

## Construction and lifting equipment hire

### 1. Scaffolding hire

Specify working height, façade length, passage width and ground conditions before selecting scaffolding. Send photographs of access points and obstructions, and request an assembly layout covering platforms, edge protection and access ladders. Compare hire duration, transport, erection, dismantling, inspections and responsibility for changes. Delivery of components is not acceptance: a competent person should inspect stability, access and protection and document readiness before use.

### 2. Crane hire

Provide the load weight, dimensions, lifting points, destination, horizontal radius and required height. Photos of the access road, standing area, ground and overhead obstructions help determine capacity at the actual operating radius rather than relying on nominal crane capacity. Separate operator, mobilisation, waiting time, rigging and lift-planning costs in quotations. Check inspection records, lift plan and exclusion zone before lifting, and record completion and any damage.

### 3. Excavation equipment hire

Describe excavation depth, trench width and length, spoil type and available manoeuvring space. A compact excavator may suit restricted access, but bucket size, reach and transport limits affect selection. Share site photographs, known buried services and spoil-removal arrangements. Compare wet or dry hire, operator, fuel, attachments, delivery and chargeable hours. Check leaks, tracks or tyres and record hour-meter readings at handover and return; underground services and excavation hazards must be assessed before digging.

### 4. Compaction equipment hire

State the fill area, layer thickness, soil or granular material and proximity to walls or utilities. A rammer, vibrating plate or roller suits different widths and compaction depths. Request machine weight, working width, power requirements and noise or vibration limitations. Compare rental duration, delivery, operation and maintenance. Test starting and vibration at handover; a fixed number of passes alone does not prove target density, so specify field compaction testing where the project requires it.

### 5. Concrete mixer hire

Describe batch volume, material type, working location and water, power or fuel supply. A small mixer may suit repairs, but nominal drum capacity differs from usable batch volume; request actual mixing capacity, approximate output and cleaning requirements. Compare rental, delivery, accessories and downtime terms. Inspect the drum, moving-part guards and controls at handover and agree responsibility for removing hardened residue on return. Site mixing still requires an agreed concrete specification and controlled proportions.

### 6. Concrete cutting equipment hire

Identify whether the cut is in a slab or wall, its thickness, cut length and depth, and supply photographs showing known reinforcement and services. Floor saws, wall saws and wet-cutting systems have different capacity, water, power and slurry-control needs. Clarify blade type, wear charges, dust and noise controls, operator and waste-water handling. Structural elements require competent review of drawings and consequences before cutting. On completion check cut alignment, edge condition and damage to adjacent services.

### 7. General project equipment hire

Instead of requesting unspecified 'project equipment', list each task, location, duration, available power or fuel and storage space. Separate access, lifting, cutting, pumping and lighting equipment because their operating and safety needs differ. Request a schedule with model, capacity, accessories, inspection, delivery, maintenance and breakdown replacement terms. Compare total cost, usable operating hours and return conditions rather than daily rate alone. At handover match serial numbers, condition and accessories and photograph any defects.

## Light tools and equipment hire

### 8. Generator hire

For a site without adequate electrical supply, list the loads in kW or kVA, operating voltage, motor starting demand and expected running hours. Share equipment nameplate photographs, the proposed generator position and cable routes, and explain whether power is continuous or standby. Compare continuous rather than peak rating, fuel, cables, earthing, noise, servicing, delivery and collection. At handover request a suitable load test, check protection and leaks, and record running hours and fuel level. Connection to a building installation needs a competent designer and safe isolation arrangements.

### 9. Cleaning equipment hire

Identify the surface, area, type of soiling and available water, power and drainage before choosing a floor scrubber, wet vacuum or pressure washer. A carpet extractor is not suitable for every floor finish; ask about brush and chemical compatibility. Provide photos of stains, obstacles and access routes, and clarify tank capacity, cleaning width, attachments and included detergents. Compare transport, consumables and return-cleaning charges. Test suction, leaks, cables and brushes on collection and record the machine's condition after use.

### 10. Air compressor hire

Pneumatic tools require specified working pressure and airflow. Send each tool's specifications, the number used simultaneously and hose lengths. Compressors differ in sustained output and air quality, especially for spraying or applications sensitive to oil and moisture. Compare tank, hoses, connectors, filters, fuel or power and delivery arrangements. Inspect the safety valve, gauge, hoses and leaks and request condensate-draining instructions. Do not use a compressor unsuited to the required pressure.

### 11. Power tool hire

Describe the operation, material and thickness—for example drilling concrete, cutting timber or grinding metal—rather than asking for generic power tools. Provide work-area photographs, hole diameter or cut depth, power supply and whether the site is occupied. Compare model, bits or discs, consumable charges, dust controls and rental period. Inspect the lead, handles, guards and switch, then test the tool without load and under a suitable working load before accepting it.

### 12. Welding equipment hire

Specify the metal, thickness, joint type and approximate weld length, and whether the work is in a workshop or an exposed site. Arc, wire-feed and other welding processes call for different machines, supplies and fixturing. Request the operating current, duty cycle, leads, return clamp and whether wire, electrodes or shielding gas are included. Compare consumables, transport and downtime terms. Check insulation, connections and cooling at handover; arrange fire precautions, ventilation and protection of nearby people before work starts.

### 13. Water pump hire

Describe the water source, volume to remove, vertical lift, discharge distance and hose diameter, and whether the water contains sediment. Surface drainage, excavation dewatering and pumping to an elevated tank require different equipment. Ask for the pump performance curve at the actual head rather than relying on the advertised maximum flow. Send intake and discharge photos and ensure discharge will not damage neighbouring areas. Compare hoses, fittings, power or fuel, maintenance and delivery. Test flow, leaks and dry-run protection at handover.

### 14. Garden equipment hire

Specify the area, plants and task: mowing, hedge trimming, shredding waste or preparing soil. Share photos of slopes, paths and obstacles and identify noise or working-hour constraints. Compare cutting width or machine capacity, fuel or batteries, blades, protective equipment and transport. At handover inspect blade guards, stop controls, tyres or batteries and record fuel or charge level. Avoid using unsuitable cutting equipment near stones or exposed irrigation lines.


## Aluminium and glass factories

> This specialty is marked as currently non-selectable in the taxonomy source. Its inclusion here does not activate a category or certify a factory. Biet Al Reef customer service can receive a project enquiry with supporting details.

### 15. Aluminium profile manufacturing

For extruded profiles intended for façades, frames or further fabrication, provide approved section drawings showing geometry, wall thickness, cut lengths and quantities. “Heavy-duty profile” is not a measurable specification: identify the alloy, temper, finish and dimensional tolerances appropriate to the application, with section samples where necessary. Compare extrusion, cutting, surface treatment, coating, packing, delivery and any tooling charges separately. On receipt, reconcile quantities and markings, measure sample sections, and check straightness and coating for scratches or peeling.

### 16. Door and window manufacturing

Prepare an opening schedule with each width, height, level, handing and intended use, plus photographs of surrounding walls and sills. Specify the framing system, glazing, hardware, seals and required weather performance; identify who removes old frames and repairs adjacent finishes. Compare shop drawings, hinges, locks, rollers, fixing methods and drainage provisions rather than frame price alone. At handover check smooth operation, locking, alignment, seal contact and any agreed water test. Final fabrication dimensions should be confirmed against actual openings.

### 17. Toughened glass manufacturing

Explain whether each pane serves as a door, screen, tabletop or façade component. Supply a drawing with length, width, thickness, edge finish, holes and fixing positions. Cutting and drilling are generally completed before toughening, making final approval of the fabrication drawing especially important. Compare glass type, tint, processing, edge treatment, protection and delivery, and request application-specific technical documentation rather than assuming toughened glass alone is suitable everywhere. Inspect dimensions, edges, surface damage and hole alignment at delivery; reject panes with damaged critical edges for installation pending technical assessment.

### 18. Glass façade preparation

Send elevations, architectural sections, levels, glazed areas and opening positions, and clarify whether the order is for glass units alone or a complete façade system. Compare thermal and solar performance, insulating glass build-up, support systems, joints, drainage, and responsibility for structural calculations, lifting and installation. Require coordinated details showing how the façade connects to the existing structure before production approval. At handover review joint alignment, seals, glazing condition and contractually specified performance tests; visual inspection does not replace engineering design verification.

### 19. Partition manufacturing

State whether partitions are fixed or demountable, glazed or opaque, and whether doors are included. Provide a floor plan, ceiling heights and the locations of lighting, air conditioning, sprinklers and other services. Compare frame sections, infill panels, glazing, acoustic requirements where relevant, floor and ceiling fixings, and making-good works. Check alignment, frame stability, door operation and safe edge finishes at handover, ensuring partitions do not obstruct service access or existing systems.

### 20. Glass supply

Provide a pane schedule with quantities, dimensions, thicknesses, glass type and intended use. Clear, laminated, insulating and toughened glass cannot automatically be substituted for one another. Include access photographs and lift or unloading constraints; clarify whether cutting, edgework and protective packing are included or whether the order is delivery-only. Compare each pane or unit specification, available product documentation, delivery programme and responsibility for transit damage. Check counts, labels, sizes, corners and edges at receipt, recording chips or scratches before acceptance.

### 21. Project order fulfilment

For repeated batches of doors, profiles or glazing units, issue a bill of quantities tied to approved drawing numbers and revisions, with delivery batches aligned to the site programme. Request sample and shop-drawing approvals, a production schedule, and clear packing and identification so units for different floors or openings cannot be mixed up. Compare tooling, waste, changes, transport, testing, storage and remanufacture provisions, not merely the total quotation. At each delivery, reconcile packing lists, drawings and inspection records, and log shortages or damage and agreed remedies before closing the batch.


## Marble and stone factories

> This specialty is currently not selectable in the taxonomy. Editorial preparation does not imply an approved factory or service availability.

### 22. Stone and marble cutting

Cutting is needed when supplied slabs must be sized for floors, wall cladding or thresholds. Provide a cutting schedule with each finished dimension, thickness, stone type and vein direction, plus photographs of corners and openings. Specify straight, curved or pipe-opening cuts. Compare quotes on cutting tolerances, expected offcuts, piece identification, edge protection and responsibility for recutting incorrectly sized pieces. At receipt, match labels to the schedule and inspect dimensions, corners and vein orientation; nominal drawing dimensions should not replace final site measurements.

### 23. Kitchen worktop fabrication

Fabrication should follow final templating after cabinets and sink, hob and tap positions are fixed. Supply a layout showing runs, depths, thickness, overhangs, joints and appliance cut-outs. Identify whether the material is natural stone or an engineered product because support and care requirements differ. Compare edge profiles, cut-out reinforcement, polishing, transport, fitting and sealing. At handover check level, appliance clearance, joint finish and cracks near internal corners, with an agreed remedy for pieces that do not fit.

### 24. Stone stair tread fabrication

Measure every riser and tread against finished floor levels rather than assuming all steps match a sample. Provide a stair drawing and photographs of landings, nosings and skirting junctions, together with the project's required slip-resistance finish. Quotes should distinguish supply, cutting, nosing profiles, numbering, fitting and tolerances, and agree acceptable colour variation. Check the numbered pieces, dimensions, edge finishes and chips before installation; after fitting, verify consistent riser heights and secure edges.

### 25. Façade stone preparation

Exterior cladding preparation must follow elevation drawings and the fixing system specified by the designer, rather than ordinary interior tile cutting. Provide elevations, sections, panel dimensions, thicknesses, surface finishes, corner details, joints and anchor holes or slots. Compare material test information, technical submittals, packing, identification and spare-piece allowances. Neither stone nor anchors should be assumed adequate for external loads without engineering review. At delivery reconcile labels, sizes and finishes and inspect fixing points and fractures before lifting panels into place.

### 26. Stone engraving and decorative work

For engraved or inlaid stone, supply a full-scale drawing or vector artwork showing lines, depth and placement, with a material and colour sample. Hand carving, machine engraving and inlay have different outcomes; approve a small physical sample before production. Compare artwork preparation, templates, cutting depth, edge treatment, packing and any installation work. Inspect the motif's alignment, scale, finish consistency and delicate sections for hairline fractures on delivery.

### 27. Marble polishing and treatment

Start by identifying whether the issue is general dullness, scratches, absorbed staining or acid etching, since treatments differ. Provide side-lit photographs, surface area, stone identification and any previous cleaning chemicals, and request a small discreet test patch. The quote should describe grinding or polishing stages, protective products, adjacent-surface protection and whether crack repairs are included. Inspect sheen consistency in natural light, machine swirl marks and the aftercare and curing instructions for any impregnating sealer.

### 28. Preparing contractors' stone orders

For a contractor's multi-area order, use a piece schedule tied to approved drawing revisions, floors and installation locations rather than only a total square-metre figure. Supply dimensions, thicknesses, finishes, vein direction and a delivery sequence based on site readiness. Compare sample and shop-drawing approvals, identification, packing, waste allowances, substitutions, batch dates and responsibility for drawing changes. At each receipt reconcile packing lists, piece codes and quantities, recording damage or shortages before signing delivery acceptance.

## Tools and equipment retailers

This specialty is currently marked non-selectable in the taxonomy source. The text is buying guidance, not confirmation of a registered retailer or available stock. Biet Al Reef customer service can receive a quotation request even without a published company.

### 29. Hand tools sales

For a maintenance crew or workshop, begin with the actual tasks rather than an unspecified tool kit. State wrench, screwdriver and socket sizes and the fasteners involved; supply photographs or model numbers where tools must match existing equipment. Compare material quality, size coverage, included pieces, warranties and replacement terms rather than the number of items alone. On receipt check tips, jaws, hinges and marked dimensions against the invoice.

### 30. Power tools sales

A drill, grinder or saw should be selected for the material, thickness, expected duty cycle, available power and workspace. Provide the intended application and details of any existing battery platform or power supply. Request a specific model, power and speed specifications, guarding and included accessories. Compare the bare tool separately from batteries, chargers, blades and discs. At handover carry out a safe basic function check and reconcile serial number, instructions, warranty and accessories.

### 31. Safety equipment sales

Protective equipment must address a defined hazard, not merely a preferred helmet or vest style. Describe exposure to dust, flying particles, noise or falls and provide the site's risk assessment where available. Request manufacturer data, sizes, limitations and applicable product conformity evidence; one item cannot be assumed suitable for every task. Compare fit, shelf life and replaceable components. Inspect packaging, production dates, straps and closures on receipt. Fall-protection systems require competent inspection and use.

### 32. Workshop equipment sales

When equipping a joinery or metalworking shop, supply a floor plan with access aisles, electrical points, ventilation and material storage. Specify each machine's function, duty requirements and workpiece dimensions, then request machine footprints, safe operating clearances, anchoring requirements and commissioning or training provisions. Compare delivery, installation, trial operation, consumables and servicing, not just purchase price. Accept equipment only after agreed functional checks and verification of guards, emergency stops and documentation.

### 33. Cleaning equipment sales

Before purchasing a scrubber-dryer or industrial vacuum, identify floor types, total area, debris, operating hours and water filling or disposal arrangements. Provide photographs of narrow passages and thresholds. Ask for cleaning width, tank or battery capacity, power supply, brush options and compatible chemicals. Compare practical runtime and the ongoing cost of filters, brushes and maintenance. Test the unit on a representative surface at handover and check suction, leakage, brush marks and daily cleaning instructions.

### 34. Spare parts sales

Parts that look alike may not fit or operate safely in the same machine. Supply the equipment nameplate, model and serial number, old part number if available, and photographs of mounts and connectors. Obtain written compatibility confirmation and clarity on genuine versus aftermarket supply, warranty and return terms, particularly for electrical or safety-critical components. Compare specifications and lead times alongside price. On receipt check part numbers, connectors and dimensions; safety-relevant fitting and testing should be performed by a competent technician.

### 35. Equipment maintenance

Record the fault, operating conditions, hours, noises and error codes before seeking repair, and stop use where an obvious hazard exists. Supply the model, nameplate photograph and service history. Request diagnosis distinguishing routine maintenance, component replacement and calibration. The quotation should separate inspection charges, parts, labour, testing and workmanship warranty, with approval required before extra repairs. At handover obtain a record of replaced parts and test results and confirm guards and protective devices have been reinstated correctly.

## Furniture and property moving

### 36. Household furniture moving

Before requesting a household move, list rooms, large items and sensitive appliances, and record dimensions of pieces that may not pass through doors or lifts. Send photographs of entrances and the loading route, and state the floor, lift availability and building access windows after confirming them with management. Ask for a quotation separating packing materials, dismantling and reassembly, crew size, vehicle type and waiting time. Compare floor and corner protection and the handling of glass, then reconcile every item against the inventory at delivery and record scratches or shortages before signing.

### 37. Office furniture moving

An office move needs an inventory of desks, chairs, cabinets, equipment and files before vehicle and crew requirements can be assessed. Number cartons and workstations and map each item to its destination. Identify who will disconnect computers, networks and equipment that requires a qualified IT technician. Provide floor plans and lift and loading-area photographs, and state acceptable downtime so operations can be planned. Compare packing, labelling, dismantling, reassembly, transport and loading rather than counting trips alone. At handover, reconcile the inventory, inspect furniture and confirm that equipment and files are placed at the agreed locations with no missing accessories.

### 38. Furniture dismantling and reassembly

Use this service when furniture must be taken apart for a move, a joint needs repair or a unit must be reassembled in a different room. Send front and rear photographs, fixing details, the brand or model if known, and images of special screws, hinges and fittings. The quotation should state whether existing hardware can be reused or replacements are required, and whether door and drawer adjustment or wall anchoring is included where appropriate. Compare experience with timber, glass and moving mechanisms as well as the cost of extra parts. Check alignment, stability and smooth operation at handover; do not allow drilling until concealed service routes have been checked.

### 39. Furniture wrapping and packing

Packing methods depend on the surface and the duration of transport or storage: polished timber, glass, fabric and appliances need different protection. Provide an item list and photographs of current condition, identifying fragile pieces and surfaces vulnerable to moisture or scratches. Ask which wrapping materials and protective layers will be used, how corners are protected, how items are secured inside the vehicle and whether every package will be numbered against the inventory. Compare materials, labour, unpacking at destination and waste removal rather than the number of rolls. Photograph items before packing and inspect package condition on delivery before removing the protection.

### 40. Loading labour

State the number of items, approximate weights, floors and carrying distance between the property and vehicle. Identify stairs, narrow corridors and pieces that require a team lift. Photographs of the route and heavy items help avoid estimating crew size from property area alone. Request a quotation showing crew size, supervisor, working hours, minimum booking, tools and lifting equipment where needed, plus the basis for waiting time or overtime. Compare responsibility allocation, corridor protection and fragile-item handling. After loading, reconcile the inventory, check the route for damage and confirm that items are secured safely.

### 41. Temporary storage

Before reserving storage, estimate furniture volume, carton count and likely duration, and identify items sensitive to heat or moisture. Provide an inventory and photographs of valuable or delicate pieces. Ask about warehouse conditions, ventilation, protection from water ingress and pests, access control and inspection arrangements. The quotation should separate intake, release and handling charges, extra-period rates, insurance or liability limits and access terms. Compare item identification and movement records rather than storage area alone. Record item condition and quantities at intake; on release, reconcile the inventory and inspect packaging and contents before closing the request.

### 42. Inter-emirate property moving

Provide exact collection and destination addresses, the type and quantity of property, dimensions of large items and the requested date, together with photographs of access and loading areas at both ends. Clarify whether the service includes packing, dismantling, reassembly or storage, or transport only, and whether there are multiple stops or staged deliveries. Compare vehicle type, crew, distance, waiting-time basis, loading, packing and responsibility for transit damage. Do not rely on a final estimate until the inventory and access at both addresses have been reviewed. At destination, reconcile furniture and cartons against the list, inspect their condition and record discrepancies before accepting delivery.


## Project materials and equipment transport

### 43. Building materials transport

Identify material types, quantities, package weights and dimensions, and whether goods are palletised or loose. Send the quantity schedule and photographs of loading and delivery locations. State entrance width and height, vehicle turning space and available unloading method. The quotation should identify vehicle type, trip count, lifting and securing equipment, waiting time and responsibility for placing materials after unloading. Compare transport with handling, protection from weather and dust, and damage exposure rather than nominal vehicle capacity alone. On arrival, reconcile quantities and package condition against the delivery note and record shortages or breakage before acceptance.

### 44. Timber transport

MDF boards, natural timber, doors and long sections need a vehicle that supports their length and protects edges and faces from bending, scratches and moisture. Provide board lengths, widths, thicknesses, bundle counts and packing method, with photographs of the storage point and vehicle access route. Ask for truck-bed length, support points, protective separators and securing straps, and whether loading and unloading are included. Compare trip count, protection, handling of long pieces and waiting charges. Inspect boards for breakage, bowing and edge damage at delivery, and match sizes and quantities to the supply order.

### 45. Marble transport

Marble is heavy and may crack if it is poorly supported or exposed to impact, so describe each slab or piece, its dimensions, thickness, quantity and approximate weight before pricing. Provide photographs of edges, finishes and loading points. Ask which racks, padded separators, lifting method, restraints and transport orientation will be used. Compare vehicle type, handling equipment, crew size, loading and unloading responsibility, and any stated insurance or liability limits. A quotation that merely says “marble transport” does not define protection for each piece. At delivery inspect corners, faces and fine cracks and reconcile piece labels with the installation layout.

### 46. Equipment transport

Provide the equipment name and model, weight, dimensions, centre of gravity, wheel or track arrangement and manufacturer-designated lifting points where available. Photograph both sites and the loading route. State whether utilities must be disconnected, fluids drained or moving parts secured before transport. A competent planner should select the truck or trailer and lifting and restraint equipment from the load data; the quotation should identify preparation, movement, loading, waiting and unloading. Compare operator responsibility, mobilisation and contingency arrangements. Record the machine condition before loading and after delivery, then inspect restraints and sensitive components at handover.

### 47. Waste transport

Identify the waste source, material type, approximate volume and weight where possible, and separate reusable items from demolition or packaging waste. Send photographs of containers and the collection area and explain how the vehicle will exit the site and whether manual or mechanical loading is needed. The quotation should state vehicle type, trip count, crew, waiting charges, destination and any available receipt or disposal record. Do not mix hazardous or unidentified materials with ordinary waste; obtain specialist assessment and verify the authorised handler before transport. At completion, check that the area is clear and reconcile trips and loads against the agreement.

### 48. Project truck provision

Specify the load type, dimensions, weight, required trip count, collection and delivery points and site entry windows. Provide a vehicle route and standing and unloading-area photographs. Explain whether the load needs a box truck, flatbed or vehicle with lifting equipment. The quotation should state whether charging is per trip, hour or reserved period and identify fuel, waiting, loading, unloading, operator and additional equipment. Compare usable payload, deck dimensions, securing arrangements and a replacement-vehicle plan in case of breakdown. Record arrival and departure times and delivered quantities for each trip so handover and account reconciliation are clear.

### 49. Loading and unloading services

This service is useful when a vehicle is arranged but the project needs labour and equipment to handle materials or machinery on site. Specify load type, weights, dimensions, unit count, lifting height and internal carrying distance, and provide photographs of corridors and storage points. Request crew and supervisor numbers, handling equipment, protective materials, work duration and damage responsibility; clarify whether a forklift, trolley or other lifting aid is required. Compare crew capability, experience and the basis for time and waiting charges. At completion, reconcile moved quantities, confirm placement at the agreed locations and check that routes and packaging remain in good condition.
