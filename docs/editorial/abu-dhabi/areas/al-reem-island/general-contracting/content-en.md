## Building and villa construction

### 1. Villa construction

A villa construction enquiry needs plot dimensions, floor count, room requirements, parking and service access. Provide project drawings or clarify the design stage before seeking comparable bids: a rate per square metre does not define foundations or finish quality. Compare itemised excavation, concrete, waterproofing and building-services schedules, inspection duties and interfaces. Accept work in documented stages against approved drawings, test records and defect lists.

### 2. Residential building construction

Multi-unit residential buildings require planning for shared circulation, vertical services and plant spaces. Provide unit counts, areas, drawings and lift requirements where relevant. Ask bidders to separate structural work, common systems, individual-unit finishes and connections, identifying commissioning responsibility. Handover must cover corridors, plant rooms and operating tests as well as the apartments.

### 3. Commercial building construction

The intended commercial use affects loading, delivery access, space division and utility provision. Supply drawings, a use brief and the boundary between landlord works and tenant fit-out rather than requesting a generic building price. Compare electrical, ventilation, cooling and façade specifications together with exclusions. Handover should check as-built documents, maintenance access and system commissioning.

### 4. Building works execution

Building works can mean blockwork, structural work or limited alterations, so the enquiry must break the scope into measurable tasks. Supply site photographs, dimensions and drawings; clarify demolition, waste removal and protection of occupied spaces. Compare quantities, units, materials and preparation allowances. Check alignment, dimensions and fixings before finishes conceal the work.

### 5. Concrete works execution

Identify the concrete element, location, dimensions and design-specified grade, and supply reinforcement, formwork and opening details. The quotation should cover supply, pumping, curing and agreed testing. Reinforcement changes require competent design review rather than assumptions from photographs. Inspection begins before the pour and continues through pour records, test results and checks after striking formwork.

### 6. Structural frame construction

The structural frame comprises foundations, columns, beams and slabs whose quantities cannot be inferred from floor area alone. Supply structural drawings, steel and concrete schedules and coordinated openings. Compare formwork, temporary supports, pour sequencing and testing allowances. Record inspections and resolve defects before structural elements are concealed.

### 7. Turnkey project delivery

Turnkey delivery must be defined through a responsibility schedule stating whether design, coordination, structure, building services, finishes, furnishings and external works are included. Provide drawings, expected finish standards and material schedules; compare exclusions, payment stages and approvals. Completion requires operating tests, warranties, documents and closure of defects—not merely finished paintwork.

### 8. New villa building

For a new villa, define future household needs, room sizes, privacy, circulation and equipment locations before freezing the design. Ask bidders to explain construction stages, material procurement, external works and service connections. Compare specifications and quantities rather than reference images alone. Inspect waterproofing, building systems and finishes progressively and collect final documentation.

### 9. Residential annex construction

An annex needs measured available space, its relationship to the existing building, access and drainage and electrical connection points. The design must address excavation effects, junctions, waterproofing and movement joints. Supply photographs, an existing-site plan and dimensions; price connections and reinstatement separately. Handover checks interfaces, leaks and finishes.

### 10. Outdoor majlis construction

An outdoor majlis brief should state seating capacity, floor area, entrances, washrooms and ancillary facilities before sizing the building. Review shade, insulation, cooling, ceiling heights and service circulation using measured drawings. Compare structure, finishes, systems and surrounding works separately. Handover checks cooling, drainage, lighting and potential water ingress.

### 11. Villa extensions

Before extending an existing villa, identify the elements being altered and compare existing and proposed plans. A qualified specialist must assess structural capacity and existing services. Document junctions, worker access and protection of occupied rooms. Price demolition, strengthening, new work and reinstatement separately. Inspect old-to-new connections, waterproofing, cracks and operation at handover.

### 12. Residential building alterations

Alterations may involve partition changes, new openings or a changed room function, each with different structural and service implications. Describe existing and proposed use and provide measured plans and photographs. Establish whether walls are load-bearing before removal and have the relevant professional check project-specific approval requirements. Compare reinstatement scope and test affected systems after completion.

### 13. Villa restoration

Villa restoration starts by diagnosing deterioration rather than covering it with new paint or cladding. Photograph damp patches, cracks and material decay; record when symptoms began and earlier repairs. Request a scope distinguishing structural treatment, waterproofing and finishes, with inspection criteria. At handover confirm the cause has been addressed and affected areas tested.

### 14. Residential building refurbishment

A residential refurbishment should inventory affected apartments, corridors and shared systems and define phasing if residents remain. Prepare a condition schedule for finishes, doors, electrical and plumbing works, and specify replacement materials, resident protection and waste handling. Compare the same items and service shutdown windows. Handover covers shared areas, tests and recorded defects.

## Restoration and renovation

### 15. Older building restoration

Restoring an older building begins with a condition survey of load-bearing elements, roofs and services, with dated photographs of cracks and moisture. Request a plan distinguishing retained, strengthened and replaced components, including occupant protection. Compare repair methods, tests and reinstatement, and obtain treatment records and inspection results at handover.

### 16. Villa renovation

For villa renovation, identify rooms and any electrical, plumbing or cooling changes. Supply measured photographs of existing doorways and circulation. Seek a finish schedule, approved samples and a phasing plan for an occupied home. Compare removal, repair, supply and installation separately; test outlets and equipment and inspect finishes room by room.

### 17. Apartment renovation

Apartment renovation needs defined unit boundaries, delivery arrangements and protection of shared lifts and corridors. Provide plans, photographs and measurements of kitchens, bathrooms and floors. Compare waterproofing, service alterations and finishes with waste removal clearly assigned; test drainage, leakage and electrical circuits before closing up.

### 18. Building rehabilitation

Building rehabilitation aims to restore fitness for use, so structural condition and building systems may need assessment before cosmetic work. Provide defect records, available drawings and intended use. Require defined investigation, repair and commissioning stages; compare lifecycle implications and warranties, and document system performance before acceptance.

### 19. Structural crack treatment

Photograph cracks with a scale, locations and dates, noting any change. Injection or painting should not precede diagnosis. A competent structural professional must assess the member, loads and possible movement and decide whether monitoring or strengthening is needed. Compare investigation and repair methods; handover includes measurement and monitoring records, not appearance alone.

### 20. Façade refurbishment

Façade refurbishment requires identification of existing cladding, working heights, damaged areas and water ingress. Provide overview and close-up photographs. Request safe access arrangements, removal of unsound elements, joint treatment and replacement specifications. Compare areas, systems and testing; inspect fixings, finish consistency and water resistance at handover.

### 21. Preparing buildings for reuse

Preparing a building for reuse starts with its former and proposed functions, existing services, entrances and load needs. Provide as-built information and photographs of affected spaces. A qualified assessment should establish necessary alterations and project-specific approvals. Compare strip-out, upgrades, testing and new installations; collect operating and maintenance documentation.

## Demolition and concrete works

### 22. Demolition works

Demolition scope must distinguish removed and retained elements using plans and photographs, including concealed services and access. Require a method covering utility isolation, temporary support, dust and noise control and debris removal. Compare boundaries, plant and waste handling; accept a safe cleared site with condition records.

### 23. Building and annex removal

Removing a whole building or annex requires checking connections to adjacent structures and water, power and drainage. Supply a site plan, removal limits and surrounding photographs, and seek a method protecting retained foundations. Compare breaking, loading, transport and ground reinstatement; confirm service isolation and removal of hazardous voids and debris.

### 24. Concrete cutting and coring

Concrete cutting and coring enquiries need the opening location, size, member thickness and purpose, plus structural drawings and known services. A competent specialist must assess reinforcement and load implications before cutting. Compare cutting method, slurry collection, debris handling and protection; check opening dimensions, edges and surrounding elements.

### 25. Footing and foundation pours

Foundation pouring needs the geotechnical report, approved structural drawings, footing dimensions and excavation levels, including access and any groundwater issues. Compare formwork, reinforcement, concrete, pumping, testing and curing. Inspect bearing conditions, steel and cover before pouring, and document samples and curing afterwards.

### 26. Concrete slab pours

Concrete slab pours require area, thickness, levels, openings and concentrated loads, with reinforcement and service drawings. Request formwork, propping, pour sequencing and curing plans and pre-pour inspections. Check levels, honeycombing, cracking and test records, with support removal governed by the engineering plan.

### 27. Concrete wall construction

For concrete walls, specify length, height, thickness and purpose, including whether soil or water is retained. Provide reinforcement, joint and waterproofing details. Compare formwork, ties, placing, curing and sealing of tie holes. Inspect alignment, levels, joints, waterproofing and concrete test records against the specification.

### 28. Demolition waste removal

Demolition waste removal needs estimated volume, material types, stockpile location and truck access, with potentially hazardous materials identified separately. Request loading, containers, transport and appropriate disposal arrangements. Compare trips, final cleaning and disposal evidence; accept a cleared site without damage to retained services.

## Garden landscaping and maintenance

### 29. Home garden design

Home garden design starts with a measured plan, levels, use patterns, services and shade, supported by site photographs. Request a layout covering paths, planting, irrigation, lighting and maintenance with plant choices suited to intended use and budget. Compare drawings, quantities and running costs; approve the design before procurement.

### 30. Villa landscaping

Villa landscaping needs measured planting zones, seating, paths, play areas, privacy requirements and water supply. Compare soil preparation, irrigation, plants, edging and finishes with an initial care plan. Check ground levels, edge stability, irrigation coverage and plant condition at handover.

### 31. Outdoor space design

External-space design should document intended use, pedestrian and vehicle movement, power, water and drainage points. Supply dimensions and photographs and seek alternatives for paving, shade and lighting with maintenance needs. Compare accessibility, slip resistance, drainage and quantities; accept measurable construction drawings.

### 32. Green-area installation

Green-area installation needs measured area, water source, proposed turf type and usage intensity. The quotation should cover grading, soil improvement, irrigation, planting and establishment care. Compare coverage, material quantities and maintenance plans; test irrigation and assess even establishment before acceptance.

### 33. Tree and shrub planting

Tree and shrub planting requires locations, clearances from walls and services, delivered plant sizes, photographs and irrigation details. Compare pits, soil, stakes, fertilisation and plant-replacement terms, with an initial watering plan. Inspect planting depth, stability, foliage condition and drainage.

### 34. Natural turf installation

Natural turf installation needs measured area, levels, shade photographs and expected foot traffic, because turf selection depends on site and irrigation. Compare weed removal, soil preparation, sod or seeding, irrigation and establishment care. Inspect root contact, surface evenness and water distribution.

### 35. Artificial turf installation

Artificial turf needs intended use, measured area, base type, drainage and heat or wear exposure, with site photographs. Compare pile height and density, base layers, seams and edges rather than sample colour alone. Check surface levels, secure joins and edges and practical drainage at handover.

## Irrigation networks

### 36. Plant and tree installation

List species, nursery sizes and quantities, noting clearance from utilities and foundations and the available shade and water. Ask for planting-pit sizes, soil mix, tree support and delivery sequence; compare replacement obligations during establishment. Inspect planting depth, stability, drainage and plant health at handover.

### 37. Tree pruning and shaping

Provide photographs showing tree height and branches near buildings or paths. Specify whether crown lifting, deadwood removal or selective thinning is needed. Request safe access and debris handling, avoiding excessive cutting that harms the tree. Compare pruning scope and frequency, then check cut quality and clear walkways.

### 38. Lawn and green-area maintenance

State the area, turf type, weak patches and irrigation source, with photos of yellowing or standing water. Maintenance may require mowing, aeration, patch repair and irrigation adjustment according to actual conditions. Compare visit frequency, consumables and patch-replacement terms; request work records and evidence of improved coverage.

### 39. Plant fertilisation and treatment

Do not select fertiliser before recording symptoms, watering history and growing medium. Share foliage and root photographs where practical, and request diagnosis distinguishing nutrient deficiency from overwatering or salinity. Compare product, dosage, application method and follow-up; obtain treatment records and care instructions.

### 40. Garden pest management

Identify affected plants, visible pests and spread using clear photographs, noting children, pets or food areas nearby. Ask for inspection and consideration of pruning or targeted treatment before blanket spraying, following product safety instructions. Compare visits, follow-up and measurable evidence of reduced infestation.

### 41. Garden cleaning

Measure the garden and distinguish leaves, branches, loose soil and construction debris. Identify collection points and equipment access. Separate path and planter cleaning from pruning or heavy waste removal unless included. Compare labour, loading and final clean-up, checking that paths and drains are clear.

### 42. Garden maintenance contracts

Provide planted-area plans, species, irrigation details and a baseline condition record. Request a seasonal schedule separating mowing, pruning, cleaning, inspections and urgent treatment, with explicit consumables and replacement exclusions. Compare response times, reporting and performance measures; photograph conditions at contract start and review points.

### 43. Irrigation network design

Supply planting plans, turf areas, levels, water source and available pressure, identifying zones with different watering needs. Request hydraulic zoning, flow calculations, pipe sizes, valves, controls and drainage. Compare drawings, bills of quantities and maintenance access; accept buildable plans with test points.

### 44. Drip irrigation installation

Specify planting rows, bed lengths and water supply. Request details of filtration, pressure regulation, emitter type and spacing. Compare pipe restraint, protection and flushing access rather than price per metre alone. Run each zone at handover, checking emitter output, leaks and line ends.

### 45. Sprinkler installation

Provide lawn dimensions, paths, walls and relevant wind exposure, plus supply pressure. Ask for a coverage-overlap layout, head selection, levels and protection from vehicles. Compare zoning, water use and adjustable arcs; witness a coverage test that avoids spraying entrances.

### 46. Irrigation tanks and pumps

Describe daily demand, refill source, tank space, pump location, network levels and required pressure. Request tank sizing, pump flow and head calculations, electrical protection, mounting and maintenance access. Compare controls, warranty and backup provisions; test start-stop operation and leak tightness.

### 47. Irrigation network maintenance

Record dry zones and pooling areas, with photographs of heads, valves and controller. Ask for pressure, filter, programming and joint checks before replacing unconfirmed faulty parts. Compare cleaning, repair, spare parts and follow-up; request pressure readings and an operational test for every zone.

### 48. Automatic irrigation programming

Provide plant types, soil, seasonal needs, use periods, zone count and controller model. Request independent run schedules and verification of rain or moisture sensors where fitted, including access permissions and settings backup. Compare user training and follow-up; obtain the program schedule and witness a complete cycle.

### 49. Irrigation leak detection

Document rising consumption, pressure loss and persistently wet patches, with known pipe routes and valves. Request zone isolation and suitable pressure or detection testing before excavation to limit surface damage. Compare diagnosis, repair and reinstatement separately; require a repeat network test at handover.

## Shade structures and external works

### 50. Car shade installation

Provide parking-bay dimensions, vehicle clearance, door swing and known buried services with site photographs. Request drawings for columns, foundations, canopy covering and drainage, with structural loading addressed by the approved design. Compare frame and cover materials and warranty; check anchorage, clearances and drainage.

### 51. Pergola construction

State seating area, height, desired shade and whether the structure fixes to ground or building; provide measurements and photographs. Request framing, cladding, corrosion protection and drainage details for solid roofs. Compare finishes, maintenance and foundations; inspect alignment, connections and safe edges.

### 52. Outdoor seating area construction

Specify seating capacity, footprint, circulation, power, lighting and drainage, with existing levels photographed. Ask for a bill separating paving, shade, fixed seating and planting. Compare slip resistance, cleaning and materials; inspect levels, seating stability and clear routes.

### 53. External fence installation

Provide verified property boundaries, fence lengths, heights, gates and ground conditions; do not use visual estimates to establish the boundary. Request route, design and site-specific requirement checks before work, including posts, footings and corrosion protection. Compare materials, gates and finishes; inspect alignment, stability and gate operation.

### 54. Garden path construction

Mark path route, width, length, entrance levels and drainage, stating pedestrian or trolley use. Request base-layer sections, edging, paving or stone type and surface falls. Compare excavation, compaction, materials and any lighting; inspect evenness, drainage and secure edges.

### 55. Interlocking paving installation

Measure paved area, vehicle or pedestrian loading, manhole and threshold levels and drainage. Request subbase, compaction, paver thickness, laying pattern and edge details, with an approved sample. Compare quantities, wastage and manhole adjustments; inspect levels, settlement and joint stability.

### 56. Waterfalls and fountain construction

Specify basin size, depth, location, child access, water and power sources, desired flow and acceptable noise. Request waterproofing, pump, filtration, drainage, maintenance access and electrical protection details. Compare operating demand, water consumption and warranty; witness leak testing and sustained safe operation.
