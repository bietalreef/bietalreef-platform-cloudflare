# General maintenance and finishing in Al Reem Island

This editorial guide covers company and establishment services, not a claim that a provider is available. Scope depends on the property and diagnosed defect; any applicable regulatory requirements must be checked with the relevant authority before work.

## General maintenance

### 1. Scheduled villa maintenance

Set a preventive schedule for air conditioning, pumps, plumbing, doors and waterproofing based on equipment age and manufacturers’ guidance. Provide equipment counts, fault history and nameplate photographs. Separate scheduled visits, consumables and emergency repairs in quotations; obtain dated inspection records, operating readings and a list of unresolved defects.

### 2. Scheduled building maintenance

List common areas, plant rooms and any lifts, and clarify access arrangements with building management. Request an asset register, inspection frequency and fault-response scope rather than an undefined all-inclusive package. Check visit logs, corrective actions and test records produced by appropriately qualified specialists where required.

### 3. Interior defect repairs

Photograph each defect with a scale and describe when it appeared: peeling paint, a damaged corner or detached cladding may have different causes. Require diagnosis before cosmetic repair and clarify removal, disposal, cleaning and colour matching. Inspect the repaired surface after curing; fresh paint must not conceal an unresolved moisture source.

### 4. Door and window repairs

Identify frame material, binding or leakage locations, and photograph hinges, locks and seals. Ask the contractor to check alignment, fixings and drainage before proposing complete replacement; price replacement parts separately. Test repeated opening, closing and locking, and inspect perimeter seals at handover.

### 5. Moisture treatment

Record stain locations and timing, including any link to rain, air-conditioning or water use. The cause may be leakage, condensation or failed waterproofing, so stripping paint alone is not a remedy. Compare diagnosis, drying, source repair and reinstatement as separate stages; monitor the affected area before final close-out.

### 6. Crack treatment

Photograph each crack against a ruler, noting its orientation, length and any change over time. Hairline finish cracks differ from possible structural movement; signs of distress warrant assessment by a qualified engineer rather than filler alone. Obtain the repair material specification, monitoring record and finish reinstatement details.

### 7. Pre-handover property preparation

Prepare a room-by-room snagging list covering doors, sockets, sanitary fittings, air conditioning, paint and joints. Photograph defects, assign responsibility and record close-out dates; final cleaning is not a substitute for rectification. Receive test records, close-out photographs and applicable equipment warranties under the contract.

### 8. Minor household maintenance

Combine minor tasks into one photographed checklist, such as adjusting a hinge, fixing a handle or repairing trim. Agree the number of visits, materials and whether a return trip for unavailable parts is included. Do not disguise specialist electrical or structural work as handyman tasks; test each completed item.

### 9. Curtain installation

Provide window width, ceiling height, proposed track length and photographs of fixing points. Specify fabric weight, wall substrate and clearance from vents and openings, and agree the required light control and drape. Check track level, secure fixings, smooth travel and even hems.

### 10. Shelf installation

State shelf dimensions, intended load, wall construction and possible concealed services. Compare visible and concealed brackets and suitable anchors; plasterboard cannot be assumed to carry concrete-wall loads. Confirm level and stability, with safe loading checks consistent with the fixing system specification.

### 11. TV mounting

Share screen size, weight, VESA pattern and socket and cable positions. Agree viewing height, mount type and fixings appropriate to the wall, and check for concealed services before drilling. Test bracket movement, ventilation clearance and cable routing without stressing connectors.

### 12. Accessory installation

Mark positions for towel rails, mirrors, hooks and handles on photographs or a simple layout. Review user heights, moisture resistance and fixing type, particularly near tiled or waterproofed areas. Check alignment, secure attachment and intact surrounding finishes and concealed services.

### 13. Furniture assembly

Provide box photographs, model numbers, furniture dimensions, assembly space and access routes. Require an inventory of panels and hardware before assembly and appropriate anti-tip anchoring for tall units when specified by the manufacturer. Test drawers, doors and stability, and retain spare fittings and care instructions.

### 14. Minor fault repairs

Describe the fault, its frequency and previous repair attempts, with safe photographs or a short video. Quotations should separate diagnosis and replacement parts and state when a licensed specialist is needed, particularly for electrical or gas-related faults. Require an operational test and a record of the diagnosed cause and work performed.


## Integrated finishing

### 15. Villa finishing

Villa finishing should begin with the structural condition, service layouts, approved drawings and interfaces between trades. Supply the built-up area, number of floors and rooms, electrical and plumbing drawings, and site photographs. Request a schedule separating plaster, waterproofing, floors, ceilings, paint, doors and sanitary fittings. Compare measured quantities, material brands and thicknesses, approved samples, exclusions and the phased programme. At handover, check floor levels, falls in wet areas, service tests before concealment, finish consistency and the closure of recorded defects.

### 16. Apartment finishing

For a new or refurbished apartment, record room dimensions, existing floor and wall finishes and ceiling conditions. Establish whether removal, waste disposal and protection of lifts and corridors are included. Photograph kitchen and bathroom connections, distribution boards and air-conditioning outlets; coordinate work affecting shared services with building management. Compare supply, installation, warranties and final cleaning using the same quantities rather than vague lump sums. Inspect joints, door operation, sockets and sanitary fixtures, and confirm any damage to shared areas has been repaired.

### 17. Retail shop finishing

A shop fit-out depends on the trading activity, customer flow, display arrangements and stock storage. Provide a dimensioned plan, ceiling heights, shopfront details and requirements for lighting, ventilation, signage, power and data. Separate partitions, floors and painting from joinery, glazing and operational systems. Specify finishes suitable for the expected wear and verify applicable safety requirements with the relevant authorities and property management. Compare bills of quantities, shop drawings, lead times and testing. At handover, inspect doors, service outlets, point-of-sale areas and shopfront detailing.

### 18. Office finishing

Start with staff numbers, meeting rooms, printer and server locations, and circulation routes. A coordinated layout must align partitions with air conditioning, lighting, electrical points and network cabling. Supply net floor area, ceiling heights, acoustic requirements and floor finishes suitable for rolling chairs. Distinguish fixed finishes from furniture and IT equipment so responsibility remains clear. At handover, test power and data points against the desk plan, check lighting and door operation, and collect test records and as-built layouts.

### 19. Interior space renovation

Renovation need not mean a full strip-out. Mark which floors, doors and services remain and which are to change, supported by room photographs and measurements. Investigate dampness, cracks and concealed services before pricing removal of finishes. For occupied properties, specify furniture protection and separation of work areas. Ask for separately priced repair and replacement options, waste removal and reinstatement of adjoining surfaces. Approve samples and transitions between old and new materials, then inspect alignment, functionality and site cleanliness.

### 20. Decorative fit-out works

Convert reference images into dimensioned details for gypsum features, wall cladding, concealed lighting and decorative elements, including maintenance access. Provide wall lengths, ceiling heights and positions of AC outlets and electrical points. A rendering alone is insufficient without sections showing substrates, fixing methods and materials. Compare panel or timber specifications, finishes, fixings and access provisions, and approve a physical colour and texture sample. Inspect joints, corners, lighting alignment, secure attachment and removable access covers.

### 21. Integrated finishing handover

This service coordinates acceptance of a complete finishing package rather than a single trade. Request the approved scope schedule, revised drawings, materials register, warranties and relevant waterproofing, plumbing and electrical test records. Walk through each room and log defects with photographs, locations, priorities, correction dates and reinspection requirements; freshly painted surfaces do not by themselves constitute acceptance. Reconcile contract items with actual quantities and completed works. Confirm equipment operation, waste removal, keys and agreed documents before final sign-off.
