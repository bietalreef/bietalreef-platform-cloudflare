# General maintenance and finishing in Al Reem Island

This editorial guide covers company and establishment services, not a claim that a provider is available. Scope depends on the property and diagnosed defect; any applicable regulatory requirements must be checked with the relevant authority before work.

## General maintenance

### 1. Scheduled villa maintenance

Set a preventive schedule for air conditioning, pumps, plumbing, doors and waterproofing based on equipment age and manufacturers’ guidance. Provide equipment counts, fault history and nameplate photographs. Separate scheduled visits, consumables and emergency repairs in quotations; obtain dated inspection records, operating readings and a list of unresolved defects.

### 2. Scheduled building maintenance

List common areas, plant rooms and any lifts, and clarify access arrangements with building management. Request an asset register, inspection frequency and fault-response scope rather than an undefined all-inclusive package. Check visit logs, corrective actions and test records produced by appropriately qualified specialists where required.

### 3. Interior defect repairs

Photograph each defect with a scale and describe when it appeared: peeling paint, a damaged corner or detached cladding may have different causes. Require diagnosis before cosmetic repair and clarify removal, disposal, cleaning and colour matching. Inspect the repaired surface after curing; fresh paint must not conceal an unresolved moisture source.

### 4. Door and window repairs

Identify frame material, binding or leakage locations, and photograph hinges, locks and seals. Ask the contractor to check alignment, fixings and drainage before proposing complete replacement; price replacement parts separately. Test repeated opening, closing and locking, and inspect perimeter seals at handover.

### 5. Moisture treatment

Record stain locations and timing, including any link to rain, air-conditioning or water use. The cause may be leakage, condensation or failed waterproofing, so stripping paint alone is not a remedy. Compare diagnosis, drying, source repair and reinstatement as separate stages; monitor the affected area before final close-out.

### 6. Crack treatment

Photograph each crack against a ruler, noting its orientation, length and any change over time. Hairline finish cracks differ from possible structural movement; signs of distress warrant assessment by a qualified engineer rather than filler alone. Obtain the repair material specification, monitoring record and finish reinstatement details.

### 7. Pre-handover property preparation

Prepare a room-by-room snagging list covering doors, sockets, sanitary fittings, air conditioning, paint and joints. Photograph defects, assign responsibility and record close-out dates; final cleaning is not a substitute for rectification. Receive test records, close-out photographs and applicable equipment warranties under the contract.

### 8. Minor household maintenance

Combine minor tasks into one photographed checklist, such as adjusting a hinge, fixing a handle or repairing trim. Agree the number of visits, materials and whether a return trip for unavailable parts is included. Do not disguise specialist electrical or structural work as handyman tasks; test each completed item.

### 9. Curtain installation

Provide window width, ceiling height, proposed track length and photographs of fixing points. Specify fabric weight, wall substrate and clearance from vents and openings, and agree the required light control and drape. Check track level, secure fixings, smooth travel and even hems.

### 10. Shelf installation

State shelf dimensions, intended load, wall construction and possible concealed services. Compare visible and concealed brackets and suitable anchors; plasterboard cannot be assumed to carry concrete-wall loads. Confirm level and stability, with safe loading checks consistent with the fixing system specification.

### 11. TV mounting

Share screen size, weight, VESA pattern and socket and cable positions. Agree viewing height, mount type and fixings appropriate to the wall, and check for concealed services before drilling. Test bracket movement, ventilation clearance and cable routing without stressing connectors.

### 12. Accessory installation

Mark positions for towel rails, mirrors, hooks and handles on photographs or a simple layout. Review user heights, moisture resistance and fixing type, particularly near tiled or waterproofed areas. Check alignment, secure attachment and intact surrounding finishes and concealed services.

### 13. Furniture assembly

Provide box photographs, model numbers, furniture dimensions, assembly space and access routes. Require an inventory of panels and hardware before assembly and appropriate anti-tip anchoring for tall units when specified by the manufacturer. Test drawers, doors and stability, and retain spare fittings and care instructions.

### 14. Minor fault repairs

Describe the fault, its frequency and previous repair attempts, with safe photographs or a short video. Quotations should separate diagnosis and replacement parts and state when a licensed specialist is needed, particularly for electrical or gas-related faults. Require an operational test and a record of the diagnosed cause and work performed.


## Integrated finishing

### 15. Villa finishing

Villa finishing should begin with the structural condition, service layouts, approved drawings and interfaces between trades. Supply the built-up area, number of floors and rooms, electrical and plumbing drawings, and site photographs. Request a schedule separating plaster, waterproofing, floors, ceilings, paint, doors and sanitary fittings. Compare measured quantities, material brands and thicknesses, approved samples, exclusions and the phased programme. At handover, check floor levels, falls in wet areas, service tests before concealment, finish consistency and the closure of recorded defects.

### 16. Apartment finishing

For a new or refurbished apartment, record room dimensions, existing floor and wall finishes and ceiling conditions. Establish whether removal, waste disposal and protection of lifts and corridors are included. Photograph kitchen and bathroom connections, distribution boards and air-conditioning outlets; coordinate work affecting shared services with building management. Compare supply, installation, warranties and final cleaning using the same quantities rather than vague lump sums. Inspect joints, door operation, sockets and sanitary fixtures, and confirm any damage to shared areas has been repaired.

### 17. Retail shop finishing

A shop fit-out depends on the trading activity, customer flow, display arrangements and stock storage. Provide a dimensioned plan, ceiling heights, shopfront details and requirements for lighting, ventilation, signage, power and data. Separate partitions, floors and painting from joinery, glazing and operational systems. Specify finishes suitable for the expected wear and verify applicable safety requirements with the relevant authorities and property management. Compare bills of quantities, shop drawings, lead times and testing. At handover, inspect doors, service outlets, point-of-sale areas and shopfront detailing.

### 18. Office finishing

Start with staff numbers, meeting rooms, printer and server locations, and circulation routes. A coordinated layout must align partitions with air conditioning, lighting, electrical points and network cabling. Supply net floor area, ceiling heights, acoustic requirements and floor finishes suitable for rolling chairs. Distinguish fixed finishes from furniture and IT equipment so responsibility remains clear. At handover, test power and data points against the desk plan, check lighting and door operation, and collect test records and as-built layouts.

### 19. Interior space renovation

Renovation need not mean a full strip-out. Mark which floors, doors and services remain and which are to change, supported by room photographs and measurements. Investigate dampness, cracks and concealed services before pricing removal of finishes. For occupied properties, specify furniture protection and separation of work areas. Ask for separately priced repair and replacement options, waste removal and reinstatement of adjoining surfaces. Approve samples and transitions between old and new materials, then inspect alignment, functionality and site cleanliness.

### 20. Decorative fit-out works

Convert reference images into dimensioned details for gypsum features, wall cladding, concealed lighting and decorative elements, including maintenance access. Provide wall lengths, ceiling heights and positions of AC outlets and electrical points. A rendering alone is insufficient without sections showing substrates, fixing methods and materials. Compare panel or timber specifications, finishes, fixings and access provisions, and approve a physical colour and texture sample. Inspect joints, corners, lighting alignment, secure attachment and removable access covers.

### 21. Integrated finishing handover

This service coordinates acceptance of a complete finishing package rather than a single trade. Request the approved scope schedule, revised drawings, materials register, warranties and relevant waterproofing, plumbing and electrical test records. Walk through each room and log defects with photographs, locations, priorities, correction dates and reinspection requirements; freshly painted surfaces do not by themselves constitute acceptance. Reconcile contract items with actual quantities and completed works. Confirm equipment operation, waste removal, keys and agreed documents before final sign-off.


## Gypsum and decorative works

### 22. Gypsum ceiling installation

Before closing the ceiling, establish the finished level, clear room height, air-conditioning outlets, sprinklers, lights and maintenance hatches. Supply a dimensioned room plan and photographs of existing beams and services. Ask for the board specification, its suitability for moisture or fire-performance requirements where applicable to the approved design, hanger spacing, metal framing and joint treatment. Compare quotations per square metre including framing, finishing and cut-outs rather than board cost alone. At handover, check levelness, secure suspension, cracks around openings and access to concealed services.

### 23. Gypsum partition construction

Explain whether the partition divides an office or a residential room and whether acoustic insulation, moisture resistance or support for cabinets and screens is needed. Provide length, height, door positions and service layouts. Request details of stud and board thickness, insulation infill, internal reinforcement and floor and ceiling connections. Electrical work, doors and painting should be separately identified in the quotation, along with the treatment of junctions with existing walls. Inspect plumbness, rigidity, finished joints and door openings for vibration or misalignment.

### 24. Concealed lighting coves

A lighting cove conceals the light source and shapes its direction, so specify the intended lighting effect, glare control and how LED strips or drivers can be replaced. Send perimeter measurements, ceiling height, switching and power locations. Request a section showing recess depth, LED setback, ventilation and maintenance access. Compare gypsum construction separately from LED fittings, drivers and controls. At handover, test the lights in a darkened room for even illumination, unwanted shadows and access to drivers without damaging the finish.

### 25. Decorative gypsum features

Begin with a reference photograph and a layout locating cornices, frames and ornaments relative to doors and furniture. Measure actual wall lengths, corners and heights. Ask for a sample profile, fixing method, filler and paint specification, and confirmation whether bespoke moulds are included. Compare linear-metre rates, corner pieces and surface preparation rather than relying on a lump sum. Inspect repeated patterns, straight edges and the absence of visible joints or fixing marks under grazing light.

### 26. Wood-look decorative panel installation

Specify whether the panels will be used on an interior wall or in an area exposed to moisture or impacts; a wood-look label does not establish the material or its performance. Supply wall dimensions and photographs showing sockets and corners. Request the material composition, thickness, moisture limitations, fixing method, allowance for thermal movement and edge details. Compare covered area, waste, trims and joints, and approve a physical colour and texture sample. At handover, check grain direction, attachment, cut edges and access to electrical outlets.

### 27. Marble-look decorative panel installation

Inspect wall flatness and possible exposure to heat, water and cleaning chemicals before selecting panels; some decorative products are unsuitable behind heat sources or in continuously wet locations. Provide surface dimensions, socket cut-outs, corners and site photographs. Request material and thickness data, use limitations, adhesive and substrate preparation details and joint treatment. Compare actual coverage, number of seams and edge finishing rather than appearance alone. Inspect vein alignment, adhesion, bulging, voids and sharp edges.

### 28. Gypsum repair and maintenance

Identify whether the problem is a board-joint crack, sagging ceiling or water stain; do not apply filler before finding the cause. Send close and wide photographs, crack length and its position relative to AC or plumbing services. Request an inspection of framing, hangers and moisture sources before deciding between local repair and replacement. Compare the area to be opened and reinstated, joint treatment, paint matching and waste disposal. After repair, check board stability, dryness, joint alignment and whether the marks recur during an agreed observation period.

## Flooring and cladding

### 29. Ceramic tile installation

Specify whether the floor is in a bathroom, kitchen or corridor: slip resistance, water absorption and tile thickness depend on the use. Provide floor area, drain and threshold positions, and photographs of the substrate. Ask for tile dimensions, adhesive, grout joints and falls in wet areas, with removal, levelling and waterproofing priced separately from laying. Compare cutting waste and detailing around corners and drains. At handover inspect joint alignment, secure bonding, extensive hollow-sounding areas and drainage falls with a controlled water test.

### 30. Porcelain tile installation

Large-format porcelain requires checking substrate flatness and the access route for bringing slabs into the property. Supply dimensions, doors, stairs and corner photographs, and state whether the work is flooring or wall cladding. Require a compatible adhesive, edge-levelling method, handling and cutting plan, and movement-joint details. Compare slab dimensions, thickness, layout, waste, cuts and skirting. At handover check lippage, alignment, cracks and chipped edges.

### 31. Parquet flooring installation

Distinguish solid wood, engineered wood and wood-look products before specifying parquet, as moisture and maintenance requirements differ. Provide each room's area, existing substrate, doors and AC locations, and check substrate moisture against the product instructions. Require underlay or adhesive details, perimeter expansion gaps, skirting and room transitions. Compare product grade, wear rating, warranty and separate substrate preparation. Inspect board stability, squeaks, door clearance, joint consistency and edge protection from water.

### 32. Vinyl flooring installation

Vinyl may be supplied as sheet, glued planks or floating click flooring. Define traffic, rolling-chair use and water exposure before choosing. Supply room measurements and photographs of substrate cracks and joints; require a check of smoothness and dryness because defects can telegraph through. Quotations should identify wear-layer thickness, adhesive or locking method, seams, trims and skirting. Compare downtime and the permitted time before use. Inspect edges, flatness, bubbles and raised joints.

### 33. Interlocking paving installation

Interlocking pavers should be specified for pedestrian or vehicle loads, ground conditions and stormwater drainage—not colour alone. Provide site dimensions, entrance and drain levels and ground photographs. Request excavation depth, base layers, compaction, paver thickness and edge restraints, with spoil removal separately identified. Compare area, layer thicknesses and cutting around manholes and trees. At handover check levels, falls, restrained edges and local settlement after compaction.

### 34. Wall cladding installation

Fixing methods differ for stone, ceramic, composite and timber wall cladding. State whether the wall is internal or external, its height, area, openings and any supported loads; photograph plaster condition and dampness. Require framing, adhesive or anchors appropriate to the cladding weight, plus corner, joint, movement and exposure details. Compare physical samples, cutting waste and access to concealed services. Inspect secure attachment, alignment, edges and detailing around windows and outlets.

### 35. Floor repair

A damaged floor may involve loose tiles, cracks, swollen parquet or substrate settlement; the cause matters. Photograph defects close up and in context, record affected area and any recent leaks or movement. Require diagnosis before pricing and distinguish local replacement from wider removal and substrate or waterproofing repair. Compare matching material availability, acceptable colour variation, downtime and waste removal. Inspect stability, levels, joints and affected drains or doors at handover.

## Electrical systems and lighting

### 36. Building electrical first fix

Plan the installation from load schedules and room layouts before closing walls. Supply floor plans, circuit counts, major appliances and AC and kitchen points. Request a qualified designer's circuit schedule covering conduit routes, cable sizing, protective devices and earthing. Compare chasing, reinstatement and testing as separate items. Photograph concealed routes before closing and obtain continuity, insulation and earthing test records from a competent electrician.

### 37. Wire and cable installation

An added circuit or replacement of aged wiring requires more than a cable-length estimate. Document the supply point, anticipated load, route and protection from heat and abrasion. Specify conductor material, insulation rating, cross-section, conduit pulling and identification at both ends. Compare actual cable lengths, access openings and making good. Accept only after insulation, termination and circuit-labelling checks; do not energise untested circuits.

### 38. Distribution board installation

For a new or replacement board, inventory the circuits and loads and check working clearance. Provide photographs of the existing board, main protective device and connected equipment. Ask a qualified professional to review a single-line diagram, circuit schedule and suitable protective devices. Price the enclosure, assembly, reconnection, outage arrangements and testing separately. Check circuit labels, secure covers, access and recorded protective-device tests at handover.

### 39. Socket and switch installation

State the number, positions, mounting heights and expected use of outlets and switches, especially near water or behind furniture. Supply wall photographs and known wiring routes. Specify fittings, suitable protection and fixing methods. Compare a new point with replacement of an existing faceplate, including chasing and repainting. Have a competent electrician check operation, secure mounting, polarity and appropriate protection before acceptance.

### 40. Electrical supply to annexes

A separate annex supply needs the distance from the source, equipment loads and whether the route is external or buried. Share a site plan, passage photographs and entry points. Request load and voltage-drop calculations, cable and protective-device selection, earthing and isolation arrangements. Compare trenching, weather-resistant containment and surface reinstatement separately. Record the buried route before backfilling and obtain insulation and protection tests before gradually loading the supply.

### 41. Electrical network refurbishment

Repeated trips or undocumented alterations call for a circuit survey before proposing complete rewiring. Gather board photographs, fault history, previous modifications and sensitive equipment details. The report should distinguish termination repairs, circuit replacement and board upgrades, including safe phased isolation. Compare inspection scope, as-built diagrams and downtime. Handover should include measurements, an updated circuit schedule and a record of outstanding defects.

### 42. Electrical load assessment

Assess loads before adding high-demand equipment or where breakers overheat or trip repeatedly. Provide equipment ratings, operating patterns and board photographs. Request representative current measurements, phase balance and connection checks under realistic operation. Compare quotations on whether they deliver measured results and redistribution recommendations, not just a visit. Obtain a report of readings, conditions, instruments and actions; upsizing a breaker without checking the wiring is not a solution.

### 43. Indoor lighting installation

Choose fittings around room use, ceiling height, finishes and furniture. Share a plan marking power points, switching and downlight positions. Specify wattage, colour temperature, beam angle and maintenance access. Compare supply, installation, gypsum cutting and wiring alterations separately. Inspect actual light distribution, flicker and alignment against the approved layout.

### 44. Outdoor lighting installation

Entrances, paths and façades require fittings suited to dust, water exposure and maintenance access. Provide site photographs, fixing distances, cable routes and drainage positions, and distinguish security lighting from architectural accent lighting. Specify ingress protection, safe junction positions and light aiming that avoids neighbouring windows. Compare excavation, mounting and controls. Test at night and inspect sealing and water drainage around connections.

### 45. Chandelier installation

A chandelier's weight, ceiling construction, suspension point and clear height matter as much as its appearance. Provide ceiling photographs, dimensions, product weight and assembly details. Require verification that the structural fixing can carry the load independently of the electrical cable. Compare access equipment, assembly, fitting and final alignment. Check secure suspension, balance, clearance and operation of every lighting group.

### 46. Concealed strip lighting

Coordinate concealed LED strips with the gypsum recess, heat dissipation and an accessible driver location. Supply run length, recess section, ceiling photographs and switch positions. Specify strip output, aluminium profile, diffuser and controller. Compare power feeds, drivers and gypsum modifications separately. Inspect a uniform light line, absence of harsh visible dots or abnormal heating, and service access to the driver.

### 47. Garden lighting installation

Map paths, trees, irrigation zones and water services before positioning garden lights. Supply approximate bed dimensions, site photographs and power points. Specify moisture-suitable fittings and protected underground joints, keeping cables clear of routine digging. Compare fitting count, cable routes, excavation, replanting and timers. Test after dark and check for exposed cables or water pooling around connections.

### 48. Motion sensor installation

Select sensors for the actual purpose—corridor, entrance or utility-area lighting—because range, field of view and switch-off delay vary. Provide entrance layouts, mounting heights and photographs of false-trigger sources such as AC discharge or moving trees. Specify sensor type, compatible lighting load, sensitivity and timing. Compare wiring points and switch modifications. Walk-test detection in daylight and darkness and check for nuisance activation.

### 49. Lighting fixture maintenance

For flickering or failed lights, record affected fittings, models and when the fault occurs before replacing everything. Provide label, ceiling and control photographs. Request checks of supply, terminations, drivers and cooling before identifying failed parts. Compare original or compatible parts, access equipment, labour and warranty. Run the repaired lights for an agreed test period and check stable output and safe connection temperatures.

## Plumbing and leak detection

### 50. Water supply pipework installation

Plan new supply pipework around the number and location of outlets and expected supply pressure, rather than pipe length alone. Share kitchen and bathroom layouts and wall-route photographs; request pipe diameters, materials, jointing, isolation valves and maintenance access. Compare chasing and reinstatement separately from pipe installation. Before concealment, obtain a documented pressure test, route photographs and an as-built outlet plan.

### 51. Drainage pipework installation

Drainage performance depends on gradients, venting and cleanout access before finishes are selected. Provide fixture locations, finished-floor levels and the existing connection point. Ask for pipe sizes, routing, inspection openings and any necessary acoustic treatment. Separate excavation, backfill, reinstatement and waste removal in quotes. Accept only after flow and leakage checks and confirmation that maintenance access remains possible.

### 52. Sanitary fixture installation

Before fitting a toilet, basin or mixer, check the product dimensions against water and waste connections and comfortable mounting heights. Share connection photographs, model references and dimensions; specify brackets, seals, silicone and isolation valves. Distinguish product supply, fitting, removal of the old unit and any pipe alterations. Test supply and drainage and inspect stability and every joint for seepage.

### 53. Water tank installation

A tank installation needs a defined capacity, support location, lifting route and cleaning access. Supply base photographs, dimensions and demand information; require a check of support capacity, tank material, cover, vent, overflow and drain. Compare base preparation, insulation, pipework and lifting equipment separately. At handover inspect anchorage, watertight joints, fill valve operation and overflow routing.

### 54. Water pump installation

Select a pump from measured incoming pressure, required delivery head and simultaneous demand. Share the existing nameplate where available, installation space and electrical supply. Request a suitable performance curve, dry-run protection and vibration/noise isolation. Compare pump, pressure vessel, connections, warranty and maintenance obligations. Test pressure at distant outlets and verify leak-free automatic starting and stopping.

### 55. Pipework rehabilitation

Repeated faults in ageing pipes do not automatically justify full replacement. Gather joint photographs, leak history and valve locations; request a survey distinguishing local repairs, section replacement and rerouting. Compare demolition, reinstatement, water shutdowns and pressure testing. Handover should include updated routing records, test results for new connections and identification of retained older pipework.

### 56. Drainage network maintenance

Slow drainage or recurring odours may reflect a blockage, trap fault or inadequate venting. Identify affected outlets, when symptoms occur and inspection-chamber photographs. Request diagnosis before choosing rodding, mechanical cleaning or camera inspection. Compare included pipe length, waste disposal and exclusions for repairs. Verify drainage from multiple outlets, site cleanliness and no wastewater backup.

### 57. Water leak detection

Before opening a damp wall, record meter readings with fixtures off, photographs, onset date and nearby pipe routes. Ask for tests that distinguish pressurised supply leaks from drainage, condensation and waterproofing defects, including equipment limitations. Compare detection fees with excluded opening and repair work. Obtain a report identifying the probable source, evidence and confirmation steps rather than an unconditional no-damage promise.

### 58. Leaking pipe repair

Isolate the affected line and identify pipe material, diameter and defect location before selecting a fitting or replacement length. Share leak and surrounding-area photographs and available pressure information. Specify replacement extent, jointing method and material compatibility. Price wall opening, protection and finish reinstatement separately. Require an appropriate pressure check and joint observation, documenting the repair before concealment.

### 59. Water tank leak investigation

Falling tank level alone does not prove that the tank shell leaks: fill valves, connections and overflows can be responsible. Log level changes over a defined interval and photograph the base, walls and connections. Request separate checks of the shell and attached pipework. Compare draining, cleaning, testing and repair options. Accept a documented level or integrity test identifying the fault.

### 60. Bathroom waterproofing

Coordinate bathroom waterproofing with floor falls, drains and pipe penetrations before tiling. Provide dimensions and photographs of floors, walls and penetrations. Specify substrate preparation, upstand height, corners and joint detailing. Compare membrane layers, protection, flood testing and finish reinstatement. Do not accept concealed waterproofing without a suitable documented test and correction of visible leaks or ponding.

### 61. Roof waterproofing

Investigate roof ponding, outlets, joints and service penetrations before covering defects with another coating. Supply roof area, layout and photographs of cracks and wet spots. Request removal of failed layers, falls correction and perimeter detailing. Compare membrane system, thickness, protection and actual warranty terms. Handover should include drainage inspection, detailing checks and a suitable integrity test before reuse.

### 62. Water tank waterproofing

Tank waterproofing depends on tank construction, condition and whether it stores drinking water. Provide dimensions, photographs of cracks and old coatings, and access and ventilation details. Specify materials suitable for the intended water use and cleaning, preparation and joint treatment. Compare downtime, testing, disinfection and recommissioning. Verify coating condition, watertightness and safe confined-space procedures before acceptance.

### 63. Treatment of leak-related damp

Find the moisture source before repainting: condensation, pressurised pipe leaks, drainage and failed waterproofing need different remedies. Record patch location, growth, weather or usage patterns and photographs from both sides where possible. Request moisture measurements, diagnosis, drying scope and removal of damaged materials. Compare source repair, drying and redecorating separately. Accept after drying checks and an agreed observation period without recurrence.

## Request a quotation

Biet Al Reef customer service accepts requests whether or not companies are published. Provide your name, contact number, Al Reem Island location, service, property type, fault description, photographs, dimensions or equipment counts where relevant, and access constraints. A request is not a confirmation of provider availability, price or appointment.
