# Engineering consultancy, design and interiors on Al Reem Island

Start by identifying property use, the required design stage and the exact drawings or deliverables. This guidance does not imply that a firm is listed or an authority approval has been granted.

## Architectural design and drawings

### 1. Villa design

Begin with a room schedule, occupancy and privacy requirements, supported by plot dimensions and existing plans. Compare guest and service entrances, corridor widths, kitchen and stair locations. Accept coordinated dimensioned plans, elevations and area schedules rather than presentation images alone.

### 2. Residential building design

Specify the proposed unit mix, parking and shared facilities. Test resident circulation, lifts, stairs, plant rooms and service risers; reconcile the area schedule with plans and structural and HVAC coordination before accepting the design package.

### 3. Commercial building design

Describe the actual business, customer circulation, deliveries, storage and operating patterns; offices and retail premises require different layouts. Compare flexibility, service access and equipment locations, and require drawings defining landlord versus tenant fit-out scope and maintenance access.

### 4. Architectural drawing preparation

State whether drawings are conceptual, coordination-level or construction-ready. Agree on plans, sections, elevations, levels, dimensions and opening schedules as appropriate, with a revision register. Check dimensions across sheets before handing the package to a contractor.

### 5. Façade development

Supply elevation photos, dimensions, openings and exposure conditions. Compare cladding options for weight, fixing, cleaning, drainage and window junctions; request material samples and inspectable connection details rather than relying on renderings.

### 6. 3D model preparation

Agree whether the model is for visualisation or multidisciplinary clash review. Provide current drawing revisions, define the required level of detail and mark unsurveyed areas. Review views against dimensions and revision dates; a concept model is not an as-built survey.

### 7. Existing plan amendments

Provide the approved original, site photographs and a precise change list. Distinguish partition changes from alterations affecting structure or services; require marked-up differences, revised sheet registers and discipline coordination before construction use.

### 8. Permit drawing preparation

Identify the property, work type and competent authority before pricing the submission package. Agree on drawing and document lists, submission responsibilities and revision milestones. Check areas, setbacks and elevations for consistency; preparing drawings does not constitute approval.

### 9. Building application submission

Request the document and authorisation checklist, with authority charges separated from consultant fees. Retain submission references, dates, comments and responses; track any revised drawings. Filing an application is not permission to commence works.

### 10. Amendment of approved drawings

Record the requested change, its reason and impact on areas, exits, structure and services. Require a clear before-and-after comparison, identify disciplines requiring renewed review, and control superseded drawings when new revisions are issued.

### 11. Maintenance permit applications

Describe whether the work repairs finishes or alters services or structural elements. Ask the qualified consultant to identify property-specific requirements and documents; distinguish application preparation from permit issuance and retain actual authorisation where required.

### 12. Extension permit applications

Provide existing-condition drawings, photos, dimensions and the proposed extension location. Request assessment of foundations, services and floor-area implications, followed by a documented submission checklist. A proposed extension sketch is not an execution approval.

### 13. As-built drawing preparation

Define the survey boundary: layouts, openings, levels and visible services, excluding concealed elements unless separately investigated. Require dated measurements and photos, flag unverified zones and cross-check critical dimensions before design reuse.

### 14. Government approval coordination

Identify relevant authorities and current official submission requirements for the specific project. Require a tracking log of correspondence, comments, dates and responsibilities, separating statutory fees from coordination fees. Never describe an approval as guaranteed or complete without the issued record.

## Structural design

### 15. Concrete structural frame design

Provide occupancy, spans, loading requirements, architectural drawings and relevant ground investigation. Compare column and beam layouts against usable space; require coordinated structural drawings, reinforcement details and engineer-reviewed calculations before construction.

### 16. Foundation design

Do not choose footings from building area alone. Supply ground investigation findings, column loads and groundwater information where available. Require explicit bearing and settlement assumptions, service coordination, foundation levels and details aligned with the geotechnical report.

### 17. Structural drawing preparation

Define the required issue stage and revision control. Request structural plans, sections, schedules and reinforcement or connection details appropriate to the system. Check grids, levels and openings against architectural and services drawings, recording clashes before tender.

### 18. Structural load review

Explain the trigger, such as changed occupancy, new equipment or tanks, and supply drawings and actual load data. Require assumptions, load paths, affected elements and limitations to be documented; a visual inspection alone cannot certify capacity.

### 19. Building structural safety assessment

Collect dated crack, settlement and leakage photos plus drawings and alteration history. Agree inspection access, testing scope and limitations; require a report separating observations from diagnosis and identifying urgent actions where warranted, without unsupported safety assurances.

### 20. Structural strengthening design

Start with a diagnosis identifying deficiencies, affected members and present and future loads. Compare strengthening methods for constructability, temporary support and protection of services; require fixing details, work sequence, inspection hold points and acceptance criteria.

### 21. Structural engineering reports

Define the engineering question: cracking, alterations, capacity or a specific member. Provide plans, photos and test results; require documented methods, findings, assumptions, limitations, recommendations and the responsible engineer's sign-off rather than an informal verbal opinion.

## Engineering supervision and consultancy

### 22. Construction supervision

Before appointing a supervisor for construction or fit-out works on Al Reem Island, define whether the service involves periodic visits or resident supervision, which disciplines are covered, and where the designer's and contractor's responsibilities begin and end. Require inspection hold points before concealed work is closed, dated site photographs, issue logs and corrective responses. At handover, check visit records, test results and closed defects; supervision does not transfer the contractor's execution obligations.

### 23. Project management

Project management is distinct from technical supervision: it coordinates the programme, budget, procurement, risks and project participants. Supply contracts, drawings, milestones and target dates, and specify who may instruct changes or approve variations. Compare management proposals by meeting frequency, reporting, and claims-control procedures. Deliverables should include decision logs, updated schedules, cost forecasts and a record of approved changes.

### 24. Workmanship quality review

Set inspection criteria for the relevant stage: floor flatness, wet-area falls, cladding fixings, weld quality or applicable test results. Finished-surface photographs cannot establish the quality of concealed waterproofing or services; request test records and pre-closure photographs. The review should identify each defect's location, reference, significance and corrective action, followed by a documented reinspection rather than a generic quality statement.

### 25. Project stage inspections and acceptance

Agree on inspection and acceptance points before work starts, such as foundations, structure, waterproofing, services and finishes, as applicable to the project. For each stage, provide current approved drawings, inspection requests, results and photographs before covering the work. Record accepted items, outstanding defects, responsible parties and reinspection dates. Stage acceptance is neither final project handover nor an authority approval.

### 26. Construction progress reporting

Useful reporting requires the baseline programme, updates, measured completed quantities, delivery records and a log of constraints. Distinguish physical progress from payments: an advance payment does not prove equivalent work on site. Require dated photographs, planned-versus-actual comparisons, critical activities, decisions needed and recovery measures, with the source and limitations of reported percentages stated.

### 27. Quantity surveying and measurement

Measure against drawings with identified revision numbers, agreed measurement rules and explicit units for each item. Separate paint areas from tiling areas, lengths from counts, and explain openings, wastage, supply and installation without double counting. Reconcile the bill of quantities with plans and details, and request auditable calculations identifying assumptions and provisional items. Tender estimates are not final measurements of executed work.

### 28. Contractor tender evaluation

Do not compare headline totals alone. Issue the same scope, drawings and specifications to bidders, then review exclusions, substitutions, materials, warranties, programme and payment terms. Prepare a comparison schedule highlighting unpriced items, quantity differences and variation exposure. The deliverable is a reasoned recommendation and written clarification questions, not a promise that the lowest bidder is suitable or available.
