# Al Wathba — Building materials and supply

This guide helps customers prepare specifications, quantities and delivery arrangements; it is not a list of available suppliers. The source currently marks the “Building and finishing materials” specialty as not selectable. A service description does not imply a published company. Biet Al Reef customer service can receive a quotation request whether or not a supplier is listed.

## Building and finishing materials

### 1. Cement supply

Specify the cement type required by the mix design, bag weight, batch quantity and a dry storage location. Structural concrete and masonry mortar may call for different specifications, so a price per bag alone is insufficient. On delivery, inspect packing dates, damaged or hardened bags, pallet counts and the delivery note.

### 2. Sand supply

Distinguish plastering sand from concrete sand and backfill; specify grading and a consistent quantity unit. Share access photographs and gate dimensions before pricing and confirm whether haulage is included. Check the load for contamination and reconcile delivered volume against the order.

### 3. Aggregate supply

Match nominal aggregate size to the concrete mix specification rather than choosing solely on price. State quantity, segregated storage arrangements and weighing method. Inspect for excessive fines or off-size material during unloading and retain the weighbridge ticket.

### 4. Block and masonry unit supply

Specify block dimensions, wall thickness and intended use, then calculate units with a realistic breakage allowance. Request a sample and strength information appropriate to the drawings; compare stacking and unloading arrangements. Count damaged units before approving the invoice.

### 5. Reinforcing steel supply

Attach a bar schedule listing diameter, grade, length and weight for each item, and clarify whether cutting and bending are included. Do not substitute a steel grade without the responsible designer's approval. Check bar markings, product certificates, diameters and delivered quantities.

### 6. Ready-mix concrete supply

Prepare the specified concrete grade, pour volume, timing, pumping needs and truck access. Clarify delivery windows, waiting charges and the project sampling plan. Reconcile truck tickets, volumes and site test records before accepting the pour.

### 7. Project-wide construction material supply

Break the supply schedule into foundations, structure and finishes with quantities, specifications and required dates to avoid damage from premature storage. Compare line items in consistent units, and clarify shortages and returns. Match each delivery to its purchase order.

### 8. Gypsum board and plaster supply

State whether the order is for ceiling boards, partitions or finishing plaster, including thickness, dimensions and moisture-performance requirements where relevant. Include framing, tape and joint compound rather than counting boards alone. Check edges and packaging for moisture damage on receipt.

### 9. Paint materials supply

Approve the colour sample, sheen, substrate and coat system, then calculate area using realistic coverage rates. Confirm compatibility between filler, primer and topcoat and request matching colour batch numbers. Check seals and dates on delivered containers.

### 10. Waterproofing materials supply

Identify whether the system is for an exposed roof, bathroom or tank, including substrate, joints and penetrations. Request a complete compatible system with primer, detailing and protection rather than one isolated product. Compare technical data, shelf life and container integrity.

### 11. Flooring supply

Provide room plans, laying direction, piece dimensions and intended use, allowing for pattern-dependent cutting waste. Compare thickness, wear layer, underlay and skirting requirements. Match colours, batch references and quantities on delivery.

### 12. Sanitary fixture supply

Confirm basin, toilet and mixer dimensions against water and drainage connections before ordering, including finish and accessories. Compare warranty, spare-part support and whether hoses and valves are included. Inspect ceramics for cracks and verify model numbers.

### 13. Electrical installation materials supply

Tie cable, breaker and conduit schedules to drawings and load requirements, specifying sizes, ratings and reel lengths. Do not approve protective-device substitutions without qualified review. Check markings, ratings, counts and retain product certificates.

### 14. Plumbing installation materials supply

Specify pipe material, diameter, pressure rating and jointing method; separate potable-water and drainage items in the schedule. Compare compatibility of fittings, valves and joining consumables. Inspect markings, sizes and pipe ends on receipt.

### 15. Building materials retail

For counter purchases, bring a precise list of quantities, units and product codes or photographs rather than relying on verbal descriptions. Compare returns policies and delivery charges alongside unit prices. Obtain an itemised receipt and verify the goods before collection.

### 16. Sanitaryware retail

Before buying a replacement tap or toilet, photograph existing connections and measure clearances and openings. Ask about included fittings, spare parts and warranty terms. Inspect plating and ceramic surfaces before transporting the item.

### 17. Electrical supplies retail

Identify the component's purpose, size and electrical rating from a drawing or qualified technician, particularly for breakers and sockets. Compare manufacturer ratings and conformity documentation, not appearance alone. Retain packaging and receipts for traceability.

### 18. Plumbing supplies retail

Bring pipe dimensions, material type and a photo of the old fitting to select the correct part, checking whether it suits pressure or drainage service. Confirm gaskets and accessories before paying. Verify dimensions without damaging the fitting in a trial assembly.

### 19. Paint retail

Test a small colour sample on the actual surface before buying in bulk, specifying sheen, paint type and primer. Compare pack size and coverage rather than price alone, and record colour codes and batch details for later top-ups.

### 20. Waterproofing retail

Show photographs of the leak and substrate, but do not treat buying a waterproofing product as a substitute for identifying the water source. Ask for substrate preparation, coat sequence, tools and compatible accessories. Check expiry and storage conditions.

### 21. Delivery of materials to site

Provide site location, contact details, access restrictions, unloading gate dimensions and lifting requirements, with a delivery slot aligned to the programme. Compare whether loading, waiting and on-site unloading are included or kerbside only. Photograph goods before and after unloading and record shortages.

## Timber and boards

### 22. MDF board supply

Specify board thickness, density and any moisture-resistance grade required for cabinets or interior doors, with a cutting plan and access dimensions for delivery. Compare full-sheet prices with cut-and-edged costs and material waste. On receipt check flatness, swollen edges, sheet count, thickness and the finish colour where applicable.

### 23. Solid timber supply

State the timber species, structural or decorative purpose, finished dimensions and preferred grain orientation; ask how the wood was dried and what moisture condition is suitable for installation. Request photographs of representative pieces and distinguish rough-sawn from machined pricing. Check twisting, splits, unacceptable knots and lengths on delivery.

### 24. Plywood supply

Identify whether the plywood is for formwork, furniture or moisture-exposed locations, specifying thickness, face quality and intended performance. Plywood grades are not automatically interchangeable. Request information on bonding and panel classification; inspect for delamination, corner damage and bowing, and reconcile quantities against the purchase order.

### 25. Melamine-faced board supply

Provide a finish code or physical sample and cabinet drawings, specifying core thickness, texture and whether both faces must match. Compare matching edge-band availability as well as cutting, packing and delivery charges. Verify colour and batch references and reject scratched surfaces or peeling edges before acceptance.

### 26. HPL panel supply

Distinguish thin high-pressure laminate sheets from thick compact HPL panels, and specify thickness, finish and intended wear conditions. Approve a sample, colour and pattern direction, and identify substrate and adhesive where bonding is required. Check dimensions, edges and surface integrity before accepting delivery.

### 27. Joinery hardware supply

Prepare a schedule of hinges, runners, handles and cabinet locks, including door weights, drawer dimensions and opening configurations. Compare load ratings, operating cycles, soft-close function and warranty rather than appearance alone. Test representative hinges and slides and reconcile product codes and counts at handover.

### 28. Board cutting and preparation

Provide a millimetre-based cutting schedule showing each piece, quantity, grain direction, saw-kerf allowance and edge-banding requirements. Agree tolerances, part labels and corner protection, and confirm whether drilling and edging are included. Measure sample pieces against the drawings and inspect cut quality and labels at collection.

## Marble and granite

### 29. Natural marble supply

Approve a stone type and colour sample, slab thickness, cut sizes and vein direction, with access photographs for delivery. Clarify whether slab selection, cutting and transport protection are included. Inspect natural variation, cracks and any disclosed repairs, and record slab identifiers before acceptance.

### 30. Granite supply

Specify whether the stone is for floors, stairs or countertops, including thickness and finish such as polished or slip-resistant. Compare quotes using net area, cutting waste, openings and transport charges. Check batch consistency, edge quality and breakage before accepting the consignment.

### 31. Marble cutting to size

Supply a piece schedule with dimensions, openings, joints and vein direction, allowing for final site measurements and agreed tolerances. Request a slab layout before cutting to manage waste and colour variation. Receive numbered pieces and check dimensions, diagonals and finished edges.

### 32. Marble grinding and polishing

Provide floor area, stone type and close-up photographs of scratches and stains; request a small test patch before choosing a polishing process. Compare grinding stages, protection and final cleaning, without assuming deep stains can always be removed. Inspect finish consistency and any pitting or chemical marks.

### 33. Marble floor installation

Share room dimensions, door thresholds, movement joints and drain positions, and specify laying pattern, stone thickness and suitable bedding system. Require substrate and level checks before pricing. At handover check flatness, consistent joints, secure bedding and drainage falls where relevant.

### 34. Marble wall and façade installation

Provide wall heights, opening drawings, substrate details and exposure conditions, and require a qualified specialist to define the fixing method. Compare anchors, joint details, access and safety arrangements, not just the square-metre rate. Inspect alignment and documented fixings before they are concealed.

### 35. Marble repair and treatment

Photograph cracks, loose pieces or stains and determine whether the cause is superficial, moisture-related or substrate movement. Seek a diagnosis before filling, resetting or repolishing, and approve a repair colour sample. Check repaired sections for stability and acceptable appearance after cleaning.

## Kitchen countertops and vanities

### 36. Kitchen countertop fabrication

Measure the installed cabinets and actual wall geometry, then specify slab material, thickness, colour, edge profile and backsplash. Approve a slab layout showing veins, seams and cut-outs before fabrication. Compare material, machining, reinforcement, transport and waste separately. Inspect dimensions, straight edges and cracks at delivery; appliance openings must not be cut from guessed measurements.


### 37. Kitchen countertop installation

Check that cabinet carcasses are level, secured and capable of supporting the selected surface, and coordinate sink and appliance services first. Ask how the slabs will be fixed and how joints and wall gaps will be sealed. Compare lifting, protection and final sealing in the quotation. At handover inspect levels, seams and cabinet damage, then test for water pooling around the sink.


### 38. Marble vanity fabrication

Provide the vanity width, height, tap and waste positions, and photographs of the wall or support frame. Distinguish a carved stone basin from a stone top carrying a separate basin. Confirm bowl depth, drainage falls, support and waterproof joints before cutting. Compare edge finishing and fittings, then test secure fixing, drainage and leaks at handover.


### 39. Sink and appliance cut-out fabrication

Supply the exact sink, hob or appliance model and its manufacturer's cutting template rather than a generic photograph. Check clearance to slab edges and seams and coordinate cabinetry and pipes below. Agree corner radii and reinforcement of narrow sections. Measure the opening against the template and inspect cut-out corners for hairline cracks before fitting the appliance.


### 40. Quartz countertop installation

Select a specific quartz product, thickness and physical colour sample, and check the manufacturer's restrictions on direct heat exposure. Approve seam locations, pattern alignment, lifting and fixing arrangements. Compare the inclusion of sink cut-outs and backsplashes. Inspect levels, seam finish and colour consistency, and follow the product's care guidance rather than assuming hot-pan resistance.


### 41. Porcelain slab countertop installation

Large-format porcelain needs planned cutting, suitable handling equipment and support, especially near sink openings and thin edges. Provide dimensions, cabinet construction and joint positions; request the slab thickness, support design and compatible bonding system. Compare edge finishing and delivery arrangements. Inspect flatness, seams, chips and cracks after installation.


### 42. Countertop repair and repolishing

Identify the surface material before selecting a treatment: natural marble, engineered quartz, porcelain and solid surfaces cannot all be polished in the same way. Photograph scratches, stains and chips and provide product information if available. Request a small test patch, compare local repair against replacement and inspect the resulting colour, texture and edges without assuming every mark can be removed.

## Ceramic and porcelain tiles

### 43. Ceramic tile supply

Send measured floor or wall areas, a laying plan, tile size and intended use, with an explicit allowance for cutting waste. Ask for absorption and slip-performance information where relevant, and confirm shade, grade and batch codes. Compare supply, adhesive and delivery costs. Open sample boxes on receipt and check breakage, dimensions and colour.


### 44. Porcelain tile supply

Specify indoor or outdoor use, tile dimensions, thickness and finish; glossy tiles should not be selected for wet areas without checking slip performance. Request product sheets, edge type and batch codes, and account for cutting waste and spare tiles. Inspect cartons and sample pieces for shade variation, bowing and damage before installation.


### 45. Outdoor paving tile supply

Explain whether the paving is for footpaths, parking or a courtyard, and provide photographs of the existing base, drainage and expected loads. Compare thickness, slip resistance, bedding and fixing method rather than appearance alone. On delivery check thickness, breakage and consistency, then approve a small laid sample showing pattern, joints and falls.


### 46. Tile cutting and preparation

Supply a laying drawing showing cuts around doors, drains and corners, with verified dimensions and pattern direction. Distinguish straight cuts from mitred edges and circular openings, and agree tolerances and edge protection for transport. Inspect labelled pieces for clean cuts and correct dimensions before installation to reduce waste.


### 47. Ceramic tile installation

Inspect the substrate for flatness, cleanliness and moisture, then agree the laying pattern, movement joints, thresholds and drain levels. Specify a suitable adhesive or mortar system and protect existing waterproofing. Price removal of old finishes separately where required. At handover inspect alignment, joints, adhesion and falls in wet areas.


### 48. Porcelain tile installation

Check tile format and substrate flatness before pricing: large tiles require careful preparation, handling and a compatible fixing system. Agree joint layout, cuts, adhesive coverage and preservation of existing movement joints. At handover inspect lippage, alignment, damaged pieces and secure bedding.


### 49. Skirting tile supply and installation

State the skirting height, net wall lengths and whether the material matches the floor, with photographs of corners and door frames. Agree mitred corners, frame junctions, fixing and any required sealing. Compare the linear-metre price including cuts, adhesives and finishing. Inspect top-line straightness, secure attachment and neat joints.
