# Factory and equipment rental services in Al Wathba

This is an editorial guide to company and establishment services, not a claim that any supplier or machine is available. Individual tradespeople are listed separately.

## Construction and lifting equipment rental

### 1. Scaffolding rental

Specify working-platform height, façade length and the number of working levels, supported by photographs of the ground and access routes. Request an erection plan, edge protection and a pre-use inspection; price transport, assembly, dismantling and rental duration separately.

### 2. Crane rental

Provide the load weight and dimensions, pick-up and set-down positions, and lifting radius rather than weight alone. Compare rated capacity at the actual radius, outrigger footprint, lift plan, operator, insurance and mobilisation; lifting must await site-specific checks.

### 3. Excavation equipment rental

State excavation dimensions, depth, ground conditions, machine turning space and site access, with buried utilities identified before work. Compare excavator size, bucket type, operating hours, haulage and fuel; agree responsibility for spoil removal and edge protection.

### 4. Compaction equipment rental

Define layer area, thickness, fill type and access: a heavy roller is not automatically suitable beside walls or services. Agree the passes and density-testing requirement where specified, with operator, fuel and transport costs identified.

### 5. Concrete mixer rental

Share required batch volume, loading position, power or fuel supply and distance to placement. Compare usable drum capacity, cleaning arrangements and hire hours; a site mixer must not replace specified structural concrete without technical approval.

### 6. Concrete cutting equipment rental

Identify element thickness, cut length, reinforcement and concealed services, with access and slurry-drainage photos. Compare blade and saw selection, cutting depth, dust/noise control and waste capture; structural assessment is required before cutting a load-bearing element.

### 7. General project equipment rental

List equipment against project phases instead of requesting an unspecified package, including delivery dates and access constraints. Check inspection records, maintenance history, breakdown replacement and handover/return terms; separate daily hire from operation and consumables.

## Light tools and equipment rental

### 8. Generator rental

Calculate running loads, motor starting currents and operating hours, then identify location, ventilation and cable routes. Compare kVA rating, fuel autonomy, noise, earthing and protection; connections require competent electrical review.

### 9. Cleaning equipment rental

Describe floor material, area and sensitivity to water or chemicals, and whether washing, extraction or drying is needed. Compare working width, brushes, attachments, tank capacity and run time; test on a small area before acceptance.

### 10. Air compressor rental

Specify tool operating pressure and airflow, simultaneous tool count and hose length. Compare delivered compressor output, air quality, moisture, noise and safety devices; inspect hoses and couplings before operation.

### 11. Power tool rental

Describe the task, material, thickness and access constraints to select the appropriate drill, grinder or saw. Check cables, guards, batteries and bits; allocate consumables, damage and replacement responsibilities in the hire terms.

### 12. Welding equipment rental

State metal type, thickness, welding process, power source and available ventilation. Compare machine output, accessories, shielding gas or electrodes; require spark protection, connection checks and weld inspection before handover.

### 13. Water pump rental

State water source, suction depth, delivery head, required flow and liquid condition, with a photograph of the safe discharge point. Compare the pump performance curve, hose diameter and solids-handling capability; do not discharge to an unapproved location.

### 14. Garden equipment rental

Define working area, plant type, branch diameter and irrigation or cable positions before choosing a trimmer or mower. Compare noise, power source, guards and green-waste handling; inspect blades and safety devices at handover.

## Aluminium and glass factories

### 15. Aluminium profile fabrication

Specify alloy grade, intended use, profile length, cross-section, wall thickness and finish. Supply a drawing or sample with cutting and drilling tolerances; compare dimensional accuracy, straightness and surface protection before accepting a batch.

### 16. Door and window manufacturing

Provide an opening schedule, swing direction, frame, glazing and hardware requirements. Review fabrication sections, weather seals and drainage; compare complete units rather than bare frames and test operation and closure at handover.

### 17. Toughened glass production

Give pane dimensions, thickness, application, edge finish and all required holes before heat treatment; subsequent cutting is not a practical option. Request the relevant glass specification and testing evidence, then inspect marks, edges and visual defects.

### 18. Glass facade preparation

Provide approved opening elevations, movement allowances and design loads established by the project specialist. Compare anchors, sealing, drainage and installation sequencing, requesting performance-test records where specified.

### 19. Partition fabrication

Define whether partitions are internal or moisture-exposed, their height, privacy and any acoustic or fire requirements. Review framing, door positions and service penetrations; inspect alignment, joints and edge finishes.

### 20. Glass supply

List glass type, thickness, dimensions, quantities and required condition with delivery dates. Compare product documentation, packing, edge protection and breakage liability, inspecting each pane before signing receipt.

### 21. Project-specific fabrication orders

Submit an approved fabrication schedule with piece IDs, opening references, shop drawings and staged delivery dates. Separate tooling, transport and installation charges and approve a first article before repeat production.

## Marble and stone factories

### 22. Marble and stone cutting

Supply cutting layouts, slab dimensions, grain direction, thickness and openings. Compare cutting methods, corner protection and waste allowance, checking dimensions and finished edges before dispatch.

### 23. Kitchen countertop fabrication

Measure cabinetry, appliances, sinks and service positions on site; plan joints, edges, overhangs and support. Compare stone type, thickness and treatment, and verify appliance clearances during installation.

### 24. Stair fabrication

Survey tread, riser and landing dimensions, surface direction and slip-resistance requirements from the design. Require piece numbering and fixing details; inspect level consistency and edges before handover.

### 25. Facade stone preparation

Specify cladding anchorage, elevation height, external exposure and approved dimensions. Obtain specialist anchor, joint and movement details; inspect slab identification and edges before installation.

### 26. Decorative carving and detailing

Supply scaled artwork, relief depth, stone type and fragile-detail locations. Approve a physical sample for texture and colour; compare machining time, edge-breakage risk and treatment of finished pieces.

### 27. Marble polishing and treatment

Photograph stains and scratches and identify the stone and existing coating before selecting grinding, polishing or treatment. Require a small trial patch and compare the resulting sheen; avoid acids without confirmed compatibility.

### 28. Contractor stone orders

Send a bill of quantities, cutting drawings, delivery locations and project stages, identifying who verifies final dimensions. Compare unit cost, wastage, packing and storage, approving a prototype before volume production.

## Tools and equipment stores

### 29. Hand-tool sales

Specify intended tasks, hand fit and the material being worked. Compare grip quality, measurement range, warranty and replacement consumables, checking hinges and working faces on purchase.

### 30. Power-tool sales

State tool power, voltage, supply type, duty cycle and work material. Compare accessories, warranty and service support; test controls, guards and operation before accepting the tool.

### 31. Safety-equipment sales

Identify the actual hazard—falling objects, dust, noise or work at height—before choosing protective equipment. Check fit, markings, expiry and use instructions; appearance alone is not a selection criterion.

### 32. Workshop-equipment sales

Define workshop floor space, loads, operating hours, power points and ventilation. Compare machine capacity, footprint, anchorage, training and maintenance; test emergency stops and guards on delivery.

### 33. Cleaning-equipment sales

Describe surfaces, soil types, cleaning area and water or power supply. Compare extraction, brushes, approved detergents, attachments and servicing; test on a representative surface before acceptance.

### 34. Spare-parts sales

Supply a photo of the machine nameplate, exact model, old part and connection dimensions. Confirm functional compatibility rather than appearance alone; compare provenance, warranty and return terms before fitting.

### 35. Equipment servicing

Describe the fault, operating hours, symptoms and maintenance history, requesting written diagnosis before replacing parts. Compare inspection labour, components and final testing; accept the machine after a documented trial.

## Furniture and property moving

### 36. Home furniture moving

Provide a room-by-room inventory, heavy-item photos and lift, stair and access details. Compare wrapping, dismantling, transport, reassembly and any offered insurance; inspect against the inventory at destination.

### 37. Office furniture moving

List desks, devices, cabinets, sensitive files and the available business downtime. Require labelled boxes, an agreed route and device-disconnection responsibility; check placement against the new office plan.

### 38. Furniture dismantling and reassembly

Photograph fittings, hinges and glass elements, identifying items to dismantle. Request numbered panels and hardware and a condition record before moving; inspect stability and alignment after reassembly.

### 39. Furniture packing

Separate glass, polished timber, electronics and fragile items. Compare padding, corner guards, box selection and content labels, checking that items cannot rub or move inside the packaging.

### 40. Loading labour supply

State item counts, weights, loading floors and carrying distance. Require a handling plan for heavy loads and named supervision; compare staff, hours and handling aids without assuming transport is included.

### 41. Temporary storage

Specify furniture volume, storage duration, moisture and heat sensitivity and access needs. Compare inventory controls, packing, protection, insurance terms and retrieval arrangements; inspect seals and contents on collection.

### 42. Inter-emirate property moving

Provide origin, destination, entry constraints, delivery window and item dimensions. Review haulage, packing, loading, unloading and delay terms; compare true door-to-door service scope.

## Project materials and equipment transport

### 43. Building-material transport

Supply a materials schedule listing blocks, steel, bags and bulk loads, with weights and unloading points. Compare permitted payload, securing and weather protection; verify quantities and condition on arrival.

### 44. Timber transport

Specify timber lengths, grades, bending sensitivity, weather exposure and lifting points. Require supports, spacers and restraints to prevent movement; inspect straightness, ends and piece counts at unloading.

### 45. Marble transport

Provide slab sizes, weights, stacking method and breakage sensitivity. Review transport racks, separators, edge protection and restraint; inspect cracks and edges before signing delivery.

### 46. Equipment transport

Identify machine type, mass, dimensions, centre of gravity, operating condition and designated lifting points. Compare transport vehicle, securing and route-specific permissions; record condition at loading and delivery.

### 47. Waste transport

Segregate waste at source and never mix hazardous material into ordinary construction debris. Specify container size, loading method and an appropriately authorised disposal destination where required; request delivery evidence under applicable rules.

### 48. Project truck hire

Specify load weight, dimensions, trip count, loading windows and truck manoeuvring space. Compare vehicle type, any truck-mounted crane, waiting charges and fuel; confirm site access before booking.

### 49. Loading and unloading services

Supply piece counts, weights, lifting heights and breakage or tipping risks. Clarify whether forklift or crane and securing are included; check quantities and condition and record discrepancies at handover.

## Concrete, block and cement-product factories

### 50. Ready-mix concrete production

Specify design-required concrete strength, workability, pour volume, pump location and transit window. Review batch tickets, loading and placement times and sampling arrangements; never substitute a commercial estimate for the structural specification.

### 51. Concrete block production

Specify block size, type, use, quantity and strength requirements from the drawings. Compare dimensional consistency, density, breakage and packing, inspecting samples from deliveries before acceptance.

### 52. Cement-product manufacturing

Supply drawings, dimensions, finish, anticipated loads and curing requirements for precast cement items. Approve a sample before production; compare strength, edge uniformity and storage and delivery methods.

### 53. Project product supply

Provide a bill of quantities, site address, pour or installation sequence and unloading points. Compare delivery windows, minimum orders, haulage and rejected-batch terms, recording counts and damage at each receipt.
