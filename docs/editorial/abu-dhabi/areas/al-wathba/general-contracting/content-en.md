# Building and villa construction in Al Wathba — editorial draft

This guide explains the scope of contracting-company services in Al Wathba without implying a listed provider, confirmed price or available appointment. Source categories marked as not selectable are not presented as bookable.

## Building and villa construction specialty

### 1. Villa construction

Begin with the approved design package, plot dimensions and a clear boundary between structural, waterproofing and finishing works. Ask for a bill of quantities, concrete and reinforcement specifications, material schedules and milestone programme. Compare inclusions, exclusions and testing duties; record inspections and photographs before concealed work is covered.

### 2. Residential building construction

For a multi-unit building, supply floor and apartment counts, service shafts, access routes and parking arrangements. The scope should distinguish structure, lifts, waterproofing, building systems and subcontractor coordination. Compare quantities by floor, inspection hold points and phased handover of apartments and common areas.

### 3. Commercial building construction

Commercial projects depend on intended use, floor loads, utilities and customer or delivery access. Provide drawings, unit areas and known tenant requirements, then distinguish shell-and-core from tenant fit-out. Compare service capacities, commissioning deliverables and the contractual interface between landlord and occupier.

### 4. Building works execution

This covers specified construction packages rather than necessarily a whole-project contract. Attach working drawings, quantities, levels and work-zone limits; identify who supplies materials, access equipment and waste removal. At handover check dimensions, alignment and conformity to drawings, with variations agreed before execution.

### 5. Concrete works execution

Identify whether the package covers footings, columns, beams or slabs, with dimensions, reinforcement drawings and project-specified concrete grades. Clarify formwork, pumping, curing, sampling and pre-pour inspections. A cubic-metre rate is not comparable unless reinforcement, formwork, labour and striking periods are accounted for.

### 6. Structural frame construction

The structural frame follows the engineered sequence of foundations, columns, beams, slabs and other load-bearing elements. Supply structural drawings and coordinated service penetrations; request a pour and procurement schedule with quality controls. Verify reinforcement positions, cover and levels before pours and retain test records.

### 7. Turnkey project delivery

A turnkey contract needs a written definition of ready-for-use completion. State which party handles design, permissions, procurement, finishes, commissioning, training and warranties. Link payments to verifiable milestones and require a defects list plus operating and maintenance records before final acceptance.

### 8. New villa building

For a new villa, establish the room schedule, floor heights, access and service locations before agreeing the programme. Separate earthworks, structure, waterproofing, finishes and external works in the price. Check window, HVAC and flooring specifications and inspect waterproofing and concealed services before closure.

### 9. Residential annex construction

Measure the available footprint, connection to the existing home and routes for drainage, power and cooling. State whether the annex is detached or attached and whether foundations, waterproofing, finishes and utility tie-ins are included. Compare surveyed levels and inspect drainage and waterproofing interfaces at handover.

### 10. Outdoor majlis construction

Specify seating capacity, room dimensions, doors, washrooms, cooling and guest circulation. Separate structure, waterproofing, services and finishes from optional furniture. Check ceiling heights, air distribution and drainage; test lighting and cooling before acceptance.

### 11. Villa extensions

An extension affects an existing structure rather than a standalone plot. Request an as-built survey and engineering assessment of connections, loads and utilities before selecting the construction method. Price protection of occupied rooms, temporary diversions and making good; inspect joints, waterproofing and level transitions.

### 12. Residential building alterations

Alterations may change room layouts, openings or services, with different risks where load-bearing elements are involved. Provide as-built plans and photographs of walls and utilities and identify what must remain. Structural cutting requires qualified engineering review; acceptance includes service tests and reinstated finishes.

### 13. Villa restoration

Restoration addresses existing defects, so record cracks, moisture and deterioration and investigate their causes before choosing repair materials. Request a condition report, dated photographs, removal limits and repair method, separating structural remediation from cosmetic work. Verify the cause was addressed, not merely concealed.

### 14. Residential building renovation

For an occupied building, identify affected units, work sequencing, shutdown windows and resident protection. List finishes and services to be replaced and compare itemised prices by unit, including debris removal. Handover requires service testing and snagging of apartments and shared circulation areas.

### 15. Old building restoration

Start with a condition survey documenting settlement, cracks, corrosion and damp areas through dated photographs and as-built plans. A request for a 'full restoration' is too vague: distinguish elements to retain from those to remove, and separate structural safety assessment from plaster and paint repairs. Ask for temporary support methods, repair materials, protection of sound fabric and a way to measure concealed quantities. At handover review inspection records, photographs of covered work and evidence that the underlying defects were addressed.

### 16. Villa renovation

Prepare a room-by-room schedule with floor areas and photographs of bathrooms, kitchens, finishes and façades, identifying elements that must remain. Compare quotations by demolition, waste disposal, concealed services, waterproofing and finish specifications rather than by lump sum alone. Agree material samples, utility shutdown windows and furniture protection. Accept each room after checking electrical, plumbing and cooling systems and closing the snagging list.

### 17. Apartment renovation

Apartment work brings access, lift, shared-area and neighbour considerations that differ from villa projects. Supply a unit plan and photographs of bathrooms, kitchens and ceilings, and check working-hour arrangements with building management without assuming a specific permit. Require corridor protection, materials and debris handling and post-renovation bathroom leak testing in the scope. Inspect door clearances, floor levels and finishes, and test drainage and sockets before handover.

### 18. Building rehabilitation

Rehabilitation goes beyond repainting: it can restore degraded building components and services so the property performs for its intended use. Commission a condition review covering structure, electrical and mechanical systems, faults and repair priorities. Separate urgent remediation from optional upgrades in the quantities schedule and define commissioning tests and documentation for each system. Acceptance should rely on inspection and operating results as well as updated maintenance records, not appearance alone.

### 19. Structural crack remediation

Photograph each crack with a scale, date and plan location, and establish whether its width is changing before specifying injection or superficial repairs. A qualified structural engineer should assess the cause and safety implications: a paint crack is not treated like a crack in a load-bearing member. Request a repair method statement, product details, substrate preparation and inspection checkpoints. Retain the survey, test results and any required monitoring plan at handover.

### 20. Façade refurbishment

Record the existing façade material, approximate area, working height and photographs of failed coatings, leaks or damaged cladding. The quote should identify access platforms or scaffolding, removal of loose elements, joint and waterproofing repairs and approved colour or texture samples. Compare fixing methods, weather resistance and protection of entrances and passers-by; obtain engineering review where cladding support is uncertain. Inspect alignment, fixings, joints and water-entry points before acceptance.

### 21. Preparing a building for reuse

Before reoccupying a vacant building or changing its use, identify the intended activity, occupancy, exits and the condition of cooling, power, water and drainage. Gather as-built plans and maintenance records and arrange specialist checks of dormant systems and required alterations. Compare repairs, commissioning trials and handover documents separately, and verify any applicable official approvals with the responsible authorities for the specific proposal. Cleaning and repainting alone do not establish readiness for occupation.

## Demolition and concrete works

### 22. Demolition works

Before pricing demolition, mark the exact removal boundaries on plans and photographs and identify any adjoining structure that must remain or live electrical and water services. The contractor's scope should address verified service isolation, protection of neighbouring property, dust control, demolition method and debris-loading zones. Work should not begin before a safety assessment and verification of applicable site permissions with the competent authority. Compare temporary support, removal boundaries, haulage and lawful disposal rather than a square-metre rate alone. At handover inspect retained elements, cleared limits and waste-disposal records.

### 23. Removal of buildings and annexes

Removing a detached annex differs from clearing an entire building, particularly where foundations meet a wall or an occupied structure. Provide entrance and height photographs, approximate dimensions and known underground service routes. Ask how retained structures will be protected, whether buried foundations will be removed, and how excavations will be backfilled. Quotations should explicitly include or exclude slabs, paving, fences, debris haulage and ground levelling. Check final ground levels and agreed boundaries; burying rubble on site is not an acceptable substitute for the agreed removal.

### 24. Concrete cutting and core drilling

Openings for services or alterations can affect reinforcement and load-bearing concrete. Provide the proposed hole diameter or cut length, element thickness, position on a plan and photographs from both sides where accessible. A competent structural assessment is needed before cutting potentially structural elements. The method should identify reinforcement and hidden services, manage slurry or dust and secure falling sections. Compare cutting depth, access, waste removal and finish protection. Check the final opening dimensions, edges and surrounding concrete before acceptance.

### 25. Foundation and footing concrete pouring

Foundation quotations depend on ground information, structural drawings, excavation levels and footing dimensions, not just concrete volume. Ask for excavation, backfill, formwork, reinforcement and any specified waterproofing to be itemised, alongside concrete grade, delivery, placement, curing and tests. The responsible technical team should inspect formation, steel placement, cover and levels before pouring. Compare quantities and exclusions on the same basis and retain inspection records, sample results and pour logs before the work is concealed.

### 26. Concrete roof and slab pouring

Supply structural drawings showing spans, slab thickness, reinforcement and service openings, together with concrete-truck and pump access. The work plan should cover formwork and props, coordinated electrical and mechanical penetrations, pour sequence, compaction and curing. Compare prices with formwork, steel, concrete, testing and the engineer-specified propping period identified separately. Handover evidence includes levels, opening positions, surface condition and test records. Props must not be removed on an arbitrary timetable.

### 27. Concrete wall construction

A concrete wall may be load-bearing, retaining or separating, so its function must be established before selecting thickness, reinforcement and waterproofing. Provide lengths, heights, openings, joints and photographs of adjoining ground or construction. The quotation should describe formwork, steel, concrete, anchorage and construction-joint treatment, plus designed drainage and waterproofing where it is a retaining wall. Inspect alignment, levels, openings, surface quality and pour records; unexpected honeycombing or cracking requires technical assessment.

### 28. Demolition debris removal

Estimate waste types and volumes, separating concrete rubble, timber and metals from materials that may need special handling. Photographs of stockpiles, loading access, entrance width and truck standing space help define trips and loading equipment. Compare container provision, labour, haulage, fees and evidence of delivery to an appropriate receiving facility. Do not assume every material can enter ordinary waste streams. At completion check for remaining rubble and sharp debris and retain relevant transport and disposal records.
