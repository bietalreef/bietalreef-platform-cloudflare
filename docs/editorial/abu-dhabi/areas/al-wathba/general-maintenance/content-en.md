# General maintenance and finishing services in Al Wathba

This editorial draft describes company and establishment services, not confirmed availability or prices. Biet Al Reef customer service can receive a quotation request even when no company is listed. Include the customer's name, contact number, area, service, photographs, measurements, fault description and preferred timing. Verify technical and regulatory requirements for the actual work before proceeding.

## General maintenance

### 1. Villa preventive maintenance

A villa maintenance plan should list pumps, air conditioning, plumbing and doors as separate assets rather than promise a generic visit. Provide floor count, equipment photographs and fault history; compare inspection intervals, consumables and exclusions. Accept each visit against signed readings, repairs and follow-up recommendations.

### 2. Building preventive maintenance

For a multi-unit building, identify common areas, plant rooms, equipment and the division of responsibility between owner and occupants. Share operating drawings and fault logs. Compare planned visits, response arrangements, covered systems and reporting; verify closed work orders before approving payment.

### 3. Interior damage repairs

Photograph the defect with a scale and distinguish scratches, cracks, swelling and moisture staining. Establish the cause before filling or replacing finishes and protect adjacent furniture and flooring. Compare repair area, materials and colour-matching limits; inspect adhesion and surface evenness after curing.

### 4. Door and window repairs

Describe whether the fault affects hinges, locks, frames or seals; include product labels and opening dimensions where possible. Alignment adjustment may be preferable to full replacement. Compare parts, removal and refitting costs, then test smooth operation, locking and weather sealing where relevant.

### 5. Moisture remediation

Do not repaint a damp patch before distinguishing condensation, plumbing leaks and water ingress. Supply dated photos and note whether it worsens with water use or rainfall. Compare diagnosis, drying, source repair and verification methods; monitor the area before signing off.

### 6. Crack treatment

Photograph cracks beside a ruler, noting orientation, length, first appearance and any movement, settlement or sticking doors. Suspected structural or active cracks require qualified assessment before cosmetic filling. Compare inspection, preparation and repair specifications; verify curing and monitoring rather than appearance alone.

### 7. Pre-handover building preparation

Before handover, prepare a room-by-room snag list covering doors, paint, switches, leaks, drainage and lighting. Number each photo and specify repair, cleaning or testing. Quotations should map each item to an acceptance criterion; walk through and close the list with the recipient.

### 8. Minor household maintenance

Group small jobs into one photographed list, noting wall substrates and access, such as hinge adjustment or fixing a loose fitting. Compare call-out charges, fasteners, travel and task limits; do not hide hazardous electrical or plumbing work under a general handyman description. Check each repair and site cleanliness.

### 9. Curtain installation

Measure opening width, ceiling height and fixing position; identify curtain type, track and fabric weight. Photograph the wall and nearby AC or lighting obstructions. Compare inclusion of tracks, brackets and drilling; test travel, alignment and secure fixings at handover.

### 10. Shelf installation

State shelf dimensions, intended loads and whether the wall is plasterboard, blockwork or concrete. Require checks for concealed services and suitable anchors; compare shelf count and supplied fittings. Inspect level and stability using a safe representative load.

### 11. TV wall mounting

Provide screen size, weight, VESA pattern, wall type and preferred viewing height. Agree socket positions, cable routing and whether a tilting or articulating mount is needed. Compare bracket and cable-cover inclusions; verify secure fixing, movement and cable slack.

### 12. Accessory installation

Accessories such as towel rails, mirrors and hooks need different heights, fixings and substrates. Supply item photos and intended locations, including any wet-area sealing requirements around penetrations. Compare anchors and consumables; inspect alignment, secure attachment and undamaged tiles.

### 13. Furniture assembly

Share package photos, model numbers, manuals and unit counts; confirm room dimensions and assembly space. Clarify missing-part handling, anti-tip anchoring where appropriate and packaging disposal. Check drawer travel, door alignment and unit stability at completion.

### 14. Minor fault repairs

Describe the symptom rather than request a vague repair: a squeaking hinge, loose handle or non-functioning fitting, supported by a short video if useful. Separate on-site adjustments from parts replacement or work requiring a suitably qualified specialist. Compare diagnostic charges and parts; retest the original function.

## Integrated finishing

### 15. Villa finishing

Start with room, bathroom and kitchen drawings and coordinated HVAC and electrical points before seeking prices. Provide net areas, ceiling heights, structural-stage photographs and specified tile, door and paint samples. Ask for measured quantities separating substrate preparation, waterproofing, testing, protection, materials, installation and exclusions. Accept the work in stages: inspect waterproofing before concealment, then floor levels, equipment operation and closure of photographed defects.

### 16. Apartment finishing

Define the work inside the apartment separately from access through lifts, corridors and shared services. Supply a furniture layout, room measurements, utility locations and current-condition photos, and establish building access rules before deliveries. Compare measured quantities, protection of existing finishes, debris removal and disruption to services. At handover inspect tile edges, doors, drainage and lighting and record defects in a numbered snag list.

### 17. Retail shop finishing

The scope depends on whether the shop needs display areas, storage, visitor circulation or specialist equipment. Share a floor plan, storefront dimensions, ceiling height, display loads and utility positions. Separate storefronts, counters, lighting and flooring from specialist systems in the quotation. Confirm responsibility for building-management and relevant authority coordination without promising approvals. Compare durability and maintenance needs, then inspect safe routes, edges, lighting and door operation.

### 18. Office finishing

Establish workstation numbers, meeting spaces, acoustic privacy and data requirements before choosing partitions and finishes. Provide the staff layout, air outlets, power points and daylight conditions. Request flooring and wall-finish durability and cleaning specifications. Compare partitions, ceilings, flooring, services and reinstatement separately rather than relying on a single area rate. Test doors, lights, outlets and data points during acceptance.

### 19. Interior space refurbishment

A refurbishment may replace finishes without changing the layout, or rearrange spaces that require engineering review of walls and services. Photograph existing conditions, identify items to retain and measure openings, wall lengths, floor levels and damp areas. Quotations should distinguish limited demolition, protection, waste disposal and concealed repairs that cannot be firmly priced before opening up. Inspect transitions between old and new finishes and verify equipment operation and final cleaning.

### 20. Decorative fit-out works

Provide reference drawings or photographs with the dimensions, lighting locations and materials of each feature rather than a vague premium-design description. Confirm that walls or ceilings can support the proposed elements and that concealed services remain accessible. Request colour and finish samples, edge details, joints, fixings and maintenance access. Compare quantities, complex detailing, materials and any included lighting. Inspect alignment, symmetry, secure fixing and lighting consistency.

### 21. Integrated finishing handover

Handover means checking contractual completion, not simply receiving finished photographs. Assemble the quantity schedule, approved drawings, variation register and warranties. Request test records, inspection sheets and a record of installed materials. Compare the final payment application with completed work, log outstanding items and agree who closes them and when. Test drainage, doors, lighting and equipment, then confirm cleaning, keys and document delivery before signing.

## Gypsum and decorative finishes

### 22. Gypsum ceiling installation

Set the finished ceiling level and confirm space for ducts, cables and maintenance hatches before framing. Share room dimensions, lighting layouts and photographs of existing services. Request board type, metal framing, suspension spacing and joint treatment appropriate to the design. Compare whether light and AC openings, insulation, finishing and paint are included. Inspect levelness, corners, joints and continued access to concealed equipment.

### 23. Gypsum partition construction

State whether the partition provides visual separation, acoustic privacy or a service enclosure, and measure its height, length and door openings. Board type, studs, internal insulation and supported wall fixtures change the design. Request details of floor and ceiling anchorage and wall junctions, and identify backing for screens or cabinets in advance. Inspect plumbness, door operation and finished joints; do not assume a fire-resistance rating without appropriate certified documentation.

### 24. Recessed cove lighting construction

A cove needs a section drawing showing recess depth, width, light direction and driver location. Provide room measurements, luminaire layout and desired colour temperature, while retaining access for LED-strip replacement. Compare gypsum construction, electrical preparation, strips and drivers as distinct items and check component compatibility. Test after dark for even illumination, visible hot spots and exposed wiring.

### 25. Decorative gypsum mouldings

Cornices, frames and ornaments require profiles showing repeated details and junctions at corners and doorways. Supply wall heights, substrate photos and the intended pattern; ask for a short sample before full installation. Compare moulding material, bonding or mechanical fixing, filling and painting, including corner finishing. Inspect consistent profiles, secure attachment and smooth joints without gaps or protrusions.

### 26. Wood-look wall panel installation

Identify whether the surface faces moisture, sunlight or impact: products sold as wood alternatives vary substantially. Request a physical sample, dimensions, thickness and cleaning guidance. Measure walls, openings and edge details. Quotations should specify surface preparation, fixing method, corners, joints and any movement allowance. Check batch colour consistency, alignment, secure fixing and unobstructed sockets and service hatches.

### 27. Marble-look panel installation

A marble appearance does not establish heat or moisture resistance; verify the actual material for its intended location. Supply wall dimensions, basin and outlet positions, corners and a preferred vein pattern. Request a joint-layout sample and compare thickness, cutting, adhesive and edge treatments, not only area. Inspect adhesion, joint alignment and suitable perimeter sealing; decorative panels must not be treated as waterproofing by default.

### 28. Gypsum repair and maintenance

First identify whether damage comes from a leak, moving joint, impact or weak fixing. Photograph both close-up and wider views with a scale, and note hidden services or loose sections. Repair moisture sources or structural fixing faults before closing the ceiling or partition. Match board type, patch backing and joint treatment; compare removal, replacement and repainting. Inspect adhesion and flatness and monitor for renewed staining where relevant.

## Flooring and wall cladding

### 29. Ceramic tile installation

Measure floors or walls, recording doors, drains, corners and cuts; nominal tile size alone does not determine waste. Check substrate flatness, moisture and waterproofing condition in wet rooms. Request samples of tile and grout and an adhesive specification suited to the substrate. Compare quotations for preparation, laying direction, joint width, skirting, sanitary-fitting cuts and disposal rather than a single square-metre rate. At handover inspect joint alignment, tile stability, drainage falls where applicable and freedom from scratches or adhesive residue.

### 30. Porcelain tile installation

Large-format porcelain requires different handling, cutting and installation planning from smaller ceramic tiles, especially around openings and long edges. Supply room measurements, a proposed layout, product references and thickness, and distinguish wall from floor use. Ask how the installer will prepare the substrate, select adhesive, achieve adequate backing coverage and accommodate joints and movement. Compare cutting, waste, thresholds and levelling costs. Inspect edge lippage, pattern continuity and tile stability using agreed inspection criteria.

### 31. Parquet flooring installation

Specify whether the product is solid wood, engineered wood or a composite floor: moisture tolerance, cleaning and fixing methods differ. Measure the rooms, door clearances and existing floor levels, and record daylight direction. Request substrate moisture assessment and any required underlay or moisture barrier. Quotations should identify fixing method, expansion gaps, skirting, transitions to tiles and protection during later works. Walk the entire floor at handover, checking raised or moving sections, board ends and thresholds.

### 32. Vinyl flooring installation

Choose vinyl for the actual foot traffic, water exposure and loads from wheeled furniture or equipment. Provide a room plan, substrate photographs and intended use; request a physical sample, wear-layer specification and cleaning instructions. Fully bonded sheet or tile systems differ from click-lock planks, so clarify levelling, crack treatment, adhesives, joints and edge details. Compare preparation, material, warranty conditions and maintenance requirements. Check joints, straight rows, adhesion and the absence of bubbles or lifting edges.

### 33. Interlocking paving installation

Paths and vehicle parking require more than a count of paving blocks: intended loading and water drainage influence the base design. Supply dimensions, levels and ground-condition photographs, identifying pedestrian and vehicle zones. Ask for specified excavation, compaction, bedding, edge restraints, laying pattern and block thickness appropriate to the design. Compare excavation and waste removal, sub-base construction and finishing as separate items. At acceptance check falls, levels, secure edges and settlement under suitable trial use.

### 34. Wall cladding installation

Cladding may consist of stone, tile, sheet panels or lightweight decorative materials; weight and wall construction determine suitable fixings. Photograph and measure the wall, noting openings, outlets, damp patches and heat exposure. Request samples and drawings showing joints and corners. The scope should state substrate preparation, adhesive or mechanical fixing, movement detailing and access to service points. Compare edge cutting and reinforcement as well as material costs. Inspect alignment, anchorage, colour consistency and unobstructed vents and service equipment.

### 35. Flooring repairs

Identify the floor material and photograph both the defect and surrounding area: loose tiles, cracks, swollen wood, peeling vinyl or sunken paving. Investigate causes such as water leakage, weak bedding or possible structural movement before replacing only the visible damaged section. Provide affected dimensions and details of any spare matching materials. Ask quotations to separate removal, underlying repairs and replacement, and compare local patching with wider renewal when defects recur. At handover check levels, stability, joints and transitions to adjacent flooring.

## Electrical installation and lighting

### 36. Building electrical rough-in

Begin with room uses, equipment locations and anticipated loads rather than a simple count of outlets. Provide architectural plans and kitchen, cooling and water-heater layouts. Request circuit and conduit routing drawings, spare capacity, conductor and protective-device specifications, earthing provisions, making-good work and test scope. Have a qualified designer check the arrangement. Photograph concealed routes before walls close; at handover obtain insulation and earth-continuity test results and labelled circuit schedules.

### 37. Wire and cable installation

Explain whether cables are being added or replacing an existing run, with approximate route lengths, load type and indoor or outdoor exposure. Cable sizing depends on installation method, grouping, ambient temperature and protection as well as appliance power. Ask for cable type, cross-section, conduit route and accessible termination arrangements. Compare material, pulling and testing costs separately. Check terminal identification and insulation-test records before energising.

### 38. Distribution board installation

Supply photographs of the existing board, supply details and an inventory of connected circuits and loads. A qualified specialist should check board capacity, protective-device coordination, earth-leakage protection, ventilation and safe access. Request a single-line diagram, circuit schedule, breaker specifications and labelling, identifying any incoming supply alteration. Compare the protection design, not merely the breaker count. Handover should include connection checks, earthing and appropriate protection tests.

### 39. Socket and switch installation

Mark locations, mounting heights, nearby water sources, furniture and appliances, and provide wall photographs. Replacing a sound faceplate differs from installing a new point that needs wiring and suitable protection. Specify device ratings, mounting boxes and finish repairs; do not assume ordinary sockets suit high loads or wet locations. Check cover alignment and firmness, and have a qualified electrician verify polarity, earthing and operation.

### 40. Electrical supply to annexes

Describe whether the annex will be a majlis, kitchen, storage room or conditioned space, since each has different circuit demands. Provide the distance from the supply, crossing routes, annex plans and outdoor photographs. Request a supply-capacity assessment, cable protection and routing, lighting and socket circuits and suitable separation of loads. Applicable project approvals must be checked with the relevant authorities before work. Handover includes circuit drawings, tests and proper reinstatement of trenches.

### 41. Electrical network rehabilitation

Repeated trips, heat marks or ageing wiring warrant a documented inspection before deciding on wholesale replacement. Provide fault history, board photographs and the affected rooms, and explain whether occupants will remain during works. Quotations should separate diagnosis, cable or board replacement, wall repairs and final testing. Compare whether proposals address the root cause and protection compatibility. Obtain final inspection records and updated as-built drawings.

### 42. Electrical load assessment

Request this when adding high-demand equipment, experiencing repeated trips or planning an extension. Prepare an appliance inventory with ratings and operating periods, plus board photographs. A qualified professional should take suitable current measurements and examine circuit and phase loading where relevant. Distinguish nameplate ratings from simultaneous demand and check cable and breaker capacity before recommending upgrades. Compare measurement periods, reporting and recommendations; obtain recorded findings rather than verbal assurances.

### 43. Indoor lighting installation

Define whether each room is used for reading, work, relaxation or circulation, then provide dimensions, ceiling height, finishes and furniture positions. Request a layout considering illuminance, glare, colour temperature and maintainability rather than an arbitrary spotlight count. Compare luminaires, drivers, controls, ceiling cut-outs and finishing repairs. At handover assess light uniformity, switch operation, objectionable flicker and maintenance access.

### 44. Outdoor lighting installation

Identify paths, entrances, façades and seating areas and where equipment faces water or dust. Supply site photographs and proposed supply routes. Specify luminaires suited to their exposure, protected wiring and light direction that avoids neighbouring windows. Compare environmental ratings, mounting materials, controls and commissioning tests. Check sealed connections, secure fittings and adequate path coverage without glare or hazardous shadows.

### 45. Chandelier installation

Check fixture weight, ceiling height, supporting structure, suspension location and cleaning access before purchase. Provide the chandelier specification, supply-point photograph and clearance over furniture or walking routes. Heavy fittings must not depend on plasterboard alone; suitable structural anchorage and electrical coordination are required. Specify who handles lifting, assembly, fixing and commissioning. Verify balance, height, suspension security and wiring at handover.

### 46. Concealed lighting installation

Provide a section showing recess depth, strip or luminaire position, driver location and maintenance access. Share ceiling dimensions and plasterboard photographs, and request a lighting sample before final closure. Compare strip density, diffuser quality, heat dissipation, power supplies and controls, including accessible joints. At handover check continuous light lines, colour consistency, absence of visible hot spots and abnormal heating.

### 47. Garden lighting installation

Map paths, planting beds, trees and irrigation points, distinguishing route illumination from feature lighting. Request protected cable routes away from excavation and watering works, and fittings appropriate to burial or moisture exposure at their actual locations. Compare trenching, wiring, supports, controls and future servicing. Test the result after dark for safe movement without glare, and inspect connection sealing after irrigation testing.

### 48. Motion sensor installation

State whether the sensor serves an internal corridor, outdoor entrance or parking area, as detection patterns, timing and ambient-light settings vary. Provide installation height, door locations and likely unwanted triggers. Check compatibility with the lighting type and load, operating delay and any required manual override. Compare coverage, weather resistance and adjustability. Test approach from realistic directions, time-out and reactivation before acceptance.

### 49. Lighting fixture maintenance

Describe whether the fault is flicker, outage, dim output, overheating or a damaged cover; provide photographs and model numbers if available. Safe diagnosis should distinguish the luminaire from its driver, switch or supply rather than replacing parts indiscriminately. Request compatible spares, warranty details and safe access provisions for elevated fittings. Compare repair costs with an equivalent replacement and verify sustained operation, secure fixing and no recurring fault.


## Plumbing and leak detection

### 50. Water supply pipework installation

For a new build or relocated kitchen and bathrooms, begin with a drawing showing outlets, pipe routes, diameters, isolation valves and penetrations. Share room dimensions, supply location and existing-route photographs. Ask for pipe material, jointing, supports and protection at wall crossings. Compare quotations by outlet count, lengths, testing and making good rather than a point rate alone. Record a pressure test and inspect for leakage before closing concealed runs; retain the valve and pipe locations.

### 51. Drainage pipework installation

Drainage differs from pressurised supply: gradients, ventilation, cleaning access and connection levels govern performance. Provide fixture positions, available levels and floor drawings. Specify pipe sizes, traps, junctions and accessible cleanouts. Confirm whether excavation, backfill, leak tests and floor reinstatement are included. Before concealment, inspect installed falls and test flow through the system; check for persistent odours or backflow.

### 52. Sanitary fixture installation

Confirm actual dimensions and models of toilets, basins, mixers and showers before fixing supply and waste outlets. Photographs and clearances to walls and doors help avoid clashes. Ask whether the quotation includes traps, flexible connectors, sealants, supports and removal of old fittings. Compare serviceability and warranties as well as product quality. Check fixing stability, sealed edges, drainage and any drips while fixtures operate.

### 53. Water tank installation

Establish expected demand, available space, cleaning access, base position and fill and overflow routes before selecting a tank. Supply dimensions, site photographs and proposed tank details. Ask for base load checks, material suitability for the intended water use, valves, float control, venting and secure access cover. Separate tank, base, lifting, pipework and insulation costs. Handover should include filling and draining checks, leak inspection and safe access for future cleaning.

### 54. Water pump installation

Low pressure does not automatically justify a larger pump: first assess pressure, flow, building height and existing pipe restrictions. Share the number of bathrooms and floors, plus tank, power-supply and installation-space photographs. Request the pump duty curve, dry-run protection, vibration and noise provisions, isolation and non-return valves. Compare equipment, controls, pipework and servicing costs. Test simultaneous demand, automatic start-stop and leakage before acceptance.

### 55. Pipework rehabilitation

Recurring leaks or weak flow in older services call for diagnosis before wholesale replacement. Collect repair history, known pipe age and photographs of finishes and access points. Ask which sections can remain, proposed alternative routes, wall and floor disruption and planned water shutoffs. Compare lengths, pipe grades, testing and reinstatement. Obtain updated as-built routing, a pressure test for renewed sections and photographs of concealed joints.

### 56. Drainage network maintenance

Slow drainage, smells or backflow can result from a local blockage, poor falls or inadequate venting. Describe affected fixtures and when symptoms occur, with inspection-chamber photographs if available. Request a cause-led inspection before rodding or opening floors, with protection for existing fixtures. Compare diagnosis, clearing, pipe repairs and waste disposal separately. Test discharge from several points and confirm that the site is clean and backflow has stopped.

### 57. Water leak detection

Unexpected consumption or damp patches require locating the water source before breaking finishes. Share available meter readings, stain photographs, nearby fixtures and symptom timing. Depending on conditions, isolation tests, pressure measurements, moisture surveys or thermal imaging may help; no single technique proves every leak. Request a report identifying suspected location, confidence and a limited opening plan. Compare diagnostic evidence, then verify the repair with an appropriate follow-up test.

### 58. Pipe leak repair

Once the leak is located, establish pipe material, condition and access before choosing a coupling or replacement length. Provide damp-area photographs and any diagnostic findings. Ask for separate pricing of detection, opening, repair and finish reinstatement. Painting over water damage is not a repair. Pressure-test the repaired joint, allow sufficient drying before enclosure and retain a photograph of the repaired concealed route.

### 59. Water tank leak detection

A falling tank level can arise from its shell, connections, float valve or overflow. Investigate these possibilities in sequence. Provide tank type, capacity, location, base and connection photographs, and a record of level changes if possible. Ask for safe testing suited to the tank material, including inlet and outlet isolation. Do not assume an internal coating is appropriate before confirming the source and stored-water compatibility. Obtain the test findings and a repeat filling check.

### 60. Bathroom waterproofing

Bathroom waterproofing must coordinate floor falls, drains and pipe penetrations before tiling. Provide floor area, shower and drain positions and the intended wall upstand extent. Specify substrate preparation, membrane layers, curing and protection during subsequent trades. Compare the coverage of corners and penetrations and the proposed test, not only the price per square metre. Inspect and document a suitable flood test before tiling begins.

### 61. Roof waterproofing

Before replacing a roof membrane where water ponds or ceiling stains appear, inspect drainage falls, outlets, joints and existing layers. Share roof dimensions and photographs of ponding, parapets and equipment. Specify a system suited to exposed or protected conditions, substrate preparation, junction details and protection against weather and movement. Compare thickness, details and warranty exclusions. Inspect adhesion and terminations and perform a suitable leak test before handover.

### 62. Water tank waterproofing

Tank waterproofing differs from roofing because the coating contacts stored water and the interior must remain cleanable. Provide tank construction, dimensions, condition and photographs of cracks and joints. Require evidence that the proposed system suits the intended water use, with preparation, curing and rinsing procedures before return to service. Price crack repairs separately from the waterproofing layers and state the outage duration. Verify watertightness, surface condition and cleaning records.

### 63. Treatment of moisture caused by leaks

Peeling paint or mould after a leak cannot be solved by repainting alone. Record the water source, moisture readings, affected finishes and damage extent, then repair the leak before drying materials. Separate diagnosis, repair, drying, removal of damaged finishes and plaster and paint restoration in the quotation. Extensive fungal growth warrants appropriate occupant precautions and specialist assessment. At handover, review suitable dryness indicators and monitor for returning stains once services are running.


## Air conditioning and cooling

### 64. Split air-conditioner installation

Size the room and record its openings, outdoor-unit location and condensate drain route before selecting capacity. Supply wall and electrical-panel photos and an approximate inter-unit distance. Ask for mounting brackets, copper-pipe length, insulation, wiring, penetrations and making-good to be itemised. Compare installation warranties and evacuation and leak-testing methods; accept after cooling, drainage and noise checks.

### 65. Central air-conditioning installation

Start with zone-by-zone cooling-load assessment rather than floor area alone. Review ceiling heights, occupancy, envelope insulation, duct routes and electrical supply. Request coordinated unit, supply-and-return duct, service-access and drain drawings, plus a capacity and commissioning schedule. Compare zoning, controls, insulation and maintainability. Record airflow, temperature, condensate and commissioning results at handover.

### 66. Concealed air-conditioning installation

Concealed units require sufficient ceiling void, filter access and return-air provision before gypsum closure. Share reflected-ceiling drawings, beam dimensions and any lighting or sprinkler positions. Request coordinated unit, duct and drainage layouts. Compare unit depth, available static pressure, acoustic treatment and filter replacement access. Verify service clearance, condensate drainage and airflow balance before closing ceilings.

### 67. Air-conditioning refrigerant pipework

Confirm refrigerant type, route length, height difference and penetrations before sizing lines; pipe diameters should follow equipment requirements. Record routing conflicts with electrical and drainage services and photograph openings. The quote should identify copper specification, insulation, protection, supports, testing and reinstatement. Obtain documented pressure testing and evacuation appropriate to the equipment, plus concealed-route photographs before closure.

### 68. Air-duct installation

Ductwork should be designed around required room airflow, ceiling space and other building services. Provide plans, corridor dimensions and diffuser positions. Specify duct sizes, material and gauge, insulation, hangers, access points and balancing devices. Compare more than sheet-metal rates: include dampers, flexible connections, terminals and testing. Check joints and insulation and record outlet airflow measurements.

### 69. Air-conditioning equipment replacement

Before replacing a failed unit, obtain a diagnosis distinguishing repairable faults from inadequate capacity or poor air distribution. Photograph the nameplate, location and existing connections. Check replacement compatibility with power, pipework and ducts. Compare removal, transport, disposal, modifications, commissioning and warranty as separate costs. Accept only after performance and drainage checks with the new unit details recorded.

### 70. HVAC commissioning and inspection

For a new or substantially altered HVAC system, request a commissioning plan covering fan rotation, controls, protection, drainage and airflow distribution. Provide as-built drawings, equipment IDs and fault history, and agree occupancy requirements during tests. Compare recorded measurements and reporting rather than a technician visit alone. Handover should include resolved defects and baseline readings for future servicing.

### 71. Air-conditioner cleaning

Reduced airflow or odour may involve dirty filters, coils or condensate pans, although other faults are possible. List unit types and quantities, filter photos and access constraints. Ask exactly which parts will be cleaned and how electrics and furniture will be protected. Distinguish surface cleaning from coil, pan and drain cleaning in quotations. Inspect cleanliness, drainage and leakage after restart.

### 72. Refrigerant charging

Low refrigerant charge calls for leak investigation rather than repeated top-ups without diagnosis. Provide the equipment model, nameplate and service history. Require the correct refrigerant, specified charge and appropriate recovery or handling procedures under the equipment instructions. Price leak detection, repair, testing and charging separately. Obtain operating measurements and recorded charge quantity, and verify that leakage has been addressed.

### 73. Air-conditioning fault repair

For a stopped or intermittently cooling unit, record error codes, timing, breaker status and noises without opening electrical components yourself. Request diagnosis distinguishing controls, sensors, condenser, compressor and airflow issues, with the failed component identified before replacement. Compare diagnosis, parts, labour and warranty separately. Test a complete operating cycle and check that the fault does not recur.

### 74. Air-duct cleaning

Before ordering duct cleaning, establish whether there is actual dust accumulation, contamination or obstruction; whole-network cleaning is not automatically necessary. Provide duct layouts, access photos and filter condition. Specify access, containment, debris capture and protection of occupied areas. Compare the duct sections included and before-and-after inspection records. Check insulation and dampers and obtain a location-specific completion report.

### 75. Central AC unit maintenance

Central plant maintenance should cover filters, coils, belts where fitted, fans, drains and controls, with preventive work distinguished from repairs. Supply an asset register, running hours and alarm history. Require unit-specific checklists and before-and-after readings. Compare visit frequency, consumables, expected downtime and response terms. Collect measurements, unresolved defects and prioritised recommendations.

### 76. HVAC spare-parts replacement

For a replacement component, request its part number, failure diagnosis and compatibility with the unit model rather than a generic description. Share nameplate and part photos where possible. Confirm whether the item is new or refurbished and the warranty terms. Compare fitted-and-tested cost including any reset or calibration. Retain the invoice, part identification and operating-test result.

### 77. Scheduled HVAC maintenance contracts

A useful maintenance contract defines asset counts, types, locations and visit intervals before annual pricing. Share the equipment register and recurring fault history. Separate inspections, cleaning, spare parts, emergency call-outs, response times and exclusions. Compare visit checklists, evidence of work and escalation arrangements, not just visit counts. Review the first service report and corrective-action plan before continuing.

## Smart systems and surveillance

### 78. Smart lighting control

Inventory lighting circuits, switch positions, lamp types and dimming compatibility before choosing controllers. Provide distribution-board and switch-box photographs and check whether a neutral conductor is available where required. Specify local versus remote operation. Compare load ratings, manual operation during network failure and future expansion. At handover, test every circuit and scene, including physical switches after disconnecting the network.

### 79. Smart air-conditioning control

Record AC models and existing control interfaces first: some systems need proprietary integration while others accept infrared commands. Photograph equipment labels, identify rooms and sensor positions, and define intended setpoint limits. The quotation should distinguish actual supported functions, connectivity dependencies and any recurring subscriptions. Test start, stop and setpoint changes from both the unit and phone without conflicting commands.

### 80. Smart lock installation

Measure door thickness, backset, existing latch and lock arrangement before selecting hardware. Share photographs of both door faces and the frame edge. Require an emergency-entry method for depleted batteries and clear temporary-access management. Compare non-invasive fitting with drilling or cutting and check suitability for exposure. Test mechanical latching, inside escape and backup entry, and receive recovery keys or codes securely.

### 81. Motorised curtain installation

Motor selection depends on fabric weight, track length, curtain style and opening direction. Provide opening width and height, ceiling photographs and the power location; identify single or double tracks. Compare motor capacity, noise, backup or manual operation and warranty coverage. At handover, inspect track alignment, end limits and fabric clearance, and cycle the curtains repeatedly.

### 82. Connecting devices to a phone app

List each device brand, model and existing hub before promising a single-app experience. Devices differ in ecosystem support and remote-control functions; request a written compatibility matrix and feature limits. Compare local hub requirements, cloud accounts, subscription costs and household access levels. Test separate user accounts, revoke a user and transfer administrator ownership to the client at handover.

### 83. Smart-home automation scenes

Describe each automation as a trigger, conditions, affected devices and cancellation rule; for example, turning off selected lights when leaving without disabling security lighting. Supply device and sensor locations and exceptions. Require safeguards against contradictory commands and a clear description of offline or power-loss behaviour. Compare the number of included scenes and change costs. Test normal and exception cases at handover.

### 84. Smart-home system maintenance

When controls fail, document affected devices, error messages and fault times before indiscriminately resetting equipment. Diagnosis should examine power, connectivity, hubs, software and integrations while preserving configuration. Request separate pricing for investigation, updates and replacement, with restricted account access; do not send passwords in open messages. Test core functions and receive a usable configuration backup after service.

### 85. CCTV camera installation

Define each camera's purpose, field of view, lighting conditions and cable route before deciding quantity. Supply a simple layout and mounting photos. Request lens, resolution, night performance, recording storage and cable protection specifications. Avoid filming other people's private spaces and verify site-specific privacy or approval requirements rather than assuming permission. Check real coverage, recording playback and account security at handover.

### 86. Video recorder installation

Select recording hardware using camera count, resolution, frame rate and desired retention, not channel count alone. Request a storage calculation distinguishing continuous from motion-triggered recording, plus ventilation and backup-power arrangements. Compare expandability, access security and footage export. At handover, retrieve an older clip and verify time settings, user privileges and disk-failure alerts.

### 87. Alarm system installation

Distinguish intrusion alarms from fire detection and technical alerts: their functions and regulatory requirements differ. Provide an opening and circulation plan, power locations and proposed sensor positions. Require a schedule of sensors, control panel, battery and audible devices. For regulated life-safety systems, verify the relevant authority's requirements before design or installation. Test every zone, power-failure warning and documented settings.

### 88. Access control installation

Identify door and user counts, credential method, operating hours and emergency exit arrangements. Photograph doors and locks and indicate power and network routes. Compare controllers, event logs, user permissions and behaviour during outages without compromising escape routes. At handover, test entry, user revocation, attempted-entry logs and network-failure operation against the agreed design.

### 89. Intercom installation

Specify call stations, indoor monitors, cable lengths and gate-to-building distance. Provide installation photographs and check gate-lock, power and network compatibility, especially for phone answering. Compare audio/video quality, outdoor weather protection and additional monitor capacity. Test calls and gate release from every station, including recovery after network interruption.

### 90. CCTV camera maintenance

Before replacing a camera with no picture, inspect power, cabling, ports, recorder settings and lens condition: the fault may lie elsewhere. Document channel numbers, symptoms and mounting locations. Require a report distinguishing cleaning, reconfiguration, cable repair and replacement. Compare each option and its warranty while preserving existing recordings. Verify daytime and nighttime pictures, playback and alerts.

### 91. Remote CCTV viewing on phones

Record recorder model, available connectivity and supported authentication before enabling remote access; avoid exposing internet ports without a security assessment. Require owner-controlled accounts and additional authentication where supported, with defined authorised viewers and clip-sharing rules. Compare vendor cloud and secure remote access for privacy, capability and cost. Test viewing over mobile data away from the property and revoke the technician's access.

## Painting

### 92. Villa painting

Measure rooms, elevations, walls and ceilings and inspect existing filler before estimating paint quantities. Share photos of damp patches and cracks and the required colour codes. Ask for separate surface protection, sanding, primer, coat count and paint specifications. Compare product coverage rather than tin counts alone. Inspect colour consistency, cutting-in and floor cleanliness after a representative area has dried.

### 93. Apartment painting

An occupied apartment needs a plan for furniture protection, sockets and working hours. Record surfaces, doors, ceilings and existing defects with photographs; specify whether this is a colour change or local repair. Compare preparation, washability, drying times and coating systems. Inspect coverage in daylight and artificial light, then verify furniture reinstatement and site cleaning.

### 94. Exterior building painting

Check adhesion of existing coatings, salts, cracks and moisture entry before selecting façade paint. Supply elevation photos, access points and approximate surface areas. Require crack preparation, primer, weather suitability and a safe working-at-height method. Compare warranties and distinguish fixing moisture sources from painting. Inspect consistent colour, clean edges and documented repairs at handover.

### 95. Door and timber painting

Natural timber, MDF and previously coated doors require different preparation. Identify substrate, existing finish and defects, photographing edges and hinges. Request a scope covering removal or protection, sanding, filling, primer and spray or brush finish. Compare sheen, scratch resistance and return-to-service time. Open and close each door and check corners, hinges and locks for paint fouling.

### 96. Decorative paint finishes

Approve a sample on the actual wall: texture and lighting can make catalogue photographs misleading. Provide wall dimensions, furnishings and lighting photos and specify the intended stone-like, metallic or other effect without assuming exact photo matching. Request a signed-off sample, application method and cleaning or protective coat. Compare preparation, trials and touch-up allowances. Inspect texture consistency from several viewing angles.

### 97. Stripping and treating old paint

Peeling may result from moisture, weak adhesion or incompatible layers; diagnose the cause before repainting. Photograph blistering and flaking and request a limited adhesion check and a defined scraping, cleaning and treatment area. Compare local patching against full removal, including debris disposal and furniture protection. Accept only a stable, dry substrate ready for the new coating.

### 98. Paint maintenance and repainting

Before repainting a complete wall, assess whether isolated damage can be repaired without visible differences in ageing and sheen. Record scratches, stains, cracks and available colour references in clear photographs. Request explicit cleaning, filling, priming, coat count and work boundaries. Inspect repaired sections from several angles and confirm neighbouring finishes are undamaged.

## Waterproofing and thermal insulation

### 99. Roof waterproofing

Establish roof construction, falls, drains, existing membranes and pipe penetrations before choosing a system. Supply dimensions and photographs of joints, upstands and any ponding. Require substrate preparation, layer details, protection and future maintenance access. Compare system suitability and test method rather than price per square metre alone. Obtain appropriate test records and photos of drains and edges before covering.

### 100. Bathroom waterproofing

Coordinate floor waterproofing with drain falls, wall upstands and pipe penetrations before tiling. Provide bathroom dimensions and photos of drains and thresholds. Specify corner and joint treatment, curing time and a suitable ponding test where appropriate. Compare waterproofing, screed falls and tiling as distinct priced items. Do not cover the membrane until testing and any repairs are documented.

### 101. Water tank waterproofing

Inspect tank construction, cracks, joints, existing coatings and intended water use before selecting materials. Share internal photos, access details, dimensions and emptying arrangements. For potable-water tanks, request documented suitability of products for drinking-water contact. Compare cleaning, substrate repairs, coating, curing and recommissioning separately. Obtain inspection and appropriate tightness-test records and cleaning instructions before reuse.

### 102. Building thermal insulation

Identify whether the target is a roof, wall or façade and review existing build-up, thermal bridges and available space. Provide drawings or dimensions, existing thicknesses and junction photographs. Request thermal performance, fire characteristics, moisture compatibility and fixing details for the selected system. Compare installed assembly performance, not board thickness alone. Inspect continuity and closed gaps before finishes conceal the insulation.

### 103. External wall insulation

Clarify whether the problem is rain penetration or heat transfer: they need different systems. Photograph cracks, peeling, openings and edges and establish working heights. Require crack repairs, layer build-up, window junctions, drainage and safe access. Compare whether the proposal addresses the underlying cause and works with the façade finish. Inspect adhesion, edges and recorded treatment areas at handover.

### 104. Rainwater leak repairs

Trace water entry from roofs, façade joints or windows before applying indiscriminate sealant. Record when leaks appear and photograph staining after rain, noting proximity to drains and joints. Request staged investigation separating the entry point from the internal stain. Compare drainage, joint, membrane and finish repairs as separate items. Obtain a controlled water test where safe and suitable, with follow-up after later rainfall.

### 105. Waterproofing maintenance

Repairing an existing membrane requires its system type, age and damage location; an incompatible patch can worsen the defect. Provide photos of blisters, cracks and drains and any old product records. Ask whether local repairs or wider replacement are appropriate. Compare cleaning, joint preparation, compatibility, testing and warranty. Receive pre-cover photographs, suitable tightness-test results and a list of areas needing monitoring.
