# Engineering consultancy and design in Al Wathba — editorial draft

This draft explains company and consultancy services without asserting provider availability or government approval. Biet Al Reef customer service can receive a quotation enquiry even when no company is listed. This batch covers architectural design and drawings only; other specialities remain unwritten.

## Architectural design and drawings

### 1. Villa design

Start with a room schedule, occupants, plot dimensions, access and setbacks shown in available documents. Request plans, elevations and sections indicating heights, circulation and connections between guest and service spaces. Compare the number of design options, revisions and structural/MEP coordination; accept legible drawing files with a reconciled area schedule.

### 2. Residential building design

Define unit and floor counts, entrances, parking, lifts and shared services before developing apartment layouts. Review circulation, privacy, ventilation and maintenance access, and clarify the boundary between architectural and structural work. Ask for a unit-and-area schedule and coordinated plans for review before accepting the stage.

### 3. Commercial building design

State the intended commercial use, unit sizes, loading requirements, access and utility needs. Separate base-building design from tenant fit-out and identify stairs, lifts and plant rooms. Compare proposed deliverables—plans, sections, technical coordination and flexibility for future unit changes—rather than judging renders alone.

### 4. Architectural drawing preparation

Provide measured surveys or reference plans and specify whether drawings are for concept, tender or construction. Agree on scales, levels, dimensions, room names and door/window schedules appropriate to the stage. At handover, cross-check plans against elevations and sections and verify revision identifiers.

### 5. Facade development

Supply photographs or elevation drawings, opening dimensions and solar orientation. Request material and colour options, junction details around windows, maintenance access and rainwater management. Compare proposals by measured areas, materials and fixing methods; do not accept a rendering as a substitute for buildable details.

### 6. 3D model preparation

Specify whether the model is interior or exterior, its level of detail and required camera views. Supply approved plans, levels and finishes so the model follows the intended design. Agree on image outputs and revision rounds; check proportions of doors, windows and furniture before sign-off.

### 7. Existing plan modifications

Attach the latest drawings and identify the requested change and its purpose, with site photographs for existing buildings. Check effects on circulation, structure and services rather than editing architectural lines in isolation. Require marked-up changes and a revision log; revised drawings do not themselves constitute official approval.

### 8. Permit drawing preparation

Identify the project type and relevant authority before confirming required submissions; requirements depend on the works and location. Ask the consultant to specify included drawings, forms, revisions and owner responsibilities. Retain submitted plans, application records and responses; no approval outcome should be promised.

### 9. Building application submission

Prepare owner and project details and documents required by the competent authority, including valid authorisation where needed. Separate application-follow-up fees from official charges and additional consultancy. Request the application reference, deficiency notices and formal decisions rather than relying on verbal status updates.

### 10. Changes to approved drawings

Identify the approved drawing reference and revision and list the proposed changes and reasons. A qualified consultant should assess structural and services impacts and applicable authority requirements before resubmission. Keep a revision comparison, submission record and outcome; do not treat unapproved drawings as authorisation to build.

### 11. Maintenance permit assistance

Describe the proposed works precisely—surface repairs, service alterations or structural intervention—because requirements differ. Ask the consultant to determine whether a permit or approval is required by the competent authority, without assuming every repair needs one. Retain the submission and formal decision before undertaking approval-dependent work.

### 12. Extension permit assistance

Provide existing-building plans, proposed extension dimensions, location and interfaces with boundaries and utilities. Have the consultant review loads, connections and current authority requirements before pricing the approval process. Compare included drawings, studies and follow-up; accept the official application outcome, not a prior promise.

### 13. As-built drawing preparation

This work requires a site survey of accessible dimensions, levels, openings and visible elements, particularly where current drawings are unavailable. Identify inaccessible areas and whether concealed services require separate investigation; visual measurement cannot confirm hidden routes. Accept dated drawings with survey limitations clearly noted.

### 14. Government approval coordination

Have the consultant verify current requirements and prepare a project-specific register of relevant authorities, documents and submission stages. Assign responsibility for responding to comments, revising drawings and preserving correspondence; separate authority fees from professional fees. At handover, request a dated register distinguishing issued approvals from pending applications.

## Structural design specialty

### 15. Concrete structural frame design

The engineer needs current architectural plans showing spans, openings, storeys and intended uses, alongside ground information and expected loads. Ask how the proposed columns, beams and slabs coordinate with building services and architectural clearances. Compare quotations by whether calculations, detailed drawings, coordination and revisions are included. Accept identified drawing revisions, construction notes and the agreed calculation deliverables; architectural visuals alone cannot establish structural member sizes.

### 16. Footing and foundation design

Foundation selection depends on the geotechnical report, loads, groundwater, site boundaries and nearby structures—not floor area alone. Supply borehole results, plans and survey levels and ask the engineer to explain appropriate alternatives, settlement checks and utility interfaces. The quotation should identify the proposed footing, raft or other justified solution, reinforcement details, levels and relevant waterproofing interfaces. Before handover, check consistency between foundation drawings, ground findings and the project design basis.

### 17. Structural drawing preparation

Specify whether the drawings are for tender, construction or alteration of an existing building, since the required level of detail differs. Plans should show grids, levels, member dimensions, reinforcement, connections and details for openings and stairs, coordinated with major service penetrations. Request a drawing register and revision history, including outstanding interdisciplinary comments. At handover, cross-check sections and details against plans and calculations, and ensure the site team uses the correct revision.

### 18. Structural load review

A load review may be necessary when changing occupancy, installing heavy equipment or tanks, adding a storey, or forming significant openings. Supply existing structural drawings and equipment weights, locations and support points, clearly identifying missing records that require investigation. The engineer should state load assumptions, load paths, uncertainties and any tests or strengthening required before works proceed. Obtain written findings and conditions; the review itself is not permission to increase loads or bypass approvals.

### 19. Building structural safety assessment

Progressive cracking, settlement, corrosion or visible distortion calls for a documented inspection of symptoms, locations and history rather than an instant diagnosis from a photograph. The engineer should scope crack monitoring, material tests or intrusive investigation where justified, and recommend precautions if a hazard is suspected. Compare proposals by site visits, tests, analysis and report scope. The final assessment should separate confirmed observations from hypotheses and set out follow-up actions. Suspected immediate danger requires restricting affected use and urgent specialist assessment.

### 20. Structural strengthening design

Strengthening may address verified deficiencies or support a proposed alteration. It should begin with a condition assessment and calculations covering load transfer during and after construction. Identify affected members, access constraints and occupancy restrictions; request material, connection, anchorage, work-sequence and temporary support details as applicable. Compare bids on testing, monitoring and staged design responsibilities, not steel or concrete quantities alone. Obtain drawings, calculations and inspection requirements before proceeding subject to applicable approvals.

### 21. Structural engineering reports

Define whether the report is for a condition assessment, proposed alteration, defect investigation or construction-stage record, because each needs different evidence. Provide drawings, revisions, photographs, logs and test results, and identify inaccessible areas. A useful report separates observations and measurements from assumptions, conclusions and recommendations, and discloses inspection limits and additional testing needs. Check element identifiers, locations, dates, appendices and professional sign-off at handover; a technical report is not itself an authority approval.
