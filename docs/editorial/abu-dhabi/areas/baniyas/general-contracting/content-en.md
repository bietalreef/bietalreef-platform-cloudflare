# Baniyas — General Contracting and Construction

## Guide scope
This guide describes company and establishment services in Baniyas; it is neither a live provider inventory nor a directory of individual tradespeople. To request a quote, provide the property type, drawings or photographs, approximate location, scope dimensions and possible survey access. A listed service does not confirm a published provider or availability. Where no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer support can receive the quotation request without guaranteeing a supplier, price or date.

## Building and villa construction
### Villa construction
Start with approved drawings showing floor areas, storeys and plot limits. Ask for a bill of quantities separating excavation, structure, waterproofing, finishes and building services. Check inspection milestones and handover requirements; a single rate per square metre is not a complete specification.

### Residential building construction
State the number of units, shared spaces, lifts and central services. The quotation should allocate responsibility for structural and MEP coordination, commissioning, and handover documentation for individual units.

### Commercial building construction
Provide the intended use, expected loads, entrances, façade requirements and service needs. Compare proposals on operational requirements, material approvals and tenant-ready interfaces rather than floor area alone.

### Building works execution
Identify whether the request covers blockwork, masonry, structural components or site works, and attach drawings and quantities. Ask how materials, access equipment, waste removal and measurement of completed work are included.

### Concrete works
Supply structural drawings and reinforcement schedules, with the specified concrete grade, placing, curing and testing requirements. At acceptance, check records, results, levels and dimensions; structural changes require designer review.

### Structural frame construction
Separate foundations, columns, beams, slabs and stairs, with hold points before closing formwork or pouring. Compare steel, concrete, formwork and testing inclusions, and request as-built records.

### Turnkey project delivery
Define precisely whether design, approvals, construction, HVAC, kitchens, external works and commissioning are included. Require explicit exclusions, milestone-linked payments and a snag list before final acceptance.

### New villa construction
Review the relationship between the proposed villa, parking, access and outdoor plant locations. Before contracting, coordinate architectural, structural and MEP drawings and assign responsibility for resolving clashes.

### Residential annex construction
Share photographs of the existing building, the annex location, available clearances and contractor access. Assess foundations and service connections, occupied-home protection, and the waterproofing and finishes at the junction.

### Outdoor majlis construction
Specify seating capacity, washrooms, pantry, guest access and cooling. Check ceiling heights, acoustic and thermal insulation, drainage and commissioning of water and air-conditioning systems.

### Villa extensions
Survey existing dimensions and assess structural and service feasibility before finalising the extension. Compare costs for connections, protecting occupied rooms, relocating utilities and reinstating finishes, not just the added floor area.

### Residential building alterations
Describe proposed openings, walls and room changes with photographs and existing plans. Never assume a wall is non-load-bearing; obtain engineering review for structural interventions and plan isolation and reinstatement of services.

### Villa restoration
Photograph cracks, dampness and deterioration, recording locations and when symptoms appeared. Request diagnosis before repair; separate treatment from cosmetic repainting and agree checks of the repaired areas.

### Residential building renovation
Clarify whether the work involves finishes, systems or layout changes, and whether occupants remain. Request a sequence covering demolition, protection, deliveries, testing and cleaning, with handover checks for each unit and common area.

## Restoration and refurbishment

### Restoration of older buildings
Commission a condition survey distinguishing superficial deterioration from load-bearing defects. Supply dated photographs showing cracks and damp patches. Request a scope for safe removal, engineer-designed strengthening if required, compatible repair materials and inspection records. Accept documented treatment, not cosmetic concealment.

### Villa refurbishment
List rooms, bathrooms, kitchens and outdoor elements, and say whether residents remain. Require a room-by-room schedule separating strip-out, plumbing, electrical work, waterproofing, joinery and decoration. Compare shutdowns, materials, exclusions and warranties; test fittings at handover.

### Apartment renovation
Confirm building-management access, working-hour and waste-handling conditions rather than assuming uniform requirements. Measure surfaces and mark plumbing, power and appliance positions. Require protection of lifts, corridors and neighbouring units, photographs of concealed works and leak and functionality checks.

### Building rehabilitation
Rehabilitation can address ageing HVAC, electrical, drainage and safety systems as well as damaged fabric. Start with condition reports, maintenance history and target performance. Price each intervention against an inspection or commissioning result, with phased access if the property remains occupied.

### Structural crack remediation
Record crack width, direction, location and changes over time. Photographs alone cannot determine whether injection or strengthening is suitable. Obtain a structural assessment of the cause and ongoing movement before choosing repairs. Handover should include materials, test records and monitoring recommendations; do not confuse this with paint-crack filling.

### Façade refurbishment
Identify cladding, render, coatings, heights and defects around windows and joints. Survey fixings and water ingress before choosing colours. Compare preparation, access equipment, pedestrian protection, waterproofing or coating specifications and suitable adhesion and visual acceptance checks.

### Preparing a building for reuse
Document the old and proposed uses, occupancy, loads, partition layout and service demands. Assess structure, ventilation, electricity, drainage and circulation before quoting alterations. Do not assume a change of use is permitted; appointed specialists should establish applicable approvals. Collect revised drawings and commissioning records at handover.


## Demolition and concrete works

### Demolition works
Identify precisely what must be removed and what must remain, supported by photographs of walls, slabs and connected services. The contractor should review available drawings, adjoining elements, utility isolation and site protection before pricing. Compare temporary support, removal sequence, plant, dust control and debris transport. At handover, inspect retained structures and finishes for unintended damage.

### Removal of buildings and annexes
Provide the footprint, number of storeys, structural type, equipment access and proximity of neighbouring structures. The estimate must distinguish superstructure removal from foundation removal and specify protection for adjacent walls and paving. Record the agreed demolition limits, inspect final levels and cleared ground, and establish any necessary permissions before work starts.

### Concrete cutting and core drilling
Mark the opening location, diameter or cut length and element thickness on a drawing, with photographs from both sides where possible. Hidden reinforcement and services must be located, and a qualified professional should assess structural implications where applicable. Compare cutting method, dust or slurry containment and finish protection; verify opening dimensions and edges at completion. Cutting is not, by itself, approval for structural alteration.

### Foundation and footing concrete pours
Provide structural drawings showing footing sizes, levels, reinforcement and specified concrete strength, together with relevant ground information where required by the design. Clarify excavation, backfill, formwork, waterproofing and testing responsibilities. Inspect reinforcement before placing and verify grid positions, levels, concrete records and curing before subsequent work proceeds.

### Concrete slab pours
State slab area, thickness, spans, openings and embedded services, with approved structural drawings. The scope should include formwork, propping, reinforcement, placing, curing, testing and engineer-directed striking of supports. Check reinforcement and penetrations before pouring; review test records, honeycombing and dimensional tolerances before accepting the stage.

### Concrete wall construction
Specify whether the wall is retaining, shear or another structural type, together with its height, thickness, loads and any groundwater exposure. A square-metre price alone misses reinforcement, joints, waterproofing and drainage behind retaining walls. Inspect alignment, joints, concrete finish, curing and the tests required by the project specification.

### Demolition waste removal
Estimate debris volume and material types; show the loading route, gate width and proposed skip or truck position. Ask whether loading, trips, access protection and final sweeping are included. Segregate materials requiring specialist handling rather than mixing them with ordinary rubble. Confirm acceptable disposal arrangements with the relevant authority and check for sharp remnants at handover.

## Quotation request checklist
Send a site plan and available drawings, dimensions or quantities, current-condition photographs, required finish standard, occupancy constraints and procurement scope. Ask for a bill of quantities, exclusions, payment milestones and inspection points. Individual tradespeople belong to a separate classification.
