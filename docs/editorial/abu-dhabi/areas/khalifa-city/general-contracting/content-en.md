# Khalifa City — General contracting and construction

Editorial guidance for company and establishment enquiries, not a claim that any contractor is available or approved. Scope should be defined by drawings, plot information and existing conditions, not the locality name alone.

## Building and villa construction

### 1. Villa construction
For a new villa, assemble the plot plan, setbacks and required floor areas, distinguishing built-up area from plot area. Have contractors price foundations, structure, waterproofing, services and finishes separately, and clarify design coordination. Record reinforcement inspections before pours and waterproofing and pressure tests; handover should include commissioning, defects and warranties rather than a visual walk-through alone.

### 2. Residential building construction
For a multi-unit residential building, specify apartment numbers and layouts, entrances, parking, meter rooms and service spaces. Drawings should show common areas, maintenance access and acoustic and water separation between units; the bill of quantities should separate shared works from apartment fit-out. Compare testing and any lift commissioning, then collect operating records, common-area keys and as-built drawings.

### 3. Commercial building construction
Define the intended commercial uses, sales, storage and loading areas before seeking a building quote: their operating requirements differ from housing. Supply expected equipment loads and ventilation, electrical and water points for professional design review. Compare shell-and-core scope with tenant fit-out, and inspect maintenance access, drainage and electrical distribution at handover.

### 4. Building works execution
For partial masonry works, send a drawing with wall lengths, heights and door or window openings, plus photographs of connections to the existing structure. Clarify whether blocks, mortar, lintels, access equipment and waste removal are included or whether labour alone is priced. Check wall lines, plumb and opening sizes before services and plastering, recording departures from the drawing.

### 5. Concrete works execution
Concrete work cannot be priced by cubic metres alone: provide element details, specified grade, pour locations and pump access. Define who supplies formwork, reinforcement, ready-mix, sampling, curing and striking. Before each pour inspect dimensions, cover, steel and embedded services; afterwards review delivery and test records and have any defects assessed by the responsible professional.

### 6. Structural frame construction
For a structure-only contract, define the boundaries: excavation, foundations, columns, slabs, stairs and associated structural waterproofing. Provide structural drawings, reinforcement schedules, openings and pour sequence; require a programme that allows for curing and support removal. Compare steel, concrete, formwork and tests rather than a lump sum, and sign off each level before subsequent trades start.

### 7. Turnkey project delivery
Turnkey is not a scope definition. Use a schedule to assign design, applications, structure, AC, plumbing, kitchen, cabinetry and external works, with explicit allowances for owner-selected finishes. Link payments to inspectable milestones and record how variations affect cost and time. Require commissioning, warranties, final drawings and a defects list before acceptance.

### 8. New villa building
When comparing new-villa bids, issue identical drawings and a finishes schedule naming flooring, doors and equipment rather than saying 'premium finish'. Check exclusions such as boundary walls, external connections and landscaping, and reconcile excavation and foundation quantities to the drawings. Record approved samples and waterproofing, cooling and water-system tests before final handover.

### 9. Residential annex construction
For an annex beside an existing villa, show its position, boundaries, separation from the house, entrances and nearby service connections. Foundation interaction, ground movement and joints may need professional assessment; do not remove load-bearing elements without it. Compare excavation, backfill, connections, waterproofing, finishes and drainage, and inspect the junctions and service tests at handover.

### 10. External majlis construction
An outdoor majlis needs a functional brief before décor choices: seating capacity, entrances, toilets, hospitality area and privacy. Specify room dimensions, ceiling height and AC and drainage routes; compare thermal and acoustic insulation and durable flooring. At handover run the cooling, doors, lighting and drainage and check that finishes can be cleaned and maintained.

### 11. Villa extensions
For a new room, floor or side extension, do not assume the existing structure has spare capacity. Supply as-built information and available foundation or slab photographs, identifying occupied areas. A qualified engineer should assess loads and connections and the protection required for residents. Compare strengthening, new services, joints and waterproofing, documenting concealed connections before finishes cover them.

### 12. Residential building alterations
Distinguish relocating an internal doorway or service route from removing a potentially load-bearing wall. Prepare before-and-after drawings, photographs, opening sizes and electrical, water and AC locations. Ask a qualified professional to identify structural checks or approvals where applicable. Compare demolition, new services, finishes and testing separately, and inspect against the final drawing rather than verbal instructions.

### 13. Villa restoration
Restoration begins with diagnosis: is dampness caused by leakage or condensation, and is a crack in plaster or a structural member? Photograph defects with a size reference and describe their history and earlier repairs. Require separate pricing for investigation, source correction, damaged material repair and refinishing, with engineering assessment where structural damage is suspected. Do not accept repainting without checking moisture and repair results.

### 14. Residential building renovation
For refurbishment of an occupied residential building, list affected flats, corridors, lifts and services, working hours and material or waste routes. Request a phased plan protecting residents and scheduling water, power and cooling interruptions; compare repeatable apartment items separately from shared works. Inspect each area in turn, test systems and close residents' defects with documented sign-off.

### 15. Restoration of older buildings

An older building needs a condition-led repair plan rather than a blanket repaint. Record dated photographs of exposed reinforcement, spalled concrete, persistent damp and cracks that appear to be changing; mark each defect on a floor plan and note earlier repairs. Ask a competent professional to investigate causes before specifying crack injection, corrosion treatment or replacement of deteriorated services. Compare quotations by opening-up extent, removal, repair materials, testing and reinstatement. Retain photographs of concealed stages and inspection sign-off before plastering closes the work.

### 16. Villa renovation

For an occupied Khalifa City villa, prepare a room-by-room schedule of what stays, what is removed and how the family's living spaces will be isolated. Photograph kitchens, bathrooms and AC points, and measure access routes for deliveries. Quotations should separate selective demolition, service upgrades, waterproofing, floors and joinery; 'complete renovation' is not a specification. At handover, check water pressure, bathroom drainage, cooling performance, finished floor levels and the approved samples, then close a documented snag list.

### 17. Apartment renovation

Renovating an apartment affects shared corridors, lifts and building services as well as the unit. Record room dimensions and photographs of moisture or service defects, and establish building-management access, work-hour and waste-removal arrangements before scheduling. Compare removal of existing tiles, substrate levelling, waterproofing and new tiling as distinct items, with responsibility for lift and entrance protection stated. Inspect wet-area test records, electrical outlets, service access panels and the condition of affected common areas.

### 18. Building rehabilitation

Building rehabilitation aims to restore reliable use, sometimes by renewing ageing systems rather than improving appearances. Start with a condition register covering roofs, façades, plant rooms, pumps and cooling equipment, prioritising defects by safety and operational impact. Request a phased programme showing which services can remain in use, necessary outages and tests after replacement. Compare bids against the asset schedule, component specifications, performance criteria and warranties. Receive test records, updated drawings and maintenance instructions at handover.

### 19. Structural crack treatment

A structural crack cannot be classified reliably by appearance alone. Record its position, direction, width and changes over time; photograph the full member with a scale and note settlement or sticking doors. A qualified structural engineer should review drawings and site conditions and determine whether monitoring or further investigation is required before selecting injection, strengthening or another intervention. The proposal should identify the suspected cause, treatment limits and evidence of stability. Concealing a crack with filler is not an acceptance test.

### 20. Façade refurbishment

For a villa façade, measure each elevation and its height, identify the existing finish and map peeling paint, loose cladding and water entry around windows. Close-up images show defects while whole-elevation views establish access and protection needs. Compare surface preparation, crack and joint repairs, coating systems or fixings and physical colour samples, not just a rate per square metre. Inspect adhesion, joints, window-sill falls and drainage; accept the work after access equipment is removed and glazing and paving are clean.

### 21. Preparing a building for reuse

A change of building use begins with the proposed activities, occupant numbers, equipment loads and service requirements, not a vague request for 'fit-out'. Gather as-built plans and photographs of electrical rooms, cooling, sanitary areas and entrances. Seek competent assessment of structural and service suitability and any relevant approvals before alterations. Compare proposals separately for defect repair, capacity upgrades, internal reconfiguration, finishes and commissioning. Receive as-built records, system test results and an explicit exclusions list.

## Demolition, concrete and subsequent specialties

### 22. Demolition works
Before any partial demolition, mark the exact elements for removal on drawings and dated photographs, and identify what must remain protected: columns, slabs, finishes and utilities. A request to 'remove a wall' is insufficient until its structural role and concealed services are assessed. The quotation should separate isolation, any required temporary support, removal sequence, waste handling and protection of access routes and neighbours. Handover involves checking demolition limits, inspecting retained fabric and documenting appropriate waste disposal.

### 23. Removal of buildings and annexes
For an entire annex or building, provide the footprint, storey count and photographs of the roof, structure, foundations and site access, noting fences and paving to retain. Ask bidders to price superstructure removal separately from footings, buried tanks and final grading, since plant and quantities differ. Utility disconnection, debris fall zones, vehicle access and any applicable permissions need review before mobilisation. Check finished ground levels, site boundaries and neighbouring structures at handover rather than treating an empty plot as sufficient.

### 24. Concrete cutting and core drilling
For service penetrations or AC openings, mark the hole centre, diameter or cut dimensions, member thickness, access face and slurry collection point on a drawing. Locate concealed utilities and reinforcement first; potential structural effects require a competent designer's assessment. Compare cutting method, vibration and dust control, safe removal of heavy cut-outs and edge making-good. Measure the finished opening and inspect surrounding cracks or damage; dimensional changes should not be improvised on site.

### 25. Foundation and footing concrete pours
To price footing pours, provide grid lines, each footing's dimensions and founding level, reinforcement details and the designer's concrete specification. Distinguish excavation, dewatering, formwork, waterproofing, backfill and testing from the concrete supply and placing package. Inspect founding surfaces, reinforcement cover and starter bars before pouring; retain delivery tickets, sample results and curing records. Backfill or follow-on construction should await the specified inspections and technical sign-off.

### 26. Concrete slab pours
Slab pours require particular attention to spans, propping, penetrations and embedded electrical or mechanical services. Supply formwork and reinforcement drawings, opening schedules and target levels, and allocate pump, placing, curing and striking responsibilities. Formwork stability, cover, reinforcement and embedded items need inspection before concrete arrives. Handover checks levels, openings, honeycombing and test records; removal of props must follow the designer's direction rather than an arbitrary site timetable.

### 27. Concrete wall construction
State whether the wall is a shear wall, retaining wall or another barrier: loading, reinforcement and joint requirements differ. Provide length, height, thickness and structural details; retaining walls also need ground levels, drainage and rear-face waterproofing arrangements. Compare formwork, footing connections, construction joints, curing and defect repair, not only square-metre rates. Check alignment, thickness, openings, joints and concrete records before acceptance.

### 28. Demolition debris removal
For debris collection, photograph the material types, approximate volume and stockpile location; note gate width, floor level and distance to loading. Heavy concrete, timber, gypsum and mixed waste have different handling needs, as do segregation, labour, skips and receiving charges. Ask for vehicle or skip capacity, trip assumptions, weight limits and protection of lifts and paving. Inspect the cleared area and access routes and request disposal or transfer records where applicable.

### 29. Home garden design
For a home garden, first map how residents move between the entrance, majlis and parking rather than selecting plants from a catalogue. Request measured drawings showing existing planters, door thresholds and drainage outlets, with morning and afternoon site photographs to identify shade. Compare designers on alternatives, construction drawings and irrigation and lighting details, not render count. Handover should include a scaled layout, planting schedule with supply sizes, and edging and material details that another contractor can price without guessing.

### 30. Villa garden landscaping
When landscaping an occupied Khalifa City villa, identify trees, paths and inspection chambers to retain and whether the family must continue using the entrance. Survey levels and locate buried services before creating beds or changing paving. Have bidders separate removal, soil preparation, irrigation, lighting, planting, paths and waste disposal. At handover, test each irrigation zone, confirm water falls away from the building and check installed species and quantities against the approved schedule.

### 31. Outdoor space design
Outdoor space design may concern a covered sitting area, children's circulation or a cooking zone rather than planting. Supply dimensions, door positions, external AC equipment and intended seating; state whether stroller or wheelchair access is required. Ask for sections showing level changes, paving falls and electrical and lighting points. Compare design, material selection and site supervision as separate deliverables, and receive dimensioned specifications rather than relying on an attractive but unbuildable perspective.

### 32. Green lawn establishment
Before establishing a green lawn, distinguish daily play from low-traffic visual cover: use affects grass selection and soil preparation. Measure the actual area and photograph shaded patches, sprinklers and locations where water pools. The quotation should specify unsuitable soil removal, grading, growing layer, establishment method, watering and the initial care period. Green appearance alone is not acceptance: inspect levels, drainage and uniform coverage, and agree in writing how failed patches will be treated during establishment.

### 33. Tree and shrub planting
Tree and shrub planting near a villa boundary is not simply a count: nursery size and future roots affect the position of each pit. Provide a planting plan with distances to walls and pipes and photographs of sun and shade. Specify botanical names, delivery sizes, excavation, soil, supports and initial irrigation. Compare nursery condition, dimensions and any agreed replacement terms; check planting depth, trunk stability and that the root collar is not buried.

### 34. Natural turf installation
Natural turf needs a grass choice suited to shade and intended foot traffic before rolls are ordered. Supply the area in square metres, soil photographs and any sprinkler layout. Ask where turf comes from, when it is cut and delivered, and how the contractor will grade, clear weeds, close joints and water immediately after laying. Compare ground preparation and aftercare, not the roll price alone. Check seams, gaps and high spots, and agree first mowing and the date normal use can resume.

### 35. Artificial grass installation
Artificial turf for decoration has different requirements from a play surface or walkway. State purpose, area and sun exposure before selecting a sample. Request actual product specimens showing pile height, density, backing and infill, plus a section detailing compacted base, drainage and perimeter fixing. Compare material and installation warranties separately, including old-surface removal. At handover inspect pile direction, seams, edges and drainage with a water test; artificial turf can still become hot and needs cleaning.

### 36. Plant and tree installation
Planting a shade tree is not the same job as setting out a hedge or filling decorative planters. Before pricing, identify species, quantities and positions on a simple plan, and photograph the beds, soil and water supply. Specify delivery size by plant height and container size, pit preparation, growing medium, staking and initial irrigation, and clarify who handles heavy-tree lifting. At handover check counts, species, planting depth, supports and water reaching the root zone; future growth cannot be guaranteed without agreed care conditions.

### 37. Tree pruning and shaping
Before pruning a tree beside a wall or walkway, photograph its crown and damaged limbs and estimate its height and clearance from wires and roofs. Ask which branches will be removed, how much canopy will be reduced, how access will be secured and how cuttings will be taken away. Severe topping is not a substitute for assessing tree health. Compare bids by tree size, access equipment and green-waste removal rather than visit count. Inspect cuts, trunk condition and surrounding property; work near power lines requires assessment by the appropriate specialist.

### 38. Lawn maintenance
Yellow turf does not automatically need fertiliser: shade, broken sprinklers or standing water may be responsible. Provide lawn area, grass type if known, photographs of affected patches and watering times. A maintenance scope should state mowing frequency and height, sprinkler checks, weed removal, aeration where appropriate and who pays for replanting. Compare the work included per visit and the inspection log. Accept dated photographs of coverage and drainage condition rather than attendance alone.

### 39. Plant fertilisation and treatment
If leaves become spotted or growth stalls, provide close-up photographs of foliage and soil and disclose recent feeding and irrigation. Do not commission an unspecified spray before diagnosis. Ask whether soil or salinity checks are needed, and require the product identity, dilution, application method, follow-up timing and precautions for children and pets. Price diagnosis, materials and return visits separately. Assess new growth after the agreed interval and record any leaf scorch rather than judging only an immediate colour change.

### 40. Garden pest management
Identify affected plants, damage patterns and where insects were seen, with photographs of both sides of leaves; beneficial insects should not automatically be treated as pests. The proposal should identify the pest and likely cause, then explain manual removal, irrigation changes or targeted treatment where needed, including product details and safe re-entry precautions. Disclose children, pets and fish ponds before work. Compare monitoring and reinspection criteria, not promises of permanent eradication.

### 41. Garden cleaning
Garden cleaning may involve leaf collection, weeding or path washing, each requiring different labour and equipment. Send photographs of paths, beds and waste piles, with an approximate area and loading point. Require separate descriptions for cutting, collecting, sweeping, washing and waste removal, including protection for planting and sprinklers. At handover check that drains are free of leaves, edges are tidy and no waste remains in beds. Confirm whether pest treatment or tree pruning is excluded.

### 42. Garden maintenance contracts
A recurring contract needs more than the phrase 'full garden maintenance'. Attach a landscape plan, tree and lawn inventory and irrigation layout, and specify visit frequency and access hours. Request a schedule separating mowing, pruning, irrigation checks, fertilisation and pest management, with approval rules for materials, spare parts and emergency calls. Compare measurable deliverables: visit logs, before-and-after photographs, leak reporting and responsibility for failed plants. Review site condition and extra charges before renewal.


## Irrigation networks — source category currently not selectable

### 43. Irrigation network design
Design starts with measured water pressure and supply, not dots for sprinklers on a drawing. Provide planting-bed and lawn dimensions, plant groups, levels and valve photographs. Require flow calculations and separate zones for trees, drip lines and turf, plus pipe sizes, filtration and controller locations. Compare hydraulic design and test provisions; accept the work only after every zone runs and an as-built drawing identifies buried routes and isolation points.

### 44. Drip irrigation installation
Drip irrigation for a hedge is not laid out like emitters around an individual tree. Supply bed lengths, planting counts, supply details and photographs of wall obstructions. The quote should identify emitter spacing and flow, filtration, pressure regulation, flush ends and protection from gardening tools. Witness water delivery at both ends of each line and obtain instructions for cleaning filters and clearing blockages.

### 45. Sprinkler installation
Map turf edges, pathways, planting beds, doorways and existing ponding before positioning spray heads. Compare nozzle throw, overlap, operating pressure and pop-up height; water should not be routinely sprayed against walls or onto paths. Watch a full watering cycle after installation, checking dry strips, leaking fittings and whether spray angles can be adjusted without repeated excavation.

### 46. Irrigation tanks and pump installation
Pump selection depends on required flow, head and pipe losses, not motor wattage alone. Share supply details, tank location, elevation changes, service area and connection photographs. Specify dry-run protection, non-return valve, electrical isolation and maintenance access; separate tank, pump, controls and pipework in the quotation. Witness starting and stopping, measured delivery and leak checks, and receive operating and cleaning instructions.

### 47. Irrigation network maintenance
Record which irrigation zones have dry patches, low pressure or abnormal running times, and photograph affected heads or emitters. A sound maintenance visit checks filters, valves, controller settings and leaks before proposing a new pump or wholesale replacement. Ask for a fault report separating cleaning, adjustment and replacement parts, with excavation and reinstatement priced separately. Witness every zone operating after repair.

### 48. Automatic irrigation controller programming
Automatic irrigation schedules should reflect plant type, soil, season and zone count, rather than applying the same runtime everywhere. Supply a controller photograph and current schedule, and note when people use the garden. Ask for programmed days, durations, sequencing and any supported rain or fault overrides. Test manual and scheduled cycles and obtain written settings plus instructions for seasonal changes and power interruptions.

### 49. Irrigation leak detection
Wet ground by a boundary wall can result from overwatering as well as a broken pipe. Note when moisture appears and whether it follows a particular zone; share photographs, any meter readings and available pipe drawings. Require zone isolation and pressure checks before authorising excavation, with pipe repair and surface reinstatement priced separately. Accept the repair after a running test and follow-up observation.

## Shades and external works — source category currently not selectable

### 50. Car shade installation
Measure the number and dimensions of parking bays, vehicle heights, door swing and turning movements before choosing a car shade. Photograph the ground, possible buried services and rainwater outlets. The quotation should separate frame, covering, corrosion protection, foundations, fixings and warranties, and identify who checks applicable permissions. Inspect clearance, anchorage, cover tension and drainage against dimensioned drawings.

### 51. Pergola construction
A pergola attached to a villa needs carefully detailed connections that preserve building waterproofing; a freestanding pergola needs its own foundation assessment. Provide seating dimensions, height, door locations, lighting points and ground photographs. Compare frame material, protective finish, shade coverage, roof, electrical work and drainage. Check post alignment, connections and maintenance access at handover.

### 52. Outdoor seating area construction
State seating capacity, furniture dimensions, access routes, privacy needs and sun exposure. Photograph the existing surface and mark nearby power and water points. Ask for a dimensioned layout showing circulation space and surface falls. Compare paving, fixed seating, lighting, shades and finishes as distinct items. At handover, test water drainage and make sure doors and pedestrian routes remain usable.

### 53. External fence installation
Use a reliable boundary plan where available rather than a rough length alone. Provide fence height, gate positions and level differences. Require details of foundations, posts, infill, finish and corrosion protection, with demolition and gates clearly included or excluded. Verify relevant requirements before work rather than assuming any proposed height is permitted. Inspect alignment, gate stability and exposed edges.

### 54. Garden pathway construction
A path between the villa entrance and garden needs a usable width and falls that direct water away from the building. Provide its route, dimensions and photographs of roots, inspection covers and ponding. Compare sub-base, edge restraint, paving, slip resistance and level transitions, not just tile appearance. Check joints, levels, stability and drainage with a water test at handover.

### 55. Interlocking paver installation
Footpaths and vehicle parking areas impose different loads on interlocking paving. Provide area, gate locations, intended traffic and ground photographs. Require excavation depth, compaction, base and bedding layers, block thickness and edge restraints in the quotation. Compare layer quantities, waste removal and drainage rather than unit rate alone. Inspect falls, joints, edge stability and movement under use.

### 56. Waterfall and fountain construction
A fountain or waterfall needs ongoing operation and maintenance, not just decorative cladding. Specify basin size, desired water effect, acceptable noise, utility connections and any nearby children. Require waterproofing, pump, filtration, make-up water, drainage, electrical protection and access to serviceable components. Witness leak and continuous-operation tests and obtain cleaning, water-use and safe shutdown instructions.

## Construction quotation information

Provide the project type (new build, restoration or extension), site limits and available drawings, floor areas, photographs and access, required stages, material/finish standards and whether premises are occupied. Ask bidders to separate design and permits—where applicable—from construction, tests, handover and warranties; record exclusions and measurement rules. If no company is listed, Biet Al Reef customer support can receive this information without confirming a contractor or authorising work.

**Common question:** Is a square-metre rate enough to compare projects? Only as an early indicator: foundations, MEP systems, finishes, fees and permit responsibilities need like-for-like quantities.
