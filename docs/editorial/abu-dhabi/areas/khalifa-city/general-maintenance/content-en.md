# Khalifa City — General maintenance and finishing

## Khalifa City enquiry context
If cooling is poor in only some Khalifa City rooms, ask for airflow and controls checks before replacing equipment. Confirm property condition, work access and inspection arrangements in the quotation request; the technical guidance below does not establish provider availability.


## General maintenance

### 1. Periodic villa maintenance

Routine villa maintenance is more than a visit after something fails. Start with an inventory of air-conditioning units, pumps, electrical panels, doors and prior faults, noting locations and dates. Ask for a visit plan separating inspection, cleaning, specialist work, emergency call-outs and replacement parts. Compare inspection frequency, response arrangements and recorded readings. At handover, require dated findings, photographs and recommendations rather than a generic statement that maintenance was completed.

### 2. Periodic building maintenance

A building maintenance agreement should identify the number of units, shared spaces, service rooms and equipment within the contractor's responsibility. Provide basic layouts, operating information and access windows. Where lifts or fire systems need specialist attention, identify that separately rather than assuming they fall within general maintenance. Compare periodic reporting, fault-ticket closure and exclusions; receive a log distinguishing recurring defects from completed repairs.

### 3. Interior damage repairs

Photograph interior damage both close up and in context, with a ruler, and identify whether the affected material is plasterboard, tile, paint or timber. A stain behind cabinetry may signal continuing moisture, so repainting should wait until the cause is investigated. Price removal, substrate preparation and matching replacement separately. Inspect a dry, even finish and request photographs of concealed work before it is covered.

### 4. Door and window repairs

For a dragging door or a draughty window, record frame material, leaf dimensions and photographs of hinges, locks and seals. Quotations should distinguish adjustment, replacement of a specific component and full-unit replacement, including hardware compatibility. Open, close and lock the unit repeatedly at handover, checking clearances and seals. Replacing a handle alone will not correct a distorted frame.

### 5. Dampness treatment

Moisture may come from pipe leakage, condensation or failed waterproofing. Record when staining appears, its position and any connection to rainfall or air-conditioning. Request investigation of the source and extent before choosing waterproof coatings or repainting. Compare diagnosis, source repair, drying and reinstatement as separate costs. Receive photographs and suitable inspection findings, including any areas that could not be verified.

### 6. Crack treatment

Photograph the crack beside a ruler and date the image; monitor any change in width or length before covering it with filler. A hairline paint crack differs from stepped cracking in masonry or around an opening, which may require assessment by a qualified engineer. Ask for the suspected cause, edge preparation, repair material and monitoring plan. Retain before-and-after evidence.

### 7. Pre-handover property preparation

Property handover preparation begins with a numbered room-by-room snag register, with photographs, location and responsible party for each item. Separate operational tests of lighting, outlets, sanitary fittings and doors from final cleaning and debris removal; appearance alone does not prove operation. Compare quotations by snag count and return-inspection visits, then close each item with a dated test and photograph in the handover record.

### 8. Minor home maintenance

Bundle small household jobs into one documented schedule: a loose handle, hinge, sealant bead or shelf, with photographs, dimensions and access details. Exposed electrical faults and active leaks should not be treated as minor handyman tasks without specialist assessment. Compare call-out fees, minimum hours and materials separately. Receive a list of replaced parts and a functional check for each completed task.

### 9. Curtain installation

Before ordering a curtain track, measure the window width, side stack-back allowance and fixing height to the floor, and identify the ceiling type and fabric weight. Some tracks require additional supports or suitable plasterboard anchoring. Compare bracket design, fixing points and curtain opening direction. Test full travel and check clearance from air-conditioning units and window handles.

### 10. Shelf installation

A book shelf carries different loads from a lightweight display shelf. State its length, depth and expected contents, and photograph wall construction and nearby services. The quotation should specify board material and thickness, bracket count and anchors appropriate for concrete, blockwork or plasterboard. Check level, stability and surrounding clearances; request a recommended working load rather than an unsafe improvised load test.

### 11. TV mounting

Record the television's weight, dimensions, VESA pattern, viewing position and candidate walls. An articulated arm places different leverage on fixings from a fixed bracket, so screen-size compatibility alone is insufficient. Agree cable routing and socket locations before drilling. At handover, check fixings, move the arm safely through its range and confirm access to ports.

### 12. Accessory installation

In bathrooms and kitchens, photograph the intended positions of towel rails, soap holders and mirrors before drilling, noting tile, stone or timber substrates. Agree suitable fixings, protection against cracked edges and sealing of holes near wet areas where appropriate. Inspect alignment, security, intact cladding and the absence of water-trapping gaps.

### 13. Furniture assembly

Send photographs of cartons, model details, assembly instructions, part counts and lift or doorway dimensions for bulky items. Price assembly, wall anchoring, relocation of old furniture and packaging removal separately. At handover, operate drawers and doors, check level and tightened joints, and document any missing component or pre-existing damage.

### 14. Minor fault repairs

Calling a fault 'minor' does not establish its cause or cost. Record when it occurs, whether it recurs after restarting and safe photographs or recordings of symptoms. The technician should separate diagnosis, parts and labour, and refer electrical hazards or active water leaks for appropriate specialist checks. Receive an operational test, identification of replaced components and an explanation of anything left unresolved.

## Requesting a quotation

Provide the address, property type, clear photographs, task list, approximate dimensions and suitable access times. Compare inspection, repair, materials and final testing scope. If no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer service can receive the enquiry without guaranteeing a provider or price. Individual tradespeople remain separate from companies and establishments.

## Integrated finishing

### 15. Villa finishing

Villa finishing starts by defining the handover level: are the structure and services already complete, or is first-fix work included? Supply floor plans, room and bathroom areas, and choices for floors, ceilings, doors, paint and lighting points. Request measured quantities and specifications for each trade, with plumbing and electrical testing before ceilings or cladding are closed. Compare material supply, labour, waste allowances and warranties separately. At handover collect test records, concealed-work photographs and a signed defects list.

### 16. Apartment finishing

Apartment finishing must account for work within the unit and protection of shared corridors and lifts. Provide room plans, ceiling heights and photographs of the existing kitchen and bathrooms; identify what will be removed and retained. Quotations should cover material and waste movement, wet-area waterproofing and transitions between new floors and door thresholds. Inspect bathroom falls, joints, socket operation and lighting before accepting the work.

### 17. Retail-shop finishing

A shop described as 'ready to open' still needs a precise fit-out scope. State the intended use, sales and storage areas, power points, lighting, cooling and signage positions. Ask for a layout and durable surface specifications, and identify façade or specialist-system work as separate responsibilities. Compare contractors against identical drawings and quantities. Test the fitted equipment and record defects at handover; do not assume any regulatory approval has been obtained.

### 18. Office finishing

Office finishing should coordinate workstation layouts with data, power, lighting, acoustics and ventilation. Provide staff numbers, meeting-room plans, printer and server positions, and permitted working windows. Request floor and partition samples, door performance details and a testing sequence for circuits and network points before furniture installation. At handover test actual workstations and check that service and maintenance access remains clear.

### 19. Interior-space refurbishment

For an occupied interior refurbishment, separate removal and protection from repairs and new finishes. Photograph existing surfaces, record cracks, moisture and floor levels, and identify rooms that must remain usable. Compare the cost of treating hidden defects with cosmetic replacement alone. Agree on real colour and material samples, then inspect edges, doors and final cleanliness after protective screens are removed.

### 20. Decorative fit-out work

Decorative fit-out needs more than a reference photograph. Start with a dimensioned drawing showing wall build-ups, finished depths, lighting, switches and air-conditioning access. Choose materials for the room's use, moisture exposure and cleaning needs, and require fixing details for edges, corners and removable service panels. Compare physical samples and shop drawings. Check joint alignment, colour consistency, secure fixing and lighting operation at handover.

### 21. Integrated finishing handover

Integrated finishing handover is a documented inspection, not simply a cleaned site and a set of keys. Request a contract-linked checklist, waterproofing, electrical and plumbing test records, product warranties and operating manuals. Inspect every room in daylight and artificial light, operate doors, drains and sockets, and log defects with photographs and rectification dates. Close the record only after reinspection of corrected items and written agreement on exceptions.

## Gypsum and decorative work

### 22. Gypsum ceiling installation

Before approving a suspended plasterboard ceiling, measure the clear finished height in every room and locate AC ducts, fire detectors, luminaires and access points for valves and connections. Supply a reflected ceiling plan with levels and hanger positions. Ask bidders to identify board grade and thickness, framing and fixing systems, and joint treatment. Compare access hatches, lighting recesses and painting separately from the square-metre rate. Inspect concealed services before closure, then check levelness, perimeter restraint and finished joints for movement or cracks.

### 23. Gypsum partition walls

A plasterboard partition needs more than a line on the floor. Mark its height, door openings, socket positions and circulation clearance, with photographs of nearby services. Specify board layers, stud spacing, acoustic infill and backing for any television or wall-mounted cabinet; lightweight partitions cannot be assumed to support arbitrary loads. Ask for separate prices for framing, insulation, services, joint finishing and painting. At handover, verify openings and outlets against the drawing, test rigidity and inspect straightness and corner finish.

### 24. Concealed-light coves

A concealed-light cove must allow both good illumination and future maintenance. Provide a section drawing showing cove width, depth and setback, plus each run length and electrical feed location. Request details of the LED strip, diffuser, driver, heat management and how components can be replaced without breaking the plasterboard. Compare cove construction, lighting equipment and electrical connections as distinct items. Test the finished effect in daylight and darkness for uneven brightness, glare and unwanted shadow lines.

### 25. Decorative gypsum features

For gypsum cornices, arches and mouldings, a dimensioned detail matters more than a reference photograph alone. Record wall and ceiling dimensions, projection depths and curve radii, and request a sample of complicated profiles before full installation. Check whether shaping, supports, joint treatment, sanding and painting are all included in the price, with responsibility for defects arising from inadequate fixing clearly stated. Inspect repeated elements, matching corners and consistent lines under side lighting that reveals surface imperfections.

### 26. Wood-effect panel installation

Wood-look cladding panels vary in composition, scratch and moisture resistance and fixing method. Measure wall height and width, door swings, sockets and corners, then approve a physical sample rather than relying on a phone image. Obtain panel thickness, cutting and edge-trim details, and a price that identifies substrate preparation, adhesive or mechanical fixing, spare pieces and joints. At handover, check slat direction, consistent gaps, secure edges and unobstructed door movement; exposed joints near water need particular attention.

### 27. Marble-effect panel installation

Before choosing marble-effect sheets, establish whether the wall faces splashing water or cooking heat; a marble appearance is not evidence of suitability for every application. Measure wall openings and outlets and draw the intended vein direction to avoid mismatched patterns. Ask for material composition, sheet thickness, adhesive and corner-finishing specifications. Compare removal of old finishes, cutting waste and substrate preparation separately. Inspect flatness, pattern continuity and edge adhesion, and do not conceal damp or swollen wall finishes without fixing the source.

### 28. Gypsum repair and maintenance

A cracked plasterboard joint is different from a swollen sheet after a leak or a sagging ceiling section. Photograph the defect in context and close up, record its length and onset, and note any spreading or moisture. Require diagnosis before pricing filler and paint: the board, framing or water source may need repair. Compare local patching with replacing the affected section, including furniture protection and waste removal. Accept only a dry, sound, even surface and inspect paint matching after it has cured.

## Flooring and wall cladding

### 29. Ceramic tile installation


Start with a measured floor and wall layout showing drains, doorways, tile sizes and the intended laying direction. In bathrooms, check drainage falls and test waterproofing before it is concealed; in dry rooms, assess substrate flatness. Ask quotations to separate removal, disposal, levelling, adhesive, grout and skirting, with joint treatment specified. At handover, test drainage and inspect alignment, bedding and neat cuts around sanitary fixtures.

### 30. Porcelain tile installation


Large-format porcelain calls for a substrate flatness check and a handling and cutting plan. Supply dimensions and photographs of access routes, including lifts where relevant. Specify a suitable adhesive system, coverage beneath slabs and movement joints. Compare thickness, cutting allowance, surface preparation and edge detailing rather than unit rate alone. Inspect lippage, consistent joints, cracks and loose tiles before acceptance.

### 31. Parquet flooring installation


First establish whether the product is solid wood, engineered wood or a wood-look alternative: moisture tolerance and installation methods differ. Share room measurements, existing floor photographs and doorway levels; request substrate moisture checks where appropriate. The quote should identify underlay, vapour or acoustic control, expansion gaps, skirting and any door trimming. Walk the completed floor to check squeaks, gaps and swelling, and obtain care and warranty instructions.

### 32. Vinyl flooring installation


Provide room dimensions, substrate condition and photographs of corners and thresholds. State whether the proposed vinyl is click-fit plank or bonded sheet, since preparation and jointing differ. High spots and moisture must be addressed before laying; vinyl will not correct a poor base. Compare wear-layer specification, seams, skirting and doorway transitions. Inspect for bubbles, lifting edges and open joints, and confirm suitability for the intended cleaning regime.

### 33. Interlocking paving installation


Measure the path or parking area, identify pedestrian versus vehicle loading and mark the required drainage direction. Ask for excavation, compaction, sub-base, bedding, edge restraints and paver thickness as separate scope items rather than a paver-only price. Check levels at doors and drains so runoff does not enter the building. At handover, inspect firm edges, even joints, falls and settlement under trial use, with photographs of concealed base layers.

### 34. Wall cladding installation


Wall cladding may use stone, ceramic or decorative panels. Supply wall dimensions and photographs showing sockets, openings and substrate condition, then specify material and weight. Assess whether the wall and fixing method can carry the proposed finish, particularly for heavy panels or wet locations; adhesive and mechanical systems are not interchangeable. Compare preparation, corners, joints and access to service points. Inspect alignment, secure fixing and finished edges.

### 35. Flooring repairs


Investigate why a tile cracked or detached before replacing it. Photograph the defect and adjacent tiles and check for moisture, substrate movement or settlement. Ask the quote to distinguish removal, substrate repair, matching replacement, regrouting and cleanup. For wet areas, specify how waterproofing will be reinstated and checked. Accept an even, secure surface with safe edges and no unagreed colour mismatch.

## Electrical installations and lighting

### 36. Building electrical installation

Before wiring a building or annex in Khalifa City, prepare a room plan showing air conditioners, kitchen equipment, water heaters, lights and sockets, with appliance ratings and the proposed distribution board. Counting outlets is not an electrical design: a qualified specialist must determine circuit allocation, conductor sizes and protective devices for the approved scheme. Ask bidders to separate containment, cables, boards, wall chasing and reinstatement. Photograph concealed conduits before closure, and receive a labelled circuit schedule, relevant test results and as-built drawings.

### 37. Cable and wiring runs

For a new air-conditioner feed or a replacement run to a room, show the start and end points, approximate distance and whether the route is concealed, above a false ceiling or outdoors. Cable selection depends on load, length, temperature and grouping, not simply the cheapest price per metre. Compare the conductor material and size, containment, termination protection and installation labour. Before finishes are closed, request route photographs and appropriate insulation and continuity test records from a qualified technician.

### 38. Distribution board installation

For a new distribution board or an extension of an existing one, supply clear photographs of the current board, available supply information and the proposed circuits and appliances. Have a specialist assess board capacity, circuit allocation, isolation and protection rather than assuming that spare physical slots mean spare electrical capacity. Quotes should itemise enclosure, breakers, protective devices, wiring and associated modifications. Handover should include a legible circuit directory, board layout and earthing and protection test results, not merely confirmation that power is on.

### 39. Socket and switch fitting

Mark photographs or a floor plan with socket and switch quantities, mounting heights and the appliances they will serve. Distinguish a like-for-like replacement from a new circuit or an extended outlet; wet or exposed locations require a different suitability assessment from dry rooms. Compare accessory make and rating, wiring, wall opening and reinstatement as separate scope items. At handover check secure faceplates, correct operation, polarity and earthing as appropriate to the circuit.

### 40. Power supply for extensions

Before supplying an annex, list expected air-conditioning, water-heating, cooking and lighting loads, the distance from the main board and how the cable will pass between buildings. A request for 'one power cable' misses outdoor containment, earthing, protective devices and existing supply capacity. Compare quotations using the proposed supply route, excavation or surface containment, sub-board and reinstatement. Require circuit tests and records before occupation; have the responsible specialist confirm any necessary approvals rather than assuming they apply or have been granted.

### 41. Electrical network rehabilitation

Rehabilitating old wiring starts by diagnosing nuisance tripping, hot outlets and deteriorated insulation, not by automatically stripping every cable. Provide a fault log, photographs of boards and outlets and access arrangements for occupied rooms. Ask for findings distinguishing circuits suitable for targeted repair from those needing replacement, together with safe isolation and wall reinstatement. Compare diagnosis, materials, tests and warranty by scope. Receive an updated circuit register and measured test records, not just a demonstration that the lights work.

### 42. Electrical load assessment

Adding air conditioners, heaters or kitchen equipment may require a load review before board changes. Supply nameplate ratings, quantities, expected use and available board and supply details. A competent specialist should evaluate likely simultaneous demand, circuit balance, cable limits and protective coordination; simply fitting a larger breaker can leave wiring inadequately protected. Compare assessments by their written assumptions, measurements, calculations and recommendations, rather than by the number of visits.

### 43. Interior lighting installation

Start a living-room or kitchen lighting plan with furniture, reading and work areas and air-conditioning positions, not a target number of downlights. Provide ceiling heights, preferred colour temperature and dimming or switching requirements. Request a layout that limits glare and shadows on worktops. Compare fitting models, drivers, gypsum cut-outs, wiring and future service access. Test the lights in the evening with furniture in place, checking switching, consistent colour and absence of flicker.

### 44. Exterior lighting installation

For villa entrances and exterior paths, provide daylight and night photographs and identify pedestrian and vehicle movement as well as surfaces that should not be illuminated. Quotations should specify mounting, cable routes and enclosure protection suitable for water and dust exposure, with excavation and façade repairs itemised. Test one fitting's beam direction before committing to all positions. At handover inspect the installation at night, check sealed connections and avoid glare to neighbours or drivers.

### 45. Chandelier installation

A heavy chandelier is a suspended load as well as a light fitting. Provide its weight, dimensions, mounting instructions, ceiling height and whether the structural slab is concealed by plasterboard. The installer must assess a suitable load-bearing fixing; plasterboard alone is not an adequate support. Compare assembly, access equipment, suspension hardware and electrical connection separately. Receive a level, stable fitting, functioning controls and wiring free from mechanical strain.

### 46. Concealed lighting installation

For cove lighting, supply a section drawing or photograph showing recess depth, each run length and access to drivers. Pricing solely by metres of LED strip ignores strip density, aluminium profiles, heat dissipation, feed length and dimming compatibility. Compare strips, drivers, profiles, gypsum work and wiring as separate items. Run the lights long enough to inspect consistent output at corners, heat management and the ability to replace a driver without breaking finishes.

### 47. Garden lighting installation

Before lighting a garden, map trees, paths, irrigation pipes, sprinklers and the supply point, identifying areas regularly washed or watered. Ask for a layout showing beam direction, mounting height, buried cable routes and suitable protection so later irrigation work does not damage wiring. Compare connection points, protective enclosures, trench reinstatement and testing. Receive photographs before backfilling and an as-built route plan; inspect the lighting after dusk, including interaction with irrigation where relevant.

### 48. Motion sensor installation

A motion sensor suitable for a driveway may not suit an indoor corridor or stairwell. Describe the approach direction, detection area, desired switch-off delay and proposed mounting position with photographs. Discuss false triggers from pets, moving vegetation or heat sources, as well as sensor type and environmental suitability. Compare equipment, wiring and setup. Test approaches from several directions and adjust sensitivity and delay before acceptance.

### 49. Lighting unit maintenance

When several lights fail, record whether the fault is constant or intermittent and whether it follows a particular switch; photograph the fitting label if accessible. The cause may be a lamp, LED driver, loose connection or supply fault, so do not approve wholesale replacement before diagnosis. Separate high-access labour or ceiling removal from component prices, and confirm replacement compatibility. After repair, cycle the lights, check secure mountings and flicker-free operation, and retain the details of replaced parts.



## Plumbing, drainage and leak detection

### 50. Water supply network installation

For a new water supply network in a Khalifa City villa, provide a drawing showing fixture feeds, pipe routes, diameters, materials, hot and cold lines, and isolation valves. Record route lengths and fixture levels before walls are closed. Quotations should separate opening-up, installation, testing and reinstatement. Accept the work only after a documented pressure test, joint inspection and confirmation that valves remain accessible—not merely because water flows.

### 51. Drainage network installation

Drainage design depends on bathroom and kitchen positions, floor levels and connections to existing lines; photographs alone cannot establish gradients. Ask for pipe diameters, cleanouts, venting arrangements, penetrations and waterproofing interfaces. Compare quotations including excavation, reinstatement and tests. Obtain evidence of leak and flow testing before pipework is concealed.

### 52. Sanitary fixture installation

For a basin, WC or tap installation, supply the model, dimensions, photographs and feed and waste connection positions. Confirm connector compatibility, installation height, service clearances and whether fittings, sealant and removal of old fixtures are included. At handover check secure mounting, drainage, operation, dry joints and the condition of surrounding tiles.

### 53. Water tank installation

Water tank installation requires an agreed capacity, tank dimensions, location and confirmation that the supporting base can carry the filled load, with safe cleaning access. Specify inlet, outlet, overflow, vent, isolation valve and float control, and whether old-tank removal is included. Compare tank materials, warranty and anchoring. Check filling, leakage and float operation; do not claim potable-water quality without appropriate testing.

### 54. Water pump installation

Do not select a water pump by motor power alone. State the number of outlets, elevation difference, available pressure, installation location and acceptable noise. Request the pump performance curve, dry-run protection, non-return valve, controls and electrical isolation details. Compare supply, installation, accessories and servicing, then verify pressure, automatic start/stop and leak-free operation.

### 55. Pipework rehabilitation

Rehabilitating old pipework begins by locating failed sections and investigating repeated blockage or leakage, with photographs of access points and existing materials. Request a plan distinguishing replacement from retained sections, temporary connections, water shutdown duration and protection of occupied rooms. Compare more than a per-metre price: include opening-up, waterproofing and reinstatement. Receive test records and photographs before closure.

### 56. Drainage maintenance

Drainage maintenance should start with the blockage location, recurrence, odours and whether several outlets are affected: the cause may be a single trap or a shared line. Ask whether mechanical clearance or camera inspection is warranted rather than assuming replacement. The quotation should define cleaning limits, waste disposal and excluded repairs. At handover test flow at affected outlets and check for backflow and leaks.

### 57. Water leak investigation

Provide meter trends, damp patches and photographs; check the meter with outlets closed when feasible. Compare acoustic, thermal and pressure methods suited to the site; request findings and uncertainty before destructive access.

### 58. Pipe leak repairs

Identify pipe material, leak point, access and whether water service must be interrupted. Compare localized repair, connector choice, partial renewal and finish reinstatement; witness a pressure test before covering.

### 59. Tank leak investigation

Record water-level loss, overflow, pipework, float valves and tank shell before blaming the lining. Compare connection checks, waterproofing inspection and joint remediation; document the loss mechanism and refill test.

### 60. Bathroom waterproofing

Measure bathroom floor, wall upstands, pipe penetrations and doorway threshold. Specify a tile-compatible waterproofing system and an agreed flood test before tiling; check substrate condition first.

### 61. Roof waterproofing

Record roof area, falls, drains, joints, equipment plinths and ponding. Compare preparation, membrane build-up and UV protection; inspect drainage and the agreed integrity test before acceptance.

### 62. Water tank waterproofing

Identify potable or non-potable use, tank substrate, cracks and entry access. Specify a lining suitable for the water use, cleaning and rinsing procedure and separate joint checks; accept a watertightness test and material record.

### 63. Leak-related dampness treatment

Record mould, peeling, dates and the suspected moisture source, then repair the leak before drying surfaces. Compare drying period, moisture checks, salt treatment and repainting; require recorded observations, not merely concealed stains.


## Air-conditioning and refrigeration

### 64. Split AC installation

Match unit capacity to the room, solar exposure and indoor/outdoor positions, noting pipe route and condensate outlet. Compare mounting, insulated lines, evacuation and commissioning; verify cooling and condensate discharge without leaks.

### 65. Central AC installation

Central cooling needs room-by-room load estimates, duct routes, access for maintenance and condensate disposal. Compare equipment, controls, filters, noise and electrical demand; request airflow balancing and temperature readings by zone.

### 66. Concealed AC installation

Verify ceiling void heights, concealed unit envelope, supply and return positions and maintenance openings. Compare insulation, suspension and drain slope; check filter access and absence of condensation on plaster.

### 67. AC refrigerant piping

Mark copper-line lengths, elevation changes, penetrations and the refrigerant specified for the unit. Compare line sizing, insulation, supports, leak testing and evacuation; inspect before any concealed run is closed.

### 68. Air duct installation

Coordinate duct sections, structural beams, lights, diffusers and returns before fabrication. Compare sheet thickness, insulation, hangers and joint sealing; request balancing and noise observations appropriate to the job.

### 69. AC equipment replacement

Record the old model, fault symptoms, capacity, available space and which services can safely be reused. Compare repair versus replacement, removal and connection adaptations; obtain equipment warranty and commissioning report.

### 70. System start-up and inspection

Inspect start-up sequence, sensors, electrical draw, airflow, drainage and filters after installation or repair. Request readings under actual operation and a fault list; fan noise alone does not demonstrate performance.

### 71. AC unit cleaning

Identify whether filters, coil, blower or drain pan need cleaning and photograph dust or mould deposits. Compare cleaning products and protection for electronics and furnishings; inspect drainage and cleanliness before restart.

### 72. Refrigerant charge

Poor cooling does not automatically mean low refrigerant: check airflow, filters, pressure and leaks first. Compare diagnosis, leak repair and charging by manufacturer specification; require operating readings, not arbitrary topping-up.

### 73. AC fault repair

Faults may involve a capacitor, controller, sensor or compressor; record the error code and when it occurs. Compare component repair and replacement with warranty terms; repeat operating cycles to check the defect is resolved.

### 74. Air duct cleaning

List outlets, inspection access, duct type and filter condition; photograph deposits before cleaning. Compare containment, extraction and access to hidden sections; request before-and-after evidence without unsupported sterilisation claims.

### 75. Central AC maintenance

Inventory central units, pumps, filters, controllers, running hours and failures. Compare preventive tasks, visit frequency and excluded parts; require trends and an unresolved-fault register.

### 76. Replacement of spare parts

Obtain model and part numbers, connector photographs and fault evidence before ordering. Compare OEM or compatible approved alternatives and lead time; test the repaired function after fitting.

### 77. Periodic AC maintenance contracts

List unit numbers, locations, use patterns and required seasonal service levels. Compare visit schedules, emergency scope, cleaning, consumables and reporting; review fault trends instead of visit counts alone.


## Smart systems and site monitoring

### 78. Smart lighting control

Identify lighting circuits and whether wall switches must remain usable without internet access. Compare dimmer/load compatibility, connectivity and manual override; test each circuit from both switch and app.

### 79. Smart air-conditioning control

List AC models, controllers and sensors and define temperature schedules for each room. Compare interface compatibility, local control and network failure behaviour; verify on/off and mode changes.

### 80. Smart lock installation

Measure door thickness, swing, cylinder type and emergency access before selecting a smart lock. Compare battery life, user permissions and administration; test inside/outside release and low-battery access.

### 81. Smart curtain installation

Specify opening width, curtain weight, power location, track path and manual-use requirement. Compare motor rating, noise and controls; inspect obstacle response and maintenance access.

### 82. Phone app device integration

Inventory device makes, firmware, home network and accounts to be linked. Compare genuine compatibility and access permissions; hand over owner-controlled accounts with recovery instructions.

### 83. Automated smart scenes

Define concrete scenes such as leaving home or bedtime and which devices each may control. Compare safeguards, offline behaviour and manual overrides; test scene transitions without overriding essential equipment.

### 84. Smart-home maintenance

Log intermittent failures, connectivity, configuration backups, sensor batteries and hub health. Compare targeted diagnosis with indiscriminate resets; preserve client settings and retest scenes after repair.

### 85. CCTV installation

Map camera positions, mounting heights, fields of view, lighting, privacy and cable routes. Compare resolution, storage and safe mounting; inspect day/night footage without monitoring unrelated private spaces.

### 86. Recording equipment installation

Identify camera count, recording rate, retention needs, disk capacity and ventilation. Compare storage, backup power and access controls; demonstrate secure search and export of recorded footage.

### 87. Alarm system installation

Map doors, windows and monitored spaces and plan for nuisance alarms. Compare batteries, communications and notification channels; test each sensor without promising third-party response that is not contracted.

### 88. Access control installation

Specify controlled doors, user groups, permitted hours, audit needs and emergency release. Compare card or PIN readers, lock interfaces and privacy safeguards; test authorised and rejected access.

### 89. Intercom installation

Photograph outdoor station, indoor handset locations, gate and cable/network routes. Compare audio/video quality, door release and integration; test every endpoint and document reset procedures.

### 90. CCTV maintenance

Record blurred images, outages, missing footage and fault timings; check power, cabling and storage. Compare camera replacement against connection repairs; verify image quality, continuous recording and reboot recovery.

### 91. Remote phone viewing setup

Identify remote viewers, recorder model, connectivity and two-factor authentication. Prefer secure integration over indiscriminate port forwarding; hand over owner login, access alerts and revoke installer access.


## Painting and surface finishes

### 92. Villa painting

Measure wall and ceiling areas, rooms, furniture and existing paint condition before deciding coat counts. Compare filling, primer, paint, masking and protection as separate items; inspect dry colour consistency and clean edges.

### 93. Apartment painting

Document cracks, stains and areas not requiring repainting, especially in occupied apartments. Compare furniture protection, ventilation and suitable low-odour materials; inspect stain treatment and floor protection.

### 94. Exterior building painting

Survey façade peeling, chalking, cracks and water/sun exposure, including access height. Compare washing, exterior primer, crack treatment and access equipment; inspect adhesion and absence of drips.

### 95. Door and wood painting

Count doors and timber items, identify existing coating and whether hardware removal is needed. Compare sanding, compatible primers and topcoats; check smooth finish and doors closing without sticking.

### 96. Decorative painting

Approve a real texture sample and technique such as trowelled or textured paint before pricing the full area. Compare sample costs, material use and future repairability; inspect texture and hue under installed lighting.

### 97. Old paint removal and preparation

Determine whether old paint is peeling, sound or moisture-damaged rather than stripping indiscriminately. Compare selective removal, dust protection and substrate repair; verify a dry, stable surface ready for primer.

### 98. Repainting and maintenance

Photograph scratches and stains, and identify existing colour code and paint system. Compare local touch-up with full-wall repainting to avoid sheen mismatch; inspect colour blending and invisible repair boundaries.


## Waterproofing and thermal insulation

### 99. Roof waterproofing

Map concrete surfaces, drains, ponding and movement joints around plant plinths before selecting a membrane. Compare removal of failed layers, preparation and protection; verify drainage and watertight junction details.

### 100. Bathroom waterproofing

Map shower area, drains, threshold and wall upstands before waterproofing. Compare tile-compatible systems and pipe-penetration details; arrange an agreed flood test before finishes are laid.

### 101. Tank waterproofing

Identify water use, tank substrate, cracks, joints, level and safe entry requirements. Compare product suitability for the stored water and cleaning/drying procedure; receive a holding test and material record.

### 102. Building thermal insulation

Record roof/wall build-up, solar exposure, available space, thermal bridges and condensation risks. Compare thickness, thermal performance, moisture response and continuity; inspect junctions without blocking necessary ventilation.

### 103. External wall insulation

Survey façade substrate, cracks, openings, balcony fixings and safe access. Compare external insulation or appropriate coating solutions against design requirements and moisture behaviour; inspect window and edge junctions.

### 104. Rainwater ingress treatment

Relate water marks to rainfall, wind direction, gutters, joints and window openings. Compare targeted water testing and entry-point sealing rather than indiscriminate repainting; verify a suitable repeat test.

### 105. Waterproofing maintenance

Review membrane age, stains, blisters, cuts around drains and movement points. Compare patch repairs with renewal of a failed section based on condition; document repair details, tests and monitoring.



## Maintenance and finishing request details

Identify the property and affected room or system, when the fault began, overview and close-up photographs, measured affected area, and any active leakage, power outage or falling-object hazard. State whether you need diagnosis only or full repair, and whether reinstatement, parts and waste removal belong in the quote. Essential system tests still matter on small jobs. Where no company is listed, use Biet Al Reef customer support's quotation form without assuming appointment or price.

**Common question:** Why can maintenance bids vary for the same symptom? One may include diagnosis, source repair, tests and reinstatement; another may only replace the visible component. Ask for an itemised scope.
