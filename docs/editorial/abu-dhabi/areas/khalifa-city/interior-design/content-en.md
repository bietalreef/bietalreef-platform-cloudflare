# Khalifa City — Engineering consultancy, design and décor

## Khalifa City enquiry context
For a Khalifa City room redesign, check ceilings, lighting and air-conditioning before fixing the furniture layout. Confirm property condition, work access and inspection arrangements in the quotation request; the technical guidance below does not establish provider availability.


## Architectural design and drawings

### 1. Villa design

For a Khalifa City villa brief, begin with a room schedule: bedrooms, a separate guest entrance, how the majlis connects to the kitchen and service areas, and rooms that may change use as the household evolves. Provide available plot documents and photographs of existing access; do not assume setbacks or building allowances from informal estimates. Ask for at least two circulation and privacy options, with a net room-area schedule that distinguishes usable space from wall thicknesses. Compare fees on measured survey work, architectural drawings, structural and MEP coordination, revision rounds and access to editable deliverables. Before sign-off, walk through guest arrival, large-furniture movement and maintenance access to cooling equipment. A photorealistic rendering is not a substitute for dimensioned plans.

### 2. Residential building design

For a multi-unit residential building, provide more than a total floor-area target. List the unit mix, shared corridors, meter and waste rooms, and service spaces. Explain whether units will be occupied or let independently, because privacy and maintainable utility access affect the layout. Compare schemes by genuinely usable room areas, daylight, ventilation, furniture delivery routes, and access to lifts and stairs. Request coordinated discipline drawings and a clash schedule before structural columns or service risers are fixed within useful space. At handover, reconcile area schedules, door clearances, levels and drawing interfaces; the responsible engineer must check applicable life-safety and escape requirements rather than presuming prior approval.

### 3. Commercial building design

Define intended uses by floor before commissioning a commercial façade: independent retail units, offices, storage or loading may demand different layouts. Supply site dimensions, access points and any available service drawings, and distinguish lettable or operational areas from plant rooms and circulation. Compare options for future unit subdivision, goods movement separated from visitors, and safe maintenance access without shutting down operations. The consultant's scope should identify circulation drawings, clear-height sections, and loading and operating assumptions for specialist review, while explicitly excluding items such as individual tenant shopfront design where relevant. Accept revision-controlled files and documented structural, mechanical and electrical interfaces, not presentation images alone.

### 4. Architectural drawing preparation

First agree what the drawing package is for: concept review, tender pricing or construction. For an existing Khalifa City property, distinguish dimensions measured on site from those copied from old plans. Require a plan for each floor showing grids, openings, door swings and usable clearances, plus elevations and sections showing levels and clear heights. Sheets need revision identifiers, and service penetrations must be coordinated with other disciplines. Compare offers by drawing detail, site visits, revision rounds and deliverable formats. Receive PDFs and editable source files where contracted, then cross-check critical site dimensions before releasing drawings for fabrication or construction.

### 5. Façade redesign

A façade upgrade is more than choosing a new colour. Photograph the full elevation and any cracks or damaged joints; measure projections, pedestrian clearances, air-conditioning units, lighting and drainage points. Identify what remains and what is removed. Ask for a section showing cladding fixings, window reveals, movement joints and rainwater paths. Compare options by weight, maintenance and replacement of damaged panels rather than renders alone. Before sign-off, inspect a physical sample in daylight and check colour consistency and material junctions. Keep aesthetic approval separate from any structural assessment or applicable authority decision.

### 6. 3D architectural modelling

Define the decision the 3D model must support: massing, ceiling-height comparison, or coordination of HVAC ducts with gypsum ceilings and furniture. Provide revision-controlled drawings and reference levels, and specify which rooms and components need modelling. Request named objects, usable dimensions and a distinction between illustrative finishes and approved products. Compare bids on model update allowances and file formats, not just the number of polished views. If an inspectable model is part of the contract, receive it and reconcile openings, doors and levels against the drawings before taking measurements from it.

### 7. Amending existing plans

Mark every proposed wall, doorway and room change on one controlled copy of the existing plan, explaining why it is needed and which clearances must remain. Photograph columns, meters, HVAC outlets and drainage runs: a seemingly lightweight partition may conceal services. Require a change register covering additions, removals and items needing structural or MEP assessment. Compare fees by survey scope, affected sheets and revision cycles. Accept dated drawings with revision clouds or a change schedule; do not use them to instruct demolition before load-bearing elements and relevant requirements have been checked.

### 8. Permit drawing preparation

A permit drawing set depends on the project and competent authority; a floor plan or façade image alone is not a complete submission. Start with the work description, plot and owner information and any previous approved records. Ask the consultant for a written document and responsibility checklist covering relevant disciplines and signatures. Separate drawing fees, authority charges and responses to review comments in the quotation. At handover, reconcile plot identifiers, project names and drawing revisions across the package and obtain submission records if included. Preparing or uploading a file does not establish that a permit has been granted.

### 9. Building application submission

Building application handling combines document administration with technical coordination, but cannot compensate for an incorrect design. Confirm who is authorised to submit, the receiving authority and the checked owner, plot and drawing records. Agree who responds to comments, uploads revised sets and pays fees, and how many follow-up cycles the service covers. Do not compare proposals by promises of rapid approval; compare traceability and responsibility. Receive the application reference, submission dates, comments and actual decision status. An application under review is not permission to start work requiring authorisation.

### 10. Revision of approved drawings

If a room, opening or extension changes after drawings were approved, a phone photograph of the proposed alteration is insufficient. Gather the approved issue, latest construction set and photographs of completed work. Require a change schedule identifying effects on loads, ventilation, power, drainage and usable areas. Compare consultant proposals by affected sheets, discipline coordination and any necessary resubmission. Receive revised drawings with clear issue numbers and a record of items needing authority review. The owner's agreement to an idea is not technical or regulatory approval.

### 11. Maintenance permit coordination

For maintenance-related permissions, describe the actual intervention rather than its marketing label: replacing tiles, relocating services or cutting into a structural element are different scopes. Supply condition photographs, available drawings, the affected unit and proposed work period. Ask a qualified specialist to identify whether a formal procedure applies and what documents, responsibilities and charges are involved. Compare offers for requirements assessment, drawings and application follow-up separately. Obtain a documented conclusion and, where applicable, the real application or decision record. Not every maintenance activity follows one permit process.

### 12. Extension permit coordination

For a proposed room, canopy or built extension, first map existing and proposed boundaries rather than relying on an approximate area. Provide dimensions, setbacks to existing walls and plot boundaries, visible support conditions and service connection points. Ask the designer to explain structural interfaces, any investigation needed and MEP coordination, distinguishing design from permit administration. Compare offers by survey work, detail level, alternatives and revision allowance. At handover, inspect dimensions, levels and connection lines; feasibility sketches or acceptance for review do not authorise construction.

### 13. As-built survey drawings

As-built surveys are useful when earlier alterations were undocumented or old drawings disagree with site dimensions. Agree whether the survey covers rooms, openings, levels, visible wall thicknesses, equipment and accessible services. Supply old plans if available, but insist that measured dimensions are distinguished from inferred information and concealed elements. Compare proposals by floor coverage, accuracy, sections and indexed photographs. Receive dated, revision-controlled drawings stating survey limitations, then remeasure critical openings before ordering fabricated kitchens or façade components.

### 14. Authority approval coordination

Approval coordination does not mean every project must approach the same authorities. Provide the project use, location, scope and current drawings, and request a matrix identifying each potentially relevant body, the reason for consultation, required documents and intended submission stage. Separate coordination fees from official charges and redesign after technical comments. Track each application by reference and correspondence, distinguishing under review, conditional outcomes and final decisions. Handover should include actual decision records and unresolved obligations, without asserting unverified requirements for the property.

## Preparing a quotation request

Provide the site, property type, approximate area, available plans and photographs of existing conditions. State required deliverables and the intended timeline. Compare drawing scope, revision rounds, coordination responsibilities and excluded fees. If no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer service can receive the enquiry without promising provider availability or a price. Individual tradespeople remain a separate directory from companies and establishments.

## Structural design

### 15. Concrete structural system design

Commission this when designing a new building or altering its load-bearing system. Supply architectural plans, floor count, intended uses and any available ground investigation. Ask for the structural scheme, column, beam and slab arrangement, movement joints, loading assumptions and coordination of service penetrations. Compare offers by calculation scope, drawings and revision allowance. Accept coordinated design documents, appropriately reviewed and signed for the project, rather than relying on 3D visualisations for member sizes.

### 16. Foundation and footing design

Before selecting foundations, provide site boundaries, levels, a suitable ground investigation report and known buried utilities. The engineer should assess isolated or combined footings or a raft against structural loads and ground conditions, and detail reinforcement, connections and settlement considerations. Compare proposals for responsibility for soil investigation, excavation interfaces and coordination. Receive dimensioned, levelled foundation plans and details after technical review.

### 17. Structural drawing preparation

This service turns the structural analysis into buildable concrete, reinforcement, connection and detailing drawings. Supply the latest architectural issue and coordinated stair, drainage and HVAC opening locations. Compare the level of reinforcement schedules, clash resolution and revision support. At handover, check grid lines, levels and openings across sheets and confirm every drawing has a clear issue identifier.

### 18. Structural load review

Request a load review when changing room use, adding heavy machinery, tanks or floors—not merely when replacing finishes. Provide equipment weights, locations, base dimensions and available original drawings. Require identification of dead, imposed and concentrated loads and their paths through the supporting structure, with unknown inputs flagged. The delivered calculation note should define its limits and whether site testing or strengthening is needed before works.

### 19. Building structural safety assessment

Progressive cracking, settlement, exposed corrosion or deformation call for documented site inspection rather than a diagnosis from photographs alone. The engineer should identify appropriate tests and distinguish visible conditions from concealed uncertainties. Compare proposals by inspection coverage, sampling, report and recommendations. Receive prioritised findings, confidence limits and urgent actions where justified. If immediate danger is suspected, restrict access to the affected area and seek qualified on-site advice promptly.

### 20. Structural strengthening design

Strengthening design follows diagnosis of the defect and verification of existing loads and members. Provide as-built information, investigation results, photographs and access restrictions. Request assessed options, such as section strengthening or additional supports, with construction sequence, temporary propping and inspection points. Accept connection details, material specifications and a monitoring plan; never cut or remove a load-bearing element based only on verbal advice.

### 21. Structural engineering reports

Define the report's purpose: crack investigation, change-of-use assessment or a condition record before adjacent works. Supply the location, photos, drawings, dates and earlier findings. The report should separate observations, measurements, assumptions and conclusions, stating test methods, access limits, results and recommendations. Compare offers by visits, testing and required appendices, and obtain a dated, scoped report identifying the responsible professional.


## Engineering supervision and consultancy

### 22. Construction supervision

Define the supervisor’s inspection authority, visit frequency and hold points before concealed works, such as reinforcement or service runs, are covered. Supply the current drawings, contractor programme and site contacts. Request visit records, photographs, nonconformance notes and follow-up actions. Compare proposals by actual attendance, reporting and coordination, distinguishing periodic supervision from daily site management. At handover, verify closure of critical observations and the exact work inspected; a report cannot certify unseen work.

### 23. Project management

This service is useful when multiple contractors and suppliers must follow one schedule and a controlled decision process. Provide the baseline budget, contracts, drawings and completion targets. Request a phased programme, responsibility matrix, decision log and risk register. Compare proposals by site presence, variation approval and claims management rather than fee percentage alone. Receive periodic progress, cost forecasts, change records and decisions, while keeping the manager’s role distinct from designer and builder.

### 24. Work quality review

Commission a defined inspection of completed plaster, tiling, waterproofing or building services. Supply contract specifications, approved samples and photographs from earlier stages. The inspector should distinguish visual checks from tests requiring laboratory work or local opening-up, and state acceptance criteria. Compare sample coverage, test methods and reporting. Receive a location-specific defect schedule with evidence and priorities, then arrange reinspection after rectification rather than accepting verbal assurances.

### 25. Project stage acceptance

Inspect a stage before later work conceals it, such as waterproofing before tiling or pipework before walls are closed. Agree the stage, drawings, specifications and necessary tests with the contractor in advance. Compare proposals by inspection visits, reinspection provision and nonconformance recording. Obtain a dated stage record distinguishing accepted, pending and rejected items and identifying work that must remain accessible until corrected.

### 26. Progress report preparation

A useful report measures actual progress against the approved programme instead of presenting photographs alone. Supply the baseline schedule, contract work items, approved quantities and meeting minutes. Request verifiable progress by work package, causes of delay, late materials, decisions required and upcoming risks. Compare providers by visit frequency and supporting evidence. Receive a dated report separating verified work from planned progress and unapproved payment claims.

### 27. Quantity surveying

A quantity take-off needs a fixed drawing revision and agreed measurement rules; structural concrete, paint and joinery use different units and allowances. Provide drawings and specifications and specify whether the output is a preliminary estimate or pricing bill. Compare proposals by item detail, waste assumptions, supply-versus-installation boundaries and treatment of omissions. Accept an auditable schedule linking quantities to drawing references, dimensions, units and stated assumptions.

### 28. Contractor bid review

Make the scope, drawings, specifications, programme and requested payment terms consistent before comparing bids. Ask the reviewer to identify omissions, differing quantities, material substitutions, warranty provisions and exclusions rather than rank headline totals. Compare review services by analysis depth, bidder clarification process and risk assessment. Receive a comparison table with scope-normalised prices and unresolved questions; the lowest initial price is not automatically the strongest offer.

## Interior design and material selection

### 29. Majlis design

Specify guest capacity, seating style, hospitality access and privacy; measure wall lengths, ceiling height, doors and AC positions. Compare circulation, seating layouts and cleanable finishes. Receive a dimensioned furniture plan, lighting locations and finish samples.

### 30. Living room design

Start with household use, screen position, storage and daily activities. Record window dimensions, power outlets, viewing distance and circulation. Compare furniture and daylight options; receive a plan showing unobstructed doors and routes.

### 31. Bedroom design

Set bed size, wardrobe capacity, blackout needs and switch positions. Measure the room, window and door swing. Compare storage and clearances around the bed; receive coordinated wardrobe elevations and electrical and lighting plans.

### 32. Kitchen design

Measure walls and appliances and locate water, drainage and extraction connections. Check working distances, drawer clearance, ventilation and surface resistance to heat and moisture. Compare board thickness and fittings; receive fabrication dimensions and service points.

### 33. Bathroom design

Measure room levels, drainage, doors and ventilation; choose shower and fixtures. Compare clearances, floor falls, waterproofing junctions and maintenance access. Receive coordinated plumbing and tile drawings identifying waterproofing zones and joints.

### 34. Furniture layout planning

Supply actual furniture dimensions and a plan showing doors, windows and sockets. Test arrangements for movement, cabinet access and cleaning; accept a scaled layout rather than symbolic furniture sizes.

### 35. Interior 3D visualisation

Define rooms, viewpoints and rendering detail; provide plans and material references. Compare the number of views and revision rounds. Receive visuals that communicate design choices, noting that screen colours do not replace physical samples.

### 36. Office design

Define headcount, meeting rooms, privacy, storage and visitor movement. Survey columns, windows and service outlets. Compare desk density, lighting and acoustics; receive a coordinated desk, power and data layout.

### 37. Retail store design

Define merchandise, stock, displays, checkout and customer flow. Measure frontage, heights and exits. Compare shelving flexibility, lighting and restocking access; receive display, counter and fabrication dimensions.

### 38. Restaurant and café design

Set seating capacity, service model and preparation, washing and storage zones. Review customer and staff routes, extraction and drainage. Compare equipment coordination and cleanable finishes; receive a service and equipment plan for technical review.

### 39. Salon design

Define workstations, washing, reception, waiting and privacy. Record equipment dimensions and water, power and ventilation points. Compare circulation, mirror lighting and cleanable surfaces; receive coordinated workstation and service plans.

### 40. Clinic interior design

Define clinical activities, patient and staff routes, examination rooms and storage. Coordinate equipment, privacy and cleaning needs with specialists and check activity-specific authority requirements. Receive functional and service plans; interior design alone is not operational approval.

### 41. Exhibition design

List exhibits, weights, visitor interaction and assembly and removal periods. Survey floor area, height and loading access. Compare lighting, display stability and reuse; receive a layout, item schedule and installation instructions.

### 42. Reception area design

Define visitor volume, peak queues, waiting and varied accessibility needs. Measure entrances, routes and equipment. Compare wayfinding, privacy and counter durability; receive circulation and service-counter dimensions.

### 43. Floor finish selection

Match each room's use, moisture, cleaning and loads to the existing substrate. Request physical samples, slip-performance data, installation method and joints. Compare material costs including substrate preparation and waste; receive a room-by-room floor schedule.

### 44. Paint colour selection

Assess daylight, window orientation, furniture and adjacent colours. Test painted wall samples under different lighting; compare sheen and washability. Receive exact colour codes, finishes and application locations.

### 45. Stone and tile selection

Define locations, moisture exposure, tile size and traffic. Inspect samples for veining, variation, absorption and texture. Compare cutting waste, adhesives and grout; receive setting-out drawings, starting points and reference samples.

### 46. Furniture specification

Set budget, dimensions, daily use, quantities and warranty expectations. Compare frames, upholstery, filling, maintenance and quoted lead times without assuming availability. Receive an itemised furniture schedule with approved dimensions, colours and materials.

### 47. Lighting selection

Identify tasks, general and accent lighting needs. Review colour temperature, glare, controls, maintenance and ceiling compatibility. Compare performance specifications, not fixture count alone; receive a luminaire schedule and control circuit plan.

### 48. Material mood board

Combine flooring, wall, fabric, metal and wood samples with supplier references. Compare colour harmony, texture, cleaning and batch variation. Receive a dated board linking each physical sample to its intended location rather than relying only on images.

### 49. Finish schedule preparation

After material approval, map each room to wall, floor, ceiling and skirting finishes, colour codes and reference quantities. Check consistency with drawings and samples and clarify supply-versus-installation scope. Receive a revision-controlled schedule suitable for pricing and execution.

## Brief before hiring a designer or consultant

Submit an as-is drawing, even a measured sketch, proposed changes, occupants, furniture to retain, photographs and an indicative budget. Specify whether the deliverable is a 3D visualisation, construction drawings, quantity schedule or site supervision; these are not interchangeable. Where no firm is listed, Biet Al Reef customer support can take the brief as a quotation enquiry without suggesting professional approval or a government permit.

**Common question:** Are renderings enough to build from? No. Agree drawings, measured dimensions, specifications, electrical and HVAC coordination and execution details.
