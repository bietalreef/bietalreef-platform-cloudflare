# Mohammed Bin Zayed City — Building materials and supply

> **Area scope:** This guide addresses service enquiries in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, Abu Dhabi. Quote requests should specify the neighbourhood or zone, building or villa reference and site access. Provider availability, travel charges and permits must be confirmed for the actual location. Shared technical guidance is not evidence of previous local work.


A useful supply request specifies units, measurements, grades and delivery phases rather than just a product name. Some source categories are currently restricted; listing them here does not establish an active supplier. If no company is displayed, customers can submit the bill of materials, photos and unloading location through Biet Al Reef customer support's quotation request form, without any promise of availability or price.

## Construction and finishing supplies

### 1. Cement supply

For a concrete pour or masonry work in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, asking for 'cement' alone is not a sufficient specification: mortar and structural concrete may call for different approved products. Send a bag-count schedule, bag weight, the cement type named in the project documents, first-use date and a photograph of the dry storage area. Request separate prices for product, transport and unloading, with manufacturer, product standard, manufacture date and damaged-bag replacement terms. At delivery, reconcile the printed batch information and bag count against the order, inspect for moisture or hard lumps, and obtain the project engineer's approval before substituting a specified type.

### 2. Sand supply

Plaster sand, backfill and sand for concrete mixes are not interchangeable specifications. State the intended use of each load and order in cubic metres or tonnes with an agreed measurement method. For a Mohammed Bin Zayed City delivery, attach photographs of the unloading point and truck approach. Ask the supplier to identify grading, material source, suitability and separate haulage or waiting charges. Check the delivery note and quantity basis on arrival, and look for clay, debris or contamination from the unloading area. Visual checks do not replace project-required material tests.

### 3. Aggregate supply

Aggregate for concrete must match the mix design and reinforcement clearances; drainage or fill aggregate has a different purpose. Prepare a schedule showing application, nominal size, grading, quantity and delivery windows, plus gate and stockpile photographs. Compare quotations using the same grading, measurement unit, weighbridge documentation and freight terms rather than a truckload price alone. On receipt, inspect visible grading consistency and contamination, reconcile load documents, and require relevant test evidence where structural specifications call for it.

### 4. Concrete block supply

Before ordering blocks for a villa or extension, derive wall thicknesses, opening dimensions and specified block types from the drawings. Calculate quantities by size with an agreed breakage allowance and mark unloading zones so pallets do not block other trades. Compare documented strength where applicable, actual dimensions, edge consistency, palletisation and phased delivery dates. Partition blocks must not be substituted for structural applications without design approval. Sample each batch, count pallets and damaged units, and record discrepancies before signing the delivery note.

### 5. Reinforcing steel supply

Reinforcement procurement starts with a bar-bending or quantity schedule tied to the current structural drawing revision, not a rough tonnage estimate. Specify diameter, length, grade, piece count and whether cutting, bending, tagging, transport and lifting are included. Request mill certificates, batch traceability and the weighing method; arrange safe truck access in advance. Compare net weight, fabrication waste and traceability, then check bar marks, diameters and lengths against the schedule. Assess corrosion or damage against project acceptance criteria before installation.

### 6. Ready-mix concrete supply

For ready-mix concrete, identify the element to be poured, measured volume, approved mix designation, pumping arrangement and distance from truck parking to the pour. Coordinate deliveries with formwork, reinforcement inspection and testing readiness; agree who bears truck waiting and rejected-load costs. The quote should identify mix details, authorised admixtures, pump charges, sampling and workability checks under the quality plan. Check each truck ticket, loading time, quantity and any site additions, recording test results. Water must not be added casually to improve placement.

### 7. Project building-material packages

For a multi-stage project, replace a vague 'materials package' request with a procurement schedule linking each item to a specification, unit, quantity, delivery location and required date. Attach one controlled bill of quantities and relevant drawing references, identifying protected storage and unloading equipment or labour. Compare offers on identical products or approved alternatives, price validity, returns, phased delivery and any included certificates or tests. Reconcile each shipment against its purchase order and delivery note, recording shortages and damage before approving payment.

### 8. Gypsum materials supply

For gypsum board deliveries to suspended ceilings or partitions, first measure each room and identify wet areas and any fire-performance requirements stated in the design. Ordinary boards should not replace specified specialist boards simply because their dimensions match. Send a schedule of board thickness, length, width, edge type and quantities, plus metal framing, screws, joint tape and compound if included. Compare quotations for the complete specified system, manufacturer information, transport, storage and delivery dates rather than board price alone. On arrival check edges, flatness, moisture damage, breakage, labels and counts; store boards flat and dry as instructed by the manufacturer.

### 9. Paint materials supply

Separate new plaster from previously painted surfaces before ordering coatings: primer, filler and finish paint perform different functions. Give the supplier measured wall and ceiling areas, substrate condition, photographs of peeling or dampness, colour codes, sheen and the specified coating sequence. Compare quotations using declared coverage, number of coats, permitted dilution, product compatibility and realistic wastage, not just tin count. At delivery verify product names, colour codes, batch numbers, pack sizes, expiry dates and intact seals. Retain an approved sample for later touch-ups and investigate any moisture source before applying finishes.

### 10. Waterproofing materials supply

Roof membranes, bathroom waterproofing and tank linings are not interchangeable. Describe the application area, substrate, slope, ponding, cracks, drains and junctions, supported by photographs. Request a quotation for the whole specified system: primer, waterproofing layers, reinforcement where required, corner detailing and protective finish, including application thicknesses from the approved design and manufacturer guidance. Compare suitability for exposure, installation method, curing time and required testing rather than a single container price. On receipt inspect batch numbers, packaging, technical data sheets and compatibility between components, and document storage conditions before application.

### 11. Floor finishes supply

Start a flooring order with a room-by-room layout showing dimensions, material changes and thresholds rather than selecting colour alone. Calculate net areas and allowances for cutting and laying pattern; specify format, thickness, slip resistance where relevant, skirtings, trims, adhesives or underlay. Ask for physical samples and compare the same grade, batch, packaging, delivery arrangements and breakage replacement terms. At receipt verify shade codes, batch consistency, dimensional accuracy and undamaged edges. Keep different batches separate until the installer has checked that any visible variation is acceptable.

### 12. Sanitary fixtures supply

For sanitaryware in a new or refurbished bathroom, provide a layout with room dimensions, water supply and drainage positions, and the fixing arrangement for basins, toilets and taps. Schedule each item by model, colour, size, quantity and required accessories, including traps, valves and fixing kits if included. Compare quotes for connection compatibility, spare parts, warranty terms and delivery rather than appearance alone. Open packages with the site representative and check ceramics for cracks, finishes for scratches and boxes for small missing parts. Confirm actual dimensions before installation begins.

### 13. Electrical installation supplies

Electrical supplies require a schedule approved by a competent designer or electrician, covering cable cross-sections, insulation type, route lengths, breaker ratings, boards, boxes and conduits. Never select protective-device ratings from product photographs or price alone. Provide the relevant load schedule or drawings and photographs of existing boards for replacement work; state whether terminations and labelling are included. Compare manufacturer data, conformity with project specifications, test documentation, traceability and replacement terms. At delivery verify markings, conductor sizes, counts and undamaged cable insulation, leaving final suitability checks to qualified personnel.

### 14. Plumbing installation supplies

Split plumbing supplies by cold water, hot water, drainage and irrigation where applicable; each system may use different pipe materials, sizes, fittings and operating conditions. Extract pipe lengths, diameters and fitting counts from the drawings, and photograph connections to existing pipes so the correct adapters can be identified. Compare quotations for pipe material, pressure and temperature ratings, compatibility of fittings and jointing methods, product documentation and phased delivery. On receipt inspect markings, diameters, missing parts, cracking and storage condition. Do not mix incompatible system components without technical approval.

### 15. Building-material retail

For counter purchases of building materials, split the shopping list into structural products, finishes and consumables, and state each actual sales unit: bag, piece, linear metre, square metre or cubic metre. Provide product codes, sizes, grades and sample photographs, and clarify whether collection or delivery to a Mohammed Bin Zayed City site is needed. Compare the full invoice including handling, transport and return terms, not only unit prices. Approve substitutions before purchase and reconcile quantities, batch identifiers and damaged packaging against the invoice before leaving the collection point.

### 16. Sanitary ware retail

Buying a basin, toilet or mixer requires installation dimensions before choosing a finish. Measure available width, mounting height, water and waste connection centres, and clearances from walls and doors. Supply site photographs and the intended model reference; ask whether traps, isolation valves, hoses, fixing kits and warranty are included. Compare access to spare parts and maintenance as well as appearance. Where possible, open cartons with the seller and inspect ceramic surfaces, tap plating, accessories and model numbers before installation.

### 17. Electrical supplies retail

Before buying a breaker, switch or socket, identify its purpose, existing model and distribution-board nameplate; visual similarity does not establish electrical compatibility. A qualified electrician should confirm voltage, current, breaking capacity, protective characteristics and mounting compatibility against the circuit design, particularly for circuit breakers. Request manufacturer data, technical specifications and warranty, and reject downgraded substitutions. At receipt verify printed ratings, terminal condition and packaging. Leave commissioning and protection tests to the competent installer.

### 18. Plumbing supplies retail

For pipes or fittings used in leak repairs or new runs, photograph the old component and establish the relevant outside or inside diameter, connection type, operating pressure and temperature. Do not mix incompatible pipe systems or jointing compounds simply because nominal sizes look similar. Ask the supplier for a complete list of elbows, couplings, valves, seals and suitable joining tools; compare warranties and suitability for potable-water applications where relevant. Inspect codes, cracks and distortion on receipt, then require a leak or pressure test before the installer closes the wall.

### 19. Paint retail

For a room touch-up or façade repaint, measure the substrate area, identify its material and condition, and record colour codes and sheen; photograph peeling or staining. Request a compatible surface-preparation, primer, filler and finish-coat system rather than a colour tin alone. Compare cost per coated area using declared spreading rates, recommended coats and waste allowance, checking whether the product is intended for interior or exterior exposure. Verify sealed packs, batch codes and shelf life on delivery, retain an approved sample, and resolve moisture sources before painting.

### 20. Waterproofing products retail

Before purchasing waterproofing products for a bathroom or roof, distinguish where water enters from where staining appears. Photograph drains, corners and movement joints, and measure the area, upstand heights, substrate condition and falls. Request the complete application system, including primer, corner reinforcement, protective layers, curing periods and manufacturer data sheets. Compare suitability for ponding, sunlight exposure or tank-water contact as applicable; do not substitute a product without technical review. Check batch numbers and intact seals on receipt and arrange the specified waterproofing test before covering the system.

### 21. Site materials delivery

Site delivery is a logistics service distinct from material sales. Prepare a load schedule listing products, weights, dimensions and pallet counts, with photographs of site access, gate clearance and the approved unloading point. Specify whether a forklift, crane or unloading crew is required and who bears waiting, failed-delivery and transit-damage costs. Compare offers on legal vehicle capacity, any available insurance, handling charges and delivery windows rather than trip price alone. On arrival count items, inspect seals and breakage, photograph discrepancies and annotate the delivery note before final sign-off.

## Timber and sheet goods

### 22. MDF board supply

Specify MDF thickness, moisture grade, surface finish, board size and cut plan. Compare density, edge integrity, cutting charges and waste; reject swollen sheets.

### 23. Natural timber supply

State timber species, section, lengths and moisture level for indoor or outdoor use. Compare straightness, knots and treatment; reject warped pieces before cutting.

### 24. Plywood supply

Identify plywood grade, bonding type, ply count and expected exposure. Compare edge voids, bonding and moisture suitability; sample thickness and delamination resistance.

### 25. Melamine board supply

Set board thickness, melamine colour, texture, units and grain direction. Compare face finish, edge banding and matching batches; check scratches before cutting.

### 26. HPL sheet supply

Specify whether HPL serves countertops, doors or wall surfaces and its thickness and bonding system. Compare scratch/moisture performance and substrate suitability; match colours across sheets.

### 27. Joinery fittings supply

List hinges, runners, handles, sizes and drawer loads. Compare load ratings, motion and warranty; check actual part models against drawings.

### 28. Board cutting and preparation

Supply labelled cut plans specifying dimensions, board type, grain direction and edge banding. Compare cutting tolerance, yield and transport; measure samples and check labels at collection.

## Natural stone and granite

### 29. Natural marble supply

Specify stone type, colour variation, slab size and thickness. Compare actual slab photographs, source and vein layout; inspect transport cracks before fabrication.

### 30. Granite supply

State whether granite is for worktops, floors or steps and its slip and weather exposure. Compare staining, finish and thickness; approve an actual stone sample.

### 31. Custom-size marble cutting

Issue dimensioned templates showing edges, openings and vein direction before cutting. Compare mitres, edge work, fabrication and packing; measure completed pieces against drawings.

### 32. Marble polishing

Identify scratch depth, prior sealers and stone type. Compare polishing grits, material removal and dust control; inspect uniform sheen without hazing.

### 33. Marble floor laying

Record floor area, stone thickness, substrate condition, joints and thresholds. Compare levelling, bedding and post-installation finishing; inspect lippage and vein direction.

### 34. Wall and façade marble installation

Measure wall height, fixing points, openings and stone weight. Compare engineered mechanical fixing or appropriate bonding and check safe anchorage and edge details.

### 35. Marble restoration

Photograph cracks, staining, wear and acid damage before specifying restoration. Compare specialist cleaning, filling, local replacement and polishing; inspect colour and edge consistency.

## Kitchen surfaces and basins

### 36. Fabricate kitchen worktops

Choose surface material, cabinet depths, run lengths, edges and joint positions. Compare slab thickness, mitred corners and hidden support; template only after cabinets are installed.

### 37. Install kitchen worktops

Measure worktop runs, walls, appliances, drainage and taps. Compare lifting, fixing, sealant and supports; inspect firm seating and sealed joints.

### 38. Fabricate marble vanities

Set vanity dimensions, basin and mixer models, marble edges and service access. Compare basin fixing, drainage and future access; inspect polished edges and falls away from standing water.

### 39. Cut sink and appliance apertures

Do not cut sink or hob openings from screen dimensions; use appliance templates or verified manufacturer measurements. Compare edge clearance and reinforcement; dry-fit appliances before final sealing.

### 40. Quartz surface installation

Specify quartz type, thickness, slab colour and sink surrounds. Compare seam location, supports, fabrication and fixing; inspect smooth joints and supported overhangs.

### 41. Porcelain slab worktop installation

Lay out large-format porcelain worktop slabs and appliance cut-outs before fabrication. Compare handling breakage, edge profiles and supports; inspect for chips and cracks.

### 42. Worktop repair and polishing

Identify staining, scratches, open seams or cracks and confirm the actual surface material. Compare cleaning, compatible polishing, patching and replacement; avoid abrasion unsuitable for the product.

## Ceramic and porcelain tiles

### 43. Ceramic tile supply

Supply measured area, use, tile size and cutting allowance. Compare shade, surface grade and pack coverage; inspect consistent batch numbers.

### 44. Porcelain tile supply

Set porcelain format, thickness and slip suitability for intended areas. Compare pack weight, cutting needs and delivery logistics; inspect edges and flatness.

### 45. Outdoor paving tile supply

Identify exterior area, pedestrians or vehicles and rainfall drainage. Compare slip texture, thickness, durability and movement details; approve a physical sample.

### 46. Tile cutting and preparation

Draw cuts around pipes, doors and material transitions before fabrication. Compare appropriate cutting methods, waste and breakage liability; dry-check pieces before laying.

### 47. Ceramic tile installation

Record substrate levels, floor/wall area, drains and ceramic adhesive system. Compare substrate preparation, joints and skirting; inspect alignment, drainage falls and complete grout.

### 48. Porcelain tile installation

Large porcelain formats need controlled levelling, lifting and proper adhesive bedding. Compare handling, breakage and movement joints; inspect lippage at tile edges.

### 49. Skirting supply and installation

Measure skirting lengths, height, ends at doorways and columns, and joint colour. Compare factory pieces with cut-tile skirting and mitred corners; inspect secure, even-height fitting.



## Material procurement request

Send an itemised list with product, unit, grade, size, quantity and justified cutting allowance; attach sample images or manufacturer codes, unloading location, gate clearance and batch dates. Clarify whether quoted terms include applicable tax, testing, packing, transport, unloading and returns, and agree substitutes in writing. Where no shop or supplier is listed, Biet Al Reef customer support can collect a pricing request without promising stock.

**Common question:** Is a tile photo sufficient? No; add size, grade, batch reference, product code and box quantity for consistent colour and quantities.
