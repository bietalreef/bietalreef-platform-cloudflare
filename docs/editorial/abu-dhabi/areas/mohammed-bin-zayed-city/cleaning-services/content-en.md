# Mohammed Bin Zayed City — Cleaning, disinfection and pest management

> **Area scope:** For service enquiries in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, Abu Dhabi, specify the zone, villa or building and access route. No provider, price or permit is implied; shared technical guidance is not evidence of locally completed work.


Cleaning an occupied apartment is not the same scope as post-construction cleaning or operating a whole building. Document surfaces, soil, dimensions, access hours and chemical or occupational risks. Service descriptions do not imply a listed provider or available crew. Where no company appears, Biet Al Reef customer support and its quotation form can receive photographs and requirements without guaranteeing outcome or pricing; specialist providers must meet applicable competency and safety requirements.

## Home and building cleaning

### 1. Villa cleaning

For a villa in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, state the number of floors, rooms, majlis areas, bathrooms, kitchen surfaces, stairs, windows and outdoor spaces, and whether the property is occupied. Photograph stubborn stains and delicate finishes. Routine upkeep, accumulated grease and dust from repairs call for different methods, equipment and time. Request an itemised scope identifying inside-cabinet work, high glazing, upholstery, parking and external areas rather than assuming they are included. Compare quotes against the same room or area schedule, crew size, working hours, materials, tools and exclusions. At handover, inspect furniture edges, corners, bathroom drains and window frames; ensure floors are dry and no chemical residue or damage remains on timber or stone.

### 2. Apartment cleaning

In an apartment, furniture density and access to windows and appliances can matter more than room count alone. Supply photographs of the kitchen, bathrooms and balcony, explain whether the job is pre-occupancy or routine cleaning, and confirm building restrictions on equipment and working hours. Agree explicitly whether the oven, refrigerator, cupboard interiors, ventilation grilles and high surfaces are included. Electrical appliances should be protected, and heavy items should not be moved without consent. Compare quotes by task list, duration, crew, drying and waste removal rather than the label 'full clean'. At handover, inspect grease around the cooker, deposits around sanitary fittings, floor edges and glass. Photograph unresolved marks and establish whether they are removable stains or permanent surface damage.

### 3. Office cleaning

Start with a plan identifying workstations, meeting rooms, reception, washrooms, pantry and visitor circulation, then choose hours that minimise disruption. Specify that drawers, records, computers and servers must not be opened, moved or handled without authorisation. Ordinary wiping is distinct from disinfection, which requires a documented product and application procedure. Record flooring, fitted carpet, glazing, bin locations and occupancy to set realistic frequencies. Compare supervision, consumables, spill response, issue logs and staff absence cover. At acceptance, check touch points, washrooms and corridors, confirm documents and devices remain in place, and review a task record that identifies what was actually done.

### 4. Building cleaning

A shared building requires a different scope from an individual apartment. Inventory floors, entrances, lifts, stairwells, corridors, parking and permitted service areas, and assign responsibility for each. Provide plans or photographs of flooring, glazing and waste collection points. Plan around resident movements and peak hours so wet floors do not create slip hazards. Require a schedule separating daily wiping from deep washing, lift and handrail cleaning and waste handling, with safe chemical storage and no incompatible product mixing. Compare visit frequency, labour hours, washroom supplies, quality checks and complaint handling. Review task records and inspect a sample of floors, lifts and entrances; recurring defects should be logged rather than accepted under a generic completion statement.

### 5. Post-construction cleaning

Final cleaning should follow the completion of dust-generating trades, with new fittings protected from further contractor traffic. Photograph plaster dust, grout haze, paint, silicone and adhesive labels on tiles, glazing and timber, noting finishes that cannot tolerate scraping, acids or moisture. Ask for a two-stage plan: safe removal of loose debris and coarse dust, followed by detailed cleaning of edges, cupboards, door frames and surfaces. Any remover should be tested on a discreet area with appropriate approval. Compare debris disposal, high-level access, return visits after snagging and the distinction between existing damage and removable marks. Use raking light at handover to inspect grout residue and dust on edges and glass, checking that finishes have not been scratched and drains have not been obstructed.

### 6. Pre-move cleaning

When preparing a home for occupancy, confirm the furniture delivery date, key handover and whether any painting or repair work will continue afterwards. List appliances, cupboard interiors, shelving, sanitary fittings and balconies, identifying which interiors need cleaning. Empty rooms allow access to corners that furniture will later conceal. Specify dust, grease and mineral-deposit removal using methods suitable for each finish, followed by adequate ventilation and drying before mattresses, rugs and timber furniture arrive. Compare quotes by room count, internal cleaning, packaging disposal and the response if handover is delayed. Inspect unusual odours and moisture in cupboards and around drains; cleaning itself is not a remedy for an active leak or mould problem requiring separate investigation.

### 7. Regular cleaning contracts

For recurring cleaning of a villa, office or building, establish a room-by-room task schedule distinguishing every-visit duties from weekly, monthly and separately requested work. Record floor areas and materials, washroom counts, waste points and delicate upholstery, and allocate responsibility for water, power, cleaning products, consumables and storage. Compare actual covered hours, supervision, substitute staff, emergency requests and exclusions such as high-level access or specialist stain removal. Require auditable attendance and task logs, with procedures for reporting damage and challenging poor performance. At the end of each period, sample inspected areas, review user complaints and recurring defects, and use those findings before approving invoices or changing service frequency.

## Upholstery and textile cleaning

### 8. Steam sofa cleaning

Before requesting steam upholstery cleaning, photograph each sofa and its stains, record the seat count and fabric-care label, and flag foam padding or timber components vulnerable to moisture. Not every textile tolerates hot-water extraction or heat: ask for a concealed colourfastness test and identify oily, dye-based or old liquid marks. Agree whether seats, backs, arms and seams are included, how extraction is performed, and how floors and padding are protected from overwetting. Compare quotes by pieces, stain treatment, chemicals and drying time rather than the word 'steam' alone. At handover, check for dye transfer, new tide marks and trapped moisture; do not use the sofa until dry.

### 9. Rug cleaning

For loose rugs, supply the number and dimensions of pieces, fibre, pile and backing, and photographs of care labels, stains and worn edges. Hand-knotted wool or silk rugs may need a different approach from synthetics; immersion or aggressive brushing should not be assumed safe without checking dyes and bindings. Decide whether cleaning takes place on site or off site, with item identification and protected transport. Request a sequence for vacuuming, spot treatment, washing, rinsing and controlled drying. Compare per-piece quotes, exclusions and realistic limits on old stains. On return, check dimensions, flatness, colour stability, odour and dryness on both sides before laying rugs down.

### 10. Fitted carpet cleaning

Fitted carpet cannot be handled like a removable rug. Provide the measured square metres of each room, photographs of seams and heavy-traffic areas, and details of furniture and underfloor services. Ask for an assessment of backing and existing moisture before choosing low-moisture cleaning or suitable extraction that will not loosen adhesive or trap water below the carpet. Compare quotes by actual area, furniture movement, stain treatment, extraction and time until the room can reopen. Require a small trial patch where appropriate. At acceptance, inspect for ripples, lifted edges and water rings, and confirm the carpet is dry before replacing furniture or allowing foot traffic.

### 11. Arabic majlis cleaning

For a traditional majlis, measure the linear metres of seating and count cushions, bolsters, rugs and curtains included in the request. Photograph textiles, trims and decorative edges sensitive to moisture. Distinguish removable covers from fixed upholstery and note whether seating is floor-level or raised. Request colour testing, targeted stain treatment and protection for timber, ornamentation and flooring. Schedule enough space to dry cushions on both sides without stacking them damp. Compare quotations by measured seating and individual items, not an undefined 'complete majlis'. At handover, inspect seams, corners, odours, residual moisture and the correct placement of cushions.

### 12. Mattress cleaning

For mattress cleaning, give each mattress's size, thickness, cover material and whether it contains latex or memory foam. Photograph stains and describe their approximate age and origin. Surface vacuuming does not prove removal of deep contamination or medical-grade disinfection; request the proposed method, likely moisture penetration and compatibility with manufacturer care instructions. Do not accept promises that every odour or allergen will disappear. Compare quotes by mattress count, sides treated, spot work, ventilation, drying and exclusions. At acceptance, ensure the core has not been saturated, detergent residue or musty smells remain absent, and bedding is not replaced until fully dry.

### 13. Curtain cleaning

For curtain cleaning, photograph each panel's width, drop, lining, fabric and decorative details, and identify whether it should be treated while hanging or removed for laundering. Some textiles shrink or lose pleats under water or steam, so review the care label and agree on a limited test before choosing a method. Specify responsibility for taking down tracks and hooks, labelling panels and reinstalling them, with protection for nearby walls, flooring and equipment. Compare quotes by metres, layers, linings, working height, transport and refitting. At handover, check length, colour, pleat alignment, intact fittings and smooth operation.

### 14. Upholstery disinfection

Disinfecting upholstery is a different claim from removing dirt or odour. First define the fabric and intended outcome, and whether there is a justified need for disinfection rather than routine cleaning. Request the product or validated process, textile compatibility instructions, required contact time, ventilation and precautions for people and pets. Fragrance sprays or steam alone do not establish pathogen reduction. Some disinfectants can damage fabric or padding, so compatibility testing and the product's labelled intended use matter. Compare quotes by preliminary cleaning, documented application, limitations and safe re-entry time. At handover, request an application record and reject promises of permanent or universal sterilisation.

## Façade and site cleaning

### 15. Glass façade cleaning

Survey glazing height, pane count, coatings, frames and seals; supply overall photographs and close-ups of mineral deposits or adhesive marks. Agree whether ground-based tools suffice or specialist access is required, and who provides barriers and protects pedestrians. Request a small compatibility test before scraping or applying chemicals. Compare measured glass area, access equipment, frame cleaning and corridor closures. Inspect in varied light for streaks, scratches, damaged seals and water ingress.

### 16. Building exterior cleaning

Identify whether the façade is painted, stone-clad, aluminium or composite, and photograph stains, cracked joints and areas prone to leakage. Include heights and access points in the enquiry. Ask for a trial patch and a method statement addressing pressure, chemicals, runoff and protection of windows. Repairs and repainting are separate unless expressly priced. Compare access, drainage and inspection stages; accept the work after drying, checking for discoloration, peeling or water entering joints.

### 17. Car park cleaning

Measure parking bays and circulation lanes by level, identify concrete or epoxy finishes, and mark oil stains, tyre deposits and drains. Agree staged vehicle clearance and quiet working hours. Quotations should distinguish sweeping, suitable degreasing, mechanical scrubbing, wastewater collection and reopening times. Compare surface area and contamination rather than a generic per-bay figure. Check that bay markings remain intact, walkways are dry and non-slippery, and drains are not filled with debris.

### 18. Industrial site cleaning

Map the areas to be cleaned and separate offices from operating machinery, oily floors and potentially hazardous waste. Supply site photos, known substance information and shutdown arrangements. Ordinary cleaning staff must not be assumed competent to handle unidentified chemicals or live equipment; require site risk assessment and qualified waste handling where necessary. Compare isolation, protective equipment, wastewater controls and waste records. Accept against an area-by-area log including exclusions and incidents, not photographs alone.

## Water tank and pool cleaning

### 19. Water tank cleaning

Record tank material, approximate capacity, location, access, water use and visible sediment. Arrange a supply interruption and safe drainage before removing deposits, brushing and rinsing without damaging liners or fittings. Enclosed tanks may present confined-space hazards and must not be entered without appropriate assessment and controls. Compare cleaning, waste removal and photo records separately from disinfection or water testing. Before restoring supply, check covers, floats, connections and the cleaning log.

### 20. Water tank disinfection

Remove sediment before proposing disinfection, and establish the tank volume, lining and intended water use. Require the product name, suitability, concentration, contact time, measurement and rinsing method under its instructions. Explain how users are prevented from drawing water during treatment. Compare documented dosing, return-to-service timing and whether independent sampling is included. Applying disinfectant does not itself establish drinking-water safety; any such claim needs appropriate test evidence. Obtain the treatment record and verify the tank is secured.

### 21. Swimming pool cleaning

Provide pool dimensions, depth, lining type, photographs of staining or algae, and filter and pump condition. Clarify routine cleaning versus full drainage, water disposal and separate repairs. Ask for a schedule covering vacuuming, walls, baskets, filtration and relevant water chemistry readings. Compare visit frequency, actual tasks and consumables; clear-looking water alone does not establish safe conditions. Inspect surfaces and equipment and obtain operating and test records before accepting the service.

## Pest management

### 22. Cockroach control

Photograph sightings and identify moisture, cracks and food sources without exposing occupants to pesticides. Ask for inspection of harbourage and entry points before selecting bait or another suitable treatment. The quote should state occupant and pet precautions, product directions, follow-up visits and the competence of the operator. Compare inspection points and monitoring outcomes, not spray counts. Obtain a treatment map and prevention advice; one visit cannot guarantee permanent eradication.

### 23. Ant control

Map ant trails, entry points, nearby food and water and potential outdoor nests. Request inspection and removal of attractants before choosing suitable bait or barriers; indiscriminate spraying may miss the colony. Disclose children, pets and food preparation areas. Compare monitoring points, follow-up timing and whether gap sealing is included. Obtain instructions on leaving bait undisturbed and a record of remaining activity, rather than relying on a brief absence of visible ants.

### 24. Rodent control

Photograph gnaw marks, droppings and gaps around doors or pipes, especially near food storage. A sound plan combines exclusion, sanitation and appropriately protected traps or bait stations. Never accept exposed poison or unrecorded placements; clarify safe removal of carcasses and contaminated material. Compare inspection intervals, station mapping and activity indicators. On completion check the station log and outstanding building repairs; one captured animal does not prove all routes are sealed.

### 25. Bed bug treatment

Bites alone do not confirm bedbugs. Request inspection of mattress seams, bed frames, adjacent furniture and curtains; photograph any insects or traces found. State room and bed counts and ask how linen and possessions should be handled without spreading infestation. Compare inspection scope, treatment preparation, resident precautions and planned follow-up visits rather than instant-eradication claims. Obtain a treated-area list, laundry instructions and a date for reinspection.

### 26. Scheduled pest contracts

A recurring pest-control contract should begin with building use, storage, waste points, grounds and historical sightings. Require a monitoring plan with inspections, traps, prevention measures and a route for urgent reports, rather than automatic spraying at every visit. Compare documented site coverage, emergency response, operator competence and corrective actions. Review dated station maps, trend records and open issues each month before approving the service.

## Disinfection services

### 27. Home surface disinfection

Define whether the household needs ordinary cleaning or targeted surface disinfection. List rooms, high-touch surfaces, vulnerable finishes, children, pets and suitable vacancy periods. Require product identification, approved surface use, contact time and ventilation instructions; fragrance or airborne spraying is not proof of disinfection. Compare pre-cleaning, surface coverage, electronics protection and records. Check the treatment log and re-entry advice without accepting promises of lasting infection prevention.

### 28. Commercial facility disinfection

List offices, meeting rooms, shared touchpoints, toilets and reception areas, with occupancy hours and moisture-sensitive devices. Assess the need for disinfection, clean visible dirt first and use a surface-compatible product with its required contact time and ventilation. Compare room coverage, out-of-hours access, consumables and documentation. Inspect the checklist, equipment condition and reopening guidance; sweeping claims of eliminating all pathogens require evidence.

## Building operation and facilities management

### 29. Maintenance management

Begin with an asset register, maintenance contracts, fault history and a priority scale for each property. State site count, response hours, approval authority and recurring equipment faults. Compare ticket intake, escalation, contractor coordination, spending limits and parts charged separately. Management and actual repair work must not be conflated. Accept the service against open and closed work orders, delay reasons and linked invoices.

### 30. Facility operations

List buildings, plant rooms, pumps, lifts, HVAC and electrical systems, operating hours and access authority. Require shift coverage, handover logs, critical-system monitoring and emergency coordination rather than mere on-site attendance. Compare staff qualifications, operating records, energy readings and repair exclusions. A facilities operator is not automatically qualified for every specialist intervention. Review daily logs, outages, corrective actions and parts requests.

### 31. Preventive maintenance

Build preventive maintenance around equipment registers, manufacturer guidance, operating hours and fault history. Supply model numbers, ages and nameplate photographs for pumps, air-conditioning and electrical assets. Require a task schedule separating inspection, cleaning, calibration, replacement and testing, with isolation procedures and parts exclusions. Compare asset coverage, measurement records and corrective follow-up. A signed attendance sheet alone is not proof that maintenance was performed.

### 32. Electrical systems monitoring

Provide distribution-board schedules, critical loads and outage history; do not open live panels merely to take photographs. Define qualified inspection, relevant measurements and safe isolation procedures. Distinguish monitoring from electrical repairs and state when thermal inspection is appropriate. Compare panel counts, visit frequency, response and reporting. Accept dated readings, risk-ranked defects and evidence that faults were rectified or referred to competent specialists.

### 33. AC systems monitoring

List HVAC units, types, locations, operating hours and rooms with inconsistent cooling. Supply nameplates and service history. Request temperature and airflow measurements and checks of filters, drainage and controls appropriate to the system. Compare monitoring frequency, energy trends and early warnings, with compressor replacement and other parts priced separately. Review before-and-after readings and causes of deviations rather than relying on a brief impression of cool air.

### 34. Plumbing and drainage monitoring

Describe water supply, tanks, pumps, drainage routes, previous leaks and meter readings. Photograph damp patches without dismantling finishes prematurely. Require a plan for appropriate pressure, leak and drain checks, and identify specialist work or opening-up as separate tasks. Compare inspection points, visit frequency and response time. Review meter logs, unresolved issues and repair tests before concealed work is closed.

### 35. Building condition reports

State whether the building-condition report supports handover, maintenance planning or visible-defect assessment; a visual survey is not a structural engineering certification. Supply age, available drawings, service history and photographs of cracks, dampness and equipment. Require clear inspection limits, inaccessible areas, tests and risk priorities. Compare professional scope and evidence, not page count. Check dated photographs, defect locations, actionable recommendations and referral of structural concerns to qualified experts.

## Workforce and operational support

### 36. Cleaning personnel provision

Provide floor area, surface types, toilets, waste points, operating hours and a task schedule distinguishing routine from deep cleaning. Clarify who supplies chemicals, machines and consumables. Compare actual staffing hours, supervision, absence cover and safe chemical handling. High-access work and hazardous substances are not automatically included. Accept through attendance records, completed checklists, sampled quality inspections and damage reporting.

### 37. Loading crew provision

Supply weights, dimensions, quantities, floor levels, carrying distances, truck access and lift availability. Photograph routes and obstacles and identify loads requiring mechanical handling. Quotes should state crew size, shift, protective equipment, packaging and responsibility for damage or delays. Compare workload and site conditions, not headcount alone. Check quantities, damage and removal of packaging without obstructing exits.

### 38. Maintenance technician provision

Identify whether the required technician is for electrical, plumbing, HVAC or finishing work, and provide fault symptoms, equipment numbers and repair history. Confirm qualifications, permitted work, tools, attendance hours and testing, with parts separate from labour. A general technician must not undertake restricted or hazardous work beyond competence. Compare response, repair documentation and warranty terms. Accept diagnostic notes, test results and outstanding issues.

### 39. Site supervisor provision

Define workforce size, trades, shifts, project stages, reporting authority and who approves changes. A site supervisor is not automatically the appointed designer or statutory supervising engineer. Provide the programme, drawings and inspection hold points. Compare relevant experience, presence, daily records, risk control and concealed-work documentation. Accept attendance, progress photographs, decisions and unresolved items rather than a generic summary.

### 40. Residential compound operations

Inventory residential buildings, units, common spaces, plant and complaint channels, and distinguish landlord, manager and tenant responsibilities. Require cleaning and maintenance plans, shifts, emergency response, supplier monitoring and privacy-conscious complaint records. Compare building coverage, staffing, performance indicators and separate material costs. Security and tenancy administration are not included unless specified. Review building-by-building reports, recurring faults and unresolved expenditure.

### 41. Commercial facility operations

Document tenant activities, public hours, circulation spaces, plant rooms, delivery slots and waste arrangements. Specify coordination of cleaning and maintenance around trading hours, incident handling and out-of-hours access. Compare coverage, emergency planning, supplier coordination and extra-work rates, with licensed specialist services separately defined. Review dated work orders, incident logs, tenant feedback and open safety issues.

### 42. Project support services

Break the broad term project support into measurable tasks: material transport, site preparation, cleaning or assistants, with quantities, weights, dates and handover points. Supply site photographs, access routes and interfaces with other contractors. Require explicit staffing, equipment, consumables, waste responsibility and exclusions. Compare defined outputs rather than an undefined all-inclusive promise. Accept against a task and quantity checklist, site condition and recorded exceptions.

## Cleaning or pest-management quotation brief

Describe site use, measured area, rooms/floors, surfaces, stains, close-up photographs, sensitive materials, access time and available water/power. Distinguish cleaning, stain removal, disinfection, pest treatment and preventive maintenance: they have different methods and outcomes. If no company is listed, Biet Al Reef customer support can collect details through the quotation form without guaranteeing a crew or result.

**Common question:** Does steam cleaning prove disinfection? No. Any claim depends on the validated process or product, contact time and surface, not the name alone.
