# Mohammed Bin Zayed City — Factories, equipment rental and logistics

> **Area scope:** For service enquiries in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, Abu Dhabi, specify the zone, villa or building and access route. No provider, price or permit is implied; shared technical guidance is not evidence of locally completed work.


Compare hire, fabrication and haulage based on duties, quantities, access, dates and operating responsibilities—not only a headline rate. Certain factory and retail specialties remain restricted or proposed in the source taxonomy; listing them does not activate a supplier. Biet Al Reef customer support can collect request details through the quotation form where no company appears, without guaranteeing availability.

## Construction and lifting equipment hire

### 1. Scaffolding hire

For façade or roof-level work that cannot be reached safely from ladders, send façade photographs and a sketch showing the run length, required platform height, doors and access points. Ask the hirer to specify the scaffold system, bay dimensions, loading platforms, ground support, and who designs, erects, inspects and alters it during hire. Compare transport, erection, dismantling, extensions and delay charges alongside the daily rate. Before use, obtain a competent inspection record and check platforms, edge protection and safe access; changed scaffolding must be reassessed rather than assumed safe.\n
### 2. Crane hire

A crane for a chiller, tank or structural element cannot be selected by load weight alone. Supply dimensions, centre of gravity, landing point, lift radius, height and an obstruction sketch. For a Mohammed Bin Zayed City enquiry, state entrance width and possible standing position without assuming the road or ground can support the crane. Compare rated capacity at the actual radius, rigging, operator, lifting plan, exclusion arrangements and standby charges. Check equipment and lifting-gear inspection records before work, and ensure no one is exposed beneath the suspended load.\n
### 3. Excavator hire

Excavating a utility trench differs from digging foundations or a pool. Describe purpose, footprint, depth, required levels, spoil stockpile position and machine access, with site photographs. Existing utility drawings and a suitable method of locating buried services must be reviewed; a clear-looking surface is not evidence that no services exist. Compare excavator size, attachments, operator, fuel, mobilization, downtime and spoil removal, and agree who provides excavation support. On completion, measure levels and dimensions and record any damage to paving or boundaries before demobilization.\n
### 4. Compaction plant hire

For backfill beneath paving, around foundations or beside buried pipes, equipment size is only one selection factor. Provide the fill area, soil type, proposed layer thickness, access width and compaction criteria specified for the project. A small plate compactor may reach edges but cannot necessarily deliver the output needed for an open site; compare productivity, vibration effects, fuel, operator and transport. Quote density testing separately where required. Do not accept a layer merely because it looks flat: use the agreed inspection and test evidence.\n
### 5. Concrete mixer hire

A mixer may suit small concrete or mortar batches where ready-mix delivery is not practical, but the choice depends on volume, specification and working time. State the quantity per batch, water and power sources, mixing area, storage and material movement. Distinguish drum capacity from usable batch output. Compare delivery, cleaning, minimum hire and breakdown support; specify who controls mix proportions and quality, because hiring a mixer does not certify concrete strength. Test rotation, emergency stop and guards on handover and photograph the equipment condition.\n
### 6. Concrete cutting equipment hire

Before hiring a concrete saw or core drill, distinguish a circular service penetration from a linear cut. Provide diameter or cut length, slab or wall thickness, work position and arrangements for cooling water, slurry and debris. Photograph both faces where possible and identify potential reinforcement and concealed services. Cutting a load-bearing element requires assessment and appropriate approval by the responsible technical professional first. Compare blade or core consumables, operator, dust and noise control, waste removal and access. Check final dimensions, edges and unintended damage at handover.\n
### 7. Project equipment hire

For a project using several machines at different stages, prepare a schedule linking each task to the plant type, output, attachments and expected delivery and return dates. Separate dry hire from operated hire and clarify fuel, transport, insurance, breakdown response and after-hours charges. For residential access, record gate width, storage space and internal movement routes rather than assuming larger machines can enter. Use a signed handover and return record for each machine, with serial number, meter hours, accessories, visible damage and a clear fault-reporting contact.\n
## Small equipment and tool hire

### 8. Generator hire

For temporary power during a fit-out, event or maintenance job, submit a load schedule in kW or kVA, identifying motors with high starting currents, operating hours and the distribution-board location. Simply adding nameplate ratings is insufficient: a qualified electrician should assess load distribution, earthing, protective devices and safe isolation from existing mains. Show exhaust clearance, ventilation and refuelling arrangements away from entrances. Compare continuous rather than peak rating, noise, fuel, cables, delivery, operator, servicing and outage terms. Record meter hours and leaks at handover, then check voltage, frequency and protection under a suitable test load.

### 9. Cleaning equipment hire

For a scrubber, wet extractor or dry vacuum, describe each floor or fabric surface, area, type of soil, water and electrical access, drainage and the available drying window. Sensitive stone may require a different brush and chemical from resilient flooring; a machine or detergent should not be assumed safe for upholstery. Request an itemised list of brushes, filters, hoses and consumables, and clarify cleaning and damage charges on return. Compare practical coverage per hour, working width, tank size, delivery and breakdown support. Test suction, leaks, brush movement and tank condition, photographing the machine at collection and return.

### 10. Air compressor hire

For pneumatic tools, spraying or light breaking, supply each tool's operating pressure and continuous airflow demand, simultaneous tool count, hose lengths and available power. Tank volume alone does not establish suitability: compare delivered airflow at working pressure and the required air quality, including moisture or oil separation. Identify ventilation, noise and hose-routing constraints and itemise couplings, service, fuel or electricity. Before acceptance, check the pressure gauge, regulator, relief device and leaks. Ordinary compressor air must never be treated as breathing air without a purpose-designed, appropriately certified system.

### 11. Power-tool hire

Specify the actual operation—drilling concrete, cutting tile, grinding metal or driving fixings—and provide material thickness, hole diameter or cut depth and working clearance. Ask for the exact tool model, power and permitted disc or bit sizes, with a clear list of batteries, chargers and chargeable consumables. Compare dust extraction, guards, cable reach and usability in confined areas, not just the rental fee. On collection inspect plug, insulation, handle, guard and accessory retention; perform a brief safe functional check and record existing defects before the hire period starts.

### 12. Welding equipment hire

Before hiring a welder, identify metal type, thickness, joint position and whether the process is stick, MIG or TIG; include joint photographs and the working location. Current, shielding gas, wire and electrodes depend on the process, so check machine output and duty cycle against the job and clarify who supplies cylinders and consumables. Indoors or near combustible material, competent personnel must plan ventilation, fire prevention and protection from sparks and fumes; a listing is not a hot-work authorisation. Compare setup, leads, work-return connections, delivery and protective accessories. Inspect cables and connectors and verify controlled operation before acceptance.

### 13. Water pump hire

For draining pooled water, a tank or an excavation, state the approximate volume, target flow, suction-to-discharge elevation difference and hose length, and whether the water contains sand or debris. A clean-water pump is not automatically suitable for dirty water, and friction losses and actual head reduce delivered flow. Photograph intake and proposed discharge points; do not assume disposal to a road or public drain is permitted. Compare pump curves, hose diameters, electrical protection, dry-running safeguards, transport and monitoring duties. At handover check actual flow, leaks, check valves and safe electrical connections.

### 14. Garden equipment hire

State whether the job is mowing, hedge trimming, branch pruning or leaf collection, and provide garden area, plant height, stones, irrigation lines and narrow access points. A lawn mower cannot substitute for a hedge trimmer or blower, and elevated tree work may require qualified workers and separate access equipment. Confirm fuel or batteries, blades, protective accessories and who removes green waste; compare operating hours, noise, cleaning charges and possible irrigation damage. Inspect guards, handles, blades and battery or fuel condition, and test the equipment in a clear safe area before work.


## Aluminium and glass production

### 15. Aluminium profile production

For a custom aluminium extrusion, supply a dimensioned cross-section showing nominal wall thickness, interlocking features and fixing zones, and explain whether it serves a window frame, façade or industrial assembly. Colour alone is not a specification: request the proposed alloy, surface treatment and coating or anodising requirements, along with lengths, quantities and agreed tolerances. Compare any die cost, minimum order, cutting waste, pre-production sample and lead time. Do not assume a profile has structural capacity without appropriate design. At receipt, measure section dimensions and straightness and check for twisting, scratches and colour consistency against the approved sample.

### 16. Door and window production

Start with a numbered opening schedule, site-verified width and height, swing direction, threshold level, glazing and specified hinges and locks. Require fabrication drawings showing framing, drainage, gaskets, fixing positions and how uneven surrounding construction will be accommodated. Compare the proposed system against project requirements for air, water, thermal, acoustic and wind performance where applicable, rather than frame appearance alone. Clarify responsibility for measurement, installation and perimeter sealing. Approve a sample unit before batch production; at handover reconcile labels and dimensions, test movement and locking, and verify drainage using an appropriate agreed check.

### 17. Toughened glass manufacture

For glass in doors, guards or façades, provide a drawing for each pane showing thickness, colour, edge treatment, holes, hinge cut-outs and intended use. Finalise geometry before heat treatment: toughened glass cannot ordinarily be cut or drilled afterwards. Request the manufacturer's product identification and relevant test information, and assess whether laminated glass or a retention system is required for fall-risk locations; toughening alone does not establish suitability everywhere. Compare edge samples, dimensional tolerances, packaging and transport. Before acceptance inspect sizes, visible optical distortion, scratches and edge damage, and record any rejected panes.

### 18. Glass façade preparation

Façade preparation starts with coordinated drawings connecting each glass identifier to building grids, elevations, framing dimensions, brackets and movement joints. A competent designer must establish loads, thermal movement, glazing selection and fixing design; the factory then needs a cutting schedule and safe lifting and installation sequence. Compare offers for the complete façade system, weather seals, tolerances and agreed factory checks rather than glass area alone. Before dispatch inspect pane labels, edges, finishes and protective packaging. At delivery reconcile each batch against the installation schedule and obtain inspection records without treating fabrication as regulatory approval.

### 19. Partition fabrication

For office or internal partitions, state whether the assembly is fixed or demountable, its length and height, door positions, adjacent furniture and electrical or air-conditioning interfaces. Acoustic privacy and visual screening require different specifications: request the full partition build-up, glass or panel thickness, seals and floor-to-ceiling junction details. Compare workshop drawings, wall connections, maintenance access, exposed-edge protection and whether dismantling or reinstatement is included. Inspect a representative junction and door before production, then check frame alignment, dimensions, door operation and finish quality at handover.

### 20. Glass product supply

For glass supply without installation, issue a pane schedule specifying dimensions, thickness, colour and type—annealed, toughened, laminated or insulating glass—plus location, exposure, edge polishing and drilled holes. Glass thickness cannot be chosen by area alone: loads and safety requirements depend on the designed supporting system. Compare available product documentation, labels, batch identification, transport racks and breakage or replacement terms before delivery. Count panes on suitable supports at receipt, inspect edges and scratches, check insulating-glass seals where relevant, and record damage before signing the delivery note.

### 21. Project batch fabrication

Repeated project orders need a unit schedule tied to installation locations: opening number, model, dimensions, colour, glazing, hardware and required batch date. Approve a first article and workshop drawings before mass production; identify who authorises changes after site measurement and how nonconforming units will be handled. Compare factory quality controls, finish consistency, packing, traceability and spare-unit provision rather than volume discounts alone. At each delivery sample-check dimensions and operation, match labels to the opening schedule, and document missing or damaged units and agreed replacement dates before acceptance.


## Stone factories and cutting shops

### 22. Stone cutting

Before cutting marble or stone slabs, issue a numbered cutting schedule with length, width, thickness, finish and vein orientation, plus photographs of the actual slabs showing natural fissures. Straight cuts, curved profiles and sink openings require different operations, so ask for separate pricing, an agreed waste allowance and ownership of offcuts. Specify dimensional tolerances and edge protection during transport. At receipt, match each piece to the drawing, check diagonals and corners, and record chips or cracks before acceptance.


### 23. Kitchen counter fabrication

Countertop fabrication should follow final cabinet installation and levelling, rather than rely solely on preliminary kitchen drawings. Provide wall returns, sink and hob positions, socket locations and photographs of corners; request a final template showing overhangs, seams and support requirements. Compare stone quotations by thickness, finish, edge profile, aperture reinforcement, delivery, installation and stated warranty rather than material name alone. On installation check level, secure joints, intact edges and any movement clearances required by the chosen system.


### 24. Stair tread fabrication

A stair-stone quotation needs more than the number of steps. Supply a measured drawing of tread depth, riser height, width, landings and irregularities in the existing structure, with close-up photographs of edges. Agree stone thickness, vein orientation, slip-resistant finish, nosing detail, fixing method and who takes final site dimensions. Compare piece numbering, spares, transport and installation responsibilities. At handover check consistent dimensions, stable seating and the absence of sharp edges or conspicuous variations between steps.


### 25. Façade stone preparation

Façade stone preparation is fabrication to a separately engineered fixing design, not merely cutting panels to elevation dimensions. Provide a piece schedule with height, width, thickness, approximate weight, openings and vein orientation, together with approved drawings and the specified fixing system. Compare the workshop's drilling and slotting tolerances, edge treatment, panel identification and protective racks. At delivery check dimensions and fixing points against drawings and inspect cracks around holes; do not treat a fabricator's description as structural approval.


### 26. Stone carving and engraving

For carved stone decoration, supply a scaled drawing or fabrication file showing panel size, engraving depth, minimum line width and clearances. Porosity, grain and manual versus machine methods affect the result; commission a sample in the actual stone before releasing the full batch. Compare post-carving finish, dust removal, corner protection and rework provisions if the output differs from the approved sample. Inspect carving consistency, legibility of letters or motifs and fragile edges for chipping.


### 27. Stone polishing and repair

Before requesting marble polishing or treatment, photograph scratches, stains and joints under side lighting and distinguish surface dirt from chemical etching or fractures requiring repair. Ask for a small-area trial showing the effect of abrasives or chemicals on the stone's colour and sheen; not every stain can be removed. Compare treatment stages, protective products, final cleaning and whether joint filling is included. On handover inspect uniformity and check for swirl marks, colour differences and damaged edges.


### 28. Contractor stone orders

For multi-item contractor orders, issue a numbered bill of pieces linking each item to its shop drawing, installation location, stone type, thickness, edge finish and required delivery date. Require sample and prototype approval before batch production, and allocate responsibility for site measurement, variations and wastage. Compare unit rates, special cuts, packaging, storage, phased delivery and replacement terms. Reconcile crate labels with the packing manifest, inspect samples from each batch and record shortages or damage immediately.


## Tools and equipment retail

### 29. Hand tools retail

When equipping an installation or maintenance crew with hand tools, start from the tasks—fastening, measuring, cutting or gripping—and specify sizes, torque range and insulated handles where necessary. Request item photographs, model numbers and an exact kit inventory; a calibrated torque wrench is not interchangeable with an ordinary spanner. Compare steel quality, replacement parts and return terms rather than the number of pieces on the box. At receipt check joint movement, sizes, cracks and corrosion, and retain an itemised invoice.

### 30. Power tools retail

Choosing a drill, grinder or electric saw depends on the material, thickness, duty cycle and power supply. Send site photographs and required bit or disc dimensions, and request voltage, power, speed settings, guarding and battery/charger details for cordless models. Compare a bare tool with a complete kit only after separating accessories and warranty. Match the serial number on delivery, inspect cords, switches and guards, and perform the manufacturer's no-load check. Never fit a disc whose rated speed is incompatible with the tool.

### 31. Safety equipment retail

Before ordering helmets, safety footwear, eye protection or fall-protection equipment, identify each task's hazards, worker sizes and quantities; protective equipment is not universally interchangeable. Request manufacturer information, applicable product standards, intended use and production or expiry dates where relevant. An advertising claim of approval is not sufficient evidence. Compare fit, compatibility, replacements and user training; fall-arrest equipment requires a complete, properly selected system. Inspect labels, packaging and condition at delivery, and refer safety-critical selection to a qualified professional.

### 32. Workshop equipment retail

For a woodworking, metalworking or vehicle-service workshop, provide a floor plan showing electrical points, ventilation, circulation and equipment weights. State each machine's purpose, capacity and accuracy rather than asking for an unspecified workshop package. Request dimensions, loads, noise information, emergency stops, guarding, consumables and operator training. Compare delivery, installation, commissioning and after-sales support. Have a qualified technician demonstrate functions at handover and record defects, accessories and serial numbers.

### 33. Cleaning equipment retail

Select a pressure washer, wet-and-dry vacuum or floor scrubber according to surface type, area, water/electricity supply and drainage. Send photographs of finishes and obstacles, and distinguish routine cleaning from post-construction residue; brushes, machines and compatible chemicals differ. Request flow and pressure or tank capacity, filters, attachments and permitted cleaning agents. Compare operating time, consumables and warranty. Inspect hoses, leaks and brushes, then trial the machine on a small safe area before acceptance.

### 34. Spare parts retail

For a pump, generator or power-tool spare part, provide the equipment nameplate, serial number, old part code and connection dimensions; visual similarity alone does not establish compatibility. Specify whether an original component or documented equivalent is acceptable, including differences in materials, rating and service life. Compare verified fit, returns, warranty and lead time without assuming stock exists. Check codes, packaging and dimensions at receipt and arrange a competent installation test before closing the fault.

### 35. Equipment servicing

If a machine stops or loses performance, describe symptoms, operating conditions, run hours and its latest service, with photographs of the nameplate and error messages. Request a diagnosis that separates fault repair from cleaning or routine consumable replacement, and obtain parts and labour estimates before authorising work. Compare parts/workmanship warranty, load testing and any transport responsibilities. At handover obtain a fault-and-repair report, identify replaced components, witness safe operation and verify guards and protective devices have been restored.

### 36. Household moving

For a household move, prepare a room-by-room inventory of large furniture, wardrobes, beds, appliances and fragile belongings. Photograph entrances, lifts, stairs and truck parking at both addresses; furniture volume alone does not establish the crew or vehicle needed. Request separate prices for packing, dismantling, loading, transport, delivery to each floor and reassembly, with clear damage liability and exclusions. On delivery reconcile numbered items with the inventory and inspect glass, corners and appliances before signing the handover record.

### 37. Office moving

An office relocation needs a transition plan, not just transport. Count desks, chairs, records and devices, and specify floors, circulation routes and the acceptable business downtime. Assign computer disconnection, backups, server handling and reconnection separately from furniture transport; sensitive media require controlled custody. Compare quotations for workstation numbering, monitor packing, lift protection, out-of-hours labour and liability exclusions. At handover reconcile the new desk layout and item labels, record damage, and leave network and systems testing to qualified IT personnel.

### 38. Furniture dismantling and reassembly

Before dismantling a bed, fitted wardrobe or assembled desk, photograph hinges, shelves, joints and connections; measure the destination if the furniture must fit a different space. Require panel numbering and labelled bags for screws and fittings, together with protection for finished faces and exposed edges. Distinguish routine reassembly from patching old fixing holes, modifying panels or installing new wall anchors, and price those separately. At completion check alignment, stability, drawer and door operation and that no components remain unaccounted for.

### 39. Furniture wrapping

Packing methods depend on the material and the next stage of the move. Glass needs rigid edge protection; lacquered timber needs non-adhering surface protection; fabrics need clean coverings suited to the expected storage period. Provide photographs and dimensions of delicate pieces and state whether items will be stored after transport. Compare the specification for cartons, cushioning, blankets, appliance boxes and restraint within the truck, including items the provider cannot safely pack. Inspect protected corners and labels before loading, and document damage before discarding wrapping at delivery.

### 40. Loading labour supply

To request loading labour, identify the item count, approximate weights, heaviest single object, carrying distance, stairs, lift availability and truck position. Crew size cannot be selected safely without handling equipment; a heavy object may require a trolley or specialist lift rather than manual carrying. Compare crew numbers, hourly minimums, waiting charges, supervision, floor protection and liability. During work keep the carrying route clear, prevent unstable stacking and check that walls, floors and door frames are not damaged.

### 41. Temporary storage

Before arranging temporary storage, photograph and inventory furniture and cartons, noting large dimensions, storage duration and whether partial retrieval may be needed. Ask about the actual storage environment, ventilation, moisture management, pest control and access procedures: paper, timber and textiles have different vulnerabilities. Compare intake and release fees, minimum periods, packing, insurance exclusions and claims procedures rather than assuming comprehensive cover. Obtain a numbered condition receipt and verify seals, item counts and condition when collecting belongings.

### 42. Inter-emirate removals

For a move between emirates, document both addresses, floors, shipment volume, large items and any building access or truck restrictions. Ask whether the vehicle is dedicated or shared, who handles packing and assembly, and precisely when carrier responsibility begins and ends. Compare quotes after accounting for road charges, waiting, extra trips, insurance and exclusions; do not assume a fixed route or arrival time. At delivery reconcile the numbered inventory, photograph any loss or damage and record discrepancies before closing the job.

### 43. Construction material haulage

Identify material type, load weight, offloading equipment, site hours and load restrictions. Compare trips, coverage and delay terms; reconcile weight notes and receipts.

### 44. Timber transport

Provide timber lengths, board sizes, weather sensitivity and stacking plans. Compare straps and edge guards; inspect damage and moisture on arrival.

### 45. Marble transport

Record slab sizes, thickness, weights and upright or flat carrying requirements. Compare racks, packing, breakage liability and offloading; inspect edge cracks before sign-off.

### 46. Equipment transport

Identify each machine's mass, dimensions, lifting points and safe transport preparation. Compare trailers, securing and access; document condition before and after the move.

### 47. Waste transport

Separate concrete, timber, metal and any special-handling wastes and specify skips. Compare loads, reception arrangements and transfer evidence; do not mix incompatible waste types.

### 48. Project truck provision

Specify truck count, payload, hourly or trip basis and site traffic coordinator. Compare driver, fuel, waiting and delay terms; check trip logs before invoicing.

### 49. Loading and offloading

Record package weight, placement, crane or dock access, stairs and fragility. Compare labour, handling aids and arrival slots; inspect stable stacking and site safety.

## Concrete, blocks and cement products

### 50. Ready-mix concrete manufacturing

Specify concrete grade, pour volume, travel time, pumping and acceptance tests from design. Compare batch tickets, mix and late-load rules; collect delivery records and samples.

### 51. Block and brick manufacturing

Set unit size, type, density or strength and pallet count. Compare dimensional control, breakage, storage and handling; count usable blocks.

### 52. Precast cement product manufacturing

Specify kerbs, paving units or precast pieces with dimensions and lifting points. Compare moulds, curing and tolerances; check cast-in fittings, surfaces and sizes.

### 53. Cement product supply to projects

Send quantities, work programme, storage and delivery sequences. Compare units, minimum orders, returns and delivery; reconcile labels and invoices.



## Equipment hire and transport request

Specify machine type, capacity or payload, hire hours/days, work area, access width, ground conditions, risks and operating window. State whether an operator, fuel, delivery, inspection, insurance and site protection are needed, and who bears stoppages and breakdowns. For transport give load dimensions, weight and loading/offloading method. If no company is listed, Biet Al Reef customer support can collect the request without confirming stock or plant.

**Common question:** Does a daily hire rate include an operator? Never assume it; separate labour, delivery, fuel, waiting and start/stop times.
