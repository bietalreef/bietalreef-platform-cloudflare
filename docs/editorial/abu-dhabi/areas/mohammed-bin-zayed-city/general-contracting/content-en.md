# Mohammed Bin Zayed City — General Contracting and Construction

> **Area scope:** This guide addresses service enquiries in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, Abu Dhabi. Quote requests should specify the neighbourhood or zone, building or villa reference and site access. Provider availability, travel charges and permits must be confirmed for the actual location. Shared technical guidance is not evidence of previous local work.


## Guide scope
This guide describes company and establishment services in Mohammed Bin Zayed City; it is neither a live provider inventory nor a directory of individual tradespeople. To request a quote, provide the property type, drawings or photographs, approximate location, scope dimensions and possible survey access. A listed service does not confirm a published provider or availability. Where no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer support can receive the quotation request without guaranteeing a supplier, price or date.

## Building and villa construction
### Villa construction
For a new villa, provide the plot boundaries, required built-up areas, storeys and coordinated design drawings before requesting a lump sum. Require a bill of quantities separating earthworks, foundations, structure, waterproofing, services and finishes; explicitly include or exclude walls, entrances and external works. Compare programmes, inspection hold points and test records, and document each concealed-work stage before it is covered.
### Residential building construction
For a multi-unit residential building, identify unit counts and sizes, shared corridors, parking, lifts, meter locations and central services. Allocate responsibility for roof and wet-area waterproofing, fire systems, cooling and common-area finishes. Request a repeatable apartment inspection checklist and individual handover records; a structure-only price cannot be compared with a commissioned building offer.
### Commercial building construction
Before pricing commercial construction, specify intended occupancy, shop or office areas, operating loads, ventilation, electrical supplies and loading access. Distinguish the building shell and shared services from future tenant fit-out. Ask for a façade and commissioning schedule with clear handover responsibilities, and have the relevant professional confirm requirements for the actual use rather than assuming approval.
### Building works execution
Building works can mean block walls, lintels, openings or external works. Mark each item on drawings and measure by area, length or volume as appropriate; show interfaces with existing construction in photographs. Define block and mortar specifications, local reinforcement, access protection and debris disposal. Accept the work against dimensions, alignment and detailed connections rather than labour completion alone.
### Concrete works
A concrete pour requires structural drawings, member dimensions, reinforcement, specified concrete performance, pump access and washout arrangements—not just a site photograph. Ask for a placing, curing and sampling plan against project specifications, recording delivery and pour times. Check cover, levels and test results before approving concealed or finished work.
### Structural frame construction
For a structural frame, separate foundations, columns, beams, slabs and stairs to avoid conflicting interpretations of scope. Supply coordinated drawings and reinforcement schedules and clarify whether formwork, lifting, backfill and concrete tests are included. Require inspection hold points before pours, checking dimensions, levels, service openings and test records; refer design clashes to the responsible engineer.
### Turnkey project delivery
Turnkey delivery is not a sufficient contract specification. List finish standards, material categories, appliances, kitchens, lighting, external works and explicit exclusions. Tie payments to inspectable deliverables and approve samples and procurement schedules. Before acceptance, commission electrical, water and cooling systems, record defects with rectification dates and collect warranties and final drawings.
### New villa construction
For a new villa in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, start with plot dimensions, entrance and parking arrangements and positions for outdoor AC equipment and tanks. Have the designer check site-specific constraints and coordinate structure with mechanical and electrical penetrations. If relevant, distinguish family, guest and service circulation. Compare insulation, finishes, testing and programme rather than room count alone.
### Residential annex construction
An annex attached to an occupied house requires photographs of the junction, boundary walls, equipment access and existing water, drainage and electrical routes. Record available clearances and obtain an assessment of foundations and structural connections. Specify whether utility diversion, paving reinstatement, waterproofing and junction treatment are included; document the home's condition before work and check for leakage and cracking afterwards.
### Outdoor majlis construction
For an outdoor majlis, specify actual seating capacity, guest circulation, washrooms, washing areas and any pantry. Supply dimensions and drainage, cooling and opening locations; coordinate ceilings, lighting and acoustics with the intended use. Compare insulation, ventilation and cleanable finishes, then commission water, cooling and lighting before furnishing the space.
### Villa extensions
Extending an occupied villa requires an accurate survey of existing rooms and façades and a record of walls and services affected. Seek structural feasibility review and a plan for occupant protection, dust control, worker access and service interruptions. The quote should itemise openings, structural connections, waterproofing, reinstatement and leakage tests; document the property's condition before and after.
### Residential building alterations
For a residential alteration, mark demolished walls, new openings, doors and changed AC or lighting points on a plan. Never classify a wall as non-load-bearing by appearance; structural interventions need professional review. Compare demolition, temporary support, service diversions and reinstatement, then test utilities and inspect finished surfaces at handover.
### Villa restoration
Villa restoration starts with a defect map: numbered photographs of cracks, moisture or loose tiles, dates first noticed and exact locations. Request a diagnosis distinguishing structural movement, water ingress and superficial deterioration before selecting repairs. Price source treatment, tests, monitoring where needed and reinstatement separately; acceptance should follow evidence of resolution, not a fresh coat of paint.
### Residential building renovation
For residential-building refurbishment, list affected units and common areas and whether occupants will remain. Separate plumbing, electrical, cooling, waterproofing and finishes, supplying existing-condition photographs and bathroom and kitchen dimensions. Request a sequence that prevents trade clashes and compare protection, strip-out, waste removal and commissioning. Handover each unit against its own checklist and concealed-work photographs.
## Restoration and refurbishment

### Restoration of older buildings
When concrete is spalling, reinforcement is corroding or render is separating in an older building, do not begin with a repainting quotation. Gather any available drawings and dated photographs, identifying the floor, elevation, damp areas and defect locations. A competent engineer should distinguish superficial deterioration from defects affecting structural safety and specify any necessary investigation before removal or strengthening. Ask bidders to price temporary protection and support, removal of unsound material, treatment of the underlying cause, rebuilding and finishes separately. Agree how occupied rooms will be protected. Accept the work against staged inspection records and photographs taken before repairs are concealed, not appearance alone.

### Villa refurbishment
For an occupied villa in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, first mark rooms that must remain in use, workforce access and waste routes on a simple plan. Prepare a separate schedule for each bathroom, kitchen and room: tile areas, skirting lengths, doors, sockets and plumbing outlets. Identify fixtures to retain rather than pricing wholesale replacement. Compare waterproofing systems and tests, furniture protection, utility shutdowns, sanitaryware, paint specifications and how hidden defects discovered during strip-out will be valued. At handover, test drainage, water pressure, cooling and door operation and keep a room-by-room snag list.

### Apartment renovation
Work in an occupied apartment block needs a different logistics plan from a detached villa. Record the floor, lift dimensions and loading arrangements confirmed with building management, and identify any work affecting balconies or shared corridors. Photograph leaks and cracks before strip-out and map furniture, sockets and air-conditioning points to avoid reopening new finishes. Obtain separate prices for lift and corridor protection, debris removal, bathroom waterproofing tests and responsibility for any agreed repairs to affected neighbouring areas. Check floor falls, thresholds and fixture operation, and collect test records before reoccupation.

### Building rehabilitation
Where a building remains usable but performs poorly, define measurable failures first: repeated cooling breakdowns, overloaded distribution, drainage leaks or deteriorating envelope components. Inventory equipment with capacities, identifiers, approximate age and maintenance history; then request a condition assessment separating repairable items from replacements. A cosmetic façade proposal is not comparable to a programme that rehabilitates building systems. Specify work phases and temporary services if occupants remain, and set acceptance criteria based on commissioning readings, test reports, warranties and instruction on newly installed equipment.

### Structural crack remediation
Before requesting injection into a cracked column, beam or load-bearing wall, mark its position on drawings and dated photographs, recording width, direction, nearby openings and observed changes. Crack appearance alone cannot establish the cause or severity: a qualified structural assessment, movement monitoring or material investigation may be needed. Require a written decision on monitoring, repair or strengthening, with proposed injection or reinforcement materials, preparation and protection of adjacent elements. Reject an undefined lump sum to 'repair all cracks' without a defect map and post-work verification; decorative plaster cracks are a separate problem.

### Façade refurbishment
For a peeling or leaking façade, send both overview and close-up photographs of windows, joints, balconies and access heights, identifying whether the finish is paint, stone or mechanically fixed panels. Investigate moisture sources and fixings before selecting a new colour; coating loose material does not cure the defect. Ask for measured repair areas, safe access arrangements, removal of unsound finishes, substrate preparation, coating or waterproofing system and protection of entrances and parked vehicles. Compare system warranties rather than paint brands alone. Approve a colour sample and inspect joints, adhesion and areas where water collects at handover.

### Preparing a building for reuse
Before changing partitions to accommodate a different use, document the former and proposed functions, anticipated occupants, operating hours, storage and equipment needs. Assemble available drawings and review electrical loads, ventilation, drainage, escape routes and accessibility with the appropriate professionals. Establish which approvals apply to the actual property and proposed activity; a service listing is not permission to change use. Structure the quotation around assessment and coordination, authorised alterations, testing and commissioning, and as-built handover documents. Decorative completion is not sufficient without functioning systems.

## Demolition and concrete works

### Demolition works
Begin with a marked removal schedule separating non-load-bearing partitions from columns, beams, slabs and elements that must remain. Photograph connected rooms and elevations, and identify meters, service runs and the route for carrying debris. A competent professional should assess retained stability, removal sequence and temporary supports before plant is selected. Compare quotations for surveys, isolation, propping, cutting, loading, haulage and repair of accidental damage as distinct items. At handover, check removal limits against the marked drawings and record the condition of retained fabric. Confirm the applicable site-specific permissions before starting.

### Removal of buildings and annexes
Removing a freestanding annex is not the same as detaching a structure from an occupied villa. Supply its footprint, height, roof and foundation type, connection details and photographs showing neighbouring buildings and boundary walls. Ask whether the quote includes buried foundations, floor slabs and abandoned services or only the visible superstructure. Specify machinery access, dust management, debris routes and protection to paving and entrances. Verify final ground levels, remaining utilities and adjoining surfaces in a documented handover.

### Concrete cutting and core drilling
Explain whether the opening is for a pipe, air duct or doorway; the purpose determines clearance and edge requirements. Mark centre points or cut lines, diameter, depth and both faces, with any known reinforcement and service information. Locate conflicts and obtain structural assessment where a load-bearing element may be affected; a marked opening is not permission to cut. Compare diamond cutting or coring by number of openings, thickness, cooling-water capture, dust control and core disposal. Measure the finished opening and inspect its edges and surrounding finishes.

### Foundation and footing concrete pours
Footing dimensions cannot be inferred from the villa floor area: use the engineer's design, ground information and loading. Supply a footing schedule with grids, excavations, levels, reinforcement and concrete requirements, and allocate excavation, dewatering, blinding, waterproofing and backfill responsibilities. Compare concrete volume, reinforcement, formwork, pumping and tests separately rather than relying on a single cubic-metre rate. Inspect excavation cleanliness, cover and starter bars before placement; retain delivery, sampling, curing and level records afterwards.

### Concrete slab pours
For a new or extended slab, provide structural drawings showing spans, thickness, beams, stair openings, service penetrations and levels. Agree formwork and propping, construction loads, pour sequence and installation of embedded electrical and mechanical services before concrete arrives. Relocating an opening afterwards is not a minor change. Compare reinforcement, concrete, pumping, testing, curing and support costs on a like-for-like basis. Check bars and penetrations before pouring; inspect curing, test results, dimensions and finish afterwards. Props should only be removed in accordance with the designer's instructions.

### Concrete wall construction
Identify the wall's function first: retaining soil, resisting lateral loads as a shear wall, or acting as another concrete barrier. Provide length, height, thickness, structural drawings, joints, openings and footing connections. Retaining walls also require defined drainage, waterproofing, backfill and protection, while shear walls depend on the specified reinforcement and structural continuity. Compare formwork, placement stages, compaction, curing and testing in the same scope. Inspect plumb, levels, joints and any honeycombing before concealment, and refer design changes to the engineer.

### Demolition waste removal
Distinguish concrete rubble, blocks, timber, glazing and mixed finishing waste before choosing a loading method or container. Photograph gate widths, truck standing space and the distance from the pile to the loading point. Estimate cubic metres or skips while marking quantities as provisional. Quotes should separate labour, machinery, trips, covered transport, floor protection and authorised disposal; suspect hazardous materials need specialist assessment and segregation. Check for sharp remnants, clean the access route and request disposal documentation where appropriate.

### Home garden design
Start with a measured site plan showing villa entrances, parking, shade and utility rooms. Mark boundary lengths, levels, seating, play and planting zones. Ask for a layout and sections showing drainage, lighting and irrigation; compare drawings, planting schedules and material specifications, not attractive renders alone. At handover check circulation, finished levels and access to service valves.

### Villa garden landscaping
For an existing garden, photograph plants, buried services and paving before requesting changes so removal and replanting are priced separately. Ask which healthy plants will be retained, the quantity and specification of new plants, soil improvement and irrigation modifications. Compare establishment care and protection of existing features; inspect plant positions, drainage and watering operation on completion.

### Outdoor space design
An outdoor scheme must coordinate movement between the entrance, majlis, garden and service areas, not just select paving. Provide path widths, thresholds, door swings and drain locations. Require a material plan covering slip resistance, solar exposure and interfaces with the façade; price loose furniture separately from built works. Review drainage falls and safe circulation before approving the design.

### Green lawn establishment
Clarify whether the green surface is for walking, play or visual planting, since each requires different ground preparation and watering. Measure net area excluding paths and planters. Specify soil depth, drainage, levels and groundcover species. Test irrigation for even coverage and ponding, and agree how failed patches will be rectified during establishment.

### Tree and shrub planting
Mark walls, underground utilities, driveways and the desired delivered plant height on a site plan. Request botanical names, pot or root-ball sizes, quantities, pit preparation, soil amendments and staking details. At handover inspect root condition, watering, support and clearances from structures, and obtain written establishment-care instructions.

### Natural turf installation
Check sunlight, shade, soil condition and any history of saline irrigation water before selecting turf. Ask for the grass variety, supplied area, cutting and delivery schedule, ground preparation and first-watering plan. A square-metre rate without grading and drainage is not comparable. Inspect seams, turf contact with soil and sprinkler coverage, and define responsibility for dry patches during establishment.

### Artificial grass installation
State whether the area is decorative, for children or for frequent foot traffic, and photograph existing surfaces where water might collect. Compare samples by pile height, density, infill and edge fixing, with a section of the base and drainage layers. Separate material and installation warranties. Inspect seam direction, levels, edge security and cleaning access; artificial turf still needs maintenance.

### Plant and tree installation
For a mixed planting order, provide a schedule of quantities, heights, beds and irrigation points. Ask for botanical names, nursery sizes, transport protection and whether excavation, soil and mulch are included. Compare replacement terms during establishment and verify the number, health and location of each plant against the approved layout.

### Tree pruning and shaping
Send full-tree photographs and details of branches near walls, buildings or services. Distinguish crown lifting, deadwood removal and clearance pruning. The quotation should identify access equipment, pruning limits and green-waste removal; severe topping can harm tree health and balance. Inspect cut locations, surrounding property and site clearance at completion.

### Lawn maintenance
When yellow patches or waterlogged areas appear, record irrigation times, shade and mowing frequency before ordering new turf. Require a programme covering mowing, aeration, sprinkler checks, weeds and feeding. Compare visit frequency and whether materials are included. Review dated condition photos and fault reports, ensuring the underlying cause is addressed before replacement.

### Plant fertilisation and treatment
Identify the plant, approximate age, symptoms and previous fertiliser applications, with photographs of leaves and soil. Diagnosis should distinguish nutrition, watering and pest problems before prescribing treatment. Ask for product names, application method, precautions and monitoring; assess new growth and adverse effects rather than treating spraying alone as success.

### Garden pest management
Photograph damaged leaves and visible pests, and disclose children, pets or nearby water features. Ask the provider to identify the pest and consider preventive or targeted measures before selecting any authorised product, with re-entry precautions explained. Compare the affected area, follow-up visits and treatment records rather than promises of permanent eradication.

### Garden cleaning
Separate leaf and green-waste collection from weeding and pressure washing; each has different labour, equipment and disposal requirements. Send photographs of the beds, paths and collection points and specify whether off-site removal is included. Compare waste quantities and protection of planting and irrigation; inspect clear drains and undamaged plants at handover.

### Garden maintenance contracts
Provide planted-area measurements, a tree inventory, irrigation layout and baseline condition photographs. Require a visit schedule for mowing, pruning, pump checks, feeding, pest monitoring and response to faults, with spare parts and replacement plants priced separately. Use dated visit logs, photographs and measurable watering and plant-health outcomes before renewing.

### Irrigation network design
Before drawing pipe routes, provide a garden plan showing lawn, planting beds, trees, the water supply and any existing meter or tank. Sprinkler quantities cannot be derived from area alone: measured source pressure and flow, level changes, pipe lengths and soil conditions matter. Ask for a zone schedule specifying demand, pipe diameters, pressure losses, filtration, isolation valves and controller position. Compare whether design quotations include installation drawings, trench routes and coordination with electrical and drainage services, rather than a conceptual sketch only. At handover, reconcile valve and service-access locations with the site and obtain drawings that can be updated if planting changes.

### Drip irrigation installation
Drip lines suit planting beds and shrub rows, but emitter spacing depends on plant spacing and soil infiltration. Supply bed lengths, planting photographs and water-source details; request emitter flow rates, spacing, maximum lateral lengths and fixing methods. The installation quote should identify filtration, pressure regulation, flushing points and whether trenching and soil reinstatement are included. Run a complete zone and compare wetting near the first and last emitters, checking joints and protected crossings under paths. Obtain a simple valve-location drawing and filter-cleaning instructions to avoid unnecessary excavation during maintenance.

### Sprinkler installation
Measure net lawn area excluding paths and beds, and mark walls, doors and electrical points. Rotary and fixed spray heads differ in flow and throw, so the installer should provide a layout based on overlapping coverage, working pressure and adjustable arcs—not merely a head count. Compare head models, finished installation heights, zone valves, pipework and any turf or paving reinstatement. Witness each zone operating and inspect coverage at the edges without soaking walls or footpaths; adjust heads spraying façades. Record any dry patches after a representative watering cycle before accepting coverage.

### Irrigation tanks and pump installation
Where the supply cannot sustain the irrigation zones, establish daily storage demand and peak flow rather than selecting a pump by motor rating alone. Supply tank capacity, location, water level and the distance to the furthest zone, and explain how the tank will refill. Separate tank and base, suction and discharge connections, valves, dry-run protection, electrical controls and maintenance access in the quotation. A qualified specialist should check pump duty against required flow and head and verify electrical protection; existing wiring must not be presumed suitable. Witness operation of the most distant zone, pressure and leak checks and a low-water shutdown test, and collect operating instructions and warranties.

### Irrigation network maintenance
Describe the actual fault: a dead zone, stuck head, weak drip output or watering that continues after the programme ends. Photograph the controller, valves and affected planting, and record when symptoms began to help distinguish programming faults from blocked filters, pressure losses or failed valves. Require a diagnostic sheet recording pressure, filter condition, valves, wiring and pipe checks, with replacement parts and labour separately priced. Do not accept wholesale pump or network replacement without test evidence. At handover run every zone manually and through the controller, checking shutdown and all disturbed joints for leaks.

### Automatic irrigation controller programming
New trees and established lawns should not automatically share the same watering duration. Prepare a zone inventory listing plant types, emitters or sprinkler heads, garden-use hours and any genuine supply limitations. Request a documented schedule of watering days, runtimes, sequencing and soak intervals, with rain-sensor or fault-stop functions enabled only where supported. Clarify whether the price covers controller settings alone or repairs to defective sensors and wiring. Obtain a copy of the programme and seasonal adjustment instructions, and test manual operation and a full automatic cycle without simultaneous zones exceeding supply capacity.

### Irrigation leak detection
For persistently wet soil, settlement or unusual water use, record whether the symptom appears only during irrigation or also when all zones are off. Send site photographs, approximate valve and pipe locations and any available meter readings. Ask the technician to isolate zones and check valves, pressure and meter movement before proposing excavation; a valve that fails to close can resemble a buried pipe leak. Quotes should distinguish diagnosis from excavation, repair and reinstatement of paving or turf. Pressure-test the repaired section, photograph the connection before backfilling and monitor the site and water use over subsequent cycles.

## Shades and external works — source category currently not selectable

### Car shade installation
Measure bays, vehicle heights, turning paths and door clearance; photograph walls and drainage. Require frame, footing, anchor, covering and runoff drawings, with applicable loads and permissions checked. Compare corrosion protection, warranty, excavation and reinstatement, not fabric area alone. Inspect clearance, fixing, sharp edges and drainage.

### Pergola construction
Clarify whether the pergola is freestanding or attached, and whether it offers shade or rain protection. Supply seating and opening dimensions and request posts, foundations, connections and façade waterproofing details. Price structure, roofing, finish, lighting and maintenance separately. Check plumb, falls, joints and leaks.

### Outdoor seating area construction
Specify seating capacity and circulation around tables and entrances; photograph ground, power, water and drains. Distinguish fixed seating from movable furniture, and separate grading, paving, drainage, shade and lighting in quotations. Inspect ponding, edges, cleaning access and service points.

### External fence installation
Use reliable boundary survey records rather than aerial imagery for lengths, levels, gates and meters. Require footing, post, height and corrosion-protection details, pricing old-fence removal separately. Verify applicable permissions. Check alignment, levels, gate operation and connections at handover.

### Garden pathway construction
State pedestrian or service-cart use and measure route width, levels, trees and drains. Photograph ponding and specify excavation, compaction, base, restraints, slip resistance and drainage. Compare groundwork as well as paving. Check trip hazards, falls and access to inspection covers.

### Interlocking paver installation
Differentiate vehicle bays from garden paths by loading and base design. Measure area and cuts around chambers; specify excavation, compaction, bedding, thickness, edges and falls. Compare old-surface removal, waste and base layers, not a bare area rate. Inspect gate levels, filled joints, edges and settlement.

### Waterfall and fountain construction
Measure basin depth and distance from seating and children's routes. Require waterproofing, recirculation, pump, filter, overflow, drainage, safe electrical supply and maintenance access. Separate civil work, cladding, equipment and cleaning costs. Witness leak, overflow, pump and noise tests and obtain servicing instructions.
