# Mohammed Bin Zayed City — General maintenance and finishing

> **Area scope:** This guide addresses service enquiries in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, Abu Dhabi. Quote requests should specify the neighbourhood or zone, building or villa reference and site access. Provider availability, travel charges and permits must be confirmed for the actual location. Shared technical guidance is not evidence of previous local work.


## General maintenance

### 1. Periodic villa maintenance

Before agreeing a villa maintenance plan, inventory AC units, pumps, heaters, doors and previous leak locations. Require a visit schedule with specific checks for each asset, separating spare parts and emergency callouts from the base fee. Accept photographs, test readings and corrective-action dates rather than a generic 'serviced' note.

### 2. Periodic building maintenance

For a multi-unit building, maintain an asset register for shared pumps, lighting, corridors and roofs and a clear tenant reporting route. Ask how service interruptions are prioritised and who arranges access to private units. Compare contracts by response times, inspections, reporting and exclusions. Review the closed-work-order log at each reporting period.

### 3. Interior damage repairs

Photograph wall, ceiling or skirting damage with a scale and establish whether impact, moisture or movement caused it. Require the underlying cause to be addressed before specifying removal, surface preparation and reinstatement. Compare the repair area, colour and texture matching and furniture protection. Inspect after curing and record whether the original symptom has returned.

### 4. Door and window repairs

Identify whether a door or window is binding, has worn hinges, or leaks air or water around its frame. Record opening dimensions, frame material and photographs of locks, tracks and seals. Ask for adjustment and replacement parts as separate quote items. Test smooth operation, locking, weather seals and drainage at handover.

### 5. Dampness treatment

Do not repaint a damp patch before investigating its source. Record where and when it appears and whether rain, plumbing or condensation may be involved. Require appropriate diagnosis and a drying plan where finishes must be removed. Compare investigation, leak or waterproofing repair and reinstatement as distinct tasks. Keep test results and concealed-work photographs.

### 6. Crack treatment

Distinguish hairline plaster cracks from cracking that may involve load-bearing elements. Photograph each crack beside a ruler and date the record; monitor movement if advised. Require diagnosis before filling, and qualified structural assessment where warning signs exist. Compare preparation and repair materials, then inspect after curing and retain the measurement record.

### 7. Pre-handover property preparation

Prepare a room-by-room pre-handover checklist covering doors, switches, drainage, sealants, paint and installed equipment. Number and photograph defects and specify functional testing and rectification, not merely cleaning. Compare quotations by return visits, retesting and responsibility for materials. Accept a signed list of closed and outstanding items.

### 8. Minor home maintenance

Group minor household jobs in one photographed list with locations and measurements, such as a loose handle, fitting or worn seal. Identify any electrical or plumbing work requiring a qualified trade rather than bundling hazardous tasks into general handyman work. Compare visit charges, materials and time, and test each repair before the technician leaves.

### 9. Curtain installation

Specify curtain type, weight and track length, and whether fixing is into concrete or plasterboard. Measure window width and finished installation height. Check for concealed services before drilling and require suitable brackets and fixing spacing. Test the full travel and check for sagging or rubbing at handover.

### 10. Shelf installation

State the intended shelf load, wall construction and potential concealed services. Measure the mounting height and clearance from doors and circulation routes, and require anchors suited to concrete, block or plasterboard as applicable. Check level and stability under the agreed safe load, not just appearance.

### 11. TV mounting

Provide screen size, weight, mounting pattern and a photograph of the wall and power/data points. Set the viewing height, required tilt or swivel and verify wall and bracket suitability. Compare quotes for fixings and cable management. At handover, test movement, security and access to ports.

### 12. Accessory installation

Bathroom accessories, mirrors and hooks have different loads and fixing requirements. Photograph the tiled or painted surface and measure positions relative to doors and sanitary fittings. Consider hidden plumbing and waterproofing before drilling. Agree appropriate anchors or adhesive and inspect alignment, strength and surface damage.

### 13. Furniture assembly

Check all cartons, components, hinges and room clearances before assembly. Follow the manufacturer's instructions without improvising holes or cutting panels, and anchor tall units against tipping where appropriate. Test doors, drawers, level and stability, record missing parts and retain care instructions.

### 14. Minor fault repairs

Describe the actual fault—dead socket, dripping tap or sticking lock—with photographs and when it occurs. Require diagnosis before replacing components, and route hazardous electrical or serious leak work to qualified specialists. Compare diagnostic and repair charges separately. Test operation afterwards and document unresolved symptoms.

## Requesting a quotation

Provide the address, property type, clear photographs, task list, approximate dimensions and suitable access times. Compare inspection, repair, materials and final testing scope. If no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer service can receive the enquiry without guaranteeing a provider or price. Individual tradespeople remain separate from companies and establishments.

## Integrated finishing

### 15. Villa finishing

Finishing a new villa is not the same scope as refurbishing an occupied one. For a new build, establish which structural and service works are already complete; for an occupied home, identify finishes to remove, rooms still in use and protected access routes. Provide floor plans, room dimensions and a clear statement of whether AC, electrical, plumbing, waterproofing, doors and kitchens are included or whether the contractor is pricing surface finishes only. Request approved physical samples and a bill of quantities using square metres, linear metres and item counts, with materials, labour and options distinguishable. Document relevant service and waterproofing tests before ceilings and wet areas are closed. Handover should check floor falls, joints, switches, drainage and door operation, with room-by-room defects and a reinspection date.

### 16. Apartment finishing

A flat's gross area is not enough to price a fit-out. Supply a room plan, lift and corridor dimensions, and photographs of existing bathrooms and floors; identify any breaking-out and debris removal. Confirm building-management access, protection and work-hour requirements rather than assuming permission. Ask for separate quantities for strip-out, waste transport, substrate levelling, waterproofing, tiling, painting, joinery and appliance reconnection. Compare material grades, quantities, exclusions and disruption periods rather than an undefined 'complete finishing' total. At handover, check wet-area drainage and leaks, doors, service connections and the condition of lifts and common corridors.

### 17. Retail-shop finishing

Define the actual business use before designing a shop fit-out: a clothing store and a premises with additional power, cooling or drainage needs will have different specifications. Provide a plan marking customer space, storage, counters, frontage, existing escape routes and power/data positions. Request dimensioned shop drawings for the frontage, displays, lighting and signage, and identify who will check landlord and applicable authority requirements without promising approval. Price fixed furniture and shopfront fabrication separately from services and surface finishes, with samples and delivery milestones. On handover, test sockets, lights and doors and review circulation widths, cleanability and maintenance access.

### 18. Office finishing

An office layout depends on workstations, meeting rooms, acoustic privacy and network demand, not floor area alone. Supply a furniture plan, printer and any server locations, a power/data outlet schedule, ceiling heights and existing air-distribution routes. Specify partition construction, glazing, doors, acoustic treatment and maintainable flooring, separating loose furniture from fixed works. Compare quotations by outlet count, partition specification and staged work that limits disruption to staff. Acceptance should include power and network checks, lighting and door operation, partition junctions and a usable record of operating and maintenance information.

### 19. Interior-space refurbishment

Refurbishing a living room may mean a cosmetic refresh or the relocation of electrical and AC points and removal of existing cladding; these are different scopes. Photograph all sides, measure walls and openings, and identify furniture and finishes to retain and protect. Request a strip-out, protection and debris plan, followed by substrate repairs before new finishes; verify site dimensions before relying on a 3D concept. Compare allowances for hidden repairs and agreed alternatives if selected products become unavailable. At handover, inspect flooring-to-skirting junctions, colours in daylight, switch positions and unobstructed door swings.

### 20. Decorative fit-out work

For decorative battens, wall panels or concealed lighting, establish whether the provider must design the details or only fabricate and install approved drawings. Send reference images and dimensioned elevations showing sockets, corners and openings; approve a physical sample for colour, texture and thickness. The quotation should identify substrate preparation, adhesive or mechanical fixing, edge details and access to lighting drivers, plus suitability for heat or moisture where relevant. Compare joint locations and detail sections, not just the total cladding area. Inspect secure fixing, aligned edges, hidden wiring and even lighting at handover.

### 21. Integrated finishing handover

This service inspects, records and closes defects in completed works; it is not a contract to carry out a new fit-out. Before inspection, obtain the agreed drawings, material schedule, warranties and previous test records. Prepare a numbered room-by-room checklist for floors, paint, doors, sanitary fixtures, electrical items and cooling systems. Photograph defects with precise locations and severity, assign responsibility and re-test dates rather than signing a generic acceptance. Quotations should distinguish the initial inspection from follow-up visits and specialist testing requiring qualified parties. Close the handover record only when accepted items and outstanding exclusions are documented in writing.

## Gypsum and decorative work

### 22. Gypsum ceiling installation

Before selecting a gypsum ceiling, request a reflected-ceiling plan showing finished heights and clearance for AC ducts, sprinklers, cables and access hatches. Measure each room and perimeter, and identify chandeliers or heavy fittings that need independent structural support rather than attachment to plasterboard. Compare board grades and moisture suitability, framing and suspension details, joint treatment, paint and service openings as separate quote items. Inspect concealed services before closure, then check flatness, corners and visible fasteners or cracks at handover.

### 23. Gypsum partition walls

A gypsum partition needs a setting-out drawing with length, height, door opening, sockets and AC interfaces. Specify acoustic privacy for bedrooms or offices and concealed backing wherever a television or cabinet will be mounted. Compare bids by board layers, stud dimensions, insulation and door-frame reinforcement; any fire-resistance requirement must come from the approved design, not an assumed board label. Inspect alignment, door stability and service locations before the second face is closed, then review finished joints under normal lighting.

### 24. Concealed-light coves

A concealed lighting cove needs a dimensioned section showing length, depth, LED setback and direction of light. Specify colour temperature, dimming method, driver location and maintenance access; a driver permanently sealed behind gypsum is difficult to replace. Approve a short illuminated sample before committing to the full room. Compare prices for gypsum, wiring, strip, driver and paint together, and test even illumination, straight edges, flicker and component access at handover.

### 25. Decorative gypsum features

For gypsum cornices, arches and projections, supply a reference photograph together with dimensions and junctions at doors, curtains and AC outlets. Repeated profiles benefit from one approved sample showing curve radius, projection and finish, especially where room ceiling levels differ. Compare quotations by linear metres, special pieces, fixing, filling and painting rather than an undefined 'full décor' allowance. Inspect symmetry, shadow lines and vulnerable edges before accepting the work.

### 26. Wood-effect panel installation

First identify whether wood-effect panels will sit behind a television, in a busy corridor or near moisture; the product name alone does not establish water or heat resistance. Measure wall width and height, switches and openings, and approve a physical colour and texture sample with the slat direction. Ask for material composition, thickness, substrate requirements, adhesive or mechanical fixing and edge trims, separating wall repairs from fitting. Check slat spacing, secure ends, outlet access and door clearance, and obtain the specified product's cleaning instructions.

### 27. Marble-effect panel installation

For marble-effect panels in an entrance or media wall, approve actual sheet dimensions and a joint layout so a prominent feature is not interrupted by an awkward seam. Photograph the substrate and identify existing paint, tiles or dampness; confirm adhesive and panel suitability for the actual heat and water exposure, since some decorative sheets are unsuitable near flames. Compare thickness, substrate preparation, outlet cut-outs, trims and edge sealing. Check for bubbles, waviness, consistent veining and sound joints before handover.

### 28. Gypsum repair and maintenance

When a gypsum joint cracks or a ceiling swells near an AC unit, photograph the defect and record whether it returns after repairs or cooling operation. Investigate moisture, framing movement and suspension condition before applying filler. Specify the opening-up area and whether boards or framing must be replaced or joint tape can be repaired, including furniture protection and waste removal. Compare investigation, materials and repainting separately; inspect after drying and confirm the moisture source has been addressed.

## Flooring and wall cladding

### 29. Ceramic tile installation

Before ordering ceramic tiling, distinguish a dry floor from a bathroom or kitchen and supply each surface's dimensions, wall-tile height, drains and door positions. A square-metre figure alone is insufficient: agree a layout showing tile direction, cuts, skirting and movement joints, and price old-tile removal and substrate levelling separately. In bathrooms, review the waterproofing test before it is covered and demonstrate falls towards the drain on site. Compare quotations by tile and adhesive specification, grout, preparation, wastage and protection. At handover, check for potentially hollow-sounding tiles, consistent joints, lippage, door clearance and water draining without ponding.

### 30. Porcelain tile installation

Porcelain differs from ceramic particularly in large formats: slab weight, handling, cutting and substrate flatness can govern installation. Provide room measurements, tile dimensions and thickness, a photograph of the product label and a layout showing vein direction where applicable. Ask for a dry layout before fixing to avoid narrow strips at doorways, and specify adhesive contact, movement joints and transitions to adjacent floors. Quotes should itemise lifting and levelling equipment, column cuts, consumables and responsibility for breakage during handling. Inspect lippage in raking light, vein alignment and secure bedding before signing off.

### 31. Parquet flooring installation

First establish whether the proposed parquet is solid timber, engineered wood or another flooring marketed as parquet; moisture sensitivity and refinishing options differ. Supply room areas, door clearances, skirting dimensions and substrate photographs, and request substrate moisture testing to the product manufacturer's requirements. Compare glued and floating installation only where the chosen system permits them, including underlay, thresholds and expansion allowances around walls and columns. Quotations should state timber grade, finish, warranty and water-exposure limitations. Walk the joints to check movement and squeaking, and compare board alignment and colour variation against the approved sample.

### 32. Vinyl flooring installation

For vinyl flooring, describe the use: a bedroom, an office with wheeled chairs or a space cleaned frequently. This informs the choice between LVT planks, sheet goods and click systems. Provide a dimensioned plan, fixed furniture and any drains; ask for substrate moisture and flatness checks because imperfections can telegraph through the finish. Quotes should identify product and wear-layer thickness, adhesive or locking method, floor preparation, thresholds and edge details, and seam welding for sheet goods where required. Do not assume all vinyl products suit wet rooms. Inspect bubbles, lifting edges, pattern continuity and stability under the expected traffic.

### 33. Interlocking paving installation

For interlocking paving, identify whether the area is a footpath, driveway or vehicle parking space: loading affects paver thickness and the base design. Supply dimensions, existing levels, stormwater direction and locations of utility covers and trees. Require excavation, removal of unsuitable material, base and bedding layers, compaction and edge restraints to be itemised, rather than accepting a paver-only square-metre rate. Approve a colour and laying-pattern sample and confirm finished levels at gates and kerbs. Test drainage with water and check for unstable blocks or settling edges under intended use.

### 34. Wall cladding installation

Wall cladding can mean natural stone, porcelain, timber or composite panels, each with different weights and fixing needs. Supply wall type, height and width, sockets, lighting and concealed services, plus photographs of corners and door junctions. Ask for a physical sample and fixing details suited to the material and substrate; relevant moisture or fire performance must be assessed against the approved project design rather than assumed. Compare preparation, support framing, cuts, joints, corner trims and removable access panels. Inspect security and alignment and ensure ventilation openings and maintenance points remain accessible.

### 35. Flooring repairs

Do not select replacement tiles before investigating why flooring cracked, lifted, sounds hollow or changed colour. Supply close-up and wide photographs with a ruler, the date the defect appeared and any evidence of leakage, settlement or movement joints. Ask for substrate and moisture assessment and a defined removal boundary before pricing; a local patch may be sufficient, or the cause may require wider reinstatement. Compare matching-material sourcing, waterproofing or levelling repairs, waste removal and skirting replacement, with concealed works photographed before closing. At handover check bond, level, walking stability and joint alignment, retaining before-and-after records.

## Electrical installations and lighting

### 36. Building electrical installation

Building electrical installation should start with a room-by-room plan identifying fixed appliances, air conditioning, lighting and power outlets—not simply a socket count. Supply architectural drawings, room dimensions and expected equipment ratings. Ask the electrical designer or contractor to set out circuits, containment routes and isolation points while coordinating with plumbing and HVAC. The quotation should distinguish concealed works, cable and conduit specifications, protective devices, chasing and making good, and any professional approvals required for the particular project. Photograph routes before walls are closed, then obtain updated circuit drawings and appropriate insulation, continuity and protection test records.

### 37. Cable and wiring runs

For cable and wiring runs, establish the origin, destination, installation route and loads served rather than estimating length from a room photograph. Cable cross-section, insulation type, installation conditions and circuit protection must be selected together; incompatible circuits should not be grouped without assessment. Compare quotations that separately identify cable pulling, conduit or trunking, access openings, reinstatement and end labelling. Before energising, a qualified electrician should document the circuits and carry out suitable continuity, insulation-resistance and termination checks. Request an accurate record of the installed routes for future maintenance.

### 38. Distribution board installation

A new or replacement distribution board requires an inventory of existing circuits, supply characteristics, connected loads and spare capacity. Provide a safe external photograph of the old board and its label; do not expose live components. Quotations should identify enclosure capacity, isolation and protective devices, circuit labelling, cable arrangement, maintenance access and any planned supply outage. Breaker selection must follow the circuit design rather than the cheapest available rating. At handover, obtain a circuit schedule fixed to the board, appropriate inspection and test results, and confirmation that covers are secured with no exposed openings.

### 39. Socket and switch fitting

Before fitting sockets and switches, mark the quantity, heights and positions relative to doors, furniture and appliances. Distinguish replacement of a damaged accessory from adding a new point, which may require additional wiring. Send photographs of existing faceplates and box dimensions without dismantling electrical connections yourself. Ask for the accessory type, rating and suitability for the environment, especially in damp locations, plus whether cutting and making good are included. Inspect alignment, secure fixing and correct operation, and have the relevant protection checks completed by a competent electrician; concealed improvised joints are not acceptable.

### 40. Power supply for extensions

Power to an extension building is not simply a cable taken from the nearest villa socket. Provide the distance between buildings, an extension plan, expected appliances and the proposed connection route, identifying buried or protected surface installation. Existing supply capacity, isolation, earthing, circuit protection and exposure to water or damage need assessment against the approved project design and applicable requirements. Compare excavation, reinstatement, containment, cables, sub-board and testing as separate items rather than relying on a per-metre price. Keep photographs of buried routes before backfilling and obtain route drawings and commissioning records.

### 41. Electrical network rehabilitation

Electrical network rehabilitation begins with evidence of the fault: repeated outages, overheating, deteriorated insulation or circuits no longer suited to current use. Collect an incident log, safe external board photographs and details of previous alterations. Request a competent assessment separating components that require replacement from those that can remain. The work may involve circuit redistribution, rewiring or revised protection, with an agreed isolation plan, occupant safety measures and making good. Compare the before-and-after circuit schedules and test reports at handover, and verify that the underlying fault has been addressed rather than merely concealed.

### 42. Electrical load assessment

An electrical load assessment is useful before installing major equipment, after repeated breaker trips or when planning increased occupancy or use. Provide an equipment list with ratings and operating patterns, plus safely accessible board information; connected nameplate totals are not necessarily simultaneous demand. A competent specialist should examine circuit and supply loading, possible voltage drop and protection coordination, deciding whether operating measurements or design changes are needed. Compare quotations by the measurement scope, written findings and recommendations, not just a site-visit fee. Obtain a report stating assumptions, results and required actions before expansion.

### 43. Interior lighting installation

Identify room use, ceiling levels, task areas, colour temperature and control points. Compare distribution, fitting types, maintenance access and power draw; inspect the actual lighting for glare and uneven coverage.

### 44. Exterior lighting installation

Map façades, paths, car entrances, cable routes and locations exposed to rain or dust. Compare outdoor-rated fittings, mountings and beam direction; test after dark and inspect sealed enclosures and safe wiring.

### 45. Chandelier installation

Supply the chandelier weight, ceiling height and substrate, supply point and manufacturer mounting details, especially beneath gypsum. Use suitable structural anchorage rather than relying on plasterboard; inspect balance, security and electrical connections.

### 46. Concealed lighting installation

Measure LED runs, feed points, driver locations and ventilation within coves. Compare driver quantities, maintenance access and colour samples; inspect for uninterrupted light with no flicker or dark patches.

### 47. Garden lighting installation

Plot trees, paths, sprinklers and irrigation trenches before burying lighting cables; identify operating voltage and protection. Request underground route drawings and wet-condition safety checks; inspect beam aim to avoid nuisance glare.

### 48. Motion sensor installation

Define detection zones, occupancy patterns, delay requirements and likely pet movement. Compare detector coverage, ingress protection and settings; test switching delay, cut-off and false activations.

### 49. Lighting unit maintenance

Record failed lights and establish whether the lamp, driver, switch or supply is at fault before replacing assemblies. Compare replacement components, access costs and availability; verify firm fittings, correct switching and stable output.


## Plumbing, drainage and leak detection

### 50. Water supply network installation

Count kitchens, bathrooms, tanks and appliances; map supply routes, target pressure and hot/cold lines. Compare pipe material, diameters, fittings and pressure testing; inspect a flushed, leak-free system before concealment.

### 51. Drainage network installation

Provide drainage plans, levels, inspection chambers, fixtures and vent positions; pipe length alone omits critical falls. Compare clean-outs, expansion details and tests; witness flow without back-up or foul odour.

### 52. Sanitary fixture installation

Identify basin, WC and mixer models, dimensions, fixing points and heights before wall tiling closes. Compare connection kits, seals and drilling; test flow, drainage, watertight joints and secure mounting.

### 53. Water tank installation

Specify tank capacity, material, location, base loads, wash-out access, vent, overflow and drain. Compare sun protection, access and fixing arrangements; test filling, float controls, overflow and leakage.

### 54. Water pump installation

Report storey count, outlet pressures, peak demand and supply tank details. Request a pump curve, dry-run protection and vibration/noise controls; commission stable delivery without leaks.

### 55. Pipework rehabilitation

Photograph corrosion, leaks and low pressure, identifying retained sections and access routes. Compare selective replacement, waste removal and reinstatement; pressure-test the connected system rather than only new pipe sections.

### 56. Drainage maintenance

Map repeat blockages, drain smells, cleaning history and chamber locations. Compare mechanical clearing and camera inspection where needed instead of indiscriminate chemicals; verify flow and document the obstruction.

### 57. Water leak investigation

Provide meter trends, damp patches and photographs; check the meter with outlets closed when feasible. Compare acoustic, thermal and pressure methods suited to the site; request findings and uncertainty before destructive access.

### 58. Pipe leak repairs

Identify pipe material, leak point, access and whether water service must be interrupted. Compare localized repair, connector choice, partial renewal and finish reinstatement; witness a pressure test before covering.

### 59. Tank leak investigation

Record water-level loss, overflow, pipework, float valves and tank shell before blaming the lining. Compare connection checks, waterproofing inspection and joint remediation; document the loss mechanism and refill test.

### 60. Bathroom waterproofing

Measure bathroom floor, wall upstands, pipe penetrations and doorway threshold. Specify a tile-compatible waterproofing system and an agreed flood test before tiling; check substrate condition first.

### 61. Roof waterproofing

Record roof area, falls, drains, joints, equipment plinths and ponding. Compare preparation, membrane build-up and UV protection; inspect drainage and the agreed integrity test before acceptance.

### 62. Water tank waterproofing

Identify potable or non-potable use, tank substrate, cracks and entry access. Specify a lining suitable for the water use, cleaning and rinsing procedure and separate joint checks; accept a watertightness test and material record.

### 63. Leak-related dampness treatment

Record mould, peeling, dates and the suspected moisture source, then repair the leak before drying surfaces. Compare drying period, moisture checks, salt treatment and repainting; require recorded observations, not merely concealed stains.


## Air-conditioning and refrigeration

### 64. Split AC installation

Match unit capacity to the room, solar exposure and indoor/outdoor positions, noting pipe route and condensate outlet. Compare mounting, insulated lines, evacuation and commissioning; verify cooling and condensate discharge without leaks.

### 65. Central AC installation

Central cooling needs room-by-room load estimates, duct routes, access for maintenance and condensate disposal. Compare equipment, controls, filters, noise and electrical demand; request airflow balancing and temperature readings by zone.

### 66. Concealed AC installation

Verify ceiling void heights, concealed unit envelope, supply and return positions and maintenance openings. Compare insulation, suspension and drain slope; check filter access and absence of condensation on plaster.

### 67. AC refrigerant piping

Mark copper-line lengths, elevation changes, penetrations and the refrigerant specified for the unit. Compare line sizing, insulation, supports, leak testing and evacuation; inspect before any concealed run is closed.

### 68. Air duct installation

Coordinate duct sections, structural beams, lights, diffusers and returns before fabrication. Compare sheet thickness, insulation, hangers and joint sealing; request balancing and noise observations appropriate to the job.

### 69. AC equipment replacement

Record the old model, fault symptoms, capacity, available space and which services can safely be reused. Compare repair versus replacement, removal and connection adaptations; obtain equipment warranty and commissioning report.

### 70. System start-up and inspection

Inspect start-up sequence, sensors, electrical draw, airflow, drainage and filters after installation or repair. Request readings under actual operation and a fault list; fan noise alone does not demonstrate performance.

### 71. AC unit cleaning

Identify whether filters, coil, blower or drain pan need cleaning and photograph dust or mould deposits. Compare cleaning products and protection for electronics and furnishings; inspect drainage and cleanliness before restart.

### 72. Refrigerant charge

Poor cooling does not automatically mean low refrigerant: check airflow, filters, pressure and leaks first. Compare diagnosis, leak repair and charging by manufacturer specification; require operating readings, not arbitrary topping-up.

### 73. AC fault repair

Faults may involve a capacitor, controller, sensor or compressor; record the error code and when it occurs. Compare component repair and replacement with warranty terms; repeat operating cycles to check the defect is resolved.

### 74. Air duct cleaning

List outlets, inspection access, duct type and filter condition; photograph deposits before cleaning. Compare containment, extraction and access to hidden sections; request before-and-after evidence without unsupported sterilisation claims.

### 75. Central AC maintenance

Inventory central units, pumps, filters, controllers, running hours and failures. Compare preventive tasks, visit frequency and excluded parts; require trends and an unresolved-fault register.

### 76. Replacement of spare parts

Obtain model and part numbers, connector photographs and fault evidence before ordering. Compare OEM or compatible approved alternatives and lead time; test the repaired function after fitting.

### 77. Periodic AC maintenance contracts

List unit numbers, locations, use patterns and required seasonal service levels. Compare visit schedules, emergency scope, cleaning, consumables and reporting; review fault trends instead of visit counts alone.


## Smart systems and site monitoring

### 78. Smart lighting control

Identify lighting circuits and whether wall switches must remain usable without internet access. Compare dimmer/load compatibility, connectivity and manual override; test each circuit from both switch and app.

### 79. Smart air-conditioning control

List AC models, controllers and sensors and define temperature schedules for each room. Compare interface compatibility, local control and network failure behaviour; verify on/off and mode changes.

### 80. Smart lock installation

Measure door thickness, swing, cylinder type and emergency access before selecting a smart lock. Compare battery life, user permissions and administration; test inside/outside release and low-battery access.

### 81. Smart curtain installation

Specify opening width, curtain weight, power location, track path and manual-use requirement. Compare motor rating, noise and controls; inspect obstacle response and maintenance access.

### 82. Phone app device integration

Inventory device makes, firmware, home network and accounts to be linked. Compare genuine compatibility and access permissions; hand over owner-controlled accounts with recovery instructions.

### 83. Automated smart scenes

Define concrete scenes such as leaving home or bedtime and which devices each may control. Compare safeguards, offline behaviour and manual overrides; test scene transitions without overriding essential equipment.

### 84. Smart-home maintenance

Log intermittent failures, connectivity, configuration backups, sensor batteries and hub health. Compare targeted diagnosis with indiscriminate resets; preserve client settings and retest scenes after repair.

### 85. CCTV installation

Map camera positions, mounting heights, fields of view, lighting, privacy and cable routes. Compare resolution, storage and safe mounting; inspect day/night footage without monitoring unrelated private spaces.

### 86. Recording equipment installation

Identify camera count, recording rate, retention needs, disk capacity and ventilation. Compare storage, backup power and access controls; demonstrate secure search and export of recorded footage.

### 87. Alarm system installation

Map doors, windows and monitored spaces and plan for nuisance alarms. Compare batteries, communications and notification channels; test each sensor without promising third-party response that is not contracted.

### 88. Access control installation

Specify controlled doors, user groups, permitted hours, audit needs and emergency release. Compare card or PIN readers, lock interfaces and privacy safeguards; test authorised and rejected access.

### 89. Intercom installation

Photograph outdoor station, indoor handset locations, gate and cable/network routes. Compare audio/video quality, door release and integration; test every endpoint and document reset procedures.

### 90. CCTV maintenance

Record blurred images, outages, missing footage and fault timings; check power, cabling and storage. Compare camera replacement against connection repairs; verify image quality, continuous recording and reboot recovery.

### 91. Remote phone viewing setup

Identify remote viewers, recorder model, connectivity and two-factor authentication. Prefer secure integration over indiscriminate port forwarding; hand over owner login, access alerts and revoke installer access.


## Painting and surface finishes

### 92. Villa painting

Measure wall and ceiling areas, rooms, furniture and existing paint condition before deciding coat counts. Compare filling, primer, paint, masking and protection as separate items; inspect dry colour consistency and clean edges.

### 93. Apartment painting

Document cracks, stains and areas not requiring repainting, especially in occupied apartments. Compare furniture protection, ventilation and suitable low-odour materials; inspect stain treatment and floor protection.

### 94. Exterior building painting

Survey façade peeling, chalking, cracks and water/sun exposure, including access height. Compare washing, exterior primer, crack treatment and access equipment; inspect adhesion and absence of drips.

### 95. Door and wood painting

Count doors and timber items, identify existing coating and whether hardware removal is needed. Compare sanding, compatible primers and topcoats; check smooth finish and doors closing without sticking.

### 96. Decorative painting

Approve a real texture sample and technique such as trowelled or textured paint before pricing the full area. Compare sample costs, material use and future repairability; inspect texture and hue under installed lighting.

### 97. Old paint removal and preparation

Determine whether old paint is peeling, sound or moisture-damaged rather than stripping indiscriminately. Compare selective removal, dust protection and substrate repair; verify a dry, stable surface ready for primer.

### 98. Repainting and maintenance

Photograph scratches and stains, and identify existing colour code and paint system. Compare local touch-up with full-wall repainting to avoid sheen mismatch; inspect colour blending and invisible repair boundaries.


## Waterproofing and thermal insulation

### 99. Roof waterproofing

Map concrete surfaces, drains, ponding and movement joints around plant plinths before selecting a membrane. Compare removal of failed layers, preparation and protection; verify drainage and watertight junction details.

### 100. Bathroom waterproofing

Map shower area, drains, threshold and wall upstands before waterproofing. Compare tile-compatible systems and pipe-penetration details; arrange an agreed flood test before finishes are laid.

### 101. Tank waterproofing

Identify water use, tank substrate, cracks, joints, level and safe entry requirements. Compare product suitability for the stored water and cleaning/drying procedure; receive a holding test and material record.

### 102. Building thermal insulation

Record roof/wall build-up, solar exposure, available space, thermal bridges and condensation risks. Compare thickness, thermal performance, moisture response and continuity; inspect junctions without blocking necessary ventilation.

### 103. External wall insulation

Survey façade substrate, cracks, openings, balcony fixings and safe access. Compare external insulation or appropriate coating solutions against design requirements and moisture behaviour; inspect window and edge junctions.

### 104. Rainwater ingress treatment

Relate water marks to rainfall, wind direction, gutters, joints and window openings. Compare targeted water testing and entry-point sealing rather than indiscriminate repainting; verify a suitable repeat test.

### 105. Waterproofing maintenance

Review membrane age, stains, blisters, cuts around drains and movement points. Compare patch repairs with renewal of a failed section based on condition; document repair details, tests and monitoring.



## Maintenance and finishing request details

Identify the property and affected room or system, when the fault began, overview and close-up photographs, measured affected area, and any active leakage, power outage or falling-object hazard. State whether you need diagnosis only or full repair, and whether reinstatement, parts and waste removal belong in the quote. Essential system tests still matter on small jobs. Where no company is listed, use Biet Al Reef customer support's quotation form without assuming appointment or price.

**Common question:** Why can maintenance bids vary for the same symptom? One may include diagnosis, source repair, tests and reinstatement; another may only replace the visible component. Ask for an itemised scope.
