# Mohammed Bin Zayed City — General maintenance and finishing

> **Area scope:** This guide addresses service enquiries in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, Abu Dhabi. Quote requests should specify the neighbourhood or zone, building or villa reference and site access. Provider availability, travel charges and permits must be confirmed for the actual location. Shared technical guidance is not evidence of previous local work.


## General maintenance

### 1. Periodic villa maintenance

Before agreeing a villa maintenance plan, inventory AC units, pumps, heaters, doors and previous leak locations. Require a visit schedule with specific checks for each asset, separating spare parts and emergency callouts from the base fee. Accept photographs, test readings and corrective-action dates rather than a generic 'serviced' note.

### 2. Periodic building maintenance

For a multi-unit building, maintain an asset register for shared pumps, lighting, corridors and roofs and a clear tenant reporting route. Ask how service interruptions are prioritised and who arranges access to private units. Compare contracts by response times, inspections, reporting and exclusions. Review the closed-work-order log at each reporting period.

### 3. Interior damage repairs

Photograph wall, ceiling or skirting damage with a scale and establish whether impact, moisture or movement caused it. Require the underlying cause to be addressed before specifying removal, surface preparation and reinstatement. Compare the repair area, colour and texture matching and furniture protection. Inspect after curing and record whether the original symptom has returned.

### 4. Door and window repairs

Identify whether a door or window is binding, has worn hinges, or leaks air or water around its frame. Record opening dimensions, frame material and photographs of locks, tracks and seals. Ask for adjustment and replacement parts as separate quote items. Test smooth operation, locking, weather seals and drainage at handover.

### 5. Dampness treatment

Do not repaint a damp patch before investigating its source. Record where and when it appears and whether rain, plumbing or condensation may be involved. Require appropriate diagnosis and a drying plan where finishes must be removed. Compare investigation, leak or waterproofing repair and reinstatement as distinct tasks. Keep test results and concealed-work photographs.

### 6. Crack treatment

Distinguish hairline plaster cracks from cracking that may involve load-bearing elements. Photograph each crack beside a ruler and date the record; monitor movement if advised. Require diagnosis before filling, and qualified structural assessment where warning signs exist. Compare preparation and repair materials, then inspect after curing and retain the measurement record.

### 7. Pre-handover property preparation

Prepare a room-by-room pre-handover checklist covering doors, switches, drainage, sealants, paint and installed equipment. Number and photograph defects and specify functional testing and rectification, not merely cleaning. Compare quotations by return visits, retesting and responsibility for materials. Accept a signed list of closed and outstanding items.

### 8. Minor home maintenance

Group minor household jobs in one photographed list with locations and measurements, such as a loose handle, fitting or worn seal. Identify any electrical or plumbing work requiring a qualified trade rather than bundling hazardous tasks into general handyman work. Compare visit charges, materials and time, and test each repair before the technician leaves.

### 9. Curtain installation

Specify curtain type, weight and track length, and whether fixing is into concrete or plasterboard. Measure window width and finished installation height. Check for concealed services before drilling and require suitable brackets and fixing spacing. Test the full travel and check for sagging or rubbing at handover.

### 10. Shelf installation

State the intended shelf load, wall construction and potential concealed services. Measure the mounting height and clearance from doors and circulation routes, and require anchors suited to concrete, block or plasterboard as applicable. Check level and stability under the agreed safe load, not just appearance.

### 11. TV mounting

Provide screen size, weight, mounting pattern and a photograph of the wall and power/data points. Set the viewing height, required tilt or swivel and verify wall and bracket suitability. Compare quotes for fixings and cable management. At handover, test movement, security and access to ports.

### 12. Accessory installation

Bathroom accessories, mirrors and hooks have different loads and fixing requirements. Photograph the tiled or painted surface and measure positions relative to doors and sanitary fittings. Consider hidden plumbing and waterproofing before drilling. Agree appropriate anchors or adhesive and inspect alignment, strength and surface damage.

### 13. Furniture assembly

Check all cartons, components, hinges and room clearances before assembly. Follow the manufacturer's instructions without improvising holes or cutting panels, and anchor tall units against tipping where appropriate. Test doors, drawers, level and stability, record missing parts and retain care instructions.

### 14. Minor fault repairs

Describe the actual fault—dead socket, dripping tap or sticking lock—with photographs and when it occurs. Require diagnosis before replacing components, and route hazardous electrical or serious leak work to qualified specialists. Compare diagnostic and repair charges separately. Test operation afterwards and document unresolved symptoms.

## Requesting a quotation

Provide the address, property type, clear photographs, task list, approximate dimensions and suitable access times. Compare inspection, repair, materials and final testing scope. If no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer service can receive the enquiry without guaranteeing a provider or price. Individual tradespeople remain separate from companies and establishments.

## Integrated finishing

### 15. Villa finishing

Finishing a new villa is not the same scope as refurbishing an occupied one. For a new build, establish which structural and service works are already complete; for an occupied home, identify finishes to remove, rooms still in use and protected access routes. Provide floor plans, room dimensions and a clear statement of whether AC, electrical, plumbing, waterproofing, doors and kitchens are included or whether the contractor is pricing surface finishes only. Request approved physical samples and a bill of quantities using square metres, linear metres and item counts, with materials, labour and options distinguishable. Document relevant service and waterproofing tests before ceilings and wet areas are closed. Handover should check floor falls, joints, switches, drainage and door operation, with room-by-room defects and a reinspection date.

### 16. Apartment finishing

A flat's gross area is not enough to price a fit-out. Supply a room plan, lift and corridor dimensions, and photographs of existing bathrooms and floors; identify any breaking-out and debris removal. Confirm building-management access, protection and work-hour requirements rather than assuming permission. Ask for separate quantities for strip-out, waste transport, substrate levelling, waterproofing, tiling, painting, joinery and appliance reconnection. Compare material grades, quantities, exclusions and disruption periods rather than an undefined 'complete finishing' total. At handover, check wet-area drainage and leaks, doors, service connections and the condition of lifts and common corridors.

### 17. Retail-shop finishing

Define the actual business use before designing a shop fit-out: a clothing store and a premises with additional power, cooling or drainage needs will have different specifications. Provide a plan marking customer space, storage, counters, frontage, existing escape routes and power/data positions. Request dimensioned shop drawings for the frontage, displays, lighting and signage, and identify who will check landlord and applicable authority requirements without promising approval. Price fixed furniture and shopfront fabrication separately from services and surface finishes, with samples and delivery milestones. On handover, test sockets, lights and doors and review circulation widths, cleanability and maintenance access.

### 18. Office finishing

An office layout depends on workstations, meeting rooms, acoustic privacy and network demand, not floor area alone. Supply a furniture plan, printer and any server locations, a power/data outlet schedule, ceiling heights and existing air-distribution routes. Specify partition construction, glazing, doors, acoustic treatment and maintainable flooring, separating loose furniture from fixed works. Compare quotations by outlet count, partition specification and staged work that limits disruption to staff. Acceptance should include power and network checks, lighting and door operation, partition junctions and a usable record of operating and maintenance information.

### 19. Interior-space refurbishment

Refurbishing a living room may mean a cosmetic refresh or the relocation of electrical and AC points and removal of existing cladding; these are different scopes. Photograph all sides, measure walls and openings, and identify furniture and finishes to retain and protect. Request a strip-out, protection and debris plan, followed by substrate repairs before new finishes; verify site dimensions before relying on a 3D concept. Compare allowances for hidden repairs and agreed alternatives if selected products become unavailable. At handover, inspect flooring-to-skirting junctions, colours in daylight, switch positions and unobstructed door swings.

### 20. Decorative fit-out work

For decorative battens, wall panels or concealed lighting, establish whether the provider must design the details or only fabricate and install approved drawings. Send reference images and dimensioned elevations showing sockets, corners and openings; approve a physical sample for colour, texture and thickness. The quotation should identify substrate preparation, adhesive or mechanical fixing, edge details and access to lighting drivers, plus suitability for heat or moisture where relevant. Compare joint locations and detail sections, not just the total cladding area. Inspect secure fixing, aligned edges, hidden wiring and even lighting at handover.

### 21. Integrated finishing handover

This service inspects, records and closes defects in completed works; it is not a contract to carry out a new fit-out. Before inspection, obtain the agreed drawings, material schedule, warranties and previous test records. Prepare a numbered room-by-room checklist for floors, paint, doors, sanitary fixtures, electrical items and cooling systems. Photograph defects with precise locations and severity, assign responsibility and re-test dates rather than signing a generic acceptance. Quotations should distinguish the initial inspection from follow-up visits and specialist testing requiring qualified parties. Close the handover record only when accepted items and outstanding exclusions are documented in writing.

## Gypsum and decorative work

### 22. Gypsum ceiling installation

Before selecting a gypsum ceiling, request a reflected-ceiling plan showing finished heights and clearance for AC ducts, sprinklers, cables and access hatches. Measure each room and perimeter, and identify chandeliers or heavy fittings that need independent structural support rather than attachment to plasterboard. Compare board grades and moisture suitability, framing and suspension details, joint treatment, paint and service openings as separate quote items. Inspect concealed services before closure, then check flatness, corners and visible fasteners or cracks at handover.

### 23. Gypsum partition walls

A gypsum partition needs a setting-out drawing with length, height, door opening, sockets and AC interfaces. Specify acoustic privacy for bedrooms or offices and concealed backing wherever a television or cabinet will be mounted. Compare bids by board layers, stud dimensions, insulation and door-frame reinforcement; any fire-resistance requirement must come from the approved design, not an assumed board label. Inspect alignment, door stability and service locations before the second face is closed, then review finished joints under normal lighting.

### 24. Concealed-light coves

A concealed lighting cove needs a dimensioned section showing length, depth, LED setback and direction of light. Specify colour temperature, dimming method, driver location and maintenance access; a driver permanently sealed behind gypsum is difficult to replace. Approve a short illuminated sample before committing to the full room. Compare prices for gypsum, wiring, strip, driver and paint together, and test even illumination, straight edges, flicker and component access at handover.

### 25. Decorative gypsum features

For gypsum cornices, arches and projections, supply a reference photograph together with dimensions and junctions at doors, curtains and AC outlets. Repeated profiles benefit from one approved sample showing curve radius, projection and finish, especially where room ceiling levels differ. Compare quotations by linear metres, special pieces, fixing, filling and painting rather than an undefined 'full décor' allowance. Inspect symmetry, shadow lines and vulnerable edges before accepting the work.

### 26. Wood-effect panel installation

First identify whether wood-effect panels will sit behind a television, in a busy corridor or near moisture; the product name alone does not establish water or heat resistance. Measure wall width and height, switches and openings, and approve a physical colour and texture sample with the slat direction. Ask for material composition, thickness, substrate requirements, adhesive or mechanical fixing and edge trims, separating wall repairs from fitting. Check slat spacing, secure ends, outlet access and door clearance, and obtain the specified product's cleaning instructions.

### 27. Marble-effect panel installation

For marble-effect panels in an entrance or media wall, approve actual sheet dimensions and a joint layout so a prominent feature is not interrupted by an awkward seam. Photograph the substrate and identify existing paint, tiles or dampness; confirm adhesive and panel suitability for the actual heat and water exposure, since some decorative sheets are unsuitable near flames. Compare thickness, substrate preparation, outlet cut-outs, trims and edge sealing. Check for bubbles, waviness, consistent veining and sound joints before handover.

### 28. Gypsum repair and maintenance

When a gypsum joint cracks or a ceiling swells near an AC unit, photograph the defect and record whether it returns after repairs or cooling operation. Investigate moisture, framing movement and suspension condition before applying filler. Specify the opening-up area and whether boards or framing must be replaced or joint tape can be repaired, including furniture protection and waste removal. Compare investigation, materials and repainting separately; inspect after drying and confirm the moisture source has been addressed.

## Flooring and wall cladding

### 29. Ceramic tile installation

Before ordering ceramic tiling, distinguish a dry floor from a bathroom or kitchen and supply each surface's dimensions, wall-tile height, drains and door positions. A square-metre figure alone is insufficient: agree a layout showing tile direction, cuts, skirting and movement joints, and price old-tile removal and substrate levelling separately. In bathrooms, review the waterproofing test before it is covered and demonstrate falls towards the drain on site. Compare quotations by tile and adhesive specification, grout, preparation, wastage and protection. At handover, check for potentially hollow-sounding tiles, consistent joints, lippage, door clearance and water draining without ponding.

### 30. Porcelain tile installation

Porcelain differs from ceramic particularly in large formats: slab weight, handling, cutting and substrate flatness can govern installation. Provide room measurements, tile dimensions and thickness, a photograph of the product label and a layout showing vein direction where applicable. Ask for a dry layout before fixing to avoid narrow strips at doorways, and specify adhesive contact, movement joints and transitions to adjacent floors. Quotes should itemise lifting and levelling equipment, column cuts, consumables and responsibility for breakage during handling. Inspect lippage in raking light, vein alignment and secure bedding before signing off.

### 31. Parquet flooring installation

First establish whether the proposed parquet is solid timber, engineered wood or another flooring marketed as parquet; moisture sensitivity and refinishing options differ. Supply room areas, door clearances, skirting dimensions and substrate photographs, and request substrate moisture testing to the product manufacturer's requirements. Compare glued and floating installation only where the chosen system permits them, including underlay, thresholds and expansion allowances around walls and columns. Quotations should state timber grade, finish, warranty and water-exposure limitations. Walk the joints to check movement and squeaking, and compare board alignment and colour variation against the approved sample.

### 32. Vinyl flooring installation

For vinyl flooring, describe the use: a bedroom, an office with wheeled chairs or a space cleaned frequently. This informs the choice between LVT planks, sheet goods and click systems. Provide a dimensioned plan, fixed furniture and any drains; ask for substrate moisture and flatness checks because imperfections can telegraph through the finish. Quotes should identify product and wear-layer thickness, adhesive or locking method, floor preparation, thresholds and edge details, and seam welding for sheet goods where required. Do not assume all vinyl products suit wet rooms. Inspect bubbles, lifting edges, pattern continuity and stability under the expected traffic.

### 33. Interlocking paving installation

For interlocking paving, identify whether the area is a footpath, driveway or vehicle parking space: loading affects paver thickness and the base design. Supply dimensions, existing levels, stormwater direction and locations of utility covers and trees. Require excavation, removal of unsuitable material, base and bedding layers, compaction and edge restraints to be itemised, rather than accepting a paver-only square-metre rate. Approve a colour and laying-pattern sample and confirm finished levels at gates and kerbs. Test drainage with water and check for unstable blocks or settling edges under intended use.

### 34. Wall cladding installation

Wall cladding can mean natural stone, porcelain, timber or composite panels, each with different weights and fixing needs. Supply wall type, height and width, sockets, lighting and concealed services, plus photographs of corners and door junctions. Ask for a physical sample and fixing details suited to the material and substrate; relevant moisture or fire performance must be assessed against the approved project design rather than assumed. Compare preparation, support framing, cuts, joints, corner trims and removable access panels. Inspect security and alignment and ensure ventilation openings and maintenance points remain accessible.

### 35. Flooring repairs

Do not select replacement tiles before investigating why flooring cracked, lifted, sounds hollow or changed colour. Supply close-up and wide photographs with a ruler, the date the defect appeared and any evidence of leakage, settlement or movement joints. Ask for substrate and moisture assessment and a defined removal boundary before pricing; a local patch may be sufficient, or the cause may require wider reinstatement. Compare matching-material sourcing, waterproofing or levelling repairs, waste removal and skirting replacement, with concealed works photographed before closing. At handover check bond, level, walking stability and joint alignment, retaining before-and-after records.

## Electrical installations and lighting

### 36. Building electrical installation

Building electrical installation should start with a room-by-room plan identifying fixed appliances, air conditioning, lighting and power outlets—not simply a socket count. Supply architectural drawings, room dimensions and expected equipment ratings. Ask the electrical designer or contractor to set out circuits, containment routes and isolation points while coordinating with plumbing and HVAC. The quotation should distinguish concealed works, cable and conduit specifications, protective devices, chasing and making good, and any professional approvals required for the particular project. Photograph routes before walls are closed, then obtain updated circuit drawings and appropriate insulation, continuity and protection test records.

### 37. Cable and wiring runs

For cable and wiring runs, establish the origin, destination, installation route and loads served rather than estimating length from a room photograph. Cable cross-section, insulation type, installation conditions and circuit protection must be selected together; incompatible circuits should not be grouped without assessment. Compare quotations that separately identify cable pulling, conduit or trunking, access openings, reinstatement and end labelling. Before energising, a qualified electrician should document the circuits and carry out suitable continuity, insulation-resistance and termination checks. Request an accurate record of the installed routes for future maintenance.

### 38. Distribution board installation

A new or replacement distribution board requires an inventory of existing circuits, supply characteristics, connected loads and spare capacity. Provide a safe external photograph of the old board and its label; do not expose live components. Quotations should identify enclosure capacity, isolation and protective devices, circuit labelling, cable arrangement, maintenance access and any planned supply outage. Breaker selection must follow the circuit design rather than the cheapest available rating. At handover, obtain a circuit schedule fixed to the board, appropriate inspection and test results, and confirmation that covers are secured with no exposed openings.

### 39. Socket and switch fitting

Before fitting sockets and switches, mark the quantity, heights and positions relative to doors, furniture and appliances. Distinguish replacement of a damaged accessory from adding a new point, which may require additional wiring. Send photographs of existing faceplates and box dimensions without dismantling electrical connections yourself. Ask for the accessory type, rating and suitability for the environment, especially in damp locations, plus whether cutting and making good are included. Inspect alignment, secure fixing and correct operation, and have the relevant protection checks completed by a competent electrician; concealed improvised joints are not acceptable.

### 40. Power supply for extensions

Power to an extension building is not simply a cable taken from the nearest villa socket. Provide the distance between buildings, an extension plan, expected appliances and the proposed connection route, identifying buried or protected surface installation. Existing supply capacity, isolation, earthing, circuit protection and exposure to water or damage need assessment against the approved project design and applicable requirements. Compare excavation, reinstatement, containment, cables, sub-board and testing as separate items rather than relying on a per-metre price. Keep photographs of buried routes before backfilling and obtain route drawings and commissioning records.

### 41. Electrical network rehabilitation

Electrical network rehabilitation begins with evidence of the fault: repeated outages, overheating, deteriorated insulation or circuits no longer suited to current use. Collect an incident log, safe external board photographs and details of previous alterations. Request a competent assessment separating components that require replacement from those that can remain. The work may involve circuit redistribution, rewiring or revised protection, with an agreed isolation plan, occupant safety measures and making good. Compare the before-and-after circuit schedules and test reports at handover, and verify that the underlying fault has been addressed rather than merely concealed.

### 42. Electrical load assessment

An electrical load assessment is useful before installing major equipment, after repeated breaker trips or when planning increased occupancy or use. Provide an equipment list with ratings and operating patterns, plus safely accessible board information; connected nameplate totals are not necessarily simultaneous demand. A competent specialist should examine circuit and supply loading, possible voltage drop and protection coordination, deciding whether operating measurements or design changes are needed. Compare quotations by the measurement scope, written findings and recommendations, not just a site-visit fee. Obtain a report stating assumptions, results and required actions before expansion.

### 43. Interior lighting installation

Start with a room and furniture plan and actual ceiling heights: a dining pendant, kitchen task lights and reading lights serve different purposes. Record dimensions, AC diffusers and detector positions, and photograph existing power and switching points before agreeing on downlights or tracks. Request a layout stating fitting quantities, wattage, colour temperature, beam angles and any dimming, with wiring, making-good and painting priced explicitly. Compare samples in the actual room. At handover, test circuits, dimming, glare and evenness of illumination, and record driver locations for future access.

### 44. Exterior lighting installation

Exterior lighting should be planned around paths, vehicle entrances, steps and shaded areas, not just façade appearance. Mark mounting heights and supply points on site photographs, and identify exposure to water, dust and heat. Specify suitable luminaires, junction boxes, fixings and protected cable routes, including whether photocells or timers are included. Compare beam patterns and fixing details as well as fixture counts. Inspect after dark from normal walking and driving positions, check for glare towards neighbours and drivers, and have a qualified electrician verify enclosure integrity and electrical protection.

### 45. Chandelier installation

Before pricing a chandelier, provide its model, weight, dimensions, ceiling construction and intended hanging height, together with any accessible photographs above a suspended ceiling. A heavy fitting cannot simply be supported by decorative plasterboard; suitable structural anchorage must be established by a competent specialist. Ask the installer to specify the bracket, anchors, lifting method, labour and floor protection, and price any relocation of the electrical point separately. Confirm head clearance and door movement. At handover check security, level, all lamp groups and switching.

### 46. Concealed lighting installation

For cove or concealed lighting, supply a dimensioned section showing recess depth and width, LED run lengths and feed points. Decide whether the objective is a continuous visible line or indirect reflected light. Compare LED strips, aluminium profiles, diffusers and drivers against the actual run length and heat dissipation needs. Require accessible driver positions and compatible dimming, and test the wiring before closing gypsum work. At handover inspect long-duration operation, continuity, colour consistency and unwanted dots, flicker or shadows.

### 47. Garden lighting installation

Provide a garden sketch locating trees, paths, planting beds, irrigation pipes and sprinklers; lighting positions must account for plant growth and water exposure. Share day and night photographs and indicate whether each fitting lights steps, guides a path or highlights planting. Ask for separate prices for fixtures, trenching, cables, controls and reinstatement, plus a record of buried cable routes and moisture protection. Compare beam adjustability as well as power and mounting. Inspect after dark for safe path coverage and unwanted light into neighbouring windows.

### 48. Motion sensor installation

Specify whether the detector serves a corridor, staircase, outdoor entrance or store, and show doors, travel directions and daylight sources on a simple sketch. Request detection angle and range, mounting height, supported electrical load and compatibility with the proposed lamps. Agree on off-delay, sensitivity and daylight threshold. Presence sensing may suit rooms where occupants remain relatively still better than basic motion detection. Compare wiring and commissioning as separate scope items; test approaches from different directions, occupancy without movement and false triggering by vehicles or foliage.

### 49. Lighting unit maintenance

List failed fittings by room and model, photograph flickering, colour changes or heat marks, and explain whether faults coincide with another circuit being switched on. A failed lamp, driver, switch and supply connection need different repairs; electrical diagnosis and isolation belong to a qualified person. Request a quotation separating fault-finding, compatible replacement parts, high-level access and making good, with warranty details. At handover run the fittings long enough to check stable output, secure mounting and correct switching, and retain the replaced-part references.


## Plumbing, drainage and leak detection

### 50. Water supply network installation

For a new villa or apartment water supply, begin with a marked plan of heaters, tanks, kitchens, bathrooms, isolation valves and service access. Photograph pipe routes before closing walls, and size lines for the intended demand rather than guessing. Ask which pipe system and jointing method will be used, how hot-water lines will be insulated, and how penetrations will be protected. Compare quotes that include valves, supports, flushing, pressure testing and reinstatement. Obtain test records, concealed-work photographs and an as-built route drawing before handover.

### 51. Drainage network installation

A new drainage system needs finished-floor levels, fixture positions, inspection chambers and existing connection points. A per-metre quote is inadequate unless it specifies falls, clean-outs and ventilation. Request a route drawing with pipe sizes and slab-penetration details, checking clashes with structural and electrical work. Compare excavation, backfilling, waterproofing and floor reinstatement as explicit items. Inspect joints and witness discharge from the most distant fixture, recording standing water or backflow before accepting the work.

### 52. Sanitary fixture installation

Before fitting a basin, wall-hung WC or shower mixer, send the exact model and dimension sheet plus a photograph of the installation location, especially where pipe outlets are already concealed. A wall-hung WC requires different support from a vanity basin, and a model change can force tile cutting or outlet relocation. Compare quotations covering connection kits, isolation valves, gaskets, sealant and alignment, not labour alone. Operate each fitting repeatedly at handover and inspect beneath it and around fixings for leaks.

### 53. Water tank installation

Select a water tank by actual demand, installation position, water use and access for opening and cleaning, not nominal capacity alone. Photograph and measure the base, and record inlet, outlet and overflow elevations; elevated supports may require specialist load assessment. Compare tank material, product suitability, float assemblies, ventilation and exposure protection. At handover check stability and lid access, then observe a complete fill cycle, overflow function and watertight connections.

### 54. Water pump installation

If upper-floor pressure is weak, measure performance during use and count simultaneous outlets before ordering a larger pump: a clogged strainer or undersized line may be responsible. Provide approximate lift, tank capacity and connection photographs. Ask for selection against the required flow and head, supported by a pump performance curve. Compare dry-run protection, vibration isolation, electrical safeguards and service access. Commission the system at multiple outlets, recording pressure stability, leaks and noise.

### 55. Pipework rehabilitation

Rehabilitating old pipework does not automatically mean replacing every line. Map leaks, corrosion and recurring pressure loss, and check compatibility where new pipework meets retained sections. Request photographs of proposed openings and a drawing identifying what remains, with tile and plaster reinstatement priced separately. Compare temporary water arrangements and protection of occupied spaces. Pressure-test the whole connected network, including old-to-new joints, before concealment and retain route photographs.

### 56. Drainage maintenance

For recurring drain blockages, record which fixtures are affected and whether water backs up through another outlet. Photograph accessible inspection chambers; grease accumulation, trapped objects and inadequate falls need different remedies. Require diagnosis before aggressive chemical treatment, and clarify whether the quote covers mechanical clearing, camera inspection or repair of damaged pipework. Compare attendance, waste removal and retesting. At handover run several fixtures together, check for backflow and document the obstruction found.

### 57. Water leak investigation

Where a concealed leak is suspected, record meter readings during a period with normal consumption isolated and photograph damp patches, their positions and when they appear. High consumption alone cannot locate a broken pipe. Ask the investigator to explain the suitability and limitations of pressure testing, acoustic detection or thermal imaging for the particular network. The report should distinguish a pressurised supply leak from drainage defects or condensation and provide measurements and a likely location before opening finishes. Compare the diagnostic scope, reporting and exclusions for opening, repair and reinstatement. Request traceable photographs and findings at handover.


### 58. Pipe leak repairs

To repair a leaking pipe, identify its material, diameter, joint type, exact location and accessibility. Replacing a damaged length is different from resealing a joint or renewing a corroded run. Photograph the area before opening it and agree on tile or plaster removal, furniture protection and water isolation. The quotation should specify compatible fittings, joining method, pressure testing, waterproofing reinstatement and finishing. Do not close the opening until the repair has been tested under appropriate operating conditions and documented; check for renewed dampness afterwards.


### 59. Tank leak investigation

If a tank loses water or its base becomes wet, record whether it is above or below ground, its construction material, capacity, pipe connections, float valve and overflow. Water may escape through external pipework or a valve rather than the tank body. Require a test that isolates possible sources and records water-level changes over an agreed interval. Compare inspection of the shell, base, joints, access requirements, draining and cleaning. Any repair material must be compatible with the tank and its intended water use. Obtain photographs of the defect and a documented repeat test before acceptance.


### 60. Bathroom waterproofing

Bathroom waterproofing needs a clear map of wet zones: shower floor, wall-to-floor junctions, corners, pipe penetrations and floor drains. Provide dimensions and photographs after removing defective finishes, together with floor levels and drainage positions. The quotation should separate substrate preparation, crack repairs, waterproofing layers, junction detailing, testing and tiling reinstatement. A product name alone is not a specification. Agree on a controlled flood test where site conditions permit and document its duration and outcome before covering the membrane. Check falls and standing water at handover.


### 61. Roof waterproofing

For a leaking roof, photograph ponding areas, joints, outlets, equipment plinths and dampness below the slab. The horizontal area alone is insufficient: measure upstands, pipe penetrations, thresholds and material transitions. Ask the contractor to justify a local repair versus complete system renewal based on the existing layers. The quotation should describe removal of failed material, substrate preparation, drainage falls, weather exposure and protection. Obtain the manufacturer's system details, specified layer thicknesses, suitable adhesion or water tests and photographs before the membrane is covered.


### 62. Water tank waterproofing

Water tank waterproofing requires the tank material, capacity, access and ventilation arrangements, previous coatings and the location of cracks and joints. Establish the cause first: structural cracking or moving joints may need a different solution from a worn protective coating. Define draining, cleaning, substrate preparation, the waterproofing system and verified suitability for contact with drinking water where relevant; do not assume an unspecified product is approved. Compare curing, testing and final cleaning as well as coating work. Obtain material records and test results before returning the tank to service.


### 63. Leak-related dampness treatment

Peeling paint or a damp wall does not automatically call for repainting. Investigate whether the moisture comes from supply pipes, drainage, the roof, condensation or residual moisture after an earlier repair. Record how the patch changes with water use or rainfall and take repeat moisture readings where useful. Compare quotations on stopping the source, removing damaged finishes, drying the substrate, treating salts or surface mould appropriately and restoring the finish. Do not accept the final paintwork until the source is controlled and drying has been demonstrated; identify any areas requiring later monitoring.



## Air-conditioning and refrigeration

### 64. Split AC installation

Size the unit from the room's heat load, glazing, insulation and occupancy rather than floor area alone. Provide photographs of the indoor location, condensate drain route, outdoor mounting point and estimated pipe run. Ask bidders to specify capacity, model, brackets, pipe insulation, electrical connection, wall openings and reinstatement. At handover check drainage, mounting, temperatures, current and noise, and obtain leak-test and evacuation records.

### 65. Central AC installation

Start with cooling-load calculations, zoning, electrical capacity, plant space and maintenance access. Require the scope to identify the actual system components—air handlers, cooling plant or packaged units, ductwork, insulation, controls and balancing as applicable. Do not compare bids with different system boundaries. Commissioning should document airflow, temperatures, protection devices, controls and condensate drainage.

### 66. Concealed AC installation

Check ceiling void depth, maintenance openings, return-air path and condensate fall before approving a concealed unit. Compare static pressure capability, capacity, sound level, duct sizing, insulation and filter accessibility, not nominal tonnage alone. Obtain access-opening drawings, air-balancing readings and a drainage test before the ceiling is closed.

### 67. AC refrigerant piping

Supply a route sketch, estimated run length, height difference between units and wall penetrations. Quotations should specify copper pipe diameters appropriate to the selected unit, insulation, supports, protection and reinstatement. Request suitable pressure testing, evacuation procedures and photographs of concealed joints before covering them.

### 68. Air duct installation

Define required airflow by room, supply and return grille locations, ceiling clearance and structural obstructions. Require duct dimensions, sheet-metal gauge, insulation, hangers, dampers and inspection access, including treatment of leakage and noise. Acceptance requires checking joints and supports and measuring and balancing airflow at outlets, not simply switching on the fan.

### 69. AC equipment replacement

Record the existing model, dimensions, fault history, electrical breaker, pipe route and drainage. Compare new-unit compatibility with existing wiring, pipework and mounting, and itemise any replacements plus removal and responsible disposal of the old equipment. At handover verify cooling, leak tightness, drainage, serial numbers and warranties.

### 70. System start-up and inspection

This is a diagnostic and commissioning scope, not automatically a repair. Provide equipment schedules, drawings, control settings and previous defects. Agree measurements of supply/return temperatures, pressures, airflow, current draw and safety controls appropriate to the system. Receive a report separating observed readings, defects and recommended corrective works, with repairs quoted separately.

### 71. AC unit cleaning

State the number and type of units, their access conditions and any odour, water leakage or poor cooling. Distinguish filter washing from deep cleaning of coils, drain trays and blower wheels. Require furniture and electrical protection and controlled wastewater handling. Check before/after photographs, drainage and vibration-free fan operation at completion.

### 72. Refrigerant charge

Weak cooling alone does not prove refrigerant loss. Ask for checks of filters, fans, operating temperatures and pressures, and a leak investigation before adding refrigerant. The refrigerant type and charge must follow the unit label and manufacturer guidance; any leak needs repair and refrigerant handling by a qualified technician. Obtain operating readings and the reason for charging, not repeated top-ups without diagnosis.

### 73. AC fault repair

Describe the error code, when the failure occurs and whether the symptom is tripping, poor cooling, noise or water leakage; attach the unit nameplate. Separate diagnostic labour from parts and repairs, and test sensors, fans, capacitors and controls before proposing expensive component replacement. Acceptance includes running under suitable load, confirming the fault is resolved and recording replaced parts.

### 74. Air duct cleaning

Identify the duct sections, access points, building use and observed dust or odour. Not every duct needs cleaning: request an inspection showing actual deposits or contamination and a plan for containment, sensitive-component protection and waste removal. Compare access and cleaning methods and require visual post-work evidence; avoid unsupported health guarantees.

### 75. Central AC maintenance

Compile equipment models, operating hours, fault logs and energy or water readings where applicable. Specify preventive checks for filters, belts, bearings, coils, pumps and protection devices appropriate to the system, with major repairs and consumables priced separately. Receive trend readings, worn-part findings, test results and a follow-up plan.

### 76. Replacement of spare parts

Provide the unit model, fault code and diagnostic findings before ordering. Require the original part number or verified compatible alternative, warranty, fitting charge and explanation of the original failure; a new control board can fail again if supply problems remain. Retain the removed part where practical and test the affected function with an itemised invoice.

### 77. Periodic AC maintenance contracts

Inventory units, locations, models, operating hours and service history. Compare visit frequency, written inspection checklist, response times, emergency coverage, spare-part exclusions, consumables and deep-cleaning allowances. Each visit should yield readings, photographs, defects, corrective actions and the next service date.


## Smart systems and site monitoring

### 78. Smart lighting control

Map lighting circuits, switch positions and lamp types first, particularly LED drivers that may not support every dimmer. Ask for a circuit schedule identifying smart modules, retained manual switches and whether operation needs internet access or a local hub. Compare load compatibility, electrical protection, programming and the number of circuits. At handover, test on/off, dimming and scenes at both switches and app, including a network outage.

### 79. Smart air-conditioning control

Record each air-conditioner model and its existing controls: infrared remote, thermostat or central controller. Confirm that the proposed interface supports the actual commands and temperature sensors without bypassing manufacturer operating limits. Compare the number of zones, schedules, manual override and integration costs. Test each unit and its recovery after a power interruption, not merely the app screen.

### 80. Smart lock installation

Photograph the door edge and existing lock; measure leaf thickness, handle-to-cylinder spacing and opening direction before selecting hardware. Specify any drilling or door alterations, backup access, guest credentials, audit logs and battery replacement. Compare mechanical fit as carefully as digital features. Test entry from both sides, auto-lock, credential revocation and low-battery access.

### 81. Smart curtain installation

Measure the opening, track length, fabric type and weight, pelmet clearance and available power supply. Heavy curtains require a suitably rated motor and track. Specify side-draw or lift movement, manual controls and travel limits. Compare noise, load capacity and maintenance access; verify a full smooth run, end stops and operation when connectivity is lost.

### 82. Phone app device integration

List device makes, models, firmware and communication protocols before promising a single app. Some products work only with their manufacturer's app or require a separate hub, so demonstrate compatibility on a representative device. Compare subscriptions, permissions, local control and settings export. Hand over owner-controlled accounts, enable additional authentication and revoke unnecessary installer access.

### 83. Automated smart scenes

Describe each automation as testable triggers and actions: for example, turning off selected lights when leaving while excluding essential cooling or safety equipment. Define delays, exceptions and manual override; avoid casually automating emergency exits or safety-critical systems. Compare quotations by programmed scenes, documentation and test cases. Check conflicting commands, network loss and power restoration.

### 84. Smart-home maintenance

Describe the symptom: an unresponsive device, delayed scene, exhausted sensor battery or recurring hub disconnection. Check power, wireless coverage, event logs and software versions before resetting anything, since a reset may erase working configurations. Compare diagnosis, replacement parts and configuration backup. Receive a report showing which devices, scenes and user permissions were restored and tested.

### 85. CCTV installation

Mark each camera's purpose on a site plan, such as an entrance or gate the owner is entitled to monitor, while avoiding neighbours' property and private areas. Document mounting height, field of view, night lighting, cable routing and power. Compare real footage quality, storage and weather protection. Inspect day and night samples, relevant blind spots and privacy settings.

### 86. Recording equipment installation

Specify channels, camera resolution, frame rate and required retention days; disk capacity depends on more than camera count. Request a storage estimate stating compression assumptions and continuous versus motion recording, plus recorder ventilation and physical security. Compare export tools, access rights and backup power. Retrieve and export an older clip and confirm its timestamp.

### 87. Alarm system installation

Clarify whether the request is for intrusion detection at openings or indoor motion sensors; fire alarm systems have distinct requirements. Count doors, windows, pets, power points and communication paths, and request a false-alarm mitigation plan. Compare sensor types, battery replacement, notification paths and who actually receives alerts. Test each zone, silencing and recovery without assuming an external response service.

### 88. Access control installation

List controlled doors, users, permitted hours and emergency or power-loss scenarios before selecting card, code or biometric readers. Require a diagram of the electric lock, exit release, safe egress and access logs, including privacy safeguards. Compare credential revocation and maintenance responsibilities. Test permitted and denied entry, safe exit and power failure, and hand over administrative control.

### 89. Intercom installation

Specify entrances and receiving apartments or rooms, and whether audio, video and gate release are needed. Photograph the outdoor station, cable or network route and distances to indoor units, checking outdoor suitability. Compare speech clarity, night visibility and replacement availability. Test calls from every station and door release, then document app access permissions.

### 90. CCTV maintenance

Provide examples and timestamps of failed recordings, and note whether one camera or an entire group is affected. Diagnose power, connectors, cabling, lenses, disks and network settings before replacing devices; missing footage may result from storage or incorrect clock settings. Compare per-point work, parts and tests. Accept only clear live images and recordings that can actually be retrieved.

### 91. Remote phone viewing setup

Identify recorder models, authorised viewers and connection type; distinguish live viewing from playback and alerts. Prefer encrypted vendor-supported access, unique passwords and additional authentication; avoid exposing public ports without a security assessment. Compare permissions, account safeguards and cloud fees. Test remote access on an outside network and revoke the installer's account afterwards.


## Painting and surface finishes

### 92. Villa painting

Before repainting an entire villa, measure walls and ceilings net of openings, record occupied rooms and identify peeling paint or moisture marks. Agree substrate repairs, primer, coating system, sheen and dust protection before fixing coat counts; trial coverage may be necessary. Compare quotations on measured areas, named materials, coats and inclusion of doors and ceilings. Approve a physical colour sample and inspect even coverage in daylight after curing.

### 93. Apartment painting

Painting an occupied apartment calls for a room-by-room plan that records furniture, air-conditioning outlets, sockets, cracks and stains. Separate treatment of moisture or smoke damage from simply covering it, and agree drying periods, ventilation and lower-odour products where appropriate. Compare floor protection, moving and replacing furniture and final cleaning as explicit items. Inspect edges around frames and check for flashing or stains under the room's actual lighting.

### 94. Exterior building painting

Exterior painting starts with a survey of failing coatings, cracks, possible salt deposits and sources of water ingress. Measure façade areas and access heights, then specify washing, drying, repairs, compatible exterior primer and finish. Require a method for protecting glazing and ground areas and for safe access. Compare access and substrate repairs separately from coating costs, then inspect adhesion at repaired locations, coverage and absence of runs after drying.

### 95. Door and wood painting

For doors and timber finishes, count the pieces and identify solid timber, veneer or other substrates, existing coatings and damage around hinges. Decide whether the finish is opaque or should reveal the grain, and whether locks and handles will be removed. Filling, sanding, primer and protective coats must suit the material. Compare on-site versus workshop work, coat schedules and odour controls; check that doors operate freely and hardware remains clean.

### 96. Decorative painting

Do not approve a decorative finish solely from a screen photograph. Request a sample panel on the actual substrate under final lighting, identifying the texture, base colour, tool direction and area. Hand-applied effects vary, so retain the accepted sample and agree tolerances. Compare preparation, sample cost, protective coats and future patch repair options. Inspect corners, joins and overall texture before acceptance.

### 97. Old paint removal and preparation

Where old paint peels, first investigate moisture or poor adhesion; stripping without fixing the cause invites failure. Photograph blisters, measure affected areas and identify the substrate. Specify dust containment, furniture protection and careful removal of loose coatings without damaging sound plaster. Compare selective stripping with full removal based on inspection, and accept a clean, dry, stable surface before primer, with repaired areas documented.

### 98. Repainting and maintenance

For paint maintenance, distinguish superficial scuffs, moisture staining and moving cracks, as each needs a different remedy. Supply photographs, any existing colour code, coating age and wall dimensions. Request a trial patch to assess colour and sheen changes caused by ageing. Compare spot repair with repainting to a natural break such as a corner. Inspect the repair after drying and verify the original defect was addressed.

### 99. Roof waterproofing

Map ponding, drains, movement joints, air-conditioning plinths and previous membranes before selecting a roof waterproofing system. Record areas, falls and substrate condition, and require a written build-up covering preparation, primer, membrane or liquid layer, protection and upstands. Compare system thickness, penetration detailing and warranty conditions. Agree a test appropriate to the system and site before covering the membrane, and photograph drain details.

### 100. Bathroom waterproofing

Before waterproofing a bathroom, locate showers, drains, thresholds, pipe penetrations and the required wall upstands; waterproofing will not correct poor floor falls. Specify a system compatible with the substrate, tile adhesive and corner details, with photographs before tiling. Compare whether tile removal, reinstatement and sanitary fixtures are included. Agree a suitable water-retention test and observation period before finishes are installed.

### 101. Tank waterproofing

Tank waterproofing requires the intended water use, tank material and dimensions, crack and joint locations and previous coating condition. A product being water-resistant does not establish suitability for drinking water; request manufacturer evidence appropriate to the intended use and curing instructions. Entry into enclosed tanks demands specialist confined-space safety assessment. Compare cleaning, repair and testing separately, and obtain material records and a leak check before reuse.

### 102. Building thermal insulation

Start thermal insulation enquiries with roof or wall construction, area, available cavity depth and condensation risks. Compare products by thermal resistance at the proposed thickness, fire characteristics, required protection and fixing method rather than by brand alone. Address joints and thermal bridges without blocking ventilation or maintenance access. Accept photographs of concealed layers and checks of thickness and continuity before enclosure.

### 103. External wall insulation

Survey façade cracks, windows, balconies, fixings and moisture before choosing external wall insulation. Clarify whether the objective is reducing heat transfer or preventing rain ingress, since solutions differ. Require a section drawing of layers, window reveals, movement joints and drainage, plus a safe access method. Compare thermal performance, moisture control, impact protection and maintenance. Inspect penetrations and junctions for gaps and exposed bridges.

### 104. Rainwater ingress treatment

To trace rain ingress, record when staining appears, wind direction, ponding, gutters, window edges and movement joints. Do not repaint before locating the entry path; a blocked drain or distant failed joint may be responsible. Ask for a controlled, localised water test that avoids damage, and price source repair separately from internal reinstatement. Accept a repeat test and dated photographs once the area dries.

### 105. Waterproofing maintenance

Review the age and type of existing waterproofing and photograph blisters, cuts and leaks around drains or equipment bases. Test adhesion and compatibility before choosing a local patch or renewal of a wider section; incompatible products may fail at overlaps. Compare removal area, preparation, overlap dimensions, protection and refitting of accessories. Receive before-and-after photographs, an appropriate test and a follow-up plan after rain or normal use.

## Maintenance and finishing request details

Identify the property and affected room or system, when the fault began, overview and close-up photographs, measured affected area, and any active leakage, power outage or falling-object hazard. State whether you need diagnosis only or full repair, and whether reinstatement, parts and waste removal belong in the quote. Essential system tests still matter on small jobs. Where no company is listed, use Biet Al Reef customer support's quotation form without assuming appointment or price.

**Common question:** Why can maintenance bids vary for the same symptom? One may include diagnosis, source repair, tests and reinstatement; another may only replace the visible component. Ask for an itemised scope.
