# Mohammed Bin Zayed City — Engineering consultancy, design and décor

> **Area scope:** This guide addresses service enquiries in Mohammed Bin Zayed City, Abu Dhabi. Quote requests should specify the neighbourhood or zone, building or villa reference and site access. Provider availability, travel charges and permits must be confirmed for the actual location. Shared technical guidance is not evidence of previous local work.


## Architectural design and drawings

### 1. Villa design

Before sketching a new villa, prepare a room brief covering bedrooms, guest reception, service circulation and step-free access where needed. Ask the architect to test two layouts against measured plot boundaries and verified site constraints rather than assumed setbacks. Compare usable room area against corridor space, stair placement and parking access. At handover, check the room schedule, dimensions, privacy and window positions against the agreed brief.

### 2. Residential building design

For a multi-unit residential building, repeating one floor plan is not enough. Specify target unit count, independent access, parking, meters and shared services. Commission an early layout of stairs, lifts, plant rooms and access routes with a schedule of net and common areas. Compare schemes for wasted circulation and clashes between columns and furniture; reconcile the unit schedule with the final plans and sections.

### 3. Commercial building design

Describe the intended commercial use, customer movement, deliveries and whether units may be leased separately. The architect needs site dimensions, frontage constraints and service/loading positions before fixing the layout. Request options showing ground-level access, subdivision flexibility and back-of-house routes. At handover check clear dimensions, storage, toilets and entrances; a proposed layout is not evidence of business-use approval.

### 4. Architectural drawing preparation

State whether the architectural drawing package is for concept decisions, contractor pricing or construction. Supply plot documents and available surveys, and agree a drawing register covering plans, elevations, sections and relevant schedules with scales and revision numbers. Compare offers for design stages and coordination with structural and MEP disciplines. Reject inconsistent room dimensions between plans and sections.

### 5. Façade redesign

An existing façade should be surveyed for levels, openings, air-conditioning outlets and drainage before selecting cladding from reference pictures. Request alternatives showing material thickness, weight, fixing, rainwater management and window junctions. Separate design fees from supply and installation in quotations. At design handover, review physical samples, edge details and safe access for cleaning and maintenance.

### 6. 3D architectural modelling

A massing model, coordinated 3D model and marketing render serve different purposes. Define views, detail level, whether dimensions can be extracted and the drawing issue used as input. Compare offers for editable source files and revision rounds. Check openings and ceiling heights against the plans; a stone colour shown in a render is not an approved procurement specification.

### 7. Amending existing plans

Mark proposed wall and room changes on a dated existing plan and photograph visible columns, openings and service routes. Ask the designer to flag structural assessment and MEP coordination needs, including impacts on door clearances and circulation. Require a clearly clouded revision set and change schedule. A concept sketch should not be mistaken for construction documentation.

### 8. Permit drawing preparation

Define the project type and competent authority for the actual site before commissioning a permit submission package. Agree architectural sheets, cross-discipline coordination, signatures and owner-supplied documents. Compare quotations for response rounds to authority comments and excluded fees. Receive a version-controlled submission checklist; preparing drawings does not mean a permit has been issued.

### 9. Building application submission

For building application follow-up, establish who holds submission authority, answers review comments and pays external fees. Supply validated property, owner and drawing details, and ask for milestones separating filing, acceptance for review and final decision. At handover obtain the application reference, status notices and correspondence. A submission receipt is not permission to begin construction.

### 10. Revision of approved drawings

When approved plans must change, require a numbered comparison between the approved issue and proposed revision, identifying affected areas, structural elements and building systems. Assess impacts on completed work and whether the authority needs to review the change. Compare design offers for coordination and comment responses. Receive a consistent drawing set bearing its actual approval status.

### 11. Maintenance permit coordination

A repaint and a change to walls or building services may follow different procedures. Describe precisely whether the maintenance is cosmetic, structural, services-related or a change of use. Ask a qualified adviser to establish the applicable process and necessary documents for this particular job. Compare follow-up responsibilities and excluded fees, and retain the actual decision before undertaking work that requires permission.

### 12. Extension permit coordination

An extension enquiry needs measured existing and proposed areas, connection points, plot boundaries and service tie-ins. Ask whether the existing structure needs assessment and who will prepare architectural, structural and services drawings. Compare proposals for survey, submissions and revisions. Neither an application number nor an initial review should be treated as authority to build.

### 13. As-built survey drawings

As-built drawings must reflect a site survey, not merely trace old plans. Request measured rooms, levels, openings and visible equipment positions, supported by location-referenced photographs; identify concealed areas that could not be checked. Verify critical dimensions on site at handover, and distinguish measured from assumed information before using drawings for cabinetry fabrication or alterations.

### 14. Authority approval coordination

Where several approvals may be involved, request a matrix showing the responsible authority, document owner, filing date and outstanding comments for each process. Avoid vague 'all approvals included' pricing: compare follow-up duties, drawing resubmissions and external fees. Obtain actual decisions, reference numbers and final status records; silence from an authority is not an approval.

## Preparing a quotation request

Provide the site, property type, approximate area, available plans and photographs of existing conditions. State required deliverables and the intended timeline. Compare drawing scope, revision rounds, coordination responsibilities and excluded fees. If no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer service can receive the enquiry without promising provider availability or a price. Individual tradespeople remain a separate directory from companies and establishments.

## Structural design

### 15. Concrete structural system design

For a new villa frame or extension, architectural floor plans alone do not establish column sizes. The structural engineer needs room uses, storey count, large openings, stair geometry, roof-tank loads and suitable ground information where applicable. Ask for an early column grid that can be checked against parking and reception-room layouts before committing to a scheme. Compare fees for analysis, reinforcement detailing and coordination with HVAC and plumbing separately. At handover request revision-controlled calculations, drawings and a record of resolved clashes; visual-model member sizes are not construction instructions.

### 16. Foundation and footing design

Foundation selection cannot be inferred from plot area: building loads and ground conditions can lead to substantially different solutions. Provide surveyed levels, excavation boundaries, adjacent structures, known buried services and an appropriate geotechnical investigation. Ask the engineer to document the assumptions behind isolated footings or a raft and the treatment of differential settlement and founding levels. Check whether soil-report review and design revisions are included in the fee. On receipt, reconcile grids, sections, anchorage lengths and column-to-foundation details rather than checking only the plan view.

### 17. Structural drawing preparation

When a contractor requests structural construction drawings, distinguish them from a concept grid showing column locations. The deliverable should cover foundations, columns, beams, slabs and stairs, with sections detailing reinforcement at openings, edges and joints. Provide the current architectural issue and coordinated air-duct and drainage penetrations; moving an opening after casting may require engineering intervention. Compare design offices by drawing detail, issue register and site-query support. Check grid references, levels and reinforcement marks across all drawings and schedules before acceptance.

### 18. Structural load review

A roof-mounted water tank or heavy air-conditioning plant requires more than a nameplate weight check. Document the fully operating weight, support points, vibration and position relative to beams; supply old drawings and accessible roof photographs. The engineer should trace load transfer through slab, beams, columns and foundations, identifying what cannot be confirmed without exposure or testing. Compare desktop analysis with site inspection and optional testing as distinct scope items. Require a written conclusion stating the conditions under which the installation is acceptable or what further investigation or strengthening is needed before loading.

### 19. Building structural safety assessment

For a widening crack, local settlement or spalling concrete, record when the symptom appeared and photograph a scale beside it. Not every crack signals structural failure, but it should not be dismissed as decorative without examination. Commission an inspection plan that distinguishes load-bearing members and foundations from moisture and finish defects, and states when monitoring or testing is warranted. Compare the number of visits and whether measurements and opening-up are included. The report should map defects, discuss plausible causes, state limitations and prioritise actions. Suspected immediate danger warrants restricting use of the affected area and urgent qualified site assessment.

### 20. Structural strengthening design

Strengthening a beam or column starts with identifying the failure mechanism, not choosing a fashionable repair product. Provide inspection results, measured member dimensions, known reinforcement, present and proposed loads and access constraints. Ask the designer to compare options and their impact on room clearances and service routes. The proposal should cover temporary propping, load-transfer sequence, hold points before concealment and responsibility for site review. Accept fixing and connection details, material specifications and appropriate testing or monitoring requirements. No structural member should be opened or support removed without the responsible engineer's approval.

### 21. Structural engineering reports

A structural report might record cracks before refurbishment, assess a proposed new load or document a building before neighbouring work; each needs a different scope. Define the question to be answered and provide dated photographs, previous drawings and repair history. Require the proposal to specify inspected members, testing methods and deliverables. Compare site visits, equipment, samples and turnaround rather than report length. The final dated document should identify the location, traceable findings, method and limitations, clearly separating observed facts from assumptions and recommendations and naming the responsible professional.

### 22. Construction supervision

Agree the supervision visit frequency and inspection hold points before work starts, especially reinforcement, plumbing runs and waterproofing that will later be concealed. Provide the latest contract drawings, programme and site contacts. Require dated visit records with photographs, actionable defects and evidence of closure. Compare supervision offers by site presence, authority to raise instructions and escalation procedure, not just visit count. At handover, reconcile outstanding observations and distinguish the supervisor's checking duties from the contractor's responsibility for execution.


### 23. Project management

Managing a villa refurbishment or new build starts with naming who approves changes, procurement and payment certificates. Ask for a linked programme that sequences interdependent work—for example, air-conditioning services before ceiling closure—and identifies long-lead purchases and decision deadlines. Compare project managers by cost forecasting, risk tracking and change control rather than a promised shorter completion date. Receive a current programme, decision log, variation register and forecast final cost, with a clear boundary between management fees and the designer's and contractor's obligations.


### 24. Work quality review

A quality review is not necessarily day-to-day supervision: it may investigate repeated paint defects, uneven tiling or a failed waterproofing test. Define the work packages, acceptance samples, applicable specifications and whether opening-up or independent testing is included. Require room- or element-referenced checklists, photographs and defect priorities. Compare offers by sampling method and provision for reinspection after correction. The final report should distinguish demonstrated nonconformities from aesthetic observations and items that could not be inspected.


### 25. Project stage acceptance

Stage acceptance should not be a signature against a percentage complete. Before a slab pour, inspect drawings, reinforcement, openings and embedded services; before closing a bathroom, check waterproofing and the agreed test record. Supply the inspection party with the contract scope, checklists and earlier stage records, and identify who may require corrective action. Compare charges for return visits and retesting. Keep a signed record of accepted, rejected and outstanding items with closure dates; this does not replace any separately required authority approval.


### 26. Progress report preparation

A useful progress report compares physical work and measured quantities with the baseline programme rather than merely presenting site photographs. Require progress by work package at a stated cut-off date, repeatable photo viewpoints and reasons for material, labour or decision delays. For an occupied home, include planned service interruptions and disruptive activities. Compare reporting providers by how they verify percentages, flag risks and track corrective actions. Receive planned-versus-actual progress, next week's critical activities and a clear distinction between verified facts and contractor estimates.


### 27. Quantity surveying

Quantity surveying requires drawings with known revision numbers and agreed measurement rules: net paint area, pipe lengths, concrete volume or door counts. Specify units, exclusions and provisional items that cannot yet be measured reliably. In an existing property, record site-measured dimensions and concealed elements that require assumptions. Reconcile the survey with contractor bill items one by one; do not combine incompatible units into a single total. Receive an editable schedule with drawing references, calculations and a register of assumptions.


### 28. Contractor bid review

Give bidders the same drawings, bill of quantities and deadline for clarifications. Separate base prices, alternatives, exclusions and provisional sums, then compare brands, material thicknesses, warranties and payment milestones. The lowest total is not comparable if it omits waterproofing, reinstatement or waste removal. Request a technical and commercial comparison matrix showing differences and risk exposure, plus a shared clarification log. Record the recommendation and its limitations before appointing a contractor.


## Interior design and material selection

### 29. Majlis design

Set the majlis guest capacity and seating format first, including floor seating or sofas and the route for serving refreshments. Measure walls, door swings and circulation, then coordinate AC outlets and lighting so they do not compromise comfort. Compare options by usable seats, clear passage widths and cleanable upholstery. Accept a dimensioned furniture plan, wall elevations and finish samples before fabrication.

### 30. Living room design

Describe how the household uses the living room: viewing, reading, children's activities and visitors. Record windows, daytime glare, power and network points and the dimensions of furniture to be retained. Test door swings and routes to adjoining rooms rather than choosing a layout solely for its render. Request coordinated furniture, lighting and storage drawings showing practical access.

### 31. Bedroom design

Confirm bed size, number of occupants, storage capacity and blackout needs. Measure finished room dimensions, windows and the actual door swing before planning wardrobes. Compare hinged and sliding doors for clearance, maintenance and access to drawers. Receive wardrobe elevations, internal sections and coordinated switch and bedside lighting positions before carpentry starts.

### 32. Kitchen design

Survey finished kitchen walls and all water, waste, power, extraction and applicable gas connections. Plan washing, preparation and cooking around the household's routines, checking that the fridge, oven and drawers can open together. Compare cabinet board thickness, hinges, worktops and accessories as separate specifications. Approve manufacturing drawings and service coordinates only after final site measurements.

### 33. Bathroom design

Measure the drainage positions, finished levels, door opening and clear space around the WC, basin and shower. Specify wet zones, waterproofing transitions and maintenance access to valves. Compare slip resistance, cleanability and spare-part availability rather than finishes alone. Accept coordinated plumbing, tile layout, floor falls and waterproofing details before construction; a render is not a waterproofing specification.

### 34. Furniture layout planning

Measure existing furniture rather than relying on standard plan symbols; also check access routes for moving large pieces into the property. Test alternative layouts with drawers and doors open and with clear routes to windows and sockets. Request a scaled plan with critical clearances and potential clashes, and mark major furniture positions on site before purchase.

### 35. Interior 3D visualisation

Clarify whether the deliverable is photorealistic decision imagery or a coordinated model suitable for design review. Specify rooms, camera angles, detail level and revision rounds, and provide current drawings and material references. Check ceiling heights, door openings and lighting positions against plans. Screen colours cannot replace physical samples, and renderings should not be treated as fabrication instructions.

### 36. Office design

Establish staffing, work patterns, meeting needs, privacy and future expansion before selecting desks. Survey columns, glazing, exits and power/data outlets, and account for storage, acoustics and visitor circulation. Compare layouts by functional workspace and maintenance access, not maximum desk count. Receive a coordinated furniture, lighting, power and data plan.

### 37. Retail store design

Identify merchandise categories, display quantities, replenishment routes, stockroom needs and checkout flow. Measure shopfront, clear heights, service points and delivery access. Compare modular shelving by flexibility, stability, cleanability and customer access. Receive fabrication dimensions for displays and counters plus lighting and electrical layouts; a fit-out design is not evidence of activity approval.

### 38. Restaurant and café design

Define service style, intended covers, order flow, food delivery and clearing routes alongside kitchen equipment and storage needs. Survey ventilation, drainage, power and staff/customer circulation. Have qualified specialists check applicable health and safety requirements for the specific operation. Compare schemes by workable service capacity, not seat count alone, and receive coordinated equipment and services drawings.

### 39. Salon design

Specify the salon's actual treatments and number of workstations because water, ventilation and storage needs vary. Survey wash stations, sockets, mirror lighting, waiting space and staff circulation. Compare finishes for resistance to the cleaning products used. Receive workstation, plumbing and electrical layouts and a finishes schedule, with activity-specific requirements checked through the relevant authority.

### 40. Clinic interior design

Start with the clinic's discipline, patient arrival route, privacy needs and supply storage. Qualified professionals must confirm equipment, cleaning and infection-control requirements for the proposed licensed activity; an interior drawing is not healthcare approval. Compare schemes for separation of movement and maintainability. Receive room layouts, door details, service points and cleanable finish specifications.

### 41. Exhibition design

Record exhibit sizes, weights, replacement frequency, interactive features and back-of-house storage. Survey columns, electrical supplies, entry and loading routes, then assess product visibility from main visitor paths. Compare displays for stability, reuse and service access. Accept fabrication drawings for supports, lighting and device cabling before manufacture.

### 42. Reception area design

Define whether reception handles check-in, waiting, wayfinding or payments, and estimate peak occupancy. Measure entrance clearances, doors and queueing space, with privacy and accessibility considered. Compare desk designs for staff workspace, cable routes, storage and cleaning. Receive plans and sections showing counter heights, power outlets and the visitor route.

### 43. Floor finish selection

Select flooring according to use, traffic, moisture exposure, sunlight and existing substrate. Request physical samples and relevant slip-resistance information, and check build-up thickness against door thresholds. Compare material cost alongside wastage, skirtings, substrate preparation and maintenance. Accept an area schedule, laying direction and trial installation before bulk ordering.

### 44. Paint colour selection

Review daylight, windows, flooring and furniture before choosing paint colours from a digital image. Test real samples on the wall at different times of day, and distinguish wall, ceiling and joinery finishes by sheen and cleanability. Compare quotes by preparation, coats and product specification rather than colour name. Receive a room-by-room colour code and approved sample record.

### 45. Stone and tile selection

Choose stone or tile for its actual application: kitchen counter, wet floor or wall cladding each has different wear, absorption and slip considerations. Request measured layouts showing vein direction, cuts, joints and exposed edges. Check thickness, fixing method, sample source and batch consistency. Approve a setting-out drawing and installed sample before full procurement.

### 46. Furniture specification

List every furniture item by function, dimensions and access route into the property, distinguishing ready-made from bespoke pieces. Compare frames, foam, fabric, fittings, care requirements and warranties rather than appearance alone. Approve material samples and shop drawings for custom work. At delivery, check stability, finish, drawers and the item schedule against the order.

### 47. Lighting selection

Divide lighting into ambient, task and accent functions, setting comfort, control and dimming needs for each space. Coordinate ceiling heights, AC equipment, furniture and available circuits before choosing fittings. Compare output, distribution, colour temperature and driver replaceability. Receive switching/circuit plans and a luminaire schedule supported by real samples.

### 48. Material mood board

A material board should identify actual floor, paint, timber, textile and metal samples with product references and intended suppliers, not just attractive photographs. Review them in daylight and the proposed artificial light, considering gloss, texture, cleaning and wear. Accept a labelled board tying each sample to a specific room and surface, with unapproved alternatives clearly marked.

### 49. Finish schedule preparation

The finishes schedule is the purchasing and installation reference. For each room, record floor, wall, ceiling, skirting and door finish codes with specification, dimensions, colour and sheen. Reconcile it with approved drawings and wet-area details, and define how substitutions are approved. Receive a versioned editable schedule linked to samples and indicative quantities, with unresolved selections assigned an owner and deadline.

## Brief before hiring a designer or consultant

Submit an as-is drawing, even a measured sketch, proposed changes, occupants, furniture to retain, photographs and an indicative budget. Specify whether the deliverable is a 3D visualisation, construction drawings, quantity schedule or site supervision; these are not interchangeable. Where no firm is listed, Biet Al Reef customer support can take the brief as a quotation enquiry without suggesting professional approval or a government permit.

**Common question:** Are renderings enough to build from? No. Agree drawings, measured dimensions, specifications, electrical and HVAC coordination and execution details.
