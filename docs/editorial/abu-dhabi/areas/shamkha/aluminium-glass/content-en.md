## Wooden kitchens, wardrobes and doors

### 1. Wooden kitchen design

Before commissioning a wooden kitchen design, provide wall-by-wall dimensions, ceiling height, sink and extractor locations, appliance sizes and photographs of corners and obstructions. The quotation should identify cabinet elevations, internal sections, worktop depth and door, drawer and refrigerator clearances. Compare board thickness, edging and hinge specifications rather than rendered images alone. Approve dimensioned shop drawings before cutting and check that the layout supports actual movement around the kitchen.

### 2. Kitchen cabinetry fabrication

Kitchen cabinet fabrication should start from approved shop drawings, not a colour sample. Supply a cabinet schedule with dimensions and openings for appliances and pipework. Ask for board type, suitable moisture resistance near wet areas, edge treatment, drawer runner and hinge models, and piece labelling for delivery. Compare itemised cabinets and hardware rather than a single linear-metre rate; inspect cut-outs, door alignment and a finish sample before dispatch.

### 3. Kitchen installation

Before fitting a manufactured kitchen, check floor and wall levels, service points and the ventilation clearance required by appliances. The installation quote should specify transport, lifting, on-site alterations, wall anchorage and sealing around the sink and worktop. Request photographs before service access is concealed. At handover, operate every drawer and door and confirm that valves and maintenance points remain accessible.

### 4. Wardrobe design

A wardrobe design should reflect hanging lengths, folded clothing, shoes, bags and drawer use, not just its visible doors. Provide wall width and height, nearby doors, sockets and air-conditioning positions. Compare internal layouts, hanging depth and hinged versus sliding door clearances. Review the drawing for access to upper shelves and safe circulation in front of the wardrobe before approval.

### 5. Wardrobe fabrication

For wardrobe fabrication, agree a component schedule showing unit sizes, shelf spans, drawers and doors. Specify board thickness, back panels, edge banding and door and drawer mechanisms: these details affect service life beyond appearance. Compare quotations by actual components and installation scope. Check dimensions, door alignment and shelf stability before accepting the fabricated units.

### 6. Walk-in closet fit-out

A walk-in closet needs a circulation plan before cabinets are selected. Photograph the entrance, windows and outlets; measure corners, walls and ceiling, and locate the mirror, seating and standing space in front of drawers. Compare open and enclosed storage, maintainable lighting, ventilation and floor-cleaning access. At handover, open each drawer without collision and check anchorage of tall units.

### 7. Kitchen and wardrobe refurbishment

Diagnose damage before refurbishing a kitchen or wardrobe: failed hinges, moisture-swollen panels or an unstable cabinet carcass require different work. Supply close-up and overall photographs with dimensions. Ask for separate costs for fronts, hardware, structural repairs and replacement. Do not conceal water-damaged boards without addressing the source. Test door alignment, drawer operation and the match with retained finishes.

### 8. Wooden door fabrication

For a wooden door, the nominal opening size is not enough. Record finished opening width and height, wall thickness, swing direction, lock position and whether moisture exposure is expected. Specify core construction, surface finish, frame, edges and hinges; separate the leaf price from installation and hardware. Inspect straightness, edge quality and lock preparation before delivery.

### 9. Door installation

Door installation requires measuring the finished opening, floor level, threshold and swing clearance. Compare hinge quantity, anchor type, frame fixing and surrounding finishes, and clarify who corrects an out-of-plumb wall. After fitting, repeatedly open and close the leaf, test the latch and lock, and check for floor rubbing or unwanted movement caused by a tilted frame.

### 10. Fire-rated door fabrication

A fire-rated door is not simply a timber leaf with special paint. Identify its position and required rating from the approved project documentation. Request evidence for the complete rated assembly, including leaf, frame, seals, hinges and closer, and verify that the evidence covers the proposed dimensions and configuration. Avoid undocumented component substitutions. Handover checks should cover labels, records, self-closing operation and gaps against the project's approved requirements.

### 11. Doorframe construction

Doorframe fabrication starts with the structural opening, wall thickness, door type and threshold rather than leaf size alone. Photograph junctions with tiles or wall cladding and specify any architraves. Compare frame material, edge protection, anchors and methods of keeping it plumb. Inspect vertical alignment, consistent clearances and stability before the leaf is hung.

### 12. Wooden partition fabrication

A timber partition may screen views, decorate a room or carry shelves; each use requires different fixings. Provide overall length, height, slat spacing, adjacent door clearance and photographs of floor, ceiling and nearby services. Compare section thickness, finish, concealed anchorage and maintenance access. At handover check rigidity, safe edges and that circulation width has not been compromised.

### 13. Door repairs

Begin door maintenance by identifying the symptom: floor rubbing, dropped hinges, a faulty lock or moisture swelling. Photograph the frame, hinges and both door edges and note any leak or recent change. Compare adjustment, hardware replacement and timber repair against replacing the leaf; cosmetic work will not restore a failed core. Test closing, latching and locking without excessive force.

### 14. Timber refinishing

Timber refinishing depends on whether the surface is solid wood, veneer or melamine and on its existing coating. Send daylight photographs of scratches and peeling, choose the intended colour and sheen, and clarify whether work is off-site. Request a sample showing preparation, primer, coating system and drying allowance. Inspect consistent colour and texture and make sure paint has not built up around hinges or edges.

## Custom furniture and decorative joinery

### 15. Bespoke furniture fabrication

Bespoke furniture should be specified around its daily use and actual location rather than an inspiration photograph alone. Provide the available width, height and depth, photographs of sockets and skirting, and delivery-route or lift dimensions where relevant. Ask for a dimensioned drawing, frame and board or timber specification, upholstery if applicable, finish, fittings, and delivery and installation scope. At handover, compare actual dimensions with the approved drawing and check stability, edges and the operation of moving parts.

### 16. TV console fabrication

A TV console must accommodate devices, cabling and ventilation, not merely fill a wall. Photograph the display position, power and data outlets; measure wall width, viewing height and drawer clearances. Request a drawing showing cable routes, fixings and how the supporting wall will be assessed for suspended elements. Compare boards, edge finishes, hinges and access for servicing equipment. On completion, check level alignment and ensure cables are not trapped by drawers or panels.

### 17. Storage unit fabrication

Before commissioning a storage unit, list what it must hold and the approximate loads. Measure the space, door swings and nearby switches, then specify adjustable shelf positions, drawer depth and secure anchoring for tall units. A comparable quote identifies carcass material, shelf thickness, runners, edges and installation method. At handover, test doors and drawers, inspect fixings and verify stability without unsafe overload testing.

### 18. Timber wall cladding

Timber wall cladding requires checking the substrate for unevenness, moisture and concealed services before installing battens or panels. Send wall measurements and photographs of corners, openings and outlets; request a physical finish sample showing grain direction and joint spacing. Compare fixing methods, service access, edge treatment and any required cavity behind the cladding. At handover, inspect consistent joints and ensure vents and access points remain usable.

### 19. CNC decorative joinery

CNC decorative joinery needs a fabrication-ready drawing with dimensions, board thickness and minimum cut-out widths; a low-resolution pattern image is insufficient. State whether the piece is a screen, decorative front or wall-mounted panel, and agree how delicate sections will be supported and fixed. For intricate patterns, approve a sample cut and the sanding and paint finish. Inspect cut edges for sharp points or splitting and check that repeated modules align.

### 20. Custom counters

A reception or sales counter must fit staff movement, customer interaction and payment equipment. Provide a floor plan, circulation dimensions, power and data locations, and the required worktop height and storage. Ask for separate specifications for the frame, cladding, counter surface and integrated fittings, including edge protection and suitability for expected wear. On handover, test access panels, drawers, cable routes and counter stability.

### 21. Retail and office furniture fabrication

Retail and office furniture should be planned around user numbers, display loads, equipment and circulation rather than ordering generic matching pieces. Supply a layout showing desks, cabinets, shelving and aisles, together with entrance dimensions for delivery. Compare item counts, dimensions, materials, fittings, assembly, defect remedies and anchoring for tall shelving. At handover, inspect alignment, stability, access for cleaning and whether the installed layout preserves intended movement.

### 22. Interior space design

Interior space design for workshop joinery establishes where fixed cabinets, wall linings and partitions will sit before timber is cut; it is not a substitute for a complete architectural design. Request scaled plans showing doors, windows, AC outlets and electrical points, and measure walls at several positions to identify deviations. Distinguish concept visuals from fabrication drawings showing board thickness, clearances and fixings. Compare design fees by drawings, revisions and samples included. At acceptance, verify circulation, door operation and access to services concealed behind panels.

### 23. Joinery fabrication and installation

Joinery fabrication and installation are distinct responsibilities: accurate cutting and assembly in the workshop, followed by secure fitting to the actual walls and floors. Provide a numbered unit schedule with dimensions, board types, finishes and hardware, plus photographs of access doors, stairs and lifts to determine transportable piece sizes. Specify who corrects uneven walls, protects floors and seals perimeter gaps. Compare fabrication, transport and fitting costs separately. Inspect components before dispatch and test alignment, anchorage and drawer travel after installation.

### 24. Majlis fit-out

Majlis fit-out starts with the actual seating arrangement rather than a decorative reference photograph. Specify seat count, routes between entrance and seating, television location, air outlets and lighting, supported by room dimensions and ceiling heights. Separate fixed timber linings and ceiling work from movable sofas, upholstery and curtains so bids cover the same scope. Request timber and fabric samples with cleaning requirements. At handover, sit in different positions to check sightlines, lighting and whether doors conflict with furniture.

### 25. Bedroom fit-out

Bedroom fit-out depends on clearance around the bed and in front of wardrobes before furniture sizes are fixed. Record finished room dimensions, sockets, switches, windows and bathroom doors, then approve a plan showing the full travel of drawers and doors. Price the bed, headboard, wardrobes, bedside units and integrated lighting separately; specify edge finishes and anchors for tall units. At handover, fully open the wardrobe, confirm access to outlets and windows, and inspect stability and sharp corners along walking routes.

### 26. Office and retail fit-out

Office and retail fit-out cannot be priced by counter length alone: map workflow, storage, customer waiting areas and circulation. Supply a floor plan showing existing power and data points and the dimensions of equipment or display stock. Clarify whether the contract covers fixed joinery only or the complete fit-out. Compare storage units, counters, fittings, lighting and service connections item by item, and identify who coordinates with designers or relevant authorities where required. Hand over a room-by-room defect list and test shelf stability, drawer operation and access to maintenance points.

### 27. Timber decorative features

Timber decorative features include wall battens, frames and ornamental panels, which serve a different purpose from load-bearing storage cabinets. Mark every feature on a dimensioned elevation, specifying pattern spacing, grain direction and finish colour; photograph sockets, damp areas and uneven substrates. Approve a physical sample of edges and corners before full production. Compare panel material, fixing method and the ability to remove a section for repairs. At acceptance, inspect consistent spacing, neat junctions at switches and the absence of projecting or loose edges.

### 28. Interior fit-out handover

Interior fit-out finishing and handover is a defined inspection stage, not a vague allowance for unspecified repairs. Gather approved drawings, variation records and material references, then check each room for aligned panels, sealed joints, functioning doors, drawers and integrated lighting, and undamaged surfaces. Record defects with photographs, location, unit number and an agreed correction date; separate defects from new additional work. Re-test corrected items before final sign-off and obtain unit keys, cleaning and maintenance instructions and any agreed warranties.

## Aluminium windows, doors and fittings

### 29. Aluminium window fabrication

Aluminium window fabrication starts with an opening schedule showing finished width and height, sill level and whether each unit is hinged or sliding. Photograph the reveals, sill, curtains and nearby equipment. Request the frame profile, glazing specification, gaskets, drainage details, locking hardware and a finish sample. Compare quotes by sash arrangement, glass build-up and system performance, not opening area alone. Before dispatch, check frame dimensions, squareness, assembly joints and drainage slots; trial the operation of a sample before repeating production.

### 30. Aluminium window installation

Installing aluminium windows requires checking the opening's alignment, levels and the substrate receiving the fixings. Agree the anchorage appropriate to the approved system, movement gaps, internal and external joint seals and protection of surrounding finishes. Compare supply, removal of old units, debris disposal and plaster or paint repairs as separate items. At handover operate every sash and lock, inspect consistent sealant and unobstructed drainage paths. If water testing is required, agree the method and acceptance criteria in advance.

### 31. Aluminium door fabrication

For aluminium door fabrication, identify whether the door is internal or external, hinged or sliding, and its expected frequency of use and privacy requirements. Provide finished opening dimensions, floor level, swing direction and photographs of nearby obstructions. The workshop quotation should specify frame and leaf profiles, infill or glass, hinges, handle, lock and threshold. Any applicable fire or safety requirements need competent project-specific review. Inspect corner squareness, joints and hardware attachment before dispatch.

### 32. Aluminium door installation

Aluminium door installation depends on setting the frame plumb and square and allowing the leaf to clear finished flooring. Specify whether an existing door will be removed, how the frame will be anchored, how gaps and thresholds will be treated, and how adjacent finishes will be protected. Compare total installation and reinstatement costs rather than labour alone. At handover repeatedly open and close the door, check for floor rubbing, and test hinges, latch, lock and joint sealing without obstructing drainage.

### 33. Insect screen installation

First clarify whether the proposed mesh is for insects or another stated purpose: ordinary insect screening is not certified fall or intrusion protection. Supply opening dimensions and photographs of window tracks and handles. Choose fixed, sliding or removable mesh based on cleaning access and window operation. Compare mesh material, weave, frame fixing and replacement options, while considering any relevant emergency exit requirements. Check mesh tension, sharp edges, removability and gaps around the frame at handover.

### 34. Door and window maintenance

For door and window maintenance, record the symptom at each opening: sticking sliders, rain ingress, draughts, noise or a failed lock. Photograph tracks, seals, hinges and rubbing points. The underlying cause may be alignment or blocked drainage rather than a defective frame. Ask for diagnosis and separate prices for cleaning, parts replacement, adjustment and resealing, with clear limits on what can be repaired. Reproduce the original fault during handover testing and confirm that drainage openings remain clear.

### 35. Glass and hardware replacement

Replacing door or window hardware or glazing requires identifying the system and photographing and measuring the damaged component: rollers, handles and locks are not universally interchangeable. For glazing, record pane dimensions, glass type, location and retention method; safety glazing must not be downgraded. Compare the component price, removal and installation labour, safe disposal of broken glass and hardware warranty. At handover test movement and locking, inspect gaskets, secure retention and edges, and check for installation damage.

### 36. Aluminium kitchen fabrication

Commission aluminium kitchen cabinets only after final wall dimensions, appliance positions and plumbing and electrical outlets are confirmed. Provide a dimensioned cabinet schedule showing width, depth, door swing and service access. Ask the fabricator to specify aluminium profiles and thicknesses, infill panels, finish suitability near wet areas, hinges and runners, with fabrication, worktop and fitting priced separately. Before dispatch, compare assembled units with the approved drawings and inspect corners, edges and appliance cut-outs.


### 37. Aluminium kitchen installation

Installing aluminium kitchen units is a separate scope from fabrication. Check finished floor and wall levels, worktop height and exposed services; photograph access routes and identify the wall substrate before choosing anchors for upper units. Compare quotations for delivery, lifting, fixing, junction sealing and hardware adjustment, explicitly excluding or including plumbing and electrical connections. At handover, operate every door and drawer, verify cabinet stability and worktop levels, and retain access to isolation valves.


### 38. Aluminium cabinet fabrication

For aluminium storage cabinets, identify the contents and approximate shelf loads before deciding spans and divisions. Supply clear opening dimensions, obstacles, sockets and preferred door swing; require a drawing of internal compartments and fixing points. Compare profile and shelf thickness, backing, hinges, locks and finish rather than a per-metre price alone. At acceptance, check level doors, secure shelves, safe edges and unobstructed circulation.


### 39. Bathroom aluminium cabinet fabrication

For bathroom cabinets, record basin, trap and shut-off valve positions and the shower splash zone, with photographs after tiling. Specify door-panel materials, seals, joint detailing, ventilation and removable plumbing access: corrosion-resistant aluminium does not make every infill or hinge waterproof. Compare quotations for pipe cut-outs, edging, worktop and mirror inclusions. At handover, test maintenance access, wall fixings, door clearance and separation from electrical fittings.


### 40. Outdoor aluminium storage fabrication

Outdoor aluminium storage needs a defined position, door clearance and inventory, including whether the unit is sheltered or directly exposed to sun and rain. Provide dimensions, floor photographs and maintenance clearance. Require details of finish, panel material, ventilation, drainage, locks and anti-tip anchorage. Compare load suitability and replaceable components as well as appearance. On delivery, check base stability, latching and drainage without trapped water.


### 41. Aluminium kitchen refurbishment

Refurbishing an existing aluminium kitchen begins with a condition survey of frames, fronts, hinges, runners and worktop. Photograph corrosion, distortion and leaks, and measure each front: replacement hardware may not fit older profiles. Where appropriate, compare itemised repair and refacing against replacement cabinetry. Approve a finish sample and fixing method, then test door and drawer movement and the sink-to-worktop junction at completion.


### 42. Kitchen hinges and accessory maintenance

For kitchen hinge and hardware repairs, describe sagging doors, rubbing, failed soft-close action or jammed drawers. Photograph the hardware, note model markings and quantities; opening angles and hole patterns may prevent direct substitution. A quotation should distinguish adjustment, hinge or runner replacement, and repair of stripped fixing points. At handover, repeatedly operate each door and drawer, checking consistent gaps and secure fasteners.


## Glass façades and installations

### 43. Glazed façade execution

A glazed building façade is not the same job as replacing a broken pane. Start with structural drawings, floor levels, measured openings and photographs showing connections to the building frame. State expectations for solar control, thermal performance, weather resistance and future maintenance access. A comparable quotation should identify the façade system, glass build-up, gaskets, movement joints, drainage, engineered shop drawings, access equipment and agreed testing. A square-metre rate alone conceals substantial differences. At handover, inspect joint alignment, protected edges and anchors, review the agreed water-test evidence and obtain maintenance information.

### 44. Toughened glass installation

Toughened safety glass may be appropriate for specified locations, but it is not automatically interchangeable with laminated fall-protection glazing. Measure finished openings and fix all holes, cut-outs and edge details before manufacture; tempered glass cannot simply be re-cut on site. Ask for glass thickness, processing identification, edge treatment and a handling and lifting plan. Compare supply, hardware and installation separately against the same specification. On delivery, reject cracked or edge-damaged panes, and check that protective setting blocks and gaskets prevent hard glass-to-metal contact.

### 45. Shopfront glazing

A shopfront must balance merchandise visibility, customer circulation and secure closure. Provide opening measurements, a sketch of door position and swing, adjacent finishes and installation access outside trading hours if necessary. Specify glazing, framing, locks, handles and any shading or security screen as distinct options. Request separate prices for removing the old frontage, protecting stock, making good and weather sealing. At handover, repeatedly test door operation, check even clearances, secure fixing and clean sealed joints rather than accepting the appearance alone.

### 46. Glass door installation

For a new glass door, establish whether it is hinged or sliding, expected traffic, clear opening width and direction of travel. Measure after floor finishes and record thresholds, substrate and available anchor points. Heavy panels need compatible, load-rated pivots or tracks. Request a drawing showing pane size, holes, handle, lock and closer where relevant. Compare the cost of glazing, fittings, installation and adjustment separately. At handover, check smooth movement without floor contact, firm handles and safe clearance from surrounding fittings.

### 47. Glass partition installation

Glass partitions can divide rooms while preserving daylight, but privacy and acoustic performance depend on the whole assembly. Provide a plan showing room divisions, actual ceiling height, doors, air-conditioning outlets, sprinklers and lighting. Request samples of clear or obscured glass and details of framing, seals and floor or ceiling fixings. Price any service relocation and floor reinstatement explicitly; a light partition should not be assumed to provide high acoustic isolation. Inspect panel alignment, safe corners, stable fixings and door closure on completion.

### 48. Glass balustrade installation

A glass balustrade is a fall-protection assembly, not simply a decorative panel. Record the stair or balcony geometry, drop, substrate and fixing positions with photographs. A competent designer must establish the applicable loading and compliant glass-and-anchor system; neither thickness nor fixing type should be selected from a picture or a price alone. Request details of laminated glazing or the approved system, base channels, anchors, edge protection and panel replacement. At handover, review design and installation records, alignment, secure connections and exposed edges; an improvised push test is not a substitute for professional inspection.

### 49. Façade glazing maintenance

Glazed façade maintenance begins by distinguishing failed perimeter seals, blocked drainage, condensation inside an insulated glass unit and defective opening hardware. Supply photographs with floor, elevation and when the symptom appears, such as after rain. The quotation should describe safe access, diagnosis, compatible replacement parts and materials, separating targeted repair from full-unit replacement. Internal condensation cannot normally be fixed by wiping the outer pane. Ask for before-and-after records and the agreed water or operating test at handover.

## Steel and metal fabrication

### 50. Steel door fabrication

Steel-door fabrication starts by identifying whether the door serves pedestrians, storage or a heavy gate. Provide finished opening dimensions, swing, lock position and photographs of the supporting wall and floor. Request a shop sketch showing frame sections, plate thickness, hinges, reinforcement and anchors. Compare corrosion protection, paint, hardware, delivery and fitting as separate items. A door on an escape route or fire separation must not be described as fire-rated without a suitable approved assembly. At handover, test leaf alignment, locking, clearances and coating at welded or cut edges.

### 51. Steel balustrade fabrication

A steel balustrade requires measured lengths and heights along the entire stair or balcony, not an approximate linear-metre figure. Identify use, children’s access and the supporting edge or slab. Request drawings showing posts, spacing, joints and terminations. Compare metal sections, welded or bolted connections, corrosion protection, anchoring and making good. Where the barrier prevents falls, competent design and load verification are essential. Inspect continuity, spacing, sharp edges and anchor records at handover rather than relying on a casual hand-shake test.

### 52. Steel shade fabrication

Before commissioning a steel carport or entrance canopy, measure vehicle envelopes, door-opening clearance, circulation, proposed posts and overhead or buried obstacles. Require a drawing showing spans, roof slope, rainwater direction, covering and foundation connections. Quotes can differ substantially in section sizes, coating, roof materials and footing works, so itemise them instead of comparing shade area alone. Obtain competent review of wind and other design loads. At handover, verify vehicle clearance, anchorage and drainage away from entrances.

### 53. Structural steelwork execution

A steel structure for an extension, platform or store needs a defined purpose, spans, heights and design loads coordinated with the project’s structural design. Site measurements alone are insufficient for fabrication. Obtain professionally prepared structural and shop drawings with connection, base and lifting details. Compare steel grades and quantities, protective treatment, fabrication, transport, erection and required inspection. At handover, check members, bolts and welds against the drawings, inspect reports and repair coating damage from handling.

### 54. Stainless-steel fabrication

Stainless-steel work may involve a handrail, counter, cladding or kitchen fitting; material grade and surface finish should reflect the application. Record dimensions, visible joints and exposure to moisture and cleaning chemicals, and approve a finish sample. Compare documented grade, sheet and section thickness, weld finishing and fixing method; a bright surface alone proves little. On completion, inspect straightness, safe edges, weld residues and iron contamination, and obtain appropriate cleaning guidance.

### 55. Metal welding services

Welding a gate hinge, assembling a frame and repairing a load-bearing connection are different jobs. Provide close photographs, metal type, thickness, access restrictions and nearby combustible materials. The quote should separate preparation, cutting, welding, finishing and corrosion protection, with spark and fire precautions. Structural welds require competent assessment and appropriate inspection rather than reliance on visual appearance. At handover, check alignment, distortion, visible defects, surface protection and any agreed inspection records.

### 56. Metalwork maintenance

Metalwork maintenance should identify whether the issue is local rust, deformation, a worn hinge, loose anchorage or a cracked weld. Photograph the affected part and state whether it carries load or prevents falls. Request an itemised comparison of cleaning and recoating, component replacement, reinforcement or full remanufacture; paint can hide structural deterioration. Restrict use of hazardous elements until professionally assessed. At handover, test moving parts and check secure connections and coating continuity at cuts and welds.

## Workshop fabrication quotation brief

Provide site-verified dimensions for every unit, opening direction, wall condition, load points, glass/timber/metal type, finish and accessories. Ask for approved shop drawings and samples, and allocate lifting, transport, fitting, edges, anchors and reinstatement. Fall-protection glazing, structural metalwork and fire-rated assemblies require competent design and system review. Customer support can receive a quotation request even without a listed workshop.

**Common question:** Are dimensions estimated from photos enough? No; small measurement errors can make doors or glass unusable. Verify on site before final cutting.
