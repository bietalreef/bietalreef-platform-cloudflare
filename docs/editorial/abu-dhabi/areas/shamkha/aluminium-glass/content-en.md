## Wooden kitchens, wardrobes and doors

### 1. Wooden kitchen design

Before commissioning a wooden kitchen design, provide wall-by-wall dimensions, ceiling height, sink and extractor locations, appliance sizes and photographs of corners and obstructions. The quotation should identify cabinet elevations, internal sections, worktop depth and door, drawer and refrigerator clearances. Compare board thickness, edging and hinge specifications rather than rendered images alone. Approve dimensioned shop drawings before cutting and check that the layout supports actual movement around the kitchen.

### 2. Kitchen cabinetry fabrication

Kitchen cabinet fabrication should start from approved shop drawings, not a colour sample. Supply a cabinet schedule with dimensions and openings for appliances and pipework. Ask for board type, suitable moisture resistance near wet areas, edge treatment, drawer runner and hinge models, and piece labelling for delivery. Compare itemised cabinets and hardware rather than a single linear-metre rate; inspect cut-outs, door alignment and a finish sample before dispatch.

### 3. Kitchen installation

Before fitting a manufactured kitchen, check floor and wall levels, service points and the ventilation clearance required by appliances. The installation quote should specify transport, lifting, on-site alterations, wall anchorage and sealing around the sink and worktop. Request photographs before service access is concealed. At handover, operate every drawer and door and confirm that valves and maintenance points remain accessible.

### 4. Wardrobe design

A wardrobe design should reflect hanging lengths, folded clothing, shoes, bags and drawer use, not just its visible doors. Provide wall width and height, nearby doors, sockets and air-conditioning positions. Compare internal layouts, hanging depth and hinged versus sliding door clearances. Review the drawing for access to upper shelves and safe circulation in front of the wardrobe before approval.

### 5. Wardrobe fabrication

For wardrobe fabrication, agree a component schedule showing unit sizes, shelf spans, drawers and doors. Specify board thickness, back panels, edge banding and door and drawer mechanisms: these details affect service life beyond appearance. Compare quotations by actual components and installation scope. Check dimensions, door alignment and shelf stability before accepting the fabricated units.

### 6. Walk-in closet fit-out

A walk-in closet needs a circulation plan before cabinets are selected. Photograph the entrance, windows and outlets; measure corners, walls and ceiling, and locate the mirror, seating and standing space in front of drawers. Compare open and enclosed storage, maintainable lighting, ventilation and floor-cleaning access. At handover, open each drawer without collision and check anchorage of tall units.

### 7. Kitchen and wardrobe refurbishment

Diagnose damage before refurbishing a kitchen or wardrobe: failed hinges, moisture-swollen panels or an unstable cabinet carcass require different work. Supply close-up and overall photographs with dimensions. Ask for separate costs for fronts, hardware, structural repairs and replacement. Do not conceal water-damaged boards without addressing the source. Test door alignment, drawer operation and the match with retained finishes.

### 8. Wooden door fabrication

For a wooden door, the nominal opening size is not enough. Record finished opening width and height, wall thickness, swing direction, lock position and whether moisture exposure is expected. Specify core construction, surface finish, frame, edges and hinges; separate the leaf price from installation and hardware. Inspect straightness, edge quality and lock preparation before delivery.

### 9. Door installation

Door installation requires measuring the finished opening, floor level, threshold and swing clearance. Compare hinge quantity, anchor type, frame fixing and surrounding finishes, and clarify who corrects an out-of-plumb wall. After fitting, repeatedly open and close the leaf, test the latch and lock, and check for floor rubbing or unwanted movement caused by a tilted frame.

### 10. Fire-rated door fabrication

A fire-rated door is not simply a timber leaf with special paint. Identify its position and required rating from the approved project documentation. Request evidence for the complete rated assembly, including leaf, frame, seals, hinges and closer, and verify that the evidence covers the proposed dimensions and configuration. Avoid undocumented component substitutions. Handover checks should cover labels, records, self-closing operation and gaps against the project's approved requirements.

### 11. Doorframe construction

Doorframe fabrication starts with the structural opening, wall thickness, door type and threshold rather than leaf size alone. Photograph junctions with tiles or wall cladding and specify any architraves. Compare frame material, edge protection, anchors and methods of keeping it plumb. Inspect vertical alignment, consistent clearances and stability before the leaf is hung.

### 12. Wooden partition fabrication

A timber partition may screen views, decorate a room or carry shelves; each use requires different fixings. Provide overall length, height, slat spacing, adjacent door clearance and photographs of floor, ceiling and nearby services. Compare section thickness, finish, concealed anchorage and maintenance access. At handover check rigidity, safe edges and that circulation width has not been compromised.

### 13. Door repairs

Begin door maintenance by identifying the symptom: floor rubbing, dropped hinges, a faulty lock or moisture swelling. Photograph the frame, hinges and both door edges and note any leak or recent change. Compare adjustment, hardware replacement and timber repair against replacing the leaf; cosmetic work will not restore a failed core. Test closing, latching and locking without excessive force.

### 14. Timber refinishing

Timber refinishing depends on whether the surface is solid wood, veneer or melamine and on its existing coating. Send daylight photographs of scratches and peeling, choose the intended colour and sheen, and clarify whether work is off-site. Request a sample showing preparation, primer, coating system and drying allowance. Inspect consistent colour and texture and make sure paint has not built up around hinges or edges.

## Custom furniture and decorative joinery

### 15. Bespoke furniture fabrication

Bespoke furniture should be specified around its daily use and actual location rather than an inspiration photograph alone. Provide the available width, height and depth, photographs of sockets and skirting, and delivery-route or lift dimensions where relevant. Ask for a dimensioned drawing, frame and board or timber specification, upholstery if applicable, finish, fittings, and delivery and installation scope. At handover, compare actual dimensions with the approved drawing and check stability, edges and the operation of moving parts.

### 16. TV console fabrication

A TV console must accommodate devices, cabling and ventilation, not merely fill a wall. Photograph the display position, power and data outlets; measure wall width, viewing height and drawer clearances. Request a drawing showing cable routes, fixings and how the supporting wall will be assessed for suspended elements. Compare boards, edge finishes, hinges and access for servicing equipment. On completion, check level alignment and ensure cables are not trapped by drawers or panels.

### 17. Storage unit fabrication

Before commissioning a storage unit, list what it must hold and the approximate loads. Measure the space, door swings and nearby switches, then specify adjustable shelf positions, drawer depth and secure anchoring for tall units. A comparable quote identifies carcass material, shelf thickness, runners, edges and installation method. At handover, test doors and drawers, inspect fixings and verify stability without unsafe overload testing.

### 18. Timber wall cladding

Timber wall cladding requires checking the substrate for unevenness, moisture and concealed services before installing battens or panels. Send wall measurements and photographs of corners, openings and outlets; request a physical finish sample showing grain direction and joint spacing. Compare fixing methods, service access, edge treatment and any required cavity behind the cladding. At handover, inspect consistent joints and ensure vents and access points remain usable.

### 19. CNC decorative joinery

CNC decorative joinery needs a fabrication-ready drawing with dimensions, board thickness and minimum cut-out widths; a low-resolution pattern image is insufficient. State whether the piece is a screen, decorative front or wall-mounted panel, and agree how delicate sections will be supported and fixed. For intricate patterns, approve a sample cut and the sanding and paint finish. Inspect cut edges for sharp points or splitting and check that repeated modules align.

### 20. Custom counters

A reception or sales counter must fit staff movement, customer interaction and payment equipment. Provide a floor plan, circulation dimensions, power and data locations, and the required worktop height and storage. Ask for separate specifications for the frame, cladding, counter surface and integrated fittings, including edge protection and suitability for expected wear. On handover, test access panels, drawers, cable routes and counter stability.

### 21. Retail and office furniture fabrication

Retail and office furniture should be planned around user numbers, display loads, equipment and circulation rather than ordering generic matching pieces. Supply a layout showing desks, cabinets, shelving and aisles, together with entrance dimensions for delivery. Compare item counts, dimensions, materials, fittings, assembly, defect remedies and anchoring for tall shelving. At handover, inspect alignment, stability, access for cleaning and whether the installed layout preserves intended movement.

### 22. Interior space design

Map space function, circulation, lighting, freestanding and fixed furniture. Compare design deliverables with optional execution; approve materials before fabrication.

### 23. Joinery fabrication and installation

Record all dimensions, site walls, lifting access, doors and lifts. Compare workshop trial assembly, transport and installation; inspect safe fixings and unobtrusive joints.

### 24. Majlis fit-out

Define seating capacity, majlis dimensions, television, AC, curtains and upholstery. Compare fitted joinery separately from movable furniture; inspect circulation and lighting in use.

### 25. Bedroom fit-out

Measure beds, wardrobes, door swings, ceiling offsets and power points. Compare unit layouts, safe edges and anchorage; inspect window and door access after fitting.

### 26. Office and retail fit-out

Record retail stock or office use, storage, counters, circulation and existing service points. Compare joinery, electrical interfaces and finishes within a defined scope; hand over zone-by-zone snags.

### 27. Timber decorative features

Document motif, finish, panel position and heights on drawings. Compare mock-ups, edge treatment and cleanability; inspect consistent repeats and secure installation.

### 28. Interior fit-out handover

Use a handover checklist for doors, drawers, surfaces, lights, joints and site variations. Compare against approved drawings and materials; provide keys, maintenance notes and replaceable-part records.

## Aluminium windows, doors and fittings

### 29. Aluminium window fabrication

Provide opening dimensions, glass type, sash action and drainage requirements before profile selection. Compare frame system, thickness and gaskets; inspect corners and fabricated dimensions.

### 30. Aluminium window installation

Re-measure finished openings, sash clearance and exposure to wind and rain. Compare frame anchors, seals and perimeter silicone; test smooth operation and appropriate water resistance.

### 31. Aluminium door fabrication

Specify opening, swing, glazing, lock and required passage clearance. Compare profiles, reinforcement, hinges and security; inspect squareness and assembled hardware.

### 32. Aluminium door installation

Confirm flooring level, threshold, handles and swing. Compare anchors, packers and seals; check smooth closure and effective dust sealing.

### 33. Insect screen installation

Measure openings and choose removable or sliding mesh panels. Compare mesh aperture, frame design and cleaning access; inspect tension and side gaps.

### 34. Door and window maintenance

Map sash rubbing, frame movement, failed seals and water entry. Compare adjustments and gasket repairs against full replacement; test seals and square closure.

### 35. Glass and hardware replacement

Provide old hardware photos, aluminium-system code and glass size/thickness. Compare compatible components and safe glazing handling; inspect secure fit and unchipped edges.

### 36. Aluminium kitchen fabrication

Give kitchen plan, loads, appliance sizes and moisture requirements for cabinets. Compare profiles, panels, fronts and hardware; inspect assembled dimensions before delivery.

### 37. Aluminium kitchen installation

Measure walls, services and floor levels before fitting units. Compare installation, junctions and edge protection; test drawers, hinges and worktop alignment.

### 38. Aluminium cabinet fabrication

Specify cupboard dimensions, shelving and storage loads. Compare panel thickness, doors and fixing; inspect sturdy shelves and protected corners.

### 39. Bathroom aluminium cabinet fabrication

Locate under-basin or bathroom storage, splash zones, airflow and leak drainage. Compare moisture-resistant panels, seals and plumbing access; check vulnerable connections are protected.

### 40. Outdoor aluminium storage fabrication

Specify outdoor position, sun/rain/wind exposure and intended stored loads. Compare finish, ventilation, anchors and locks; inspect weather exposure and access.

### 41. Aluminium kitchen refurbishment

Photograph failing fronts, hinges and bent aluminium sections and identify reusable carcasses. Compare door/hardware renewal with full units; inspect colour matching and drawer action.

### 42. Kitchen hinges and accessory maintenance

Record hinge, runner and lock models, symptoms and movement lengths. Compare compatible replacements and adjustments; test even opening without sagging.

## Glass façades and installations

### 43. Glazed façade execution

Set façade height, environmental exposure, wind design and structural fixing points. Compare curtain-wall system, glass, joints and drainage against engineered design; inspect test evidence and anchors.

### 44. Toughened glass installation

Identify pane location, function, thickness, holes and edge profile before tempering. Compare fabrication details, handling and edge protection; reject chipped or cracked pieces.

### 45. Shopfront glazing

Measure retail opening, door position, visibility, security and closure. Compare glass, framing and hardware; inspect door action, reflections and weather seals.

### 46. Glass door installation

Specify panel dimensions, swing, weight, lock, hinges and fixing substrate. Compare load-rated hardware; test balanced closing without floor scraping.

### 47. Glass partition installation

Lay out rooms, glass heights, privacy, acoustic expectations and doors. Compare clear or obscured glass, anchoring and seals; check panel stability and ceiling junctions.

### 48. Glass balustrade installation

Identify guard height, stair or balcony geometry, glass sizes and fixing positions. Have a qualified designer establish load resistance; compare glazing build-up and fixings, then inspect for movement.

### 49. Façade glazing maintenance

Record leaks, condensation, failed opening hardware and seal faults, including elevated access. Compare joint, drainage and component repairs plus access equipment; verify repaired areas with a targeted test.

## Steel and metal fabrication

### 50. Steel door fabrication

Measure door opening, weight, hinge and lock positions and corrosion exposure. Compare metal gauge, coating and anchors; inspect flatness and functional locks.

### 51. Steel balustrade fabrication

Define guard lengths, heights, corner geometry, base fixings and users. Compare member sizes, joints and finish; inspect the full length for stability.

### 52. Steel shade fabrication

Measure vehicle envelope, traffic routes, post positions, wind and rain drainage. Compare steel sections, footings and covering; inspect secure anchorage and runoff.

### 53. Structural steelwork execution

Define structural function, spans, loads, approved drawings and lifting access. Obtain engineered connection details and shop drawings; inspect geometry, joints and corrosion protection.

### 54. Stainless-steel fabrication

Identify stainless grade, finish, cleaning exposure and piece dimensions. Compare weld preparation, grinding and finish; inspect smooth joins, safe edges and no surface staining.

### 55. Metal welding services

Record metal type, thickness, defect and whether the weld is structural or a non-load-bearing repair. Compare welding procedure, preparation, fire protection and inspection; check for cracking or distortion.

### 56. Metalwork maintenance

Photograph rust, distortion, failed hinges and weld damage, noting hazards. Compare repair, reinforcement and renewal for safety and cost; test function and coating after work.



## Workshop fabrication quotation brief

Provide site-verified dimensions for every unit, opening direction, wall condition, load points, glass/timber/metal type, finish and accessories. Ask for approved shop drawings and samples, and allocate lifting, transport, fitting, edges, anchors and reinstatement. Fall-protection glazing, structural metalwork and fire-rated assemblies require competent design and system review. Customer support can receive a quotation request even without a listed workshop.

**Common question:** Are dimensions estimated from photos enough? No; small measurement errors can make doors or glass unusable. Verify on site before final cutting.
