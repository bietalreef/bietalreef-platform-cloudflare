# Al Shamkha — Building materials and supply

## Al Shamkha enquiry context
For steel or block deliveries in Al Shamkha, schedule batches around actual progress and safe storage space. Attach site photographs, measurements and access details. This guidance does not establish provider availability. If no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer support can receive a quotation enquiry without promising a price or provider.


A useful supply request specifies units, measurements, grades and delivery phases rather than just a product name. Some source categories are currently restricted; listing them here does not establish an active supplier. If no company is displayed, customers can submit the bill of materials, photos and unloading location through Biet Al Reef customer support's quotation request form, without any promise of availability or price.

## Construction and finishing supplies

### 1. Cement supply

For cement delivery to an Al Shamkha project, identify whether it is intended for concrete, mortar or plaster and use the designer's specification to select the appropriate type. Provide tonnage or bag count, the planned pour or work date, and dry storage arrangements; unloading conditions can affect the delivered cost. Ask for manufacturer, cement type, bag weight, production date, freight and return terms on the quote. Match the delivery documents to the order and reject bags showing moisture damage or hard lumps.

### 2. Sand supply

A request for a truckload of sand is not precise enough: fill material and sand for mortar or plaster have different grading and cleanliness requirements. Provide estimated cubic metres or a verified weight, plus photographs showing vehicle access and the unloading area. Compare source, measurement method, transport and discharge charges. Inspect the sample for debris and unwanted clumps; where sand forms part of a structural mix, the qualified designer must specify testing and acceptance criteria.

### 3. Aggregate supply

For concrete or site layers, give the nominal aggregate size, grading and quantity specified by the mix design or work specification. A photograph of the unloading boundary helps prevent contamination with soil or other materials. Compare quarry source, test evidence where required, net weight and haulage. At receipt, inspect apparent grading and cleanliness and reconcile the weighbridge ticket before accepting later loads.

### 4. Concrete block supply

State whether blocks are for an external wall or an internal partition, and attach drawings showing thickness, dimensions, quantities and openings. The quote should identify block type, size and specified performance, pieces per pallet, acceptable breakage and any lifting charges. Check samples from each batch for dimensions, edges and major fractures. Do not substitute a different block specification without the designer's approval.

### 5. Reinforcing steel supply

Order reinforcement from approved structural drawings or a bar-bending schedule showing diameters, grades, lengths and quantities. Compare more than the price per tonne: require mill certificates, batch traceability, cutting and bending services, loading and delivery lead time. On arrival, reconcile bar markings, counts, weight and dispatch notes. Segregate damaged bars or those with significant flaking corrosion for assessment by the responsible engineer.

### 6. Ready-mix concrete supply

Before booking ready-mix, provide pour volume in cubic metres, specified strength grade, pump location, truck access, start time and expected placing rate. Separate concrete, pumping, waiting time, admixtures and testing in the quotation, including sampling responsibilities. During the pour, check each truck ticket for the ordered mix and batching time, and follow the project engineer's acceptance procedure. Do not add water on site without approval.

### 7. Project building-material packages

Split a combined materials request into structural works, MEP installations, finishes and consumables. Record each item's unit, quantity, grade, approved alternatives and required date, and attach storage and vehicle-access photographs. Compare suppliers against the same bill of quantities, including freight, lifting, wastage and returns. Reconcile partial receipts and batch references instead of relying on one aggregate invoice.

### 8. Gypsum materials supply

Before ordering gypsum board, specify whether it is for a suspended ceiling, partition or wall lining. Provide measured areas, ceiling heights and locations of air-conditioning and lighting openings. Standard, moisture-resistant and fire-rated assemblies are not interchangeable; board colour alone is not proof of a rating. Ask for a schedule of board type, thickness and dimensions, plus framing, screws, tape and joint compound if included. Compare the installed-area quantity, cutting waste, transport and lifting rather than board price alone. At delivery check flatness, undamaged edges and protection from moisture before signing the receipt.

### 9. Paint materials supply

Prepare separate paint schedules for ceilings, walls, façades and metalwork, including measured areas, substrate conditions and photographs of peeling or damp patches. Request a compatible system covering repairs, primer, filler and finishing coats, with colour references, sheen and manufacturer-stated coverage. Compare quotations using estimated litres adjusted for substrate absorption and application loss, not simply the price per can. On receipt verify colour codes, batch numbers, shelf life and intact seals. Keep a documented quantity from the same batch for later touch-ups.

### 10. Waterproofing materials supply

Start waterproofing-material procurement by identifying the purpose: waterproofing a roof, bathroom or tank, or providing thermal insulation. These require different systems. Supply measured areas, substrate details and photographs of drains, corners, joints and upstands. Request a complete system sheet listing primer, main layer, reinforcement, protection, application thickness and substrate preparation, and check component compatibility. Compare measurable coverage quantities and require storage instructions and batch identification. Inspect packages and dates at delivery; accepting materials does not replace testing the installed waterproofing system.

### 11. Floor finishes supply

For flooring supply, submit room plans with net areas, laying direction, door openings and transitions between finishes. Identify ceramic, porcelain, vinyl or timber-based flooring, with dimensions, thickness, colour and slip-resistance needs appropriate to use. Ask about trims, skirting, adhesive, joints and spare pieces, and calculate cutting waste from the actual layout rather than assuming a universal percentage. Compare physical samples under site lighting. At receipt reconcile batch, grade, calibre and box counts so later installation batches do not vary unexpectedly in shade or size.

### 12. Sanitary fixtures supply

Prepare a fixture schedule for each bathroom before ordering: toilets, basins, mixers, showers and accessories, together with room dimensions, water and waste connection positions and photographs of existing services. Similar-looking models may have different outlet centres, fixing arrangements or operating parts. Request dimensional drawings, model codes and a list of included valves, traps and mounting kits. Compare warranties, replacement-part availability and delivery charges. Inspect ceramic, glass, finishes and small components individually and record shortages before signing the delivery note.

### 13. Electrical installation supplies

Electrical supplies should come from circuit drawings and load schedules reviewed by the responsible qualified professional, not from a generic shopping list. Specify cable lengths, conductor cross-sections, insulation types, breakers, boxes, conduits and their intended locations. Quotations should identify manufacturer, model, electrical ratings and project-required conformity evidence, distinguishing proposed substitutions from specified items. Compare price per metre or item alongside reels, accessories and freight. On receipt check printed markings, sizes, counts and batch references; a qualified electrician or engineer must assess substitutions, installation and testing.

### 14. Plumbing installation supplies

Separate cold-water, hot-water, drainage and irrigation systems into distinct plumbing bills of materials. Record each pipe material, diameter, length, pressure and temperature rating, and jointing method. Attach drawings showing elbows, valves, transitions and maintenance access: missing small fittings can stop work even when all pipes arrive. Compare suppliers on full-system compatibility, product evidence, joining accessories, freight and returns, not only price per metre. At receipt check fitting diameters, manufacturer markings and undamaged ends, and segregate mismatches before the installed network is pressure-tested.

### 15. Building-material retail

For counter purchases of building materials, separate items sold by piece from those priced by weight or volume. Bring a drawing-based shopping list or bill of quantities showing dimensions, grade and amount. Ask the merchant to confirm the available batch and quotation validity, and identify who pays for loading, transport and breakage. A visually similar substitute may not have the same performance: request its technical sheet and the responsible professional's approval where necessary. Reconcile each invoice line with what was received and record shortages or damage under the written sales terms.

### 16. Sanitary ware retail

Before buying a toilet, basin or mixer from a sanitary ware shop, measure room width, water inlet heights, waste outlet centres and clearances to walls and doors. Ask for the model number, dimension drawing and a packing list: a mixer may exclude isolation valves and hoses, while a basin may exclude its trap. Compare warranties on bodies, finishes and working components as well as replacement-part availability. Open cartons at receipt and document cracks, scratches or missing fittings before installation starts.

### 17. Electrical supplies retail

When buying switches, sockets, breakers or cable at retail, take photographs of the existing item's rating label and the circuit drawing if available. Record voltage, current, conductor size and intended application. Similar appearance does not establish electrical equivalence; a qualified electrician must check protection ratings and connection compatibility. Request manufacturer, model and project-relevant conformity documentation, and compare warranty and return terms for unused items. Inspect packaging, markings and model references before handing materials to the installer; do not improvise live tests.

### 18. Plumbing supplies retail

Buy pipes and plumbing fittings against a schedule of nominal diameters, material types and joining methods—heat fusion, solvent welding, threading or press connections—as appropriate to the water or drainage system. A photograph of an old fitting may conceal different sizing standards or pressure ratings. Include compatible adapters, seals, valves and joining materials in the quotation; compare actual quantities, spares and returns. Check diameters, markings and counts against the invoice. Pressure and leak testing remains part of the installer's commissioning work.

### 19. Paint retail

At a paint counter, provide substrate photographs and say whether the finish is for an interior wall, exterior façade or metal surface, and whether dampness is present. Supply the colour code, sheen and measured area in square metres. Request a documented system of primer, filler and topcoats, including coverage, coat count and application conditions. Compare the cost of the entire coating system, not just the final paint tin, and confirm the colour can be reproduced. Check seals, manufacturing dates, colour references and batch numbers; retain a labelled sample for future repairs.

### 20. Waterproofing products retail

Buying a waterproofing product alone does not ensure a watertight assembly. Tell the supplier whether the job concerns a roof, bathroom, buried wall or tank, and provide measured area, substrate condition and photographs of drains and joints. Ask for compatible primer, membrane or coating and protective layer specifications, consumption per square metre, applied thickness and curing period. Compare full-system quantities, accessories, storage conditions and return terms. Inspect packaging and dates on delivery; installed-system flood or integrity testing belongs to the contractor and supervisor.

### 21. Site materials delivery

For delivery to an Al Shamkha project, list load weights, dimensions and pallet counts, the usable access gate, unloading point, receiving hours and site contact. The quotation must distinguish kerbside drop-off from unloading to ground level or lifting to an upper floor, and cover waiting time, extra journeys and damage responsibility. Request an itemised dispatch note and, where practical, load photographs before departure and at arrival. Count packages and record visible damage, shortages and delivery time before signing final receipt.

### 22. MDF board supply

For MDF supply for cabinets or shelving, provide a cutting schedule with finished-part dimensions and required board thickness. State whether the installation will encounter steam or occasional moisture. Standard MDF must not be represented as waterproof: request the manufacturer's board grade, face specification and intended-use information. Compare suppliers on full-sheet yield, cutting, edge banding, offcuts and transport rather than sheet price alone. On delivery, check flatness, edges and corners for swelling or damage, and reconcile thicknesses and quantities with the cutting list before signing.

### 23. Natural timber supply

Natural timber for interior doors is not specified in the same way as timber for structural members or exterior joinery. Provide the intended component, cross-section, lengths, quantities and finish, and request an identified timber species or an agreed alternative rather than a generic 'solid wood' label. For precision joinery, ask about drying, moisture and storage; for exposed work, ask what treatment suits the exposure. Compare usable yield after defects and cutting losses, grading, machining and delivery. Inspect pieces for bowing, twisting, splits and knots at critical fixing points, recording rejected stock before workshop processing.

### 24. Plywood supply

For plywood used in furniture, partitions or temporary formwork, identify the application first: face grade, adhesive bond and moisture exposure differ between products. Supply sheet dimensions, thickness, quantities and cutting or load details, and request the manufacturer's grade rather than relying on a trade name. Check face quality for visible work; for load-bearing uses, have the designer or contractor confirm suitability. Compare like-for-like grade, thickness, usable sheet area, cutting and freight. Inspect exposed edges for voids or delamination, sample thickness and flatness, and ensure sheets have been protected from wet storage.

### 25. Melamine board supply

Melamine-faced boards for a wardrobe cannot be ordered by colour alone. Define the core material and thickness, décor reference, surface texture and grain direction, then provide a cut list identifying visible edges. Ask for matching edge banding, its thickness and application method, and check batch consistency where cabinet fronts sit side by side. Compare whole-board supply against cut-and-edged packages, including offcuts, drilling, packing and transport. Inspect under consistent lighting for scratches or shade differences, and verify grain orientation and cut dimensions before accepting fabricated parts.

### 26. HPL sheet supply

Before ordering high-pressure laminate, state whether it will cover a door, worktop or wall: sheet type, thickness and bonding method depend on the substrate and application. Provide dimensions, openings, edge details and the design code, and request manufacturer instructions for adhesive, pressing, conditioning and edge protection. Compare the full assembly cost, including substrate, preparation and installation, and clarify responsibility for chipped edges or bond failures. Check décor codes, thickness and colour batches at receipt; inspect faces and corners before cutting or bonding.

### 27. Joinery fittings supply

For hinges, drawer runners and handles, issue a hardware schedule showing door thickness, swing direction and approximate weight, plus drawer dimensions, loads and mounting method. A fitting that resembles an old part may have different hole centres, opening angles or load ratings. Request exact model numbers and installation drawings, and clarify whether screws, mounting plates and soft-close components are included. Compare complete-set prices, warranty and replacement availability. Trial-fit a sample on one cabinet and check travel, stopping action and alignment before bulk installation.

### 28. Board cutting and preparation

Board cutting requires a fabrication drawing, not an unlabelled list of dimensions. Number each component and specify material, thickness, length, width, grain direction, edges requiring banding, cutting tolerance and any drilling included. Approve the sheet nesting plan and agree who bears waste or recutting costs if dimensions are wrong; clarify packing, labelling and delivery. Compare workshop offers on equipment accuracy, edge finish and cost per finished part, not merely cutting fees. Receive labelled, sorted components, sample-check each dimension and inspect squareness and edge quality before assembly.

## Natural stone and granite

### 29. Natural marble supply

Before ordering natural marble for floors, stairs or cladding, identify the use, measured surface areas, openings and required thickness. Request photographs of the actual slabs rather than relying on a showroom swatch: natural veining and porosity vary, and book-matched patterns need an agreed slab layout before cutting. Compare quotations by stone identification, grade, thickness, finish, cutting allowance, packing, transport and unloading. Ask which surface treatment is suitable for the intended use instead of judging gloss alone. At delivery inspect each slab in good light for cracks, repairs and damaged edges, check dimensions against the schedule, and record rejected pieces before fabrication starts.

### 30. Granite supply

For granite procurement, first establish whether the stone will serve a kitchen worktop, stair or exterior paving: finish, thickness, cleaning needs and slip suitability depend on the application. Provide area drawings, runs, openings and skirting requirements. Ask for a sample from the proposed batch and photographs of the actual slabs, acknowledging natural grain and colour variation. Compare suppliers on material origin and grade, slab dimensions, edge preparation, packing, cutting and delivery rather than price per square metre alone; stain resistance should not be assumed identical across varieties. On receipt measure sample slabs, inspect corners and cracks, and match finish and colour range to the approved sample before fabrication.

### 31. Custom-size marble cutting

Custom marble cutting requires an approved fabrication schedule identifying each piece, finished length and width, thickness, edge profile, apertures and vein direction. Arrange a site template for out-of-square corners and agree dimensional tolerances, labelling and protective packing before cutting. Compare workshop bids with stone supply, cutting, edge profiling, polishing, drilling, transport and responsibility for fabrication errors shown separately. At handover check labelled pieces against the schedule, measure critical dimensions and diagonals, and inspect aperture corners and finished edges for hairline fractures before accepting them for installation.

### 32. Marble polishing

When marble looks dull or scratched, establish the stone type, extent and depth of damage and whether protective treatments were previously applied. Request a small trial in an inconspicuous location: grinding removes stone and cannot remedy every deep stain or crack. The quotation should define grinding and polishing stages, protection of adjacent finishes, water and dust control, and whether joint filling or sealing is included. Compare the proposed outcome rather than simply the number of machine passes. Inspect the completed surface under raking light for swirl marks, uneven sheen or lowered edges, and record defects needing separate repairs.

### 33. Marble floor laying

Marble floor installation begins with checking substrate flatness, doorway and drain levels, stone thickness and measured area, followed by approval of slab layout, vein direction and joint positions. The quote should identify substrate preparation, the appropriate bedding system, treatment at thresholds and walls, any required waterproofing and responsibility for waste and final finishing. Existing structural movement joints must be respected rather than hidden for appearance. During handover check row alignment, edge lippage, tile stability and joint finish; verify falls in wet areas before final polishing or sealing.

### 34. Wall and façade marble installation

Wall and façade marble installation involves more than selecting a colour: panel weight, wall height, substrate, openings and weather exposure govern the fixing approach. Some projects require engineered mechanical anchorage and review by a qualified specialist; a generic adhesive is not a substitute for assessing loads and supports. Supply elevations, levels, panel sizes and opening locations, and request shop details showing brackets, movement joints, water management and future panel replacement. Compare bids by fixing materials, design documentation, lifting method, protection and inspections. At handover examine alignment, secure support, corners and joints rather than relying on appearance in photographs.

### 35. Marble restoration

Before restoring damaged marble, distinguish absorbed stains, acid etching, cracks, loose pieces and worn edges; these do not share one treatment. Photograph each defect with a scale, record the stone type and cleaning products used, and ask for diagnosis of surface damage versus moisture or substrate failure. Compare specialist cleaning, crack filling, edge repair and panel replacement, including compatibility and colour matching of repair compounds. Approve a limited trial before full treatment. On completion check stability, flush repairs and the closest achievable visual match, and document imperfections that cannot be concealed without replacement.

## Kitchen surfaces and basins

### 36. Fabricate kitchen worktops

Fabricate a kitchen worktop only after the base cabinets have been installed, levelled and checked, rather than relying on the preliminary drawing. Record every run length, cabinet depth, out-of-square wall and overhang, and photograph the sink, hob and socket positions. Specify whether the material is natural stone, engineered quartz or porcelain, together with slab thickness, edge finish, seam positions and vein direction. The quotation should separate templating, cutting, appliance openings, overhang support, transport and lifting, and allocate responsibility for breakage before installation. Approve the cutting layout and an actual material sample first. On delivery check dimensions, edge quality, straight cuts and joint positions; sharp internal cut-out corners may create avoidable cracking risks.

### 37. Install kitchen worktops

Installing a worktop is distinct from fabricating it. Start by checking that cabinets are level, secure and adequately supported around openings and overhangs. Supply photos of the delivery route, stairs or lift, door widths, appliance clearances and tap positions. Compare installation quotations for manpower, handling, adhesives, joint treatment, sealing at sinks and walls, and responsibility for correcting uneven cabinets. Dry-fit sections and appliances before permanent bonding. At handover check that the surface does not rock, adjoining sections sit at consistent heights, seams are aligned and edges are undamaged. Test seals carefully in wet areas without flooding the cabinetry.

### 38. Fabricate marble vanities

Before fabricating a marble vanity, distinguish a top supporting a purchased basin from an integrated basin or carved drainage channel; each has different construction and maintenance implications. Provide the unit width, finished height, mirror and mixer positions, waste location, and photos of the wall and supporting cabinet. Request a stone sample showing veining, absorption characteristics and edge finish, plus a drawing of thickness, service access, drainage falls and basin fixing. Compare cutting, assembly, reinforcement, sealing, delivery and installation as identifiable quotation items. At handover check basin stability, smooth edges, access to the trap and absence of standing water around the mixer or corners. Carry out a leak test before enclosing any plumbing.

### 39. Cut sink and appliance apertures

Sink, hob and tap apertures must follow the actual appliance model and its installation instructions, not dimensions inferred from a product photograph. Supply model numbers and manufacturer templates, cabinet layout, rear upstand position and slab thickness. Check remaining material between openings, edges and seams, hob ventilation or heat-clearance requirements, and under-mount sink support; some slabs require additional reinforcement around internal corners. A quotation should identify each cut-out, cutting and finishing method, edge treatment and breakage responsibility. Dry-fit the appliance or verify against its template before final fixing, ensuring clips, pipework and cabinet structure do not conflict.

### 40. Quartz surface installation

Engineered quartz products differ in performance; request the exact product identity, thickness specification, heat limits, cleaning guidance and warranty information. Measure runs, seams, sink and hob positions and overhangs, and photograph the access route because long sections may need dividing. Compare quotations on slab provenance, batch consistency, pattern direction, edge fabrication, support and jointing material rather than price per metre alone. Check manufacturer requirements for expansion allowances, heat exposure and allowable overhangs before approving the layout. At handover inspect colour matching, level seating, secure support, smooth seams and absence of scratches. Obtain care instructions, including avoiding direct contact with very hot cookware.

### 41. Porcelain slab worktop installation

Large-format porcelain worktop slabs require more careful handling and cut-out planning than ordinary tiles because edges and internal corners can be vulnerable during fabrication and transport. Record worktop length, depth, corner radii, sink and hob positions, slab thickness and the support system specified by the manufacturer. Request a slab layout showing pattern direction, photographs of the delivery route and lift or door clearances, and the proposed cutting and handling equipment. Compare slab supply, cutting, mitred edges, reinforcement, lifting, installation and agreed wastage separately. Inspect fabricated sections for chipped corners before fixing, then check levelness, supported overhangs, sound joints and fine cracks around appliance openings.

### 42. Worktop repair and polishing

Identify the surface material before selecting polishing or repair: natural marble, engineered quartz and porcelain respond differently to abrasives and chemicals. Photograph scratches, cracks or open seams with a scale, noting moisture or heat exposure and when the defect appeared. Ask the specialist to determine whether the problem is superficial or affects the slab or its support, and to trial a discreet treatment where practical. Compare specialist cleaning, resealing joints, edge repair and full section replacement, including the possibility of a visible colour difference. At handover check seam integrity, slab stability and water resistance. Reject abrasive polishing contrary to manufacturer guidance or a cosmetic patch that merely conceals a structural crack.

## Ceramic and porcelain tiles

### 43. Ceramic tile supply

Start ceramic tile supply by identifying the floor or wall area, tile dimensions, absorption characteristics and intended use, rather than selecting colour alone. Provide a layout and substrate photos, and specify size, texture, shade, batch reference and the product's stated use classification. Calculate quantities from the laying pattern with a realistic cutting allowance and request physical samples and information on replacement batches. Compare quotations by actual square metres or boxes, coverage per box, grade, delivery, unloading and returns. At receipt match product codes and batches, open samples from different boxes to check colour, dimensions and intact edges, and store cartons dry away from site traffic.

### 44. Porcelain tile supply

Porcelain is not interchangeable with every ceramic tile. State whether it is for an interior floor, bathroom or exterior area, and whether its slip performance, texture and finish suit the location. Give the supplier dimensions, coverage, laying pattern and cut-outs; request product data for thickness, absorption, wear characteristics and fixing requirements for the selected format. Large slabs also need delivery and handling checks. Compare offers by box price, actual coverage, grade, production batch, delivery terms and breakage policy. On receipt check slab straightness, consistent shade and batch numbers; a catalogue photograph is not a substitute for an approved physical sample.

### 45. Outdoor paving tile supply

Outdoor paving tiles should be selected for expected loading, drainage and exposure to sun and water, not appearance alone. Supply site photos, pathway dimensions, doorway levels and drain locations, and explain whether the product will be laid over concrete or a paving build-up. Request slip-resistance information, thickness, intended traffic suitability and the manufacturer's fixing recommendations, including matching edge pieces. Compare supply quotations on net coverage, grade, special cuts, transport, unloading and agreed wastage. Inspect delivered batches for edge damage, shade variation, warping and cracks, and retain an approved reference sample before installation.

### 46. Tile cutting and preparation

Tile cutting and preparation may be a separate service when corners, pipe penetrations, steps and skirtings require precise fabrication. Provide the laying plan, opening dimensions, drain positions and photographs of corners, together with tile material and thickness so the workshop can select suitable equipment. Compare quotations by straight and mitred cuts, holes, edge finishing, rework responsibility and who supplies spare tiles. Verify final dimensions on site after levelling layers are completed rather than cutting from early drawings. At handover check piece dimensions, straight and smooth edges and the absence of fine cracks; label location-specific pieces to prevent mix-ups during installation.

### 47. Ceramic tile installation

Before laying ceramic tiles, check substrate soundness, flatness, dryness and waterproofing where needed; adhesive should not be used to disguise substantial level defects. Measure floor or wall coverage, drain and doorway positions, laying direction, joint width and cut locations. The quotation should separate substrate preparation, adhesive, grout, trims, waterproofing if included and removal of existing tiles. Approve a sample layout for complex patterns. At handover inspect straight courses, secure bonding, absence of hollow or proud tiles, and correct falls towards drains in wet areas before the floor enters service.

### 48. Porcelain tile installation

Porcelain installation needs an adhesive and application method suited to its low absorption and tile format; large slabs demand particularly careful substrate preparation and handling. Provide area drawings, tile sizes, cut-outs and photos showing levels and transitions. Compare installers on levelling, product-compatible materials, adhesive coverage, joints, movement accommodation, cutting and handover rather than labour price alone. Check manufacturer instructions for joint widths and curing time before foot traffic or loading. On completion inspect flatness, consistent joints, stable tiles and the absence of corner cracks or significant voids beneath the finish.

### 49. Skirting supply and installation

Skirtings protect the wall-to-floor junction and conceal the edge, but ceramic, porcelain, stone, timber and composite versions require different details. Calculate net linear metres excluding door openings, and record skirting height, edge profile, internal and external corners and transitions at cabinets and thresholds. Compare quotations by linear metre, cutting, edge finishing, adhesive, joint treatment and transport, with responsibility for uneven walls made explicit. Approve a shade and batch that suit the flooring, and do not assume all materials tolerate the same cleaning methods. At handover inspect a straight top line, neat corners, secure fixing and the absence of sharp exposed edges.

## Material procurement request

Send an itemised list with product, unit, grade, size, quantity and justified cutting allowance; attach sample images or manufacturer codes, unloading location, gate clearance and batch dates. Clarify whether quoted terms include applicable tax, testing, packing, transport, unloading and returns, and agree substitutes in writing. Where no shop or supplier is listed, Biet Al Reef customer support can collect a pricing request without promising stock.

**Common question:** Is a tile photo sufficient? No; add size, grade, batch reference, product code and box quantity for consistent colour and quantities.
