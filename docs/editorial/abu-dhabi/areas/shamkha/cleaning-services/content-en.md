# Al Shamkha — Cleaning, disinfection and pest management

## Al Shamkha enquiry context
Washing shades and external surfaces in Al Shamkha calls for drainage planning and protection of lighting and delicate finishes. Attach site photographs, measurements and access details. If no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer support can receive a quotation enquiry without promising availability.


Cleaning an occupied apartment is not the same scope as post-construction cleaning or operating a whole building. Document surfaces, soil, dimensions, access hours and chemical or occupational risks. Service descriptions do not imply a listed provider or available crew. Where no company appears, Biet Al Reef customer support and its quotation form can receive photographs and requirements without guaranteeing outcome or pricing; specialist providers must meet applicable competency and safety requirements.

## Home and building cleaning

### 1. Villa cleaning

For a villa-cleaning quotation, provide an approximate floor-by-floor plan, occupied and unused spaces, the number of bathrooms, kitchens and majlis rooms, and whether high glazing, outbuildings and the courtyard are included. Photograph stubborn soil and delicate stone or timber finishes so methods can be selected safely. Ask bidders to allocate crew time by room and specify chemicals, equipment, furniture moving and drying duties. Inspect door edges, bathroom drains and high shelves at handover rather than judging the entrance alone.

### 2. Apartment cleaning

An apartment cleaned before move-in needs a different scope from a routine visit. State the room count, kitchen layout and whether cupboards and appliances must be cleaned internally. Photograph extractor grease and floor stains, and identify lift bookings or building access windows. Compare included tasks and revisit arrangements, not merely apartment size. At completion check window tracks, tile edges, behind appliances that were authorised to be moved, and the dryness of wet areas.

### 3. Office cleaning

For an operating office, inventory workstations, meeting rooms, pantry, washrooms and floor types, then agree low-disruption service hours. Set explicit no-touch zones for documents and personal devices, and specify compatible products for screens and finishes. Compare bids on waste collection, consumable replenishment and any separately defined disinfection. Accept the work against a zone-by-zone task log and staff observations, rather than attendance alone.

### 4. Building cleaning

Whole-building cleaning may cover corridors, lifts, stairwells, entrances and authorised plant areas, while private units remain outside scope. Provide floor counts, measured common areas, photographs of problem points and available water, power and storage. Compare cleaning frequency, supplies, supervision and complaint escalation. Inspect handrails, lift buttons, corners and refuse areas, and request a dated visit record for each floor.

### 5. Post-construction cleaning

Final cleaning should follow cutting and painting, since plaster dust, grout haze and adhesive residue require different techniques. Photograph new finishes and identify tile, glazing, stone and paint types, plus areas still controlled by contractors. Require a trial on an inconspicuous patch before removing persistent marks, and price bulk debris removal separately from fine-dust work. After surfaces dry, inspect frames, joints and vents for residue, scratches and chemical marks.

### 6. Pre-move cleaning

For pre-move cleaning, confirm the furniture delivery date, available utilities and whether cupboards, drawers and appliances remain in place. An empty property differs from one containing curtains or upholstery; supply photographs of kitchens, bathrooms and any damp or odour issues. Compare cabinet interiors, drying time and waste-removal responsibility. Before handing over the keys, check storage shelves, corners and plumbing areas, allowing sufficient ventilation time.

### 7. Regular cleaning contracts

A recurring cleaning contract should distinguish daily, weekly and monthly duties from occasional deep cleans. Provide site areas, permitted access hours and a task-frequency schedule. Specify who supplies chemicals, consumables and equipment, how absent staff are covered and how extra work is authorised. Compare supervision, inspection records and complaint response, not headcount alone. Use recurring checklists, photographic defect notes and a rectification log before approving invoices.

## Upholstery and textile cleaning

### 8. Steam sofa cleaning

Start with photographs of seats, backs and armrests, the number of pieces and the upholstery care labels. Identify oily marks, ink, recurring odours and any previous cleaning. Not every fabric tolerates hot steam: request a hidden colourfastness test and an explanation of moisture control, extraction and ventilation. Compare whether removable cushions, targeted stain work and moving the furniture are included. Inspect only after the fabric has dried, looking for tide marks, colour variation and lingering damp smells; old stains may remain.

### 9. Rug cleaning

List each rug's length and width, weave or fibre where known, and photographs of the backing, edges, stains and colour changes. Handwoven or dyed rugs may require colourfastness checks and methods different from synthetic rugs. Establish whether work happens on site or at a cleaning facility, how rugs are dried, and how they are wrapped for return. Compare area-based charges, edge treatment, transport and turnaround. Lay each rug flat at handover to check edge alignment, dye transfer and residual moisture within the pile.

### 10. Fitted carpet cleaning

For wall-to-wall carpet, provide room areas, fibre information and photographs of traffic lanes and entrance stains. Identify furniture to be moved and who will put it back. Discuss the risk of moisture reaching adhesive or the subfloor and agree a suitable extraction method and ventilation plan. Quotations should distinguish preliminary dry vacuuming, spot treatment, total area treated and the period before foot traffic resumes. Once dry, inspect stain locations, odours and carpet edges, including areas beneath furniture.

### 11. Arabic majlis cleaning

Measure the lengths of majlis seating, count loose cushions and backrests, and identify upholstery types. Photograph heavily used corners and visible marks. Establish whether covers can be removed or cleaning must be performed in place, and how adjacent timber flooring or rugs will be protected. Compare bids by actual pieces and surfaces treated, cushion-filling precautions and drying time rather than linear metres alone. Inspect seat and back cushions separately once dry for changes in texture and visible cleaning boundaries.

### 12. Mattress cleaning

Record mattress size, thickness and surface material, with photographs of stains and whether previous spills penetrated the interior. Excess water can damage foam layers, so request the proposed vacuuming, targeted stain treatment, moisture control and drying method for each side. Compare whether edges and both faces are included and how long the bed must remain unused. Do not put the mattress back into service until dry and free from trapped damp odours. Cleaning alone does not prove removal of allergens or pests.

### 13. Curtain cleaning

Supply the drop and width of each curtain, number of layers, lining type, and photographs of care labels, tracks and soiled sections. Agree whether curtains will be cleaned hanging or removed for off-site treatment, and who handles dismantling, reinstallation, pressing and pleat adjustment. Fabric and lining determine the risk of shrinkage or colour loss, so compatibility testing matters. Compare transport, drying, turnaround and methods. After reinstallation, check the final drop, smooth movement and undamaged hooks or rings.

### 14. Upholstery disinfection

Do not treat 'upholstery disinfection' as a sufficient specification. Distinguish ordinary soil removal from a defined disinfection objective, and identify the textile and its use. Request the product name, manufacturer directions, compatibility with porous fabric, required contact time and precautions around children and pets. Pre-cleaning may be necessary; a quick spray or steam pass does not establish complete sterilisation. Compare documented procedures, treated areas and ventilation periods, and request an application record without accepting unsupported health guarantees.

## Façade and site cleaning

### 15. Glass façade cleaning

Glass façade cleaning should begin with a survey of height, accessible surface area, glass coatings, silicone joints and gaskets—not a choice of detergent. Supply photographs from ground level and another safe vantage point, noting entrance obstructions and occupied hours. Ask each bidder to specify authorised access equipment, exclusion zones below the work, water supply and whether frames, tracks and adhesive residues are included. Mineral deposits may need a trial patch before treatment. At handover inspect panes in oblique light for streaks or scratches and check that joints and adjacent finishes were not damaged.

### 16. Building exterior cleaning

Stone, composite aluminium and painted façades tolerate very different cleaning methods. Prepare a material-by-material area schedule, close-up photos of deposits and cracks, and a map of openings where wash water could enter. Require a discreet test patch, a suitable pressure setting, protection for plants and entrances, and a wash-water management plan. Compare bids by the actual stain treatment and access arrangements, not area alone. Check at handover for colour changes, lifted coating or disturbed cladding joints; record defects requiring repair rather than further cleaning.

### 17. Car park cleaning

For car park cleaning, map floors, entrances, oil patches, tyre marks, drains and fire-safety equipment. Provide circulation photos and agree which bays can be closed in phases. The quotation should distinguish dry sweeping, degreasing and floor washing, and explain pedestrian slip protection and how contaminated wash water will be handled. Compare the net area, reopening sequence and waste arrangements rather than a single headline rate. At completion inspect ramp dryness, unobstructed drains and any slippery detergent residue before traffic returns.

### 18. Industrial site cleaning

Industrial cleaning requires identification of the process and substances present before a crew enters. Ordinary oil, fine process dust and chemical residues are not interchangeable wastes. Ask the site manager for restricted-zone drawings, equipment isolation requirements, substance safety information and vehicle routes. Quotes must separate floors and walkways from machinery cleaning or specialist hazardous-waste handling by competent parties. Compare risk controls, protective equipment and waste documentation. Acceptance should include checking that contamination has not been spread to clean zones and closing out exceptions in a work log.

## Water tank and pool cleaning

### 19. Water tank cleaning

Before a water tank clean, identify whether the tank is elevated or underground, its capacity, hatch dimensions, isolation valves and alternative water supply during downtime. Photograph sediment, corrosion or damaged lining; confined-space entry must not be assumed safe without an appropriate risk assessment and rescue arrangements. Compare controlled draining, sediment removal, scrubbing, rinsing and protection of fittings, while specifying whether disinfection and water testing are separate. Obtain dated before-and-after photographs, inspect the hatch and float valve, and record the return-to-service procedure.

### 20. Water tank disinfection

Disinfecting a water tank does not replace physical sediment removal. State whether water is intended for drinking, the actual tank volume, lining material, reason for treatment and any earlier test results. The provider should document a suitable product, dose calculation, contact time, rinsing or neutralisation and user protection, subject to verified current requirements and product directions. Compare the plan for avoiding recontamination and any appropriate sampling. Do not accept a blanket 'safe to drink' claim without reliable test evidence where testing is required. Keep product, quantity, timing and recommissioning records.

### 21. Swimming pool cleaning

For pool cleaning, provide length, width, approximate average depth, lining type and photographs of the waterline, floor, staining and pump room. Distinguish routine in-service cleaning from recovery after prolonged closure; full draining should not be assumed. Compare debris removal, filter and strainer cleaning, wall and floor brushing, and responsibility for testing and adjusting water balance. Leak repairs and pump faults should be priced separately. At handover inspect grout lines and strainers, and obtain water-test readings and equipment notes rather than judging clarity alone.

## Pest management

### 22. Cockroach control

Cockroaches seen around kitchen drains may require a different investigation from insects inside dry storage cupboards. Photograph specimens or traces, note sighting times and moisture or food sources, and identify children or pets on site. Request an inspection of cracks, appliances and drains before treatment is selected. Compare species identification, bait or trap placement, any suitable pesticide, re-entry instructions and follow-up visits. Acceptance should be based on a treatment map and subsequent monitoring, not a promise of immediate eradication.

### 23. Ant control

When ants appear, photograph trails, entry points and food storage areas, noting whether activity is indoors or in the garden. Spraying one visible trail may conceal activity without resolving the source. Ask for an assessment of moisture, wall gaps and possible multiple colonies. Compare targeted baiting, monitoring and proofing against indiscriminate spraying, with safeguards for children and animals. Obtain instructions on cleaning around bait stations and a date for reassessment.

### 24. Rodent control

Rodent control starts with documenting droppings, gnaw marks and openings around doors or services without handling unknown waste directly. Supply a plan of stores, kitchens and pipe routes and flag exposed food, children and animals. Require entry-point proofing and attraction-source reduction alongside specialist-selected traps or secured bait stations. Compare revisit frequency, safe carcass disposal and mapped device locations. At handover inspect proofing work and monitoring results rather than the number of bait blocks placed.

### 25. Bed bug treatment

Bites alone do not establish a bed-bug infestation. Request inspection of mattress seams, bed frames and crevices, with clear specimen or trace photographs. State how many rooms are occupied and whether furniture or linens have moved; avoid shifting items between rooms without a containment plan. Compare inspection scope, suitability of heat or other treatments, laundering and room-preparation instructions, re-entry periods and follow-up visits. Handover requires a location-specific treatment record and later inspection, not a one-visit guarantee.

### 26. Scheduled pest contracts

A recurring pest-control contract is not automatically a monthly spraying schedule. Describe building use, food preparation, stores, drains, gardens and complaint history, then request a risk-based inspection plan. Compare justified visit frequency, monitoring points, non-chemical controls, urgent call-out terms and the site's own cleaning and proofing duties. Reports should identify pests observed, trends, recommendations and any products applied. Evaluate the contract by reduced sightings and corrected causes, not spray volume.

## Disinfection services

### 27. Home surface disinfection

Before requesting household disinfection, distinguish routine cleaning from a specific contamination concern; blanket room spraying without a reason is not a useful specification. Identify surfaces, delicate furniture, children, vulnerable occupants and pets, and list high-touch points. Ask for the product identity, label directions, wet contact time, ventilation and precautions around food and electricity. Compare pre-cleaning, application method and exclusions. Verify the treated-area log and safe re-entry instructions; reject unsupported claims of complete sterilisation or future infection prevention.

### 28. Commercial facility disinfection

For office and facility disinfection, map meeting rooms, reception points, shared controls, door handles and washrooms, while separating electronics from moisture-tolerant surfaces. Provide occupancy windows, ventilation arrangements and areas containing food or records. The scope should explain where cleaning is sufficient, where a suitable disinfectant is justified and who protects or moves equipment. Compare product records, contact time and damage-reporting procedures. Accept a dated area checklist and re-entry guidance, not an absolute microbe-elimination claim.

## Building operation and facilities management

### 29. Maintenance management

Maintenance management coordinates requests, contractors and budgets; it does not mean that one team performs every repair. Provide a building and asset register, repeat-fault history, approval limits and emergency escalation rules. Compare how providers triage tickets, review contractor quotations, retain warranties and measure repeat failures. Request a sample report linking each ticket to before-and-after photographs, cost and closure authorisation. Assess overdue tickets and reasons, not simply the count marked closed.

### 30. Facility operations

Facility operations need defined operating hours and clear authority to shut down systems affecting occupants. Supply plant-room layouts, lift, AC, pump and lighting inventories, existing vendor contracts and meter locations. Compare shift coverage, readings, contractor coordination and advance occupant notices. Separate daily operations from capital repairs or security duties unless expressly included. Handover should provide periodic operating logs and an exception register with named owners and deadlines.

### 31. Preventive maintenance

Preventive maintenance starts with asset IDs, models, run hours and manufacturer guidance rather than a universal calendar. Provide fault logs, filter and consumable history and identify equipment that cannot be shut down. Compare equipment-specific checklists, visit frequency, post-service tests and included or excluded spares. Keep corrective repairs distinct from preventive inspections. Obtain before-and-after readings, outstanding recommendations and the next planned service date.

### 32. Electrical systems monitoring

Electrical monitoring is structured diagnosis, not permission to work on energised panels indiscriminately. Supply distribution drawings where available, board IDs, sensitive loads and histories of trips or overheating. Require a competent inspection plan covering isolation, permitted measurements and safe access. Compare load and temperature records, visual condition and defect priorities, with repairs priced separately. Receive a board-by-board report identifying readings, faults and urgent actions; an inspection alone does not correct defects.

### 33. AC systems monitoring

For AC monitoring, link complaints to rooms, individual units and operating periods rather than one temperature reading at the entrance. Provide an equipment and thermostat register, filter-service history and energy data where available. Request suitable supply/return measurements, airflow and drain checks, and alarm review within safe access limits. Compare reporting frequency and triggers for corrective technician visits. Handover should show trends, recurring complaints and corrective actions—not just 'AC operational'.

### 34. Plumbing and drainage monitoring

Plumbing and drainage monitoring seeks early leaks and blockages; it cannot guarantee that faults never occur. Supply water-point, pump, tank and drain layouts, moisture photographs and previous blockage dates. Compare valve inspections, meter trends, appropriate pressure checks and specified drain maintenance, while excluding demolition and repair unless separately agreed. Water near electrical equipment requires site safety controls before inspection. Obtain a location-based issue log with photographs, repair decisions and recheck dates.

### 35. Building condition reports

A building condition report needs a defined purpose: purchase due diligence, handover or maintenance prioritisation. Provide known building age, available drawings, dated photographs of cracks and leaks, their locations and previous repairs. Compare whether the survey is visual only or includes specialist tests and systems review, and who is qualified to assess structural concerns. Do not accept definitive structural safety claims based on superficial photos. Require numbered evidence, defect priorities, inspection limitations and referrals for specialist assessment.

## Workforce and operational support

### 36. Cleaning personnel provision

Cleaning staff provision should be scoped by tasks, not headcount alone. State floor area, washroom count, floor finishes, waste frequency and public opening hours, and identify who supplies chemicals and equipment. Compare productive hours, supervision, absence cover, product training and access to sensitive zones. Labour supply is distinct from an outcome-based full cleaning contract. Require shift attendance, completed-task checklists and quality observations.

### 37. Loading crew provision

For loading crews, provide an item schedule with weights and dimensions, pickup and drop-off points, and photographs of doors, stairs and lifts. Flag fragile items, sharp edges and loads requiring mechanical handling instead of manual lifting. Compare crew numbers, waiting charges, packing responsibility, protection of finishes and whether a truck is included. Do not permit loads beyond vehicle or handling capacity. Complete a count and damage check and verify access routes are clear.

### 38. Maintenance technician provision

When requesting maintenance technicians, specify the discipline, fault symptoms and location, with safe photographs of the appliance nameplate or installation. Distinguish diagnosis from repair, original versus alternative spares, and any power or water isolation needed. Compare relevant competence, post-repair testing, workmanship warranty and visit boundaries; one technician is not automatically qualified for every system. Obtain a fault diagnosis, parts record, measurements and final functional test.

### 39. Site supervisor provision

A site supervisor needs an agreed mandate before appointment. Provide the project plan, crew and subcontractor counts, programme milestones and key hazards, and specify who approves work, can stop unsafe activity and receives reports. Compare actual site attendance, relevant experience, inspection forms, material coordination and issue tracking, while separating supervision from design or statutory certification. Require illustrated daily reports and an instruction/defect register with accountable close-out owners.

### 40. Residential compound operations

Residential compound operations span several services and should not be sold as one vague line item. Provide building and unit counts, common areas, lifts, pumps and amenities, peak hours and resident complaint channels. Define cleaning, maintenance, incident response, vendor coordination and resident communications, excluding major repairs unless separately commissioned. Compare service levels, response targets and building-level reporting. Review open complaints, asset logs, costs and actual visit records at handover.

### 41. Commercial facility operations

Commercial facilities vary with tenant trading hours, visitor traffic, delivery areas and plant rooms. Prepare a tenant and shared-asset register, identify critical systems and maintenance windows that minimise disruption. Compare public-area cleaning, waste handling, incident coordination and tenant notices, and define each tenant's responsibility for private premises. Accept performance records for cleaning, faults and requests alongside an incident and corrective-action log.

### 42. Project support services

Project support can mean site setup, material movement, interim cleaning or store organisation, so turn the request into measurable tasks. Provide site photographs, access drawings, material quantities, crew interfaces, duration and handover points. Compare labour, equipment, consumables, waiting time and permit or principal-contractor coordination, excluding specialist trades unless expressly agreed. Sign off each task against a short record, before-and-after photographs and remaining-material inventory; staff presence alone is not completion.

## Cleaning or pest-management quotation brief

Describe site use, measured area, rooms/floors, surfaces, stains, close-up photographs, sensitive materials, access time and available water/power. Distinguish cleaning, stain removal, disinfection, pest treatment and preventive maintenance: they have different methods and outcomes. If no company is listed, Biet Al Reef customer support can collect details through the quotation form without guaranteeing a crew or result.

**Common question:** Does steam cleaning prove disinfection? No. Any claim depends on the validated process or product, contact time and surface, not the name alone.
