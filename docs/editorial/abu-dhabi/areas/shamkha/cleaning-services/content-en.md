## Home and building cleaning

### 1. Villa cleaning

For a villa-cleaning quotation, provide an approximate floor-by-floor plan, occupied and unused spaces, the number of bathrooms, kitchens and majlis rooms, and whether high glazing, outbuildings and the courtyard are included. Photograph stubborn soil and delicate stone or timber finishes so methods can be selected safely. Ask bidders to allocate crew time by room and specify chemicals, equipment, furniture moving and drying duties. Inspect door edges, bathroom drains and high shelves at handover rather than judging the entrance alone.

### 2. Apartment cleaning

An apartment cleaned before move-in needs a different scope from a routine visit. State the room count, kitchen layout and whether cupboards and appliances must be cleaned internally. Photograph extractor grease and floor stains, and identify lift bookings or building access windows. Compare included tasks and revisit arrangements, not merely apartment size. At completion check window tracks, tile edges, behind appliances that were authorised to be moved, and the dryness of wet areas.

### 3. Office cleaning

For an operating office, inventory workstations, meeting rooms, pantry, washrooms and floor types, then agree low-disruption service hours. Set explicit no-touch zones for documents and personal devices, and specify compatible products for screens and finishes. Compare bids on waste collection, consumable replenishment and any separately defined disinfection. Accept the work against a zone-by-zone task log and staff observations, rather than attendance alone.

### 4. Building cleaning

Whole-building cleaning may cover corridors, lifts, stairwells, entrances and authorised plant areas, while private units remain outside scope. Provide floor counts, measured common areas, photographs of problem points and available water, power and storage. Compare cleaning frequency, supplies, supervision and complaint escalation. Inspect handrails, lift buttons, corners and refuse areas, and request a dated visit record for each floor.

### 5. Post-construction cleaning

Final cleaning should follow cutting and painting, since plaster dust, grout haze and adhesive residue require different techniques. Photograph new finishes and identify tile, glazing, stone and paint types, plus areas still controlled by contractors. Require a trial on an inconspicuous patch before removing persistent marks, and price bulk debris removal separately from fine-dust work. After surfaces dry, inspect frames, joints and vents for residue, scratches and chemical marks.

### 6. Pre-move cleaning

For pre-move cleaning, confirm the furniture delivery date, available utilities and whether cupboards, drawers and appliances remain in place. An empty property differs from one containing curtains or upholstery; supply photographs of kitchens, bathrooms and any damp or odour issues. Compare cabinet interiors, drying time and waste-removal responsibility. Before handing over the keys, check storage shelves, corners and plumbing areas, allowing sufficient ventilation time.

### 7. Regular cleaning contracts

A recurring cleaning contract should distinguish daily, weekly and monthly duties from occasional deep cleans. Provide site areas, permitted access hours and a task-frequency schedule. Specify who supplies chemicals, consumables and equipment, how absent staff are covered and how extra work is authorised. Compare supervision, inspection records and complaint response, not headcount alone. Use recurring checklists, photographic defect notes and a rectification log before approving invoices.

## Upholstery and textile cleaning

### 8. Steam sofa cleaning

Start with photographs of seats, backs and armrests, the number of pieces and the upholstery care labels. Identify oily marks, ink, recurring odours and any previous cleaning. Not every fabric tolerates hot steam: request a hidden colourfastness test and an explanation of moisture control, extraction and ventilation. Compare whether removable cushions, targeted stain work and moving the furniture are included. Inspect only after the fabric has dried, looking for tide marks, colour variation and lingering damp smells; old stains may remain.

### 9. Rug cleaning

List each rug's length and width, weave or fibre where known, and photographs of the backing, edges, stains and colour changes. Handwoven or dyed rugs may require colourfastness checks and methods different from synthetic rugs. Establish whether work happens on site or at a cleaning facility, how rugs are dried, and how they are wrapped for return. Compare area-based charges, edge treatment, transport and turnaround. Lay each rug flat at handover to check edge alignment, dye transfer and residual moisture within the pile.

### 10. Fitted carpet cleaning

For wall-to-wall carpet, provide room areas, fibre information and photographs of traffic lanes and entrance stains. Identify furniture to be moved and who will put it back. Discuss the risk of moisture reaching adhesive or the subfloor and agree a suitable extraction method and ventilation plan. Quotations should distinguish preliminary dry vacuuming, spot treatment, total area treated and the period before foot traffic resumes. Once dry, inspect stain locations, odours and carpet edges, including areas beneath furniture.

### 11. Arabic majlis cleaning

Measure the lengths of majlis seating, count loose cushions and backrests, and identify upholstery types. Photograph heavily used corners and visible marks. Establish whether covers can be removed or cleaning must be performed in place, and how adjacent timber flooring or rugs will be protected. Compare bids by actual pieces and surfaces treated, cushion-filling precautions and drying time rather than linear metres alone. Inspect seat and back cushions separately once dry for changes in texture and visible cleaning boundaries.

### 12. Mattress cleaning

Record mattress size, thickness and surface material, with photographs of stains and whether previous spills penetrated the interior. Excess water can damage foam layers, so request the proposed vacuuming, targeted stain treatment, moisture control and drying method for each side. Compare whether edges and both faces are included and how long the bed must remain unused. Do not put the mattress back into service until dry and free from trapped damp odours. Cleaning alone does not prove removal of allergens or pests.

### 13. Curtain cleaning

Supply the drop and width of each curtain, number of layers, lining type, and photographs of care labels, tracks and soiled sections. Agree whether curtains will be cleaned hanging or removed for off-site treatment, and who handles dismantling, reinstallation, pressing and pleat adjustment. Fabric and lining determine the risk of shrinkage or colour loss, so compatibility testing matters. Compare transport, drying, turnaround and methods. After reinstallation, check the final drop, smooth movement and undamaged hooks or rings.

### 14. Upholstery disinfection

Do not treat 'upholstery disinfection' as a sufficient specification. Distinguish ordinary soil removal from a defined disinfection objective, and identify the textile and its use. Request the product name, manufacturer directions, compatibility with porous fabric, required contact time and precautions around children and pets. Pre-cleaning may be necessary; a quick spray or steam pass does not establish complete sterilisation. Compare documented procedures, treated areas and ventilation periods, and request an application record without accepting unsupported health guarantees.

## Façade and site cleaning

### 15. Glass façade cleaning

Glass façade cleaning should begin with a survey of height, accessible surface area, glass coatings, silicone joints and gaskets—not a choice of detergent. Supply photographs from ground level and another safe vantage point, noting entrance obstructions and occupied hours. Ask each bidder to specify authorised access equipment, exclusion zones below the work, water supply and whether frames, tracks and adhesive residues are included. Mineral deposits may need a trial patch before treatment. At handover inspect panes in oblique light for streaks or scratches and check that joints and adjacent finishes were not damaged.

### 16. Building exterior cleaning

Stone, composite aluminium and painted façades tolerate very different cleaning methods. Prepare a material-by-material area schedule, close-up photos of deposits and cracks, and a map of openings where wash water could enter. Require a discreet test patch, a suitable pressure setting, protection for plants and entrances, and a wash-water management plan. Compare bids by the actual stain treatment and access arrangements, not area alone. Check at handover for colour changes, lifted coating or disturbed cladding joints; record defects requiring repair rather than further cleaning.

### 17. Car park cleaning

For car park cleaning, map floors, entrances, oil patches, tyre marks, drains and fire-safety equipment. Provide circulation photos and agree which bays can be closed in phases. The quotation should distinguish dry sweeping, degreasing and floor washing, and explain pedestrian slip protection and how contaminated wash water will be handled. Compare the net area, reopening sequence and waste arrangements rather than a single headline rate. At completion inspect ramp dryness, unobstructed drains and any slippery detergent residue before traffic returns.

### 18. Industrial site cleaning

Industrial cleaning requires identification of the process and substances present before a crew enters. Ordinary oil, fine process dust and chemical residues are not interchangeable wastes. Ask the site manager for restricted-zone drawings, equipment isolation requirements, substance safety information and vehicle routes. Quotes must separate floors and walkways from machinery cleaning or specialist hazardous-waste handling by competent parties. Compare risk controls, protective equipment and waste documentation. Acceptance should include checking that contamination has not been spread to clean zones and closing out exceptions in a work log.

## Water tank and pool cleaning

### 19. Water tank cleaning

Before a water tank clean, identify whether the tank is elevated or underground, its capacity, hatch dimensions, isolation valves and alternative water supply during downtime. Photograph sediment, corrosion or damaged lining; confined-space entry must not be assumed safe without an appropriate risk assessment and rescue arrangements. Compare controlled draining, sediment removal, scrubbing, rinsing and protection of fittings, while specifying whether disinfection and water testing are separate. Obtain dated before-and-after photographs, inspect the hatch and float valve, and record the return-to-service procedure.

### 20. Water tank disinfection

Disinfecting a water tank does not replace physical sediment removal. State whether water is intended for drinking, the actual tank volume, lining material, reason for treatment and any earlier test results. The provider should document a suitable product, dose calculation, contact time, rinsing or neutralisation and user protection, subject to verified current requirements and product directions. Compare the plan for avoiding recontamination and any appropriate sampling. Do not accept a blanket 'safe to drink' claim without reliable test evidence where testing is required. Keep product, quantity, timing and recommissioning records.

### 21. Swimming pool cleaning

For pool cleaning, provide length, width, approximate average depth, lining type and photographs of the waterline, floor, staining and pump room. Distinguish routine in-service cleaning from recovery after prolonged closure; full draining should not be assumed. Compare debris removal, filter and strainer cleaning, wall and floor brushing, and responsibility for testing and adjusting water balance. Leak repairs and pump faults should be priced separately. At handover inspect grout lines and strainers, and obtain water-test readings and equipment notes rather than judging clarity alone.

## Pest management

### 22. Cockroach control

Map cockroach sightings, likely species, food, moisture and harbourage before treatment. Compare sanitation, sealing and suitable baiting by a competent provider; request follow-up rather than instant-eradication promises.

### 23. Ant control

Trace ant trails, entry points, food and moisture sources, indoor or outdoor. Compare colony identification, baiting and monitoring rather than blanket spraying; obtain prevention recommendations.

### 24. Rodent control

Photograph gnawing and droppings, identify entry points, food stores and children’s areas. Compare proofing, protected bait stations and sanitation; document monitoring positions.

### 25. Bed bug treatment

Map rooms and bed frames, and photograph specimens where possible; bites alone do not establish the pest. Compare inspection, repeat treatment and textiles handling by a qualified provider; agree follow-up checkpoints.

### 26. Scheduled pest contracts

Identify facility type, food handling, entry points, visits and past complaints. Compare preventive inspections, reporting and response commitments; require dated records, not just spray counts.

## Disinfection services

### 27. Home surface disinfection

Identify high-touch surfaces, occupants and soil condition before selecting a disinfectant. Compare cleaning first, surface compatibility, labelled dwell time and ventilation; check an itemised list without implying air sterilisation.

### 28. Commercial facility disinfection

Map shared rooms, contact surfaces, sensitive electronics and suitable closure periods. Compare product concentration, application, equipment protection and safety data; accept area logs, not absolute infection-prevention promises.

## Building operation and facilities management

### 29. Maintenance management

List properties, systems, incident history, priorities and contractor responsibility. Compare ticketing, escalation, approvals and billing; inspect open/closed work registers.

### 30. Facility operations

Map facilities, plant rooms, operating hours, owners, tenants and access. Compare monitoring, vendor coordination and utility reporting; inspect auditable daily logs.

### 31. Preventive maintenance

Inventory assets, age, manuals, risks and failures. Compare monthly/seasonal plans, consumables and inspection evidence; review recurring faults.

### 32. Electrical systems monitoring

Identify boards, feeders, critical loads and outage history, with qualified-access rules. Compare inspections, suitable thermal surveys and isolation procedures; require readings and exceptions per board.

### 33. AC systems monitoring

List AC assets, operating hours, setpoints and complaints by zone. Compare temperature/airflow checks, logs and alerts; obtain an actionable deviation list.

### 34. Plumbing and drainage monitoring

Map pumps, tanks, drains, wet rooms and leak or blockage history. Compare patrols, meter readings and drain checks; track outstanding leak items.

### 35. Building condition reports

Provide building age, photos of façades, roof, rooms, plant and reason for review. Compare scope, defect severity and supporting photographs; accept prioritised recommendations and limitations.

## Workforce and operational support

### 36. Cleaning personnel provision

Specify cleaning area, shifts, tasks, equipment and who supplies consumables. Compare crew count, supervision, absence cover and duties; inspect attendance and task logs.

### 37. Loading crew provision

Identify load dimensions/weight, stairs, transfer distance and truck access. Compare crew size, handling aids and safe methods; verify cleared unloading areas.

### 38. Maintenance technician provision

Specify technician discipline, fault complexity and defined scope of work. Compare competence, staffing, parts and required permissions; obtain job sheets and tests.

### 39. Site supervisor provision

Set site size, headcount, shifts, safety inspections and owner reporting. Compare supervisor duties, capability, coverage and stop-work authority; inspect site reports.

### 40. Residential compound operations

Schedule shared residential areas, assets, cleaning and maintenance and complaints. Compare shift management, subcontractors and emergencies; receive building-level reports.

### 41. Commercial facility operations

Map tenants, footfall, circulation, plant rooms, security and cleaning interface. Compare contractor coordination, tenant disruption and access hours; inspect incident and task logs.

### 42. Project support services

Specify support tasks such as moving, cleaning or setup, with clear boundaries. Compare crews, tools, duration and acceptance point; document exclusions and consumable responsibility.



## Cleaning or pest-management quotation brief

Describe site use, measured area, rooms/floors, surfaces, stains, close-up photographs, sensitive materials, access time and available water/power. Distinguish cleaning, stain removal, disinfection, pest treatment and preventive maintenance: they have different methods and outcomes. **Common question:** Does steam cleaning prove disinfection? No. Any claim depends on the validated process or product, contact time and surface, not the name alone.
