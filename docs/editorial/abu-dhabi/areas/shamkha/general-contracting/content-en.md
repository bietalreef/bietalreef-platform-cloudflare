## Building and villa construction
### Villa construction
For a villa project in Al Shamkha, first state the intended floor area, number of levels, rooms, majlis and parking spaces, and supply plot drawings, levels and utility connection information where available. 'Turnkey villa' is not a priced specification: request a bill of quantities separating excavation, foundations, frame, blockwork, waterproofing, HVAC, electrical systems and finishes, with explicit exclusions. Compare bids against identical drawing revisions, material performance or brands, procurement lead times, testing and supervision duties. At handover check approved-drawing compliance, test records, system operation, defects and warranties.

### Residential building construction
A multi-unit residential building cannot be estimated from gross floor area alone. Specify apartment numbers and types, circulation areas, stairs, lifts, tanks, service rooms and parking. Share floor plans, elevations and services layouts, and ask bidders to distinguish shared infrastructure from individual-unit fit-out. Compare who coordinates mechanical and electrical installations, applicable fire-system testing and utility connections. Handover should separately inspect each apartment and then commission common areas, with maintenance records and outstanding defects documented.

### Commercial building construction
For a commercial building, the intended use determines the required access, electrical loads, ventilation and delivery arrangements. Describe the proposed activities and anticipated occupancy before pricing the structure and façade. Clarify whether tenant interiors are included or the contract ends at the shell, base services and connection points. Compare equipment capacity, maintenance access and flexibility of future partitions rather than façade appearance alone. Request as-built drawings, test records and a clear defect-resolution responsibility before occupation.

### Building works execution
'Building works' might mean block partitions, openings, stairs or external site elements rather than an entire project. Supply drawings with wall lengths, heights, thicknesses and door or window openings, together with site photographs and access constraints. The quote should specify block and mortar types, any drawing-required reinforcement, scaffolding, transport and waste removal. Inspect alignment, levels, opening dimensions and material junctions before plastering; changes to load-bearing elements require designer review.

### Concrete works
Concrete works should be itemised by element—footing, column, beam, slab or stair—with dimensions, levels, reinforcement schedules and the design-specified concrete strength. Before pouring, inspect formwork, cover, service penetrations and hold points, and assign responsibility for sampling, testing and curing. Two quotations are not comparable if one excludes pumps, formwork or tests. After pouring, retain delivery and test records, document curing and inspect honeycombing, geometry and levels with the supervising engineer; structural defects cannot be signed off as cosmetic repairs.

### Structural frame construction
For a villa or building structural frame, separate excavation, foundations, columns, beams, slabs and stairs, with payment milestones tied to measurable inspected work. Provide structural drawings, any available ground investigation and existing utility information. Ask who supplies temporary supports, manages lifting safety and inspects reinforcement before each pour. Compare steel and concrete quantities, curing and testing scope, not just a lump-sum frame price. Before finishes begin, check inspection reports, test results, levels, openings and as-built records.

### Turnkey project delivery
'Turnkey' has no reliable scope unless the contract defines it. List rooms, areas and finish standards, and state whether design, permits, structure, HVAC, kitchens, cabinetry, external works, lighting and appliances are included. Require a schedule of specifications, approved samples, substitutions, exclusions and variation approval rules. Compare offers on deliverables and commissioning obligations rather than headline price alone. Handover includes operational tests, a snag list, keys, warranties, manuals and final drawings.

### New villa construction
For a new villa in Shamkha, begin with plot boundaries, finished levels, access and parking, then reconcile the accommodation brief with architectural, structural and building-services drawings. Provide available approved plans, floor areas, opening schedules and proposed locations for cooling plant and water tanks; plot area alone cannot establish design feasibility. Request separate prices for excavation, foundations, frame, waterproofing, finishes, external works and utility connections, with clear coordination and testing responsibilities. Compare quantities, specifications and exclusions against the same drawing revision. At handover collect inspection records, commissioning results, as-built drawings and a tracked defects list.

### Residential annex construction
A residential annex needs more definition than an extra room. State its use, dimensions, height, clearance from the existing building and site access, and photograph visible services. A qualified designer should assess foundation works near the house and the capacity and connection method for electrical, water, drainage and cooling systems. Request drawings of junctions, thermal and moisture barriers, floor levels and rainwater drainage. Quotations should distinguish new construction from reinstatement of affected existing finishes. Inspect junctions, leaks, services and finishes at handover; do not assume that an annex is permitted without project-specific regulatory checks.

### Outdoor majlis construction
For an outdoor majlis, show seating capacity, circulation clearances, guest entrances, washrooms, washbasins and any pantry on a dimensioned plan. These choices determine cooling, ventilation, lighting and drainage needs rather than decoration alone. Specify roof construction, thermal and acoustic insulation, door performance and materials suitable for cleaning and moisture exposure; clarify whether external paving, shade structures and paths are included. Compare quotations by built area, equipment and maintenance access. Handover should include water and drainage tests, cooling performance, door operation and inspection of roof-to-wall junctions for leaks.

### Villa extensions
A villa extension requires an as-built survey rather than relying solely on original drawings. Identify columns, walls, openings, concealed-service routes and rooms that remain occupied. Before choosing a horizontal or vertical addition, obtain structural assessment of loads and foundations and check electrical, cooling and drainage capacity. Ask bidders to separate any strengthening, demolition, resident protection, waterproof connections, reinstatement and temporary service shutdowns. Compare sequencing as well as price. At handover inspect floor levels, joints, cracks and operational tests, and record approved changes before concealed work is closed.

### Residential building alterations
Residential alterations may involve a new doorway, removing a partition, moving a kitchen or relocating a bathroom, each with different implications. Provide existing and proposed layouts, photographs of walls and ceilings, known service routes and details of occupied units. A competent professional must assess work that could affect structural members or safety systems; temporary isolation, dust control and protected circulation also belong in the scope. Compare removal, relocation, new services, making good and testing as separate items. Handover must check layout, leaks, electrical operation, doors and ventilation, not only appearance.

### Villa restoration
Before restoring a villa, make a dated defect register of cracks, staining and peeling finishes, recording locations and whether symptoms follow rainfall or operation of plumbing or air conditioning. Distinguish surface damage from persistent moisture or structural movement; diagnosis may require leak testing or engineering assessment before choosing repair products. Request separate stages for removal, treating the cause, drying, checks before concealment and reinstating finishes, with warranty boundaries. Compare evidence of effectiveness, not just coating brands. Retain photographs of concealed repairs, test results and any recommended follow-up observations.

### Residential building renovation
For an occupied residential building refurbishment, first count units and identify corridors, lifts, roof areas and shared services, and confirm which occupants remain. The project may cover unit finishes, water and electrical services, cooling or façades; prepare a location-by-location scope and a schedule for shutdowns, protected access and waste removal. Require quantities, specifications, samples and a phased programme. Compare bids on each unit, common areas, tests and exclusions. Handover should use a separate defects checklist for every unit, followed by commissioning of shared systems and delivery of maintenance instructions and warranties.

## Restoration and refurbishment

### Restoration of older buildings
Restoring an older building starts with a condition survey, not a paint selection. Record known construction history, settlement or cracking, roof and waterproofing condition, exposed reinforcement, dated photographs, crack widths and damp areas. Ask for an assessment that distinguishes plaster repairs from corrosion treatment or structural strengthening. The quotation should address protection of sound fabric, removal of weak material, compatibility of repair products and who approves responses to hidden defects. Do not price structural strengthening as a cosmetic repair without professional diagnosis. At handover review test records, photographs of concealed work, outstanding defects and any monitoring plan.

### Villa refurbishment
Prepare a room-by-room schedule showing what remains and what is removed: floors, ceilings, doors, bathrooms, kitchens, lighting and cooling. Provide an existing layout, photographs of service routes and measured room dimensions, and say whether occupants will remain. Require separate prices for strip-out, waste disposal, waterproofing, service alterations, joinery and finishes, with shutdown windows and sample approvals. Compare warranty limits and how unexpected defects uncovered during removal will be handled. Accept each room against a checklist, testing electrical outlets, water, drainage and doors and recording defects.

### Apartment renovation
Before renovating an apartment, establish the floor level, lift and entrance dimensions, material storage arrangements and building-management working hours. Supply a plan identifying altered partitions, floor and paint areas, kitchen and bathroom positions, plus photographs. The quote should include protection of lifts and corridors, debris removal, waterproofing and coordination with shared services. Load-bearing components must not be altered without engineering assessment. Compare material alternatives, interruption periods and tests. At handover check bathroom falls, appliance operation and the condition of adjacent finishes.

### Building rehabilitation
Rehabilitation aims to restore a building's functional performance, not merely its appearance. Start with HVAC, pump, electrical and drainage fault logs, roof and façade condition, and available inspection reports. Define target performance, occupied work zones and a service-continuity plan. Each bid should distinguish replacement from repair, explain how capacity and safe operation will be demonstrated, and separate civil, mechanical and electrical work. At handover compare commissioning readings with baseline results and obtain updated drawings and maintenance records.

### Structural crack remediation
A photograph alone cannot determine whether injection or strengthening is appropriate. Record crack width, direction, position relative to columns, beams and openings, and changes over time, using a visible scale in photographs. A qualified structural engineer should assess the cause, movement and load-bearing implications; a paint crack is not equivalent to a structural crack. Require investigation, preparation, repair materials, any temporary support, acceptance criteria and monitoring in the quotation. Retain inspection results and repair records, and do not conceal a crack under finishes before the remedy is approved.

### Façade refurbishment
Measure each façade elevation, height and cladding type, and map peeling coatings, loose render and water entry around openings. Provide close and wide photographs plus access and pedestrian movement information. The proposal should cover checking cladding and fixing stability, surface preparation, joint and waterproofing treatment, the selected coating or cladding system, access equipment and protection. Compare samples, performance, tests and access costs rather than a square-metre rate alone. Handover checks should address finish consistency, fixings, joints and relevant adhesion or water-ingress testing.

### Preparing a building for reuse
A new use may change occupancy, structural loads, ventilation, water, electrical demand and circulation. Supply as-built plans, descriptions of past and proposed use and photographs of plant rooms and openings. Request technical assessments of the structure and services before pricing. The quotation should distinguish removal, repartitioning, fit-out and commissioning, and identify any regulatory review or approval that must be established for the actual change of use; never assume it is permitted. Compare service capacity, drawing scope and coordination responsibilities, and collect commissioning results, as-built drawings and a defects register.

## Demolition and concrete works

### Demolition works
Before demolishing a building or part of one in Al Shamkha, mark precisely what will be removed and retained on drawings, supported by photographs of adjoining walls, slabs and utility connections. Obtain an engineering assessment of the demolition sequence, temporary supports and service isolation; appearance alone does not establish that a wall is non-load-bearing. Quotations should identify equipment, dust and noise control, protection of retained structures, debris segregation and removal, and responsibility for any applicable permissions. At handover, check the removal boundary, adjacent structures, site safety and clearance of waste.

### Removal of buildings and annexes
Removing an existing annex is not the same as stripping an interior room. Record the annex footprint, height, connections to the villa, foundations and any services crossing it. A site survey should establish structural separation points, machinery access and container locations. Specify whether the price includes removing foundations and slabs, filling voids, compacting the ground and reinstating utilities. Compare the final excavation depth and reinstatement obligations, not demolition labour alone. Inspect for dangerous projections, verify isolated services and document the condition of the remaining building.

### Concrete cutting and core drilling
For an air-conditioning duct, pipe penetration or doorway, provide the proposed opening location, dimensions, member thickness and photographs of both faces. Locate embedded services and reinforcement, and obtain designer review before altering structural elements. Ask which cutting or coring method will be used, how equipment will be secured, how cooling water and dust will be contained, and who supports and lifts the cut-out. Compare diameter, depth, number of holes, access and debris disposal. Check finished opening dimensions, edges and surrounding damage before concealment.

### Foundation and footing concrete pours
Foundation pricing requires structural drawings, the project geotechnical report, excavation dimensions, founding levels, reinforcement schedules and concrete specifications—not merely a count of footings. Clarify whether excavation, any necessary dewatering, blinding concrete, waterproofing, formwork, backfilling and testing are included. Before pouring, the responsible technical team should record founding levels, reinforcement, cover, starter bars, penetrations and inspection hold points. At handover review delivery and test records, curing and levels. Structural defects require professional assessment rather than an improvised repair.

### Concrete slab pours
For a concrete slab pour, specify slab area, thickness, elevation, structural system, penetrations, embedded services and any required falls. The quotation should itemise formwork, propping and striking arrangements, reinforcement, concrete, pump, labour, curing and testing, with a pour sequence that controls unintended construction joints. Inspect formwork, steel, levels and fixed services before the pour; monitor delivery, compaction and curing. Handover includes records, test results and a check of finished levels and visible defects requiring technical assessment.

### Concrete wall construction
A retaining wall, shear wall and water-tank wall have different design requirements; they must not share an assumed specification. Supply drawings showing thickness, height, loading, reinforcement, joints and service penetrations, together with soil and groundwater information where relevant. Compare formwork systems, bracing, ties, concrete, waterproofing and construction-joint treatment rather than concrete volume alone. Inspect alignment, dimensions, openings, test records and applicable waterproofing at handover. Refer significant cracking or honeycombing to the designer.

### Demolition waste removal
Waste hauling is separate from the demolition operation. Photograph the material, estimate its volume, identify concrete, block, timber and any material needing special handling, and describe the collection area and on-site haul distance. Quotations should state container or trip quantities, vehicle capacities, loading labour, surface protection, truck access and disposal arrangements compliant with applicable requirements. Potentially hazardous waste must be assessed and segregated rather than mixed with ordinary rubble. Confirm that fragments, sharp debris and transport routes are cleaned at completion.

### Home garden design
Measure the plot, boundaries, shade patterns, entrances and available water and power points before commissioning a layout. Ask for scaled planting, seating, circulation and drainage plans, plus a plant schedule with quantities and supply sizes. Compare design fees by deliverables, revisions and construction details; a rendering alone is not a measurable handover.

### Villa garden landscaping
Survey levels and identify existing trees, inspection covers and buried services to protect. Separate soil preparation, irrigation, planting, edging, lighting and paths in the quotation. Confirm responsibility for waterproofing and utility protection. Inspect grading, irrigation operation and the installed plant list at handover.

### Outdoor space design
Explain whether the space is intended for play, seating, walking or cooking, including expected users and accessibility needs. The plan should resolve pedestrian routes, shade, lighting, drainage and junctions with the building. Compare usability and upkeep alongside appearance, and request dimensioned drawings and a material schedule.

### Green lawn establishment
Provide the area in square metres, intended foot traffic and available irrigation. Require assessment of soil, levels and drainage before planting. Quotes should identify soil preparation, turf establishment method, initial watering, establishment period and treatment of failed patches. Check evenness, drainage and written aftercare instructions.

### Tree and shrub planting
Mark planting positions and distances from walls and utilities, and list species, quantities and required nursery sizes. Compare root-ball quality, excavation, soil amendment, supports and watering rather than tree count alone. Check planting depth, stability, undamaged stems and an agreed establishment follow-up.

### Natural turf installation
State the lawn area, shade exposure and use, with photographs of soil and sprinkler points. Pricing should distinguish weed clearance, grading, growing medium, turf species and origin, laying and establishment watering. Check joints, bare patches and irrigation coverage; agree when heavy use can begin.

### Artificial grass installation
Measure the area and explain whether it is decorative, for walking or for children's play. Request physical samples and specifications for pile, backing, infill, base layers, drainage, seams and anchoring. Compare warranties and stated heat and wear limitations. Inspect levelness, unobtrusive joins and drainage after testing.

### Plant and tree installation
Supply a location plan, species and quantities, with photographs of sun exposure and planter dimensions. Specify delivery size, soil mix, initial feeding and irrigation for each plant group. Small nursery plants should not be priced as mature trees. Match labels and quantities at handover and inspect visible plant health and root-zone watering.

### Tree pruning and shaping
Record tree counts, heights and proximity to buildings or cables, with photographs of affected branches. Agree the extent of pruning, access equipment, protection and green-waste removal; major limb removal warrants suitable assessment. Inspect cuts, trunk condition and site clearance. Pruning is not the same scope as complete tree removal.

### Lawn maintenance
Provide lawn area, grass type and photographs of yellowing or waterlogging. The contract should specify visits, mowing, aeration where needed, weed control, sprinkler checks and how damaged patches are handled. Separate consumables from labour and assess dated condition photographs and visit records, not attendance alone.

### Plant fertilisation and treatment
Photograph leaves, stems and soil, and disclose previous watering and fertiliser use. Yellowing can reflect irrigation or salinity rather than nutrient deficiency. Request a diagnosis, product, application rate, safety measures and monitoring plan. Record subsequent growth and any adverse effects instead of accepting an unspecified chemical treatment.

### Garden pest management
Identify affected plants, damage and any visible insects; disclose pets, children and nearby water features. The provider should identify the pest and consider appropriate non-chemical measures before specifying any permitted product, application controls and re-entry precautions. Request a treatment report and follow-up criteria, not a guarantee of permanent eradication.

### Garden cleaning
Show the paths, planters, leaves and waste piles, and distinguish green-waste collection from pressure washing or weeding. Compare quotations by area, waste volume, loading, removal and protection of irrigation and planting. Check that paths and beds are clear and drains are not blocked by cuttings.

### Garden maintenance contracts
Provide landscaped area, plant inventory, irrigation layout and desired condition. Request a seasonal visit schedule separating mowing, pruning, fertilising, pest monitoring and repairs. Clarify excluded materials, spare parts, emergency call-outs and measurable service standards. Review visit logs, photographs and invoices before renewal.


## Irrigation networks — source category currently not selectable

### Irrigation network design
Provide planting zones, plant types, water source, site levels and available pressure. The designer should group plants by water demand, calculate flow and pressure losses, and specify pipe sizes, filters, valves and controller zones. Compare hydraulic calculations and installation drawings rather than sprinkler counts alone; require an as-built plan showing buried routes and isolation points.

### Drip irrigation installation
Measure beds and planting rows, identify soil type and water supply, and specify emitter spacing and discharge rates. Quotations should include filtration, pressure regulation, flushing ends and protection of exposed tubing. Run the system and compare wetting at the first and last emitters; inspect joints and routes for damage risks.

### Sprinkler installation
Send lawn dimensions and the positions of walls, paths and plants sensitive to spray. Require head type, throw radius, arc settings, operating pressure and a layout designed for even coverage without watering paving. Witness an operating test, adjust arcs and check for leaks at sprinkler connections.

### Irrigation tanks and pump installation
State storage capacity, irrigation zones, elevation changes and estimated demand. The proposal should size the tank and pump, specify dry-run protection, isolation valves, accessible maintenance and suitable electrical safeguards. Separate supply, installation and commissioning costs; witness pressure and flow tests and receive filter maintenance instructions.

### Irrigation network maintenance
Describe low pressure, dry patches or continuous running, and photograph the controller and meter where available. Diagnosis should check filters, valves, emitters, heads and buried lines before parts are replaced. Request a fault report, itemised replacements and a witnessed test of every zone after repair.

### Automatic irrigation controller programming
Provide zone count, planting types, site use and any water-supply restrictions. Configure days, runtimes, sequencing and supported rain or fault overrides instead of applying one timer to all plants. Verify manual and scheduled operation, and obtain a written programme and instructions for seasonal adjustment.

### Irrigation leak detection
Photograph wet patches, subsidence and any unusual meter readings; note whether leakage occurs in a particular zone. Isolate zones and check pressure or meter movement before excavation. Agree the suspected location, excavation limits, pipe repair and reinstatement scope; pressure-test the repaired line and compare subsequent consumption.

## Shades and external works — source category currently not selectable

### Car shade installation
Measure bay widths and lengths, vehicle heights, door swing and turning paths. Specify structural frame, covering, anchorage, drainage and design loads appropriate to the site; check any required approvals before construction. At handover, inspect clearances, fixings, fabric tension and exposed sharp edges.

### Pergola construction
Provide the seating footprint, desired shade and whether the pergola is freestanding or attached to the building. Compare frame material, protective finish, roofing, foundations and optional lighting separately; detail interfaces with waterproofing and façades. Inspect alignment, anchorage, drainage and maintenance access.

### Outdoor seating area construction
State expected seating capacity, furniture sizes, access routes and privacy needs, with photographs of existing ground and utility points. The quotation should separate paving build-up, falls, lighting, fixed seating and exposure-appropriate finishes. Inspect drainage, circulation clearances and finished levels before acceptance.

### External fence installation
Provide boundary lengths, proposed height, gate locations and level changes, using an authoritative boundary survey. Require details of posts, foundations, corrosion protection, finish and treatment of sloping ground; verify applicable permissions before installation. Check alignment, gate operation and sound connections.

### Garden pathway construction
Mark pathway width and route, intended pedestrian or light-cart use, and existing roots or ponding. Compare paving type, sub-base thickness, edge restraints, falls, slip resistance and accessibility. Inspect finished levels, trip hazards and drainage away from the building.

### Interlocking paver installation
Provide area, expected loads and whether the paving serves a path or vehicle bay, plus ground-condition photographs. Specify excavation, compaction, base and bedding depths, paver thickness, edge restraints and joint filling. Check levels, falls and restraint stability; a square-metre price without the sub-base specification is not comparable.

### Waterfall and fountain construction
Provide basin dimensions, location, desired water effect and power and water points, noting nearby children or seating. Request waterproofing, circulation, pump, filtration, overflow drainage and safe maintenance-access details. Witness operational, leak and drainage tests, and receive cleaning and water-use guidance.
