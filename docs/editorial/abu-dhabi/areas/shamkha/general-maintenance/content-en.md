## General maintenance

### 1. Periodic villa maintenance

Begin with an asset register covering villa pumps, equipment, doors, roofs and previous service dates, then prioritise failures by consequence. Request a visit schedule that distinguishes routine inspections, cleaning and functional tests from parts and emergency call-outs. Compare visit frequency, covered systems and exclusions; receive dated readings, fault logs, completed-work records and recommendations.

### 2. Periodic building maintenance

Multi-unit buildings require a distinction between shared facilities and individual units. List common pumps, panels, corridors and lifts where present, and identify who grants plant-room access and informs occupants. Compare inspection schedules, escalation procedures and reporting; require records that separate private-unit defects from shared-system work rather than assuming unlisted assets are included.

### 3. Interior damage repairs

Photograph damaged paint or cladding with a scale and establish whether impact, leakage or substrate movement caused it. Request diagnosis and a small finish-matching sample before repairs. Compare removal quantities, preparation, replacement materials and refinishing; check edge alignment, colour consistency and dryness, and record exclusions at handover.

### 4. Door and window repairs

For a rubbing door or poorly sealing window, photograph hinges, seals, frames and contact points, then measure gaps at several locations. Require separate prices for adjustment, hardware replacement, frame repair and complete replacement. Test opening, locking, drainage and weather sealing after the repair without overstressing unsuitable hinges.

### 5. Dampness treatment

A damp patch alone does not establish whether the cause is plumbing, failed waterproofing or condensation. Record when it appears, whether it follows equipment use or rain, and photograph both sides of the wall where accessible. Request source investigation before repainting and agree drying and follow-up checks. Accept a diagnosis and repair record, not simply paint covering the stain.

### 6. Crack treatment

Measure crack width, length and direction, photograph it against a scale and record any change over time or associated settlement or sticking doors. Signs of movement require structural assessment before cosmetic treatment. Compare surface repair, injection or strengthening only after diagnosis; receive material records, repair limits and any monitoring plan.

### 7. Pre-handover property preparation

Prepare a numbered room-by-room snag register covering doors, lights, drainage, finishes and fittings, with photographs of each defect. Agree who supplies materials and retests closed items; keep corrective work distinct from final cleaning. Receive a signed closure list identifying outstanding defects and witness lighting, fixture and drainage checks.

### 8. Minor home maintenance

Group minor tasks such as hinge adjustment, handle fixing and damaged-edge repairs into a photographed list with dimensions and access details. Identify tasks requiring a qualified electrician or plumber rather than treating them as general handyman work. Compare call-out charges, materials and exclusions, and test every completed item at handover.

### 9. Curtain installation

Measure window width, rail height and the overlap needed for light control, noting substrate type and fabric weight. Compare manual and motorised tracks, bracket positions and cleaning access. Check for interference with window opening or AC outlets; receive a level track and curtains that glide freely with appropriate fixings.

### 10. Shelf installation

Measure shelf length, depth and height, identify intended loads, wall construction and concealed services. Generic screws are not suitable for every substrate; specify the appropriate brackets and anchors. Compare board thickness and support points, then verify level, stability and clear load guidance.

### 11. TV mounting

Record screen dimensions, weight, mounting-hole pattern, seating position and eye level, with photographs of the wall and sockets. Ask how concealed services will be checked before drilling and confirm the bracket's movement range. Price visible and concealed cable routing separately; test security, articulation and safe connector access.

### 12. Accessory installation

List each accessory with a photograph, dimensions and proposed position, especially on bathroom tile or hollow walls. Specify substrate checks, compatible anchors and sealant where necessary. Compare quotes by item count and surface type; inspect alignment, intact tile and secure attachment after a practical test.

### 13. Furniture assembly

Provide model numbers, unit counts, packaging photographs and assembly manuals, and check doorway widths and working space. Distinguish assembly from wall anchoring, moving packages and disposal when comparing quotes. Test doors, drawers and stability on every unit and document missing fasteners or components.

### 14. Minor fault repairs

Describe when the fault occurs, whether it recurs after use or in damp conditions, and provide a short video only where safe. Request diagnosis before a quotation separating inspection labour, parts, repair and possible return visits. Test the repaired function safely at handover and obtain a record of replacements and warranty exclusions.

## Requesting a quotation

Provide the address, property type, clear photographs, task list, approximate dimensions and suitable access times. Compare inspection, repair, materials and final testing scope. ## Integrated finishing

### 15. Villa finishing

Before pricing a villa fit-out, survey the structure, installed services and room plans, measuring wall, ceiling and floor areas separately. Define finish standards room by room, specify products or performance characteristics, and approve samples before ordering. Compare contractors against one bill of quantities and schedule waterproofing, pressure and electrical tests before concealed work is closed. At handover check surface levels, doors, wet-area drainage and system operation, recording defects by room.

### 16. Apartment finishing

Finishing an apartment in an occupied building requires protection of lifts and corridors, controlled working hours and waste arrangements. Provide a floor plan, bathroom and kitchen photographs, floor levels and a clear keep-or-remove schedule. Price protection, stripping, waterproofing, tiling, painting and sanitary fixtures separately. Inspect bathroom falls, thresholds, drainage and switches, and confirm that shared areas are restored after work.

### 17. Retail-shop finishing

Start with a plan showing the business use, customer circulation, counter, storage, power, data and air-conditioning. Survey columns, actual dimensions and frontage before approving fabrication drawings, and record landlord requirements without claiming permission has been granted. Compare partitions, flooring, shopfront, lighting, signage and exclusions. Test circuits and lights, check display-unit stability and maintenance access, and inspect the cleared site.

### 18. Office finishing

Establish workstation counts, meeting rooms, circulation and network and power outlets before choosing partitions and ceilings. Photograph service locations, measure clear ceiling heights and door widths, and specify acoustic performance rather than accepting a vague 'soundproof' label. Compare material schedules, doors, lighting, data points and milestones. Verify labelled outlets, operation, partition alignment and door clearances at handover.

### 19. Interior-space refurbishment

Renovation does not always require removing every finish. Identify sound elements to retain, sources of damage and services behind cladding. Mark demolition, retention and additions on a plan with photographs, measurements and protection for occupied rooms. Compare targeted repair with full renewal, including downtime. Check transitions between old and new finishes and retest any relocated services.

### 20. Decorative fit-out work

Translate a visual concept into dimensioned installation drawings, levels and fixing details, especially where wood, metal or glass meets lighting. Approve samples for colour, texture and edges, and specify moisture or heat resistance where relevant. Quotations must distinguish lighting, wiring, painting, supply and installation. Inspect joint consistency, secure fixings and access to lighting components for maintenance.

### 21. Integrated finishing handover

Arrange staged acceptance rather than relying on appearance alone: review concealed-work test records, then inspect each room against plans and approved material schedules. Log each defect with its location, photograph, priority, owner and retest date. Cleaning does not close a technical defect. Obtain agreed as-built records, operating manuals and warranties, and witness retests before signing off corrected items.

## Gypsum and decorative work

### 22. Gypsum ceiling installation

Measure the slab level and proposed finished ceiling height, locating AC diffusers, sprinklers, lights and access panels. Request a suspension layout, appropriate fixings and board types suited to use and moisture exposure. Compare ceiling area, edge details, cut-outs and painting rather than price per square metre alone. Inspect flatness, joint cracking, opening alignment and access to overhead equipment.

### 23. Gypsum partition walls

Specify partition length and height, door positions, intended wall-mounted loads and required acoustic privacy. Check concealed services, stud sizes, board layers, insulation and moisture protection where relevant. Compare reinforcement for screens and cabinets and junction details at floors and ceilings. Verify alignment, door stability, finished joints and access to service points.

### 24. Concealed-light coves

Prepare a cove section showing recess depth, LED-strip position, cable route and an accessible driver location. Test a short on-site sample for light spread, visible diode points and unwanted shadows. Separate gypsum work from strips, drivers, controls and paint in the quotation. Run a suitable functional test and inspect heat, maintenance access and straight light lines.

### 25. Decorative gypsum features

Dimension cornices, frames and ornaments, including corner joints, and approve a sample profile before repetition. Check the substrate, fixing method, component weight and clearance around doors or curtain tracks. Compare manufacture, installation, joint finishing and painting. Inspect matching profiles, aligned corners and joints after compounds have dried.

### 26. Wood-effect panel installation

Measure wall areas, grain direction, joints, ends and corners, and check wall flatness and dampness before fixing. Approve a sample showing thickness, finish, cleaning method and suitability for the room. Compare panels, adhesive or mechanical fixings, trims and edge treatment. Verify pattern continuity, secure panels, tight joints and unobstructed sockets.

### 27. Marble-effect panel installation

Plan panel joints before cutting, particularly around basins and service openings. Measure wall areas, outlets and pipes, and request the product's moisture, heat and cleaning characteristics rather than treating it as natural stone. Compare supply, cutting, fixing, edge closure and suitable sealants. Check flatness, pattern matching, protected edges and resistance to water pooling at joints.

### 28. Gypsum repair and maintenance

Do not fill a gypsum crack before checking for movement, weak suspension or leakage above the ceiling. Photograph and measure the damage, inspect adjacent boards and services, and specify replacement boards, framing repairs and the extent of repainting. Inspect the dried surface under raking light for waves, and verify that any moisture source has been addressed.

## Flooring and wall cladding

### 29. Ceramic tile installation

Measure floor and wall areas, tile size, cutting allowance, joint widths and drain positions. Check substrate flatness and wet-area waterproofing before laying; specify adhesive, grout and cuts at corners. Compare substrate preparation and skirting separately, and inspect drainage falls, straight courses, sound bedding and safe edges.

### 30. Porcelain tile installation

Provide tile dimensions, thickness and a laying plan, especially for large-format slabs requiring careful handling and levelling. Compare adhesive coverage, levelling equipment and movement-joint treatment. Check lippage, pattern alignment and secure bedding before accepting the work.

### 31. Parquet flooring installation

Identify solid or engineered flooring, laying direction, room areas and possible substrate moisture. Specify underlay or vapour control, perimeter expansion gaps and door clearances; compare supply, fitting and skirting separately. Inspect stability, squeaks and swelling and obtain product-specific cleaning guidance.

### 32. Vinyl flooring installation

Distinguish glued, click-lock and sheet vinyl and describe room dimensions, traffic and wet exposure. The base must be smooth because defects can telegraph through vinyl; specify welded or sealed seams where required. Compare wear-layer thickness and slip properties, and inspect joints, adhesion and bubbles.

### 33. Interlocking paving installation

Define the path or parking area, pedestrian or vehicle loads, levels and stormwater drainage. Compare excavation, sub-base depth, compaction, paver thickness and edge restraints rather than the visible square-metre price alone. Check falls, joint filling and stability after compaction.

### 34. Wall cladding installation

Identify stone, panels or tiles, wall height, substrate and electrical openings. Request samples and details of fixing, loading and junctions at doors and ceilings, accounting for moisture exposure. Compare base preparation, cutting and edge trims; inspect secure attachment, joint alignment and service access.

### 35. Flooring repairs

Photograph cracks, loose tiles or swelling, noting the affected area and possible leaks or substrate settlement. Investigate the cause before replacing finishes and confirm whether matching materials are available. Compare local repairs with reinstatement of the affected section; inspect levelness, bonding and joint matching afterwards.

## Electrical installations and lighting

### 36. Building electrical installation

Map rooms, appliance loads, meter position and the main board before chasing walls; drawings should distinguish circuits and wet-area supplies. Request continuity and protection tests, labelled circuits and an as-built diagram.

### 37. Cable and wiring runs

Record route lengths, conductor sizes, insulation grade and containment method; a quotation without measured runs is difficult to compare. Check fixings, protected terminations and insulation test records before covering finishes.

### 38. Distribution board installation

Provide existing-board photographs, load schedule and proposed spare capacity; qualified personnel must coordinate protection and earthing. Compare panel layout, labels and test results, and retain a legible circuit schedule.

### 39. Socket and switch fitting

Mark outlet quantities, heights, damp zones, wall finishes and connected appliance ratings; separate accessories from circuit extensions. Test polarity, earthing, switch operation and secure faceplates without heating or looseness.

### 40. Power supply for extensions

For an annex, list rooms, AC equipment, kitchen loads, distance from the supply board and any outdoor cable route. Compare dedicated-circuit needs, weather protection and voltage drop; commission the installation under its planned loads.

### 41. Electrical network rehabilitation

Document nuisance trips, deteriorated wiring, overheated connections and installation age before deciding on full renewal. Price investigation, rewiring and reinstatement separately; accept measured test results and a new circuit register.

### 42. Electrical load assessment

Count AC units, heaters, equipment ratings, supply capacity and circuit distribution; breaker size alone is not an assessment. Obtain a documented qualified load assessment highlighting imbalance and likely future demand rather than simply upsizing breakers.

### 43. Interior lighting installation

Identify room use, ceiling levels, task areas, colour temperature and control points. Compare distribution, fitting types, maintenance access and power draw; inspect the actual lighting for glare and uneven coverage.

### 44. Exterior lighting installation

Map façades, paths, car entrances, cable routes and locations exposed to rain or dust. Compare outdoor-rated fittings, mountings and beam direction; test after dark and inspect sealed enclosures and safe wiring.

### 45. Chandelier installation

Supply the chandelier weight, ceiling height and substrate, supply point and manufacturer mounting details, especially beneath gypsum. Use suitable structural anchorage rather than relying on plasterboard; inspect balance, security and electrical connections.

### 46. Concealed lighting installation

Measure LED runs, feed points, driver locations and ventilation within coves. Compare driver quantities, maintenance access and colour samples; inspect for uninterrupted light with no flicker or dark patches.

### 47. Garden lighting installation

Plot trees, paths, sprinklers and irrigation trenches before burying lighting cables; identify operating voltage and protection. Request underground route drawings and wet-condition safety checks; inspect beam aim to avoid nuisance glare.

### 48. Motion sensor installation

Define detection zones, occupancy patterns, delay requirements and likely pet movement. Compare detector coverage, ingress protection and settings; test switching delay, cut-off and false activations.

### 49. Lighting unit maintenance

Record failed lights and establish whether the lamp, driver, switch or supply is at fault before replacing assemblies. Compare replacement components, access costs and availability; verify firm fittings, correct switching and stable output.


## Plumbing, drainage and leak detection

### 50. Water supply network installation

Count kitchens, bathrooms, tanks and appliances; map supply routes, target pressure and hot/cold lines. Compare pipe material, diameters, fittings and pressure testing; inspect a flushed, leak-free system before concealment.

### 51. Drainage network installation

Provide drainage plans, levels, inspection chambers, fixtures and vent positions; pipe length alone omits critical falls. Compare clean-outs, expansion details and tests; witness flow without back-up or foul odour.

### 52. Sanitary fixture installation

Identify basin, WC and mixer models, dimensions, fixing points and heights before wall tiling closes. Compare connection kits, seals and drilling; test flow, drainage, watertight joints and secure mounting.

### 53. Water tank installation

Specify tank capacity, material, location, base loads, wash-out access, vent, overflow and drain. Compare sun protection, access and fixing arrangements; test filling, float controls, overflow and leakage.

### 54. Water pump installation

Report storey count, outlet pressures, peak demand and supply tank details. Request a pump curve, dry-run protection and vibration/noise controls; commission stable delivery without leaks.

### 55. Pipework rehabilitation

Photograph corrosion, leaks and low pressure, identifying retained sections and access routes. Compare selective replacement, waste removal and reinstatement; pressure-test the connected system rather than only new pipe sections.

### 56. Drainage maintenance

Map repeat blockages, drain smells, cleaning history and chamber locations. Compare mechanical clearing and camera inspection where needed instead of indiscriminate chemicals; verify flow and document the obstruction.

### 57. Water leak investigation

Provide meter trends, damp patches and photographs; check the meter with outlets closed when feasible. Compare acoustic, thermal and pressure methods suited to the site; request findings and uncertainty before destructive access.

### 58. Pipe leak repairs

Identify pipe material, leak point, access and whether water service must be interrupted. Compare localized repair, connector choice, partial renewal and finish reinstatement; witness a pressure test before covering.

### 59. Tank leak investigation

Record water-level loss, overflow, pipework, float valves and tank shell before blaming the lining. Compare connection checks, waterproofing inspection and joint remediation; document the loss mechanism and refill test.

### 60. Bathroom waterproofing

Measure bathroom floor, wall upstands, pipe penetrations and doorway threshold. Specify a tile-compatible waterproofing system and an agreed flood test before tiling; check substrate condition first.

### 61. Roof waterproofing

Record roof area, falls, drains, joints, equipment plinths and ponding. Compare preparation, membrane build-up and UV protection; inspect drainage and the agreed integrity test before acceptance.

### 62. Water tank waterproofing

Identify potable or non-potable use, tank substrate, cracks and entry access. Specify a lining suitable for the water use, cleaning and rinsing procedure and separate joint checks; accept a watertightness test and material record.

### 63. Leak-related dampness treatment

Record mould, peeling, dates and the suspected moisture source, then repair the leak before drying surfaces. Compare drying period, moisture checks, salt treatment and repainting; require recorded observations, not merely concealed stains.


## Air-conditioning and refrigeration

### 64. Split AC installation

Match unit capacity to the room, solar exposure and indoor/outdoor positions, noting pipe route and condensate outlet. Compare mounting, insulated lines, evacuation and commissioning; verify cooling and condensate discharge without leaks.

### 65. Central AC installation

Central cooling needs room-by-room load estimates, duct routes, access for maintenance and condensate disposal. Compare equipment, controls, filters, noise and electrical demand; request airflow balancing and temperature readings by zone.

### 66. Concealed AC installation

Verify ceiling void heights, concealed unit envelope, supply and return positions and maintenance openings. Compare insulation, suspension and drain slope; check filter access and absence of condensation on plaster.

### 67. AC refrigerant piping

Mark copper-line lengths, elevation changes, penetrations and the refrigerant specified for the unit. Compare line sizing, insulation, supports, leak testing and evacuation; inspect before any concealed run is closed.

### 68. Air duct installation

Coordinate duct sections, structural beams, lights, diffusers and returns before fabrication. Compare sheet thickness, insulation, hangers and joint sealing; request balancing and noise observations appropriate to the job.

### 69. AC equipment replacement

Record the old model, fault symptoms, capacity, available space and which services can safely be reused. Compare repair versus replacement, removal and connection adaptations; obtain equipment warranty and commissioning report.

### 70. System start-up and inspection

Inspect start-up sequence, sensors, electrical draw, airflow, drainage and filters after installation or repair. Request readings under actual operation and a fault list; fan noise alone does not demonstrate performance.

### 71. AC unit cleaning

Identify whether filters, coil, blower or drain pan need cleaning and photograph dust or mould deposits. Compare cleaning products and protection for electronics and furnishings; inspect drainage and cleanliness before restart.

### 72. Refrigerant charge

Poor cooling does not automatically mean low refrigerant: check airflow, filters, pressure and leaks first. Compare diagnosis, leak repair and charging by manufacturer specification; require operating readings, not arbitrary topping-up.

### 73. AC fault repair

Faults may involve a capacitor, controller, sensor or compressor; record the error code and when it occurs. Compare component repair and replacement with warranty terms; repeat operating cycles to check the defect is resolved.

### 74. Air duct cleaning

List outlets, inspection access, duct type and filter condition; photograph deposits before cleaning. Compare containment, extraction and access to hidden sections; request before-and-after evidence without unsupported sterilisation claims.

### 75. Central AC maintenance

Inventory central units, pumps, filters, controllers, running hours and failures. Compare preventive tasks, visit frequency and excluded parts; require trends and an unresolved-fault register.

### 76. Replacement of spare parts

Obtain model and part numbers, connector photographs and fault evidence before ordering. Compare OEM or compatible approved alternatives and lead time; test the repaired function after fitting.

### 77. Periodic AC maintenance contracts

List unit numbers, locations, use patterns and required seasonal service levels. Compare visit schedules, emergency scope, cleaning, consumables and reporting; review fault trends instead of visit counts alone.


## Smart systems and site monitoring

### 78. Smart lighting control

Identify lighting circuits and whether wall switches must remain usable without internet access. Compare dimmer/load compatibility, connectivity and manual override; test each circuit from both switch and app.

### 79. Smart air-conditioning control

List AC models, controllers and sensors and define temperature schedules for each room. Compare interface compatibility, local control and network failure behaviour; verify on/off and mode changes.

### 80. Smart lock installation

Measure door thickness, swing, cylinder type and emergency access before selecting a smart lock. Compare battery life, user permissions and administration; test inside/outside release and low-battery access.

### 81. Smart curtain installation

Specify opening width, curtain weight, power location, track path and manual-use requirement. Compare motor rating, noise and controls; inspect obstacle response and maintenance access.

### 82. Phone app device integration

Inventory device makes, firmware, home network and accounts to be linked. Compare genuine compatibility and access permissions; hand over owner-controlled accounts with recovery instructions.

### 83. Automated smart scenes

Define concrete scenes such as leaving home or bedtime and which devices each may control. Compare safeguards, offline behaviour and manual overrides; test scene transitions without overriding essential equipment.

### 84. Smart-home maintenance

Log intermittent failures, connectivity, configuration backups, sensor batteries and hub health. Compare targeted diagnosis with indiscriminate resets; preserve client settings and retest scenes after repair.

### 85. CCTV installation

Map camera positions, mounting heights, fields of view, lighting, privacy and cable routes. Compare resolution, storage and safe mounting; inspect day/night footage without monitoring unrelated private spaces.

### 86. Recording equipment installation

Identify camera count, recording rate, retention needs, disk capacity and ventilation. Compare storage, backup power and access controls; demonstrate secure search and export of recorded footage.

### 87. Alarm system installation

Map doors, windows and monitored spaces and plan for nuisance alarms. Compare batteries, communications and notification channels; test each sensor without promising third-party response that is not contracted.

### 88. Access control installation

Specify controlled doors, user groups, permitted hours, audit needs and emergency release. Compare card or PIN readers, lock interfaces and privacy safeguards; test authorised and rejected access.

### 89. Intercom installation

Photograph outdoor station, indoor handset locations, gate and cable/network routes. Compare audio/video quality, door release and integration; test every endpoint and document reset procedures.

### 90. CCTV maintenance

Record blurred images, outages, missing footage and fault timings; check power, cabling and storage. Compare camera replacement against connection repairs; verify image quality, continuous recording and reboot recovery.

### 91. Remote phone viewing setup

Identify remote viewers, recorder model, connectivity and two-factor authentication. Prefer secure integration over indiscriminate port forwarding; hand over owner login, access alerts and revoke installer access.


## Painting and surface finishes

### 92. Villa painting

Measure wall and ceiling areas, rooms, furniture and existing paint condition before deciding coat counts. Compare filling, primer, paint, masking and protection as separate items; inspect dry colour consistency and clean edges.

### 93. Apartment painting

Document cracks, stains and areas not requiring repainting, especially in occupied apartments. Compare furniture protection, ventilation and suitable low-odour materials; inspect stain treatment and floor protection.

### 94. Exterior building painting

Survey façade peeling, chalking, cracks and water/sun exposure, including access height. Compare washing, exterior primer, crack treatment and access equipment; inspect adhesion and absence of drips.

### 95. Door and wood painting

Count doors and timber items, identify existing coating and whether hardware removal is needed. Compare sanding, compatible primers and topcoats; check smooth finish and doors closing without sticking.

### 96. Decorative painting

Approve a real texture sample and technique such as trowelled or textured paint before pricing the full area. Compare sample costs, material use and future repairability; inspect texture and hue under installed lighting.

### 97. Old paint removal and preparation

Determine whether old paint is peeling, sound or moisture-damaged rather than stripping indiscriminately. Compare selective removal, dust protection and substrate repair; verify a dry, stable surface ready for primer.

### 98. Repainting and maintenance

Photograph scratches and stains, and identify existing colour code and paint system. Compare local touch-up with full-wall repainting to avoid sheen mismatch; inspect colour blending and invisible repair boundaries.


## Waterproofing and thermal insulation

### 99. Roof waterproofing

Map concrete surfaces, drains, ponding and movement joints around plant plinths before selecting a membrane. Compare removal of failed layers, preparation and protection; verify drainage and watertight junction details.

### 100. Bathroom waterproofing

Map shower area, drains, threshold and wall upstands before waterproofing. Compare tile-compatible systems and pipe-penetration details; arrange an agreed flood test before finishes are laid.

### 101. Tank waterproofing

Identify water use, tank substrate, cracks, joints, level and safe entry requirements. Compare product suitability for the stored water and cleaning/drying procedure; receive a holding test and material record.

### 102. Building thermal insulation

Record roof/wall build-up, solar exposure, available space, thermal bridges and condensation risks. Compare thickness, thermal performance, moisture response and continuity; inspect junctions without blocking necessary ventilation.

### 103. External wall insulation

Survey façade substrate, cracks, openings, balcony fixings and safe access. Compare external insulation or appropriate coating solutions against design requirements and moisture behaviour; inspect window and edge junctions.

### 104. Rainwater ingress treatment

Relate water marks to rainfall, wind direction, gutters, joints and window openings. Compare targeted water testing and entry-point sealing rather than indiscriminate repainting; verify a suitable repeat test.

### 105. Waterproofing maintenance

Review membrane age, stains, blisters, cuts around drains and movement points. Compare patch repairs with renewal of a failed section based on condition; document repair details, tests and monitoring.



## Maintenance and finishing request details

Identify the property and affected room or system, when the fault began, overview and close-up photographs, measured affected area, and any active leakage, power outage or falling-object hazard. State whether you need diagnosis only or full repair, and whether reinstatement, parts and waste removal belong in the quote. Essential system tests still matter on small jobs.
