# Al Shamkha — Engineering consultancy, design and décor

## Al Shamkha enquiry context
An Al Shamkha villa entrance design should account for pedestrian routes, car movement and gate clearance from the start. Attach site photographs, measurements and access details. This is company-service guidance, not proof of an available provider. If no company is listed, Biet Alreef customer support can receive a quotation enquiry without promising availability or a price.


## Architectural design and drawings

### 1. Villa design

Before commissioning a villa design, prepare a brief covering bedrooms, the relationship between reception and family entrances, privacy, parking, storage and service rooms. Have the plot measured and its boundaries, levels and applicable constraints checked by the responsible professional rather than assuming a permitted floor area. Request alternative layouts, an area schedule and dimensioned circulation plans. Compare consultants by design stages, revision rounds and structural/MEP coordination, then accept only clearly versioned drawings with unresolved decisions recorded.

### 2. Residential building design

Define the intended apartment mix, privacy expectations and shared facilities before a residential building is laid out. Ask the designer to examine how entrances, lifts, stairs and service routes work together, and distinguish usable room areas from gross floor area. Proposals should specify whether parking studies, service coordination and elevation design are included. At handover, reconcile unit schedules, room dimensions, light wells and service positions across the drawing set before detailed design proceeds.

### 3. Commercial building design

Describe the proposed uses of commercial units, customer access, storage, deliveries and possible future subdivision. Obtain dimensioned layouts showing columns, entrances, circulation and plant/service areas. Compare options for operational flexibility, maintainability and coordination of air-conditioning, electrical and drainage systems. Receive an area schedule and coordinated plans, and have the appointed professional assess safety and authority requirements for the actual use without assuming approval has been granted.

### 4. Architectural drawing preparation

State whether drawings are needed for concept review, contractor pricing, interdisciplinary coordination or construction. Provide a site survey or existing plans and flag unverified dimensions. Agree which plans, elevations, sections, levels and opening schedules belong to the deliverable stage. Compare quotations by revision allowances and clash-resolution responsibility. At handover, check scales, revision numbers and dimensional consistency between sheets; pricing drawings should not automatically be treated as construction documents.

### 5. Façade redesign

Photograph every affected elevation and record window and door openings, AC units, downpipes, projections and levels. Ask for material alternatives that explain weight, fixing systems, moisture protection and future cleaning rather than visuals alone. The designer should detail interfaces at openings and exposed edges, with a qualified review of effects on existing construction. Accept physical samples, installation details and a finish schedule suitable for consistent contractor quotations.

### 6. 3D architectural modelling

Specify whether the model is for massing and spatial tests, component coordination or presentation images. Supply reference dimensions and levels, identify geometry based on approved drawings, and label illustrative furniture and finishes. Compare proposals by level of detail, file formats, number of views and revision rounds. At handover, check door openings, heights and grids against source drawings; photorealistic renders do not replace construction details.

### 7. Amending existing plans

Provide the latest drawing issue, annotated change requests and photographs of existing conditions. Separate straightforward room-layout revisions from alterations affecting load-bearing walls, slab openings or building services, which require discipline-specific assessment. Compare proposals by affected drawing count, coordination work and any approval review. Accept a dated revised set with change clouds or a revision register, and ensure superseded drawings are withdrawn.

### 8. Permit drawing preparation

Permit-submission drawings serve a different purpose from concept images or contractor pricing plans. First define the property, proposed works and plot boundaries, then have the consultant check current official submission requirements and identify the architectural, structural and building-services sheets applicable to that case. Supply site information, existing approvals and owner details; request a document and revision register with a named discipline owner. Compare quotations for preparation, responses to comments, revisions and excluded charges. At handover, reconcile project identifiers, dimensions and drawing numbers. Neither preparation nor upload of a package constitutes an approval.

### 9. Building application submission

Before engaging a consultant to submit a building application, agree the applicant's authority, any required authorisation, who prepares attachments, answers comments and pays charges. Check ownership, site and scope details against the drawings before submission and retain the uploaded document list. Clarify whether the fee covers resubmissions and responses or only the initial filing. Request the application reference, submission date, status history and correspondence log; verify the actual authority decision. An application number alone is not permission to start construction.

### 10. Revision of approved drawings

If room layouts, openings or dimensions change after drawings have been approved, provide a marked comparison of the approved and proposed versions, plus site photographs if work has started. Ask the designer to identify affected sheets, assess structural and MEP consequences and establish whether regulatory review is needed. Compare quotations by disciplines involved, coordination and reissue rounds. Receive a revision schedule and clearly numbered new drawing issue. Do not substitute revised sketches for approved site documents before completing any required process.

### 11. Maintenance permit coordination

The label 'maintenance' does not by itself determine the approval route. Describe the defect, repair location and whether walls, services or structural elements will be opened or changed; include photographs and approximate affected dimensions. Have a qualified consultant verify the current official requirements for the specific work and identify responsibility for drawings, documents and any inspection. Compare coordination fees separately from authority charges and repair work. Receive a submission record, status and copies of any issued permits before undertaking work that requires one.

### 12. Extension permit coordination

For a proposed extension, show its position on a plot plan, area, dimensions, height and connections to the existing building. Supply elevation photographs, known service routes and available structural plans; foundation and tie-in details may require separate engineering assessment. Compare proposals for survey, design, structural and MEP coordination, comment responses and explicit exclusions. At handover, check consistency across submitted drawings and records. Acceptance for assessment is not an issued construction permit.

### 13. As-built survey drawings

As-built survey drawings are useful when historic plans no longer match the site, especially before cabinetry fabrication, opening alterations or renovation. Define the measured scope: room and opening dimensions, floor and ceiling levels, visible columns and equipment, and accessible service points. Use numbered photographs and cross-check critical measurements; concealed or inferred features must be identified rather than presented as verified. Compare quotes by survey accuracy, file formats and corrective visits. Receive dated drawings that can be checked against the actual premises.

### 14. Authority approval coordination

Approval coordination is not a universal single permit. Define the proposed works, intended use and responsible parties, then have the consultant build an authority-and-document matrix using the relevant current official sources. Request a register identifying each document's owner, issue date and submission status, with clear responsibility for responding to comments, redraws and charges. Compare offers by follow-up scope and tangible deliverables, not a promise to 'obtain every approval'. At handover, check actual decisions, conditions and validity periods where stated; enquiries and filing receipts are not approvals.

### 15. Concrete structural system design

For a new concrete structural system, supply architectural plans, intended use, storey count and available ground information. Ask the engineer to identify the load-bearing scheme, load paths, column, beam and slab positions, and coordination of service openings before sizes are fixed. Compare proposals by calculations, detailing, site input and revision scope. Receive coordinated, version-controlled drawings and calculations; reinforcement sizes cannot be inferred from a 3D presentation image.

### 16. Foundation and footing design

Foundation selection depends on loads, ground conditions, groundwater and potential settlement, not simply building footprint. Provide an appropriate geotechnical investigation, structural grids and levels, and known buried services. Request an assessment of suitable foundation options, reinforcement and interfaces with vertical members, including waterproofing where relevant. When comparing design fees, establish who commissions ground investigations and coordinates excavation. At handover, reconcile founding levels, grid references and details across the drawings.

### 17. Structural drawing preparation

Construction-stage structural drawings must communicate member dimensions, reinforcement and connections, based on coordinated architectural information and the engineer's loading assumptions. Agree the required footing, column, beam, slab and stair drawings, details and reinforcement schedules. Compare consultants by detail level and responsibility for resolving HVAC and drainage penetration clashes. At handover, check grids, levels and issue numbers, and reject undocumented critical connections rather than leaving them to site interpretation.

### 18. Structural load review

A load review is appropriate when a space changes use, heavy equipment or tanks are introduced, concentrated loads increase or an existing member's capacity is uncertain. Provide equipment weights, base dimensions, positions and available as-built plans. The assessment should identify permanent, variable and concentrated actions, load paths and assumptions requiring on-site verification. Compare offers by calculation and inspection scope. Receive a clear recommendation on whether strengthening or further testing is required before proceeding.

### 19. Building structural safety assessment

Progressive cracks, settlement or exposed reinforcement corrosion call for a qualified site assessment rather than a diagnosis from photographs alone. Prepare dated photographs, crack locations, a symptom timeline and records of leaks or previous alterations. Request an inspection plan distinguishing visible findings from matters requiring tests. Compare offers by survey coverage, testing, report and responsibility limits. The report should distinguish observations, possible causes, recommendations and urgent measures. Where immediate danger is suspected, restrict use of the affected area and seek prompt on-site specialist advice.

### 20. Structural strengthening design

Strengthening should not be designed until the cause of weakness, material condition and actual loads have been assessed. Supply inspection findings, available drawings, access photographs and service locations. Ask for evaluated strengthening options, connection details, temporary supports, construction sequencing and inspection hold points, including any impact on building occupancy. Compare fees by deliverables, tests and coordination. Receive buildable, professionally reviewed details; never cut a load-bearing member on verbal instructions.

### 21. Structural engineering reports

Define whether the structural report concerns cracking, a proposed change of use, an extension or a condition survey before adjacent works. Supply drawings, dated photographs, repair history and previous test results, and identify the elements to be assessed. Require clear separation of observations, measurements, assumptions, calculations and recommendations, including limitations and missing information. Compare offers by visits, testing and appendices. Receive a dated professional report with actionable follow-up items.

### 22. Construction supervision

Before appointing a supervisor for a Shamkha project, distinguish periodic compliance inspections from a resident engineer's daily presence; the responsibilities and fees differ. Provide the drawing revision being built, the contractor's programme and dates for concrete pours, ceiling closure and electrical or HVAC rough-ins. Ask for inspection hold points with advance notice, and records identifying drawing references, defect locations, photographs and closure dates. Compare actual visit allowances, response time and reinspection arrangements. A signed visit note does not certify concealed work that the supervisor did not witness.

### 23. Project management

For a villa build or multi-trade refurbishment, project management should connect owner decisions, procurement and contractor sequencing. Provide contracts, the budget limit and non-negotiable milestones. Request a programme covering approval, ordering, installation and inspection of long-lead items such as doors and kitchens. The manager should log every proposed change with its time and cost implications before authorisation, rather than relying on scattered messages. Compare decision authority, meeting attendance and payment-claim review. Receive an up-to-date decisions register, risk log, cost forecast and programme status.

### 24. Work quality review

A tiled surface may appear level and paint may look complete while defects remain outside a general photograph. Identify the exact element, room, specified product and approved sample. For bathrooms, checking floor falls is different from testing waterproofing before it is covered. Require an inspection checklist distinguishing visual observations, measurements and tests needing a specialist. Compare the sample area, rooms covered and reinspection allowance. Each reported defect should have a reference number, location, photograph and acceptance criterion, followed by evidence of correction.

### 25. Project stage acceptance

Do not wait until final handover to discover services hidden behind plasterboard or waterproofing beneath tiles. Agree inspection hold points for structural reinforcement, water-line pressure tests, wet-area membranes and ceiling closure as applicable. Before each visit, gather current drawings, test results and photographs taken before concealment, and identify who may authorise continuation. Compare proposals by stages, visit count and repeat testing. A stage record must separate accepted, rejected and outstanding items; acceptance of one stage is not acceptance of the whole project.

### 26. Progress report preparation

To assess a delay or validate a contractor's claim, a report must link measurable work to the baseline programme. Supply schedule revisions, measured quantities, variation orders and delivery dates. Separate physical progress from expenditure: paying for a kitchen before installation does not mean installation is complete. Request dated photographs tied to work zones, a view of critical activities and decisions needed from the owner. Compare providers by site-visit frequency and evidence verification. Receive a report disclosing data sources and assumptions rather than unsupported percentages.

### 27. Quantity surveying

First specify whether the take-off supports an early budget, tender pricing or payment verification, since the detail required changes. Provide one coordinated drawing issue and finish specifications, and state whether areas are net or gross. For floors, distinguish measured room areas from cutting waste; for painting, clarify opening deductions and number of coats; for concrete, measure volumes by element. Require units, drawing references, quantities and assumptions, with material and installation separated when useful. Do not accept an untraceable grand total.

### 28. Contractor bid review

When two villa refurbishment quotations differ substantially, check whether they price the same work. One may include debris removal, waterproofing reinstatement and testing while the other excludes them. Give the reviewer one scope, quantity schedule, sample list, target completion date and payment terms. Request a line-by-line matrix of omissions, alternatives, warranties and specified product brands. Ambiguities should be sent to bidders as written clarifications before ranking. Receive a comparison showing allowance for missing items, risks and potential variations; the cheapest headline price is not automatically the best offer.

## Interior design and material selection

### 29. Majlis design

Start a majlis design with expected seating capacity, hospitality service, entrance position and access to washrooms. Supply a dimensioned plan, ceiling height and photographs of columns, windows and air-conditioning outlets. Request a layout showing circulation around seats and tables, service routes, ceiling sections, lighting and cleanable finishes. Compare proposals by construction drawings, material schedules and revision rounds. Accept a dimensioned furniture plan rather than a perspective image alone.


### 30. Living room design

A living room should be planned around everyday seating, viewing and movement before choosing a palette. Measure walls and openings, record television and equipment dimensions, and locate sockets and air outlets. Ask for alternative sofa and storage layouts showing door swings and clear walking routes, plus a lighting and device plan. Compare design fees separately from furniture supply and installation. Check final dimensions, the item schedule and finish selections before ordering.


### 31. Bedroom design

For a bedroom, establish bed size, wardrobe-door clearance and whether a desk or dressing table is needed. Photograph and measure window offsets, ceiling drops and switch positions. The design should preserve comfortable circulation around the bed, accessible storage and reading light without direct glare. Request wardrobe elevations, sections and drawer dimensions; compare board finishes, hinges and lighting. Accept a measured schedule checked against the room before fabrication.


### 32. Kitchen design

Kitchen design starts with a complete appliance list, actual appliance sizes, cooking habits, drainage, water feeds and extraction routes. Measure the finished room and photograph columns, windows and ventilation openings. Request plans, elevations and sections showing worktop heights, drawer swings and the relationship between preparation, cooking and washing. Compare cabinet carcasses, edge treatment, hinges, counters and ventilation specifications. Receive a coordinated services drawing before cabinets are fabricated.


### 33. Bathroom design

For an existing bathroom, document drainage positions, floor falls, supply heights and any known waterproofing history, alongside clear door and shower dimensions. Request a layout showing sanitary fittings, ventilation, lighting and details for wet areas, edges and joints. Do not assume a toilet or shower can be relocated without checking pipe routes and levels. Compare design offers by waterproofing details, tile and fitting selections. Receive a dimensioned product schedule and drawings allowing maintenance access.


### 34. Furniture layout planning

Furniture planning requires a reliable measured plan, not symbols placed over a photograph. List the dimensions of furniture being retained, and measure circulation routes, openings, door swings and drawer clearance. Ask for a scaled layout showing seating, storage, lighting and socket positions, with proposed item dimensions. Compare alternatives for usable circulation and whether larger pieces can pass through entrances or lifts where relevant. Accept a numbered furniture schedule cross-referenced to the plan before placing orders.


### 35. Interior 3D visualisation

Three-dimensional visualisation helps test the apparent scale, lighting and combination of finishes before work begins, but cannot replace measurements or working drawings. Supply an updated floor plan, photographs, window positions, ceiling heights and selected material references. Agree the number of views, image resolution, revision rounds and whether editable model files are included. Compare rendering fees separately from construction detailing. At handover, check furniture and opening dimensions and material references; simulated lighting is not proof of the on-site result.


### 36. Office design

Office design starts with headcount, work patterns, meeting needs and privacy rather than the maximum number of desks that fit. Supply a measured plan showing columns, windows and existing HVAC, power and data outlets. Request a layout covering circulation, printing, storage and meeting spaces, with lighting, acoustic and partition-height considerations. Compare proposals by drawings, furniture schedules and service-point coordination. At handover, verify desk capacity against actual clearances and connection details.


### 37. Retail store design

For a retail shop, begin with merchandise type, display methods, stock storage and customer and staff circulation. Measure the storefront, clear height, columns and delivery entrance; identify any special refrigeration or electrical loads. Request shelving, checkout, back-of-house and access layouts, plus detailed display elevations. Compare alternatives for merchandising flexibility and cleaning and maintenance access. Receive dimensioned fixture schedules rather than relying solely on promotional renderings.


### 38. Restaurant and café design

Restaurant or café design must distinguish customer, staff, food and waste movements before decoration is selected. Provide the intended menu, operating capacity, room dimensions and available drainage, ventilation and electrical services. Request coordinated kitchen, preparation, storage, seating and service layouts, including cleanable surfaces, lighting and acoustics. Compare design proposals by equipment and services drawings and professional review of applicable official requirements. Accept equipment schedules, dimensions and clear routes; no operating approval is implied.


### 39. Salon design

For a salon, establish the number of workstations, services offered, waiting areas, wash stations and storage. Measure the premises and photograph existing water, drainage, sockets and ventilation. The plan should show working clearances, staff movement, mirror positions and lighting suitable for colour work. Compare proposals by moisture-resistant furniture details, concealed services and ease of surface cleaning. Receive a workstation schedule and coordinated services layout ready for detailed design.


### 40. Clinic interior design

Clinic design depends on the clinical specialty, actual procedures, expected equipment and number of examination rooms; an ordinary office layout is not sufficient. Supply equipment sizes and manufacturer utility requirements, a measured plan and patient access routes. Request appropriate waiting, examination and storage zoning and a qualified professional's review of current health and regulatory requirements before approval. Compare offers by technical coordination and drawing detail. Receive equipment and services plans with unresolved matters recorded.


### 41. Exhibition design

For an exhibition space, identify display types, weights, dimensions, changeover frequency and visitor interaction. Document ceiling heights, columns, exits and power locations. Request visitor circulation, sightline, lighting and display-fixture layouts. Heavy or suspended exhibits may require a specialist's load and fixing review. Compare schemes by adaptability, exhibit protection and maintenance access. Receive support and base details and lighting schedules.


### 42. Reception area design

Reception design must serve both first impressions and everyday operations. Define expected visitors, staff tasks and whether guests wait or complete forms. Measure the entrance and desk area and locate power and data points. Request a layout showing queues, conversation privacy, accessible circulation and movement behind the counter, with durable finish specifications. Compare offers by whether joinery, lighting and signage are included. Accept construction details for the counter and a coordinated services plan.


### 43. Floor finish selection

Match each room's use, moisture, cleaning and loads to the existing substrate. Request physical samples, slip-performance data, installation method and joints. Compare material costs including substrate preparation and waste; receive a room-by-room floor schedule.

### 44. Paint colour selection

Assess daylight, window orientation, furniture and adjacent colours. Test painted wall samples under different lighting; compare sheen and washability. Receive exact colour codes, finishes and application locations.

### 45. Stone and tile selection

Define locations, moisture exposure, tile size and traffic. Inspect samples for veining, variation, absorption and texture. Compare cutting waste, adhesives and grout; receive setting-out drawings, starting points and reference samples.

### 46. Furniture specification

Set budget, dimensions, daily use, quantities and warranty expectations. Compare frames, upholstery, filling, maintenance and quoted lead times without assuming availability. Receive an itemised furniture schedule with approved dimensions, colours and materials.

### 47. Lighting selection

Identify tasks, general and accent lighting needs. Review colour temperature, glare, controls, maintenance and ceiling compatibility. Compare performance specifications, not fixture count alone; receive a luminaire schedule and control circuit plan.

### 48. Material mood board

Combine flooring, wall, fabric, metal and wood samples with supplier references. Compare colour harmony, texture, cleaning and batch variation. Receive a dated board linking each physical sample to its intended location rather than relying only on images.

### 49. Finish schedule preparation

After material approval, map each room to wall, floor, ceiling and skirting finishes, colour codes and reference quantities. Check consistency with drawings and samples and clarify supply-versus-installation scope. Receive a revision-controlled schedule suitable for pricing and execution.

## Brief before hiring a designer or consultant

Submit an as-is drawing, even a measured sketch, proposed changes, occupants, furniture to retain, photographs and an indicative budget. Specify whether the deliverable is a 3D visualisation, construction drawings, quantity schedule or site supervision; these are not interchangeable. Where no firm is listed, Biet Al Reef customer support can take the brief as a quotation enquiry without suggesting professional approval or a government permit.

**Common question:** Are renderings enough to build from? No. Agree drawings, measured dimensions, specifications, electrical and HVAC coordination and execution details.
